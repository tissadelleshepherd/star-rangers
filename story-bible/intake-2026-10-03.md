## *A hundred small later weathers*

Verbatim: *"A hundred small little weathers?"* (on S07E01C03, *Enough Is
Enough*), then *"What is a Weather in this context ?"*, then *"Change it to
'in a hundred small ways, afterwards'"*.

### The reading

The line read *a hundred small later weathers*: a count-noun stretch on the
idiom *in all weathers*, meaning the price Wender paid for leaving the
Dock Seven file open arrived as many small, later, unattributable spells of
institutional cold. The stretch caused a stop at first reading, and he
chose the plainer form put back to him. Changed in place; wording only,
sense and canon facts unchanged. A clarification in the CLAUDE.md sense,
at his direction, on a published chapter.

His reason, given after the change (verbatim: *"It was very poetic but I
worried that some readers might be confused."*): the figure was kept out
for legibility, not for being wrong. Worth holding as a working rule for
the prose, beside the Journal's *impossible to misunderstand outranks plain
language*: a figure that earns its stop stays; a figure whose stop a reader
cannot resolve from the sentence goes, however good it is. The original
line is preserved above for the record.

**Direction check:** no ruling involved; a stated preference, recorded.

### Refined, the same day: *"It is valid in dialogue, including an internal dialogue, or at the contemplative reading tier. Maybe I am underestimating the reader at the general reading tier?"*

The fact that decided it: the sentence sits inside Wender's `:::pov` block,
and the house style (`prose-style.md` §5) reads a block in its character's
idiom, free indirect. So the figure was Wender's thought, not the
Archive's narration, which the session had wrongly said the day before.
The rule as he states it is **voice, not tier**: a figure is valid in
dialogue, in internal dialogue (a POV block) and at the contemplative
tier; it is not valid in the Archive's own voice (lore, glossary, anything
outside a block). On the reader: the general tier is *deeper* and its
reader resolves such a figure routinely; the stop in the original came
from three modifiers stacked before an unusual plural, not from the plural.
Three shapes put: (1) restore with one modifier fewer, *in a hundred small
weathers, afterwards*, recommended; (2) restore the original exactly; (3)
keep the plain line.

**Ruled the same day: *"Option 1"*.** The line now reads *learned the
price of it in a hundred small weathers, afterwards*. The working rule
recorded above is amended by this: the first test is whose sentence it is,
and the resolvability test applies after that.

**Direction check:** a refinement of the morning's preference, not a
reversal; the plain line stood for a few hours and is superseded by his
own rule. Nothing open.
