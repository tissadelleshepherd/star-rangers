# Contact design — where two peoples meet, and what carries between them

Planning note; not rendered on the site. Written 20 October 2026 at Dermot's
question, verbatim: *"environmental suits or communication channels for
different forms or people to interact in different environments"*, and his
choice of option **A** the same day: a story-bible sibling on how and where two
peoples meet, covering hulls, suits, shared rooms, translating sets and
couriers. The seventh sibling of `species-design.md`, `mind-design.md`,
`language-design.md`, `starship-design.md`, `civilian-dress.md` and
`ecology-design.md`. Drafted on Opus. Every point is marked **derived** (it
follows from a page cited by path, or from real physics or physiology applied
to one) or **proposed** (a choice that fits, his to overrule). **Not
established** means the record is silent and this note leaves it so. Nothing
here is canon, and nothing here is written to `src/`.

`alien-contact-constraints.md` is a different note: it maps what canon commits
the unwritten *first-contact* prose to, in dates and motives. This one is about
the ordinary meeting after contact, the room, the wall and the channel.

**Verify before trusting.** Each quotation names its page. A quoted line can go
stale as the record grows; check the page before a chapter leans on this file.

---

## Purpose and rules

### Why this file exists

The record is precise about what refuses to carry between peoples and vague
about what does. It refuses the universal translator and the ansible, and it
gives no deck where every people breathes one air. It says *"Meetings happen in rooms somebody built"*
(`src/lore/habitable-for-whom.md`), and it names pressure hulls, a sealed
burrow, humid sections, a floater chamber, a resonance bay, a translating set
and armoured proxies, but no page says what any of them is like to stand
beside, what crosses its wall, or who speaks through it. Without that, a
chapter gets the genre's defaults: the alien in a lit tank, the box that speaks
for it, the suit with a robot's face. This file is the ground to stand on
before a chapter asks.

### What every meeting already obeys (derived)

1. **Every meeting happens in a room somebody built.** *"With no shared ground
   anywhere in the catalogue, a human and a Meridian can only ever meet inside
   an envelope one of them manufactured"* (`habitable-for-whom.md`). An
   *envelope* there is the band of gravity, pressure, chemistry, temperature
   and light a species lives in unassisted, and *"nobody's envelope is the
   universe's default."* So every room in this file belongs to somebody, and
   the record should be able to say whose.
2. **There is no universal translator.** The machine aligns, proposes and
   *"never loses a morpheme"*; the linguist decides what a word means *"and
   own[s] the error"*; the machine's output is *"candidate readings with
   confidence stated, never a voice in your ear pretending to be the alien"*
   (`src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`, *Signal and Distance*;
   `src/codex/xenolinguistics-handbook-failure-modes.md`).
3. **Nothing carries faster than a hull.** *"FTL channels transit matter; they
   do not carry standalone information"* (`src/lore/ftl-mechanics.md`). So a
   meeting between two worlds is a courier, and the Rangers are *"the one
   carrier both sides of a dispute will trust with the same package"*
   (`src/lore/formation-of-star-rangers.md`).
4. **No human carries a mind on the body**: *"no earpiece that whispers, no
   implant that speaks"* (`what-the-record-refuses.md`). **Nothing glows,
   nothing stands in the air**: displays are *"matte, still, unlit"*, light
   *"recessed and diffuse"* (same, *The Furniture of the Future*). A port, a
   hull or a translating set is unlit, and the light either side of a wall is
   the room's.
5. **The modesty bar and the tone rule** (`CLAUDE.md`). A body behind a port is
   never on display, and a wall between two peoples is never a cage or a
   threat.
6. ***Alien* is only for the unknown** (`CLAUDE.md`). Every people here is known
   and filed, so none is alien.
7. **A singular exception stays singular.** The Krenyi alone share the human
   frame and Tír na nÓg alone the unmodified human envelope (`CLAUDE.md`).
   Nothing below makes a second people who shares a human's room without
   cost.

### A suit is a small hull (derived)

The question that started this file asked about suits first. The record's
answer is that a suit is the smallest case of the same thing.

- A suit is a room built round one body and carried with it, keeping one
  envelope against another as a hull does. The record has them: *"olive
  pressure suits"* on Callisto (`src/lore/callisto-station.md`, its image).
  The Ranger field uniform is part of the way there, *"a modular shell over a
  thermal-and-pressure liner"* (`src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`),
  worn by an Ilveth *"as a cooling shell"* (`src/lore/ilveth.md`).
- A suit cannot put two bodies in one envelope. A suited human in a hydrogen
  hull is still behind a wall, and the oxygen the suit carries is what the hull
  exists to keep out (gap 1). So the Ollune page's *"unsuited"* is exact, and
  *"the physical distance is permanent"* holds with a suit on
  (`src/lore/ollune.md`, *Interspecies Notes*).
- Where the outside kills in a breath, the record sends no suit. Meridian is
  lethal within *"a few unprotected breaths"*, and the answer is a platform and
  a proxy (`src/lore/planets/meridian.md`; gap 5). A proxy is a suit with
  nobody in it.

### The two crossings (proposed, as a frame)

The Ollune page holds the clearest pattern in the record, and it generalises.
An Ollune has two channels, and *"the entire structure of Ollune law is the
consequence of not confusing them"*: **the far word** is sound, at range, and
*"binds nothing"*; **the near word** is a chemical mark, slow, attributable,
and *"binds everything"* (`ollune.md`).

Every wall in this file has the same two crossings, whoever is on either side.

- **What is heard crosses as signal.** Sound, strikes, light, posture. It can be
  carried through a wall by an instrument that passes the signal and decides
  nothing: a microphone, a plate, a camera, a relay.
- **What is kept crosses as an object or a record.** A mark, a signature, a
  log, a filed rendering. It is carried physically, by a hand or a courier, and
  the Archive's rule governs it: *"the original is the document; the
  conversion is the courtesy"* (`src/lore/timekeeping-and-the-common-record.md`).

Between the two crossings stands a named person who renders one into the other
and owns the error. Keeping the two crossings apart is the whole defence
against the speaking box: a device that passed signal *and* decided meaning
would be the refused translator.

### The traps

- **The universal translator**, in any housing: a collar, a pendant, a panel or
  a ship's voice. The test: does the device decide what anything *means*? If
  yes, it is the refused device.
- **The clear-dome alien, the aquarium.** A being in a lit tank, the human side
  dark, the glass the frame of a picture. It makes a people a spectacle, and
  implies that whoever built the tank keeps the being.
- **The speaking box.** A box from which another people's words come out in the
  listener's language, in the speaker's place: the translator with its housing
  showing. The record's version is a slate that shows a rendering, and a
  person who reads it out (`src/seasons/s10/e01/s10e01c07.md`; gap 2).
- **The suit as a robot or a monster.** An armoured shell with a blank visor
  reads as a machine or a threat. A suit is kit for the place, which *"shows
  its seals and fittings plainly, never as glowing trim"* (`civilian-dress.md`).
- **The holodeck clause.** No walk-in room fakes a place where two peoples meet
  without a wall: *"There is no such room in this record, and no scene will be
  set in one"* (`what-the-record-refuses.md`). A rig is allowed and priced.
- **The one-air bridge.** A deck where every people stands in one air, light and
  gravity, at ease. Several peoples tolerate standard air, and the pages state
  a price for most of them (gap 3).

## 1. The Ollune hull

### What the record says

- *"A handful of Ollune travel, in Federation-built pressure hulls maintaining
  a warm hydrogen envelope at Cirrane's temperate-shell conditions, at an
  expense no other member's delegates incur. Most do not."* (`ollune.md`,
  *Federation Membership*). Contact is *"conducted through hulls and
  viewports"* (*Interspecies Notes*).
- The position on assembly matters is *"carried rather than spoken, arriving by
  courier, always lagged, and read into the record by someone who is not
  Ollune"* (same). In S01E00C08 the mark *"lay before this session's reader"*,
  and *"It was read by a reader, in a rendering"*
  (`src/seasons/s01/e00/s01e00c08.md`).
- **The far word** is *"low-frequency sound, which in a dense hydrogen
  atmosphere travels several times faster than in Earth's air"*,
  *"unblockable"*, and it *"vanishes"*. **The near word** is *"secreted markers
  read at close range, slow to compose, slower to disperse, individually
  distinct to the body that produced them, and persistent enough to be
  examined days later"*, and *"you cannot mark someone who is not willing to
  hold station beside you"* (`ollune.md`).
- A mark *"is a physical, durable, chemically distinct object"*; a carrier
  *"cannot forge or alter one"* and *"can read every word they carry"*
  (`ollune.md`, *Trade*). Ollune are *"unusually literal about what other
  species sign"*, and none *"has ever seen a flame except through a Federation
  viewport"* (same).
- *"In a hull under thrust or in freefall, buoyancy stops mattering. Lift costs
  nothing"* (same). The body is an envelope *"about eight metres across"*,
  tendrils *"two or three metres long"*, with *"No face, eyes, mouth, head or
  limbs"* (`species-design.md`, *Ollune — a body from Cirrane*).

### What it derives

- **Size** (derived, from the body section). The hull must hold an envelope
  eight metres across with tendrils of three metres hanging from it, and leave
  room to turn. A clear interior of about fifteen metres is the least that
  does. A sphere carries a pressure difference with the least wall for its
  volume, so the natural form is close to a sphere or a short drum.
- **No spin** (derived, physics applied to the page). Lift on warm gas is a
  fraction of weight set by the temperature difference, so under spun weight
  the Ollune's whole bill returns. In freefall it is gone, and the page's
  *"deepest rest a body can know"* is available. So the hull rides where the
  ship or station has no spin: at a non-rotating hub, or on a ship's spine
  (`starship-design.md`, principles 6 and 7). Under a ship's thrust there is a
  floor, aft, and the page's *"they can be set down"* is meant literally: the
  Ollune rests on it.
- **Hydrogen beside air is the hazard** (derived, real chemistry). Hydrogen
  burns in air from about four to seventy-five per cent by volume, ignites from
  a tiny spark, burns almost invisibly in daylight, passes through many
  materials and embrittles high-strength steels. So the wall is doubled, with a
  watched inert gas between the skins, and nothing crosses to the air side but
  through a lock purged between its doors. The one place an Ollune could meet
  fire is a human vessel, and the hull is built so that it never does.
- **Food** (derived). The shell's web *"runs on the haze"*, photochemical
  aerosol falling in sunlight (`src/lore/planets/cirrane.md`). No haze forms in
  a hull. The Ollune must be fed.
- **Light** (derived). The Ollune sense light: they have seen flame *"through
  a Federation viewport"*. How, without eyes, is **not established**.
- **The far word does not cross a hull on its own** (derived, real acoustics).
  Sound passing from a gas into a solid wall and out into another gas loses
  most of its energy at each face, and hydrogen's acoustic impedance is about a
  quarter of air's at the same pressure, which makes the loss worse. Low
  frequencies pass a wall best, so at most a faint low hum reaches the air
  side. *"Unblockable"* at home, the far word is blocked by a hull, and must be
  picked up in the hydrogen and sounded again in the air, and the reverse.
- **If the far word lies below human hearing**, it must also be shifted up.
  Whether it does is **not established**; the Ovruhn under-song is the
  record's case of a speech-sound *"felt rather than heard by most species"*
  (`src/lore/ovruhn.md`). And the far word is **the safe channel for a
  machine**: it *"binds nothing"* and names no speaker, so an error in carrying
  it costs nobody an agreement.
- **The near word crosses as an object, through a lock** (derived). A mark is a
  movable object, so it can pass out of the hull as couriers already carry it
  across bands. It needs *"mutual consent to a shared drift"*, so the human
  counterpart must hold station at the hull, present at the lock for as long
  as the mark takes to compose, which the page says is slow.
- **A mark keeps longer sealed and cold** (derived, chemistry). It persists
  *"days"* in the open, the courier lag is months or years, and in C08 marks
  arrive and are read. Decay slows steeply with cold and stops for want of a
  reagent, so a mark kept in its own hydrogen, cold, never opened to oxygen,
  can outlast the passage. How long is **not established**.
- **A mark is read by instrument and rendered by a person** (derived). No human
  reads chemistry unaided, so an instrument samples the mark and the reader of
  C08 renders it and owns the error.
- **An answer to an Ollune is an import** (derived). *"An import has to be lifted
  by the body that accepts it, permanently"*, and *"the only imports worth
  having on Cirrane are weightless"* (`ollune.md`). A signed paper handed to an
  Ollune is a charge on that body forever once they are home.

### Proposed

Named by function. Four names are coined, and nothing else.

- **The hydrogen hull** (proposed) for the Federation-built pressure hull
  of `ollune.md`. *Warm hull* was considered and refused: the record already
  uses it for a hull kept at human warmth, where an Ilveth's thermal register
  is *"unusable"* (`ilveth.md`; `src/characters/suvra-kel.md`). The Ollune's
  own word for it is **not established**.
  - **Form.** Close to a sphere, about fifteen metres clear inside; a doubled
    wall with a watched inert interspace; carried as a module that docks at a
    non-rotating hub or rides a ship's spine, never opening to the host's air.
    About twenty metres across outside, dull, patched, unlit
    (`starship-design.md`, principle 5). A soft cradle on the aft face for
    thrust.
  - **Inside.** Hydrogen-helium at the temperate shell's conditions; a
    diffuse light from concealed fixtures on Vantine's spectrum, dim and amber;
    the Ollune and nothing else. The hull is the Federation's, and the life it
    feeds on is the Ollune's (next point).
  - **Food.** A cultured aerosol of Cirrane lineages, released into the hull's
    air. Ollune technology is *"entirely biochemical"*, *"a cultivated lineage
    of organisms doing the work at ambient temperature"* (`ollune.md`), so the
    hull is fed by an Ollune craft, grown, not a Federation synthesis.
- **The port** (proposed, the page's *viewport*). One flat, thick, plain port
  of a metre or two in the hull's side, set in a wall of the host room at a
  human's height. The light comes from each side's own room. The Ollune beyond
  it is large and amber in their own dim light, and partly out of view; they
  are never lit for the viewer, and the human side is never darkened for effect.
- **The sounding pair** (proposed). A pickup and a sounder on each side of the
  wall, carrying the far word out and sound in. It passes signal and decides
  nothing. Where the far word lies below hearing it is shifted up, and the
  shift is marked. Outward, a named interpreter hears and renders. Inward, the
  interpreter composes the far word and the sounder in the hydrogen voices it,
  as a player voices an instrument; the rendering is the interpreter's, and the
  Ollune knows a far word for what it is, a thing that binds nothing.
- **The mark lock** (proposed). A small lock in the hull's wall, with an inert
  purge between its doors. The Ollune marks a carrier presented on the
  hydrogen side while the counterpart holds station at the outer face. The
  carrier goes into a mark case.
- **The mark case** (proposed). A sealed vessel filled with the hull's own gas
  and kept cold, never opened in air. It has a sampling port, so a reader's
  instrument can take a trace without exposing the mark. This is what lies on
  the table in C08. The Federation file keeps a reference mark from each
  Ollune whose marks it reads, as a register keeps a specimen signature, so
  that a reading can say *whose*: a mark is *"individually distinct to the
  body"*, and an assay can match one body's marks only against a reference.
- **The reader** (derived, C08's word): the named officer, not Ollune, who
  renders a mark into the record.
- **A human's binding answer** (proposed). The Ollune hold a signature binding.
  The signed original is shown at the port and kept by the carrier and the
  Archive, never handed into the hull, because an import is lifted for as long
  as it is owned; its terms reach the Ollune in the far word, which carries
  them and binds nothing, while the signature on file is what binds. How an
  Ollune verifies a human signature is **not established**.
- **The courier.** Ranger couriers carry mark cases between worlds, on the
  charter role the record already gives them; a courier can read every mark and
  forge none.

### The Kalypsis Dawn floater chamber (a note, nothing changed)

The Lee-class interior has *"A low-gravity floater chamber with electromagnetic
scaffolding, for atmosphere-dwelling passengers with no use for a deck"*
(`src/lore/kalypsis-dawn.md`). The page does not say what gas it holds. As
written it cannot be assumed to serve an Ollune: an Ollune needs hydrogen at
the temperate shell's conditions, and hydrogen inside a ship of air needs the
doubled wall, the watched interspace and the purged lock above, none of which
the page gives. Not a contradiction, since the page names no people. **Derived
reading:** an Ollune aboard a Lee-class travels in their own hydrogen hull, docked
to the spine, unless a later page fits the chamber as one. Nothing in `src/` is
changed by this file.

### Traps

- **The aquarium.** No lit tank or clear dome, no Ollune in a spotlight with
  people looking in from the dark: a plain port in a wall, each room's own light.
- **The lantern.** No glowing core; the amber is light through a translucent
  body (`species-design.md`).
- **The Ollune ship.** The Ollune *"could never have reached space"*
  (`ollune.md`); the hull is the Federation's and never drawn as theirs.
- **The romance of the wall.** The Ollune *"are very easy to romanticise"*
  (`ollune.md`, *The Krenyi*). The port is not a parable about distance; it is
  where the work is done.

### What a page would need

If a chapter puts an Ollune in a hull: one paragraph on `ollune.md`, under
*Federation Membership*, giving the hull's name, the port, the sounding pair
and the mark lock in a sentence each. That would be an extension. The
temperate shell's numbers stay unfixed unless a chapter needs one.

## 2. Translating sets and interpreters

### What the record says

- **On a call**, an *"interpretive layer"* renders *"environment, posture and
  signal"* with confidence stated: *"What it never does is smooth them out"*
  (`what-the-record-refuses.md`). **Aboard the Lee-class**, Helion-3's output
  reaches xenolinguists *"never as a voice, never as a rendering, and never as
  an answer"* (`kalypsis-dawn.md`).
- **At Tsittak**, an account is *"rendered by Tsittak's translating set, with
  the set's declined renderings kept"*, the set standing *"on the same plate"*
  (`src/codex/nothing-turned.md`); it is drawn as *"a small plain sealed matte
  housing"* (`images.md`, Akkit's frame).
- **At Eden**, a Chthonari engineer *"said nothing in the air"* and *"struck
  something into the plating that the human engineer read off a slate, and
  said"* it (`s10e01c07.md`).
- **On Drithane**, a Chthonari engineer speaks to Sorcha *"through the flat
  little voice of a translation pendant"*, and later *"with what the pendant
  rendered as care"* (`src/seasons/s02/e04/s02e04c01.md`, Sorcha's POV).
- **With the Cerebraun**, *"every exchange between a Cerebraun and a human passes
  through machine translation"*: the instruments render the stated position,
  and *"The skin they leave as it was"* (`src/lore/cerebraun.md`).
- **At Meridian**, a petition is *"rendered by the relay station's translation
  instrumentation"*, after *"the usual half-hour of the instrumentation arguing
  with itself over connotation"* (`src/seasons/s03/e01/s03e01c03.md`).
- **At the assembly**, *"Three worlds' translations of it did not agree on the
  preposition"*, and a witness's voice is taken by *"the recorder's housing at
  the chair's end"* (`s01e00c08.md`).

### What it derives

- **One machine half, many housings** (derived). The interpretive layer,
  Helion-3, a translating set, the relay station's instrumentation and the
  Fliade relay's classifier all do the same job: they align, propose and grade.
  None owns a meaning. The record's examples already show the honest outputs:
  a reading with its confidence (the layer), a declined rendering (the set),
  an argument over connotation (the relay station), a class with no name on it
  (the Fliade unit, *"sound, rising"*, S14E02C01).
- **The house form for a rendering is a sheet, read by a person** (derived from
  C07). The engineer strikes, the slate shows, a human reads it aloud and is
  the one who said it.
- **The pendant on Drithane** (a reading, proposed and settled here; nothing
  changed).
  S02E04C01 is a published chapter and binds; so does the refusal of any
  device that *"renders an unknown tongue at first hearing"* or *"speaks
  anyone's language back to them a beat behind their lips"*. Both stand on one
  reading. *Translation pendant* is Sorcha's word, in Sorcha's POV, for a thing
  she is meeting for the first time. What it does is give voice to words the engineer
  composes, in Sorcha's own language, by striking: the slate of C07 read aloud
  by a housing rather than by a colleague. It decides nothing, so the refusal
  is untouched, and *rendered as care* is Sorcha hearing the engineer's own
  marked tone. The Chthonari who serve already write in the Corps' hand
  (*"a crew's work log"*, `nothing-turned.md`, receiving note), so composing in
  a human language is within the page. Precedent for leaving the chapter as
  written: of *shuttle*, *"published chapters that already use it stand as
  written"* (`CLAUDE.md`, *No orbital towers*).
- **The reverse direction is the gap** (derived). Every case above runs from the
  other people to the human. How a human's words reach a Chthonari, who is deaf
  to air, is **not established** on any page.
- **A room has no house interpreter** (derived from C08). Three worlds' own
  translations disagreed on one preposition, and the assembly called a witness
  rather than an interpreter to settle what was said. So each seat hears
  through its own translators, and the disagreement among them is itself on the
  record. This is consistent with shape (a) of `language-design.md`'s open
  item, *every standing delegation works in the other side's language, with
  named translators and error bars*, and with the same file's *How a Ranger crew
  talks on deck* (no Corps tongue; interpreters where a crew shares nothing),
  and decides nothing that item leaves open.
- **The speaker's own words are kept** (derived). C08's recorder takes
  Ulessa's voice; the Archive's rule files the original with the conversion
  beside it.

### Proposed

- **Translating set** (proposed as the house word) for the machine half where it
  stands as a housing in a room: a plain sealed matte box, unlit, standing on
  or at the medium it reads (on the plate for the Chthonari; at the hull for
  the Ollune). Its output goes to a sheet, with its declines marked, or to a
  voice for words a person composed. It never speaks a meaning of its own.
- **Three named roles**, all in the record already: the **xenolinguist** (the
  handbook's *"you"*), who decides and owns the reading; the **reader** (C08),
  who renders a kept thing, a mark, into the record; the **translator** of a
  seat, who renders for that seat alone (C08's *"three worlds' translations"*).
  A rendering is entered with its maker's name: *"your name goes on the
  reading"* (handbook, failure 8).
- **Human to Chthonari** (proposed, minimal): writing, on a sheet the
  Chthonari reads. It is the C07 slate run the other way, and it needs no new
  device. A translating set on the plate may strike a composed text into the
  structure for a Chthonari who prefers it; whether any does is not proposed.
- **A mixed room** is served by each seat's own translators, a recorder at the
  chair, and, for a people that cannot be in the room, a reader.

### Traps

- **The earpiece and the collar on a person.** Refused for humans by the gate.
  A Chthonari's pendant voices a Chthonari's composed words; a human never
  wears a device that speaks to them, or for them.
- **The ship's voice.** Helion-3 never speaks as an interpreter.
- **The smoothed room.** A meeting where everyone understands everyone at once.
  C08 is the counter-example: three translations, one witness, and a minute that
  says *noted*.
- **The weekend fluency** (`language-design.md`): no character picks up a
  language between chapters.

### What a page would need

Nothing yet. If a chapter shows the reverse channel to a Chthonari, one
sentence on `src/lore/chthonari.md`, *Interspecies Notes*, would carry it as an
extension. If a page ever names a pendant, it should say what the pendant does
in the Archive's voice, as the reading above gives it.

## 3. Shared rooms

### What the record says

- **The facilities standard.** The Chthonari, Mnemari, Verdani and Prismeri
  tolerate *"standard humanoid life-support"* (their pages), and the word is the
  standard's: a Mnemari *"shares a room, an atmosphere and a table with a human
  without special arrangement. It was never a description of the body"*
  (`src/lore/mnemari.md`).
- **The C08 room**: *"long and pale, the table long and low, the light from a
  slot above it, soft and even and, to a kinbed, worth nothing at all"*. Ulessa
  comes in *"not through the door so much as past it"*; Isren *"had her vanes
  folded, which was correct in a room and cost her"*; the Prismere delegate is
  *"on the floor for it, which was not cowering"* (`s01e00c08.md`).
- **The Chthonari** need *"a sealed, pressurized burrow"* (`chthonari.md`); on
  Eden *"the module was sealed, the crew was four on one deck"*
  (`s10e01c02.md`); the Undersong form of a rank marks whether the officer is
  felt *"aboard her own station"* or *"over a relay"*
  (`src/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`).
- **The Pelagene**: Mira *"performs best in humid, high-pressure working
  sections"* (`src/characters/mira-of-brine.md`); *"A Pelagene ashore is slowly
  drying"* (`src/lore/pelagene-littoral.md`).
- **The Lee-class** carries *"A pressurised aquatic corridor"* and *"A
  vibration-stable resonance bay for people whose speech is carried in a solid
  medium, isolated from the ship's own structural noise"* (`kalypsis-dawn.md`).
- **Aspenar** links the Doreth's sealed sections to human domes *"only through
  pressure-locked transit corridors rather than shared air"*
  (`src/lore/planets/aspenar.md`).
- **Prices on the pages.** A Cerebraun in station air *"is not harmed by it"*
  but comes *"to dislike it"* (`src/lore/cerebraun.md`); Ilveth keep *"chilled
  quarters"* (`ilveth.md`); for Prismeri *"what no contract can supply is a
  ceiling worth hanging from"* (`src/lore/prismeri.md`); the Ovruhn under-song
  is *"felt through deck plate by anyone with the sense to read it"*
  (`ovruhn.md`).

### What it derives

- **A room is five choices, each somebody's** (derived from
  `habitable-for-whom.md`): air, gravity, floor, light and door. The
  facilities standard fixes the first and says nothing of the rest. So a
  people *"fully compatible"* with the standard still meets a room built for
  other bodies at the door, the table and the light, as Ulessa does. Compatible
  means breathable, not fitted, and C08 is not in conflict with `mnemari.md`.
- **Every people in a shared room pays something, and the record shows it**
  (derived from C08). Ulessa at the door, Isren with vanes folded in a slot of
  light, the Prismere seat on the floor with no beam. No room in the record
  fits everyone, and the C08 room fits nobody but the people who built it,
  whom the chapter does not name.
- **The Chthonari need a floor that carries** (derived). They hear through
  *"the hull, through the deck plating"* (`chthonari.md`). A deck damped or
  floated against vibration, which human ships build for comfort, is a deaf
  floor to them. The Lee-class resonance bay is isolated from the ship's own
  noise, not from the people in it. In a mixed room, a human's footfalls are
  noise in the Chthonari channel, and an Ovruhn's under-song on the same plate
  is felt by any Chthonari standing on it: two peoples' channels meeting in one
  structure.
- **A Chthonari away from the module is over a relay** (derived from the ladder
  page): the Undersong can be carried, and the grammar marks that it was.
- **The Pelagene section, in numbers** (derived, physiology). *Humid* means air
  near saturation; *high-pressure* means above standard. A section a few tenths
  of a bar above standard is a few metres of seawater's worth, a range divers'
  tables treat as carrying no decompression stop, so human crew can work in it
  and walk out. Two consequences follow. The oxygen fraction must be lowered so
  that its partial pressure stays at standard, or the section's fire risk rises
  with its pressure. And near saturation a human cannot cool by sweating, so
  the section must be kept cool. Its cold panels carry condensation, which is
  what Mira reads (*"Condensation patterns on cold panels"*).
- **Locks between envelopes are already the record's answer** (derived from
  Aspenar). Where two peoples' airs cannot mix, the record links them by a
  pressure-locked corridor, not by a membrane or a field.

### Proposed

- **The kept room** (C08's own phrase, proposed as the type). A Federation room
  in the facilities standard's air and weight, kept for sessions, built to no
  people's proportions in particular and to some people's in fact. It is fitted
  for each seat by what can be carried in, never rebuilt. A beam for a
  Prismeri seat where one can be hung (`mind-design.md`: *"hold meetings
  somewhere with a beam"*), a full-spectrum lamp for a Verdani seat, a contact
  plate for a Chthonari, a recorder at the chair. Where a fitting is missing,
  the delegate pays, and the record shows it.
- **The contact plate** (proposed). A plate set in a mixed room's floor, coupled
  by a structural member or an Undersong relay to the Chthonari module, so a
  Chthonari at the table is felt by the crew. The courtesy that comes with it:
  others keep their feet off the plate. On `language-design.md`'s rule, a
  channel that leaks gets its manners in the same breath.
- **The humid section** (proposed). Air near saturation, a few tenths of a bar
  over standard, oxygen fraction lowered to hold the partial pressure,
  temperature cool, cold-panelled. Humans enter it in the field shell and
  stay. It is a working section, not an aquarium, and the Lee-class aquatic
  corridor is a different room (the Pelagene breathe air on a station).
- **A cold room** for an Ilveth seat (proposed, from *"chilled quarters"*). In a
  warm room an Ilveth speaks click-speech only, and the record says so.
- **A Cerebraun seat** (proposed) sits where the delegate's upper skin is in view of those
  who can read it, since the second channel is the meeting (handbook,
  failure 2).

### Traps

- **The cantina and the bridge of many species.** A room where many peoples
  stand at ease in one air and one light, nobody paying.
- **The adapting room.** Walls that reshape, fields that hold one air apart from
  another, floors that change their gravity under one seat. The record links
  envelopes by locks and builds rooms of matter.
- **The species as furniture.** A non-human seat drawn as decor round a human
  table. In C08 the viewpoints are a Verdani's and a Mnemari's, and the room is
  seen from theirs.

### What a page would need

Nothing for C08: the chapter carries its own room. If a chapter sets a scene in
a Pelagene section, one sentence on `pelagene-littoral.md` could fix the
section's conditions as an extension. A contact plate would enter on
`chthonari.md` only when a chapter uses one.

## 4. Fliade

### What the record says

- The air *"is breathable without apparatus"*, and the surface party works *"in
  cold-weather clothing with nothing over the face"* (`src/lore/planets/fliade.md`).
- The **line** is where the party *"stopped... where Nakagawa said far
  enough"*: *"no unit be placed past the line and nobody cross it"*, and *"The
  party fits the squeeze"* where *"an adult Pandoid does not"* (S11E02C01).
- The **relay unit**, *"the humming stone, to the people"* (S11E01C01), wakes
  *"to a thermal trigger at zero range"* (S11E02C01) and keeps a sound channel
  in three classes (S14E02C01).
- The **knock** is *"a stone of the upper levels, the planet's own"*, set down
  first, under the rule *"nothing left behind that cannot be taken back at
  will"*, and answered when the adult *"bent and moved a rock"* (S11E02C02,
  C03). The adults *"withdraw from the moving shadow the party's light throws"*
  before the light (S11E02C02), and the handheld goes *"pointed low"* (S14).
- **Not recording**: the unit's *"sound channel off by Nakagawa's standing
  order"* for the knock, and *"No word said at the line is in any record"*
  (S14E02C01, C02); in S14E02C03 the people's speech is heard directly for the
  first time, and *"nothing said is recorded"*.

### What it derives

- **No suit, and rule 1 at its smallest** (derived). Fliade is the one place in
  this file where two peoples meet in one air on ground nobody built. The air
  is breathable but the world is cold, so the envelope the up-people
  manufacture is their clothing, and the room is the Told's own cavern. What
  keeps the two apart is a rule and a squeeze, not a wall: the line and the
  party's own order.
- **The contact gear is already in the chapters** (derived). A stone of the
  upper levels; a light held low; a relay at the junction; a handheld with its
  sound channel off; cold-weather clothing. Each was chosen in the chapters
  for a reason the chapters give.
- **What the survey does not carry is part of the method** (derived). The
  record keeps a people's speech unrecorded on purpose, so a translating set at
  the line would undo what S14E02C02 ordered.
- **The light is a channel** (derived). A people with no moving light below the
  roof read a moving shadow before a beam, so how a lamp is carried is said
  before anything is.

### Proposed

- **Nothing new.** The relay unit keeps its survey name and the people's
  *humming stone*; nothing else is named.
- **Any new item at Fliade keeps three rules already in the chapters** (proposed
  as the test): it can be taken back at will; it goes no further than the
  line; and it records nothing at the line that the order of S14E02C02 keeps
  off the record.
- **The cold-weather clothing** is clothing, with the face bare: a parka, not a
  suit. Dermot's remark that *"A human in a cold suit would almost be the size
  of a Pandoid"* (`species-design.md`, *The uniform equivalent*;
  `intake-2026-10-07.md`) is read as this clothing, and its *black and white
  markings* stay off the page, as `civilian-dress.md` already leaves them.

### Traps

- **The first-contact gadget.** No device at the line that speaks to the Told,
  and no screen that shows them pictures.
- **The Told behind glass.** There is no wall at Fliade, and none is to be drawn.
- **The beam as threat.** No light swung across them. The chapters have it
  swung once into the dark from the line (S11E01C02, S11E02C01), and the
  people read a moving shadow before a beam.
- **The xenolinguist by default.** Whether the Branch has been asked for stays
  open in `language-design.md`; this file does not answer it.

### What a page would need

Nothing. The Fliade page and the chapters hold all of it.

## 5. Meridian proxies

### What the record says

- Humans *"operate exclusively from sealed orbital or submersible platforms,
  and only ever meet Meridian sapients through translation instrumentation and
  armored remote proxies"*, and only *"on the rare occasion the Hegemony grants
  entry"* (`meridian.md`).
- The air is *"thick, halogen-heavy"*, the seas *"ammonia-saline"*, and apex
  hunters *"mass several tonnes"* (same).
- The people speak by *"claw-percussion against shell and structure — audible
  and readable underwater at considerable range"* and a slower chemical
  channel; the first address was percussion *"answering the anchor survey's own
  test pulses, pattern for pattern"* (same). A **relay station** keeps the
  *"chemical-and-percussion translation channel"* (`s03e01c03.md`).

### What it derives

- **A proxy is a suit with nobody in it** (derived). Where the outside kills in a
  breath, a breach is not an injury but a death, so the record sends a machine
  and keeps the person on a platform.
- **A proxy under water is tethered or linked by sound** (derived, real physics).
  Radio dies within metres in a conducting brine, and light within tens of
  metres in a turbid sea. So a proxy is either on a cable from a submersible
  platform or in acoustic contact with one, and the operator is near, not in
  orbit; from orbit the link passes through a platform in the water.
- **Armour is against the place** (derived). Halogens attack most metals and
  ammonia attacks copper and its alloys, so a proxy's shell is ceramic,
  composite or a passivated alloy, and its armour is against the chemistry, the
  pressure and the multi-tonne fauna. No page arms one, and a Ranger hull is
  unarmed by contract (`kalypsis-dawn.md`).
- **Percussion is the channel a proxy can answer in** (derived). The first
  exchange on Meridian was pulse for pulse. A proxy that strikes can address;
  what it strikes is composed by a person, and the chemical channel is read by
  instrument and rendered.
- **Driving a proxy is rig work** (derived). A rig is permitted and *"inherently
  tiring and a psychological stress"* (`what-the-record-refuses.md`), so an
  operator's hours are logged as exposure.

### Proposed

- **The proxy** keeps the page's word. Low, plain, mineral-grey or dull, a
  striker on a short arm, a sampling inlet for the chemical channel, a cable or
  an acoustic head. Not shaped like a Meridian body, and not like a human one.
- **Who owns it.** The Hegemony, since entry is its grant (proposed); whether the
  Rangers bring their own is **not established**.
- **Who speaks.** The operator strikes what a named linguist composed, and the
  relay station's instrumentation renders what comes back, with its
  connotation argued and its confidence stated.

### Traps

- **The crab robot and the mech.** A proxy drawn as a giant mechanical
  crustacean, or as a walking armoured suit with a visor. The first mimics the
  people, which is the borrowed-silhouette caution (`src/codex/the-borrowed-silhouette.md`);
  the second is the robot-suit trap.
- **The armed drone.** Lights, guns, a searchlight eye. None.
- **The telepresence holodeck.** The operator is in a rig and knows it, and
  tires.

### What a page would need

If a chapter shows a proxy at work: one sentence on `meridian.md` saying it is
tethered or acoustically linked from a submersible platform, as an extension.

## Inconsistencies found

1. **`language-design.md` gave the Ollune one channel in air** (fixed in this
   PR, as a clarification). Its question 1 read *"a coherent low-frequency
   signal carried in air"*. `ollune.md` gives two channels, the far word in a
   *"dense hydrogen atmosphere"* and the near word in chemistry. The bullet now
   says so; the opposite-poles-on-cost point it makes is unchanged, since that
   is the far word's.
2. **The Kalypsis Dawn floater chamber** does not name its gas, so it cannot be
   read as an Ollune berth (gap 1, the note). Nothing changed.
3. **The *translation pendant* of S02E04C01** reads against the refusal of the
   translator, and is reconciled by the reading under gap 2: Sorcha's word, a
   voice for composed words. Settled at Opus's level on the *shuttle*
   precedent; nothing changed.
4. **One sentence of `what-the-record-refuses.md` reads two ways.** It names
   translators *"like the [Ollune's]"* as *"declining, politely, to render a
   thing precisely"*, where `ollune.md` and the handbook have Chthonari
   translators declining to render the Ollune's terms. Read as *the
   translators of the Ollune's terms*, no fact conflicts. Nothing changed.
5. **The `alien` tag.** Thirteen character pages for known peoples carry
   `alien` among their tags (`mira-of-brine.md`, `qiren-tal.md`,
   `stone-first.md` among them). Tags may drive filtering, so nothing is
   changed here; a later sweep could retire the tag under the usage rule after
   checking `lib/content-filter.js` and the editions' `TOPICS`.

## Where this goes

**Nothing is published from this file.** A hull, a room or a proxy reaches its
lore page only when a chapter needs it, and then by extension, never by
rewriting. Each gap's *What a page would need* names the smallest step.

**For image prompts.** A prompt for a hull, a port, a shared room or a proxy
starts here, and from `starship-design.md` where a vessel carries it. These are
physics prompts, so they are Opus's (`CLAUDE.md`, *Which model does which
work*). Vocabulary to start from:

- **The hydrogen hull, from outside:** *a near-spherical pressure module about
  twenty metres across, dull grey, patched, doubled seams visible, docked at a
  station's non-rotating hub, unlit; no windows but one small flat port.*
- **At the port:** *a plain flat thick port in a matte pale wall at a person's
  height, light from concealed fixtures on the near side, beyond it dim amber
  haze and part of a broad translucent envelope with a dark furred cap, too
  large for the port; a person standing at an ordinary distance, at work.*
- **The kept room:** *a long pale room, a long low table, light from one slot
  above, delegates of several non-human bodies each in a posture that costs
  them something; a recorder housing at the chair's end; nothing lit, nothing
  in the air.*
**Standard negative**, for all three: *aquarium, fish tank, glass dome, bubble,
spotlight, display case, glowing, bioluminescence, hologram, screen, speaking
box, speaker grille, robot, mech, crab robot, visor face, weapons, searchlight,
force field, 3D render, CGI.*
