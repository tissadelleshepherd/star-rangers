# Intake — 26 August 2026

Open questions raised here are indexed in `open-questions.md`, per the standing
convention.

Dermot's direction, given verbatim:

> Rejecting the trope of the corrupt politician but I can identity with the
> burnt out leader.

## Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

- **"can identity with"** is read as *can identify with* — the typo-flagging
  convention from `intake-2026-08-23.md` applied.
- ~~**The scope of the rejection** is read as a *trope* rejection, not a ban
  on corruption existing in the world~~ — **ruled by Dermot, 26 August 2026,
  verbatim: "Cannot rule out minor corruption in minor or supporting
  characters."** The trope reading stands, and the ruling refines it into a
  workable line: what is refused is corruption as the *engine* of a leader or
  major figure — the venal politician as the plot's free energy source — while
  **minor corruption at the margins remains possible**: minor scale, in minor
  or supporting characters, as texture the record can carry the way it
  carries petty crime. Two bounds follow from the wording itself: both
  qualifiers are load-bearing (*minor* corruption, in *minor or supporting*
  characters), so neither grand corruption anywhere nor petty corruption in a
  protagonist is licensed by it; and "cannot rule out" is permission for the
  world to contain it, not an instruction to write it in. Note this ruling
  does **not** decide the complicit-leader question from the Smith
  comparandum — that figure is a different axis (integrity spent at major
  scale under pressure, not petty venality) and stays open below.
- **"I can identify with"** is read as a licence for *interiority*: the
  burnt-out leader may be written from inside, as a POV position, not only as
  a condition other characters observe. Under `life-lessons.md`'s own rule the
  structure enters the work and the circumstances don't — what generalises is
  the mechanism (responsibility that costs more energy than the office
  returns; the depleted leader still administratively capable; laying an
  office down as a decision rather than a failure), never any real
  particulars.

## Canon check

**The direction names a practice the corpus already runs on** — the same shape
as the canon-stability ruling of 2026-08-25. Nothing published writes the
corrupt-politician trope, and the burnt-out leader is already on the page in
several registers:

- **The Imperium's final Administrator-Supreme** (`src/lore/the-imperium.md`)
  is the strongest instance, because it sits where the trope would be
  cheapest: the setting's one authoritarian regime ends not on a venal apex
  but on an office-holder "administratively capable but politically
  exhausted," and the High Council exists to "spread the *appearance* of
  responsibility at exactly the point where the reality of it had become
  unbearable to hold alone." Burnout is written into the structure of the
  regime's end.
- **Thorne in *The Harmonic Price*** (`s01e02c05`): "the specific exhaustion
  of a man who had made exactly one correct decision under time pressure that
  morning and had not yet decided whether he trusted himself to make a second
  one calmly" — command fatigue as the scene's live material, and the rule
  the scene writes exists so a depleted officer doesn't have to be fresh to
  be safe.
- **The local administrator in `s01e03c04`**: "voice brittle with
  defensiveness worn thin by exhaustion" — a minor official defending a
  protocol failure, drawn tired rather than venal.
- **Wendell Albercombe** (`src/characters/wendell-albercombe.md`, via
  `jeeves.md`): Eden's welfare program judged a household intelligence
  "cheaper than the paperwork for a burnout" — burnout as a real
  institutional category the setting budgets against.
- **Isren Farrowkin** (`src/characters/isren-farrowkin.md`) is the
  counter-case that proves the trope refusal: the corpus's one career
  politician is written as principled persistence — eleven years losing an
  argument by a narrower margin each time, no venality anywhere on the page.
- **The thematic ground already exists**: `ideas.md`'s Finite Energy ("energy
  is a more important constraint than time") and The Limits of Virtue ("every
  virtue has a liability point"), and `life-lessons.md`'s "Energy is more
  precious than time." The burnt-out leader is those themes at the scale of
  an office.
- **One adjacent structure worth knowing about before writing**: the Mnemari
  custom of relinquishing office (`src/lore/mnemari.md`) — turnover "chosen
  rather than imposed" — is the setting's existing model of laying an office
  down well, which is the constructive half of the burnout theme.

No conflicts found. The direction is an extension — it commits future
political and command writing to a posture the published corpus already
holds, and forecloses a trope nothing published uses.

## What was done with it

- **A theme section in `ideas.md`** — *The Burnt-Out Leader, Not the Corrupt
  Politician* — carrying the corpus survey above, the connections to the
  existing themes, and what the survey found thin (nobody yet carries burnout
  as a lived arc with a recovery or a laying-down; the corpus shows the
  condition mostly at crisis or in decline-era history).
- **One distilled line added to `life-lessons.md`**: "Leadership fails by
  depletion more often than by corruption." Proposed as the generalised form
  of the direction, under that file's generalise-then-fictionalise rule;
  Dermot's to reword or drop.
- **Index lines in `open-questions.md`** for the readings above.
- **No canon touched** — no lore, glossary, codex or narrative change; this
  is a story-bible-only capture.

## Second direction, same day — the John Smith comparandum

Dermot, verbatim:

> Compare with John Smith as the antihero of The Man in the High Castle.

The comparison is worked into `ideas.md`'s burnt-out-leader section
(*Comparandum: John Smith*). The short form: Smith — the Amazon series'
invention, not Dick's — is **neither pole of the direction**. He is not the
corrupt-politician trope (venality is never his engine; the show refuses the
cartoon), and he is not the burnt-out honest leader (his exhaustion is
downstream of complicity, not of duty honestly held). He is a third figure:
the **complicit leader**, integrity spent under pressure choice by defensible
choice rather than sold. ~~The comparison therefore sharpens the
scope-of-rejection question already flagged above... a reading, not a
ruling.~~ — **ruled by Dermot, 26 August 2026, verbatim: "A major character
could be a complicit leader who repents."** The figure is in scope at
major-character scale, and the ruling names the arc's shape: **the
repentance is part of the licence.** That is the corpus doctrine applied —
repentance on the table and here *taken*, the lie killed and the person left
standing — and it is precisely the ending Smith's story forecloses, which
makes the ruled figure the corpus's answer to Smith rather than a copy of
him. Two readings taken on the wording, lightly held: "could be" is
permission, not a commitment to write one; and the ruling licenses the
*repenting* version — whether an unrepentant complicit leader is also
writable at major scale is left undecided rather than licensed. Indexed in
`open-questions.md`.

One finding worth keeping: Smith maps onto the detective doctrine's
lie-maintenance mechanics with almost no translation. The corpus's nearest
existing figures are Maren Solveig Krast (the Jehu comparandum — ended a
rotten house, kept its older idol) and the Imperium officer corps at
historical distance; nobody carries the arc from inside. Detail in
`ideas.md`.

## Third direction, same day — prayer, magic, and Levril perception

Dermot, verbatim:

> Prayers are recorded as answered only in codex or in the subjective POV
> eexperience of some characters. Likewise magic is a subjective POV
> experience. Levril interventions are often interpreted as answers to prayer
> and Levrils are more likely to be perceived by sensitive or deeply
> spiritual individuals.

("eexperience" read as *experience* — no ambiguity, noted for the record.)

### Canon check

**Three of the four assertions are already canon in Archive voice**; the
fourth is the extension.

- **"Prayers recorded as answered only in codex..."** —
  `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` states it as doctrine: *"Prayers are
  not answered by the cosmology... What devotional traditions make of that is
  theirs to argue — in the Codex, attributed, and never as the record's own
  voice."* The Tideward Sisterhood codex (`what-prayer-is-for.md`) shows the
  codex side cutting both ways — an attributed voice may also honestly record
  the answered-prayers column as *empty*.
- **"...or in the subjective POV experience of some characters"** — this is
  the extension, and it needs one consequence stated because chapters are
  canon: a POV block carrying an answered-prayer experience makes the
  **experience** canon, never the interpretation. The three-way reading
  (divine influence / physical account / contested transmission) stays open
  at record level. Operational rule that follows: **no `canon_facts` entry
  may assert that a prayer was answered** — the fact is that the character
  experienced it so.
- **"Magic is a subjective POV experience"** — already canon, nearly
  verbatim: `src/glossary/etheric.md` calls magic *"a real word for a real
  phenomenon, spoken by people who have not correctly modelled it, and never
  a narration-level claim"*; the Physics Comparison's governing sentence and
  What the Record Refuses ("Magic — refused as a category") carry the
  record-side half.
- **"Levril interventions often interpreted as answers to prayer"** — on the
  page for one tradition already: `ask-and-it-will-be-reviewed.md` reads
  Levril gradient work as the permissions office's ordinary mercies
  ("permitted development"), and `levrils.md` lists "misinterpretation as
  omnipotent or mythic entities" among Material-side observations. The
  direction generalises it beyond the church-space telling: *any* tradition
  may so interpret, and the interpretation stays the tradition's, never the
  record's. Extension, no conflict.
- **"More likely to be perceived by sensitive or deeply spiritual
  individuals"** — the one genuinely new assertion, and it has a boundary to
  respect: at Dock Seven *"every material witness present recorded what
  answered the intrusion as dragons"* (`levrils.md`, `s05e02c03`), so
  differential perceptibility must operate **below that salience threshold**
  — high-salience enforcement events stay visible to everyone.

### Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

- **The differential perception is read as rendering, not reception.** Canon
  already holds that a Levril has no receivable form — *"the perceiver
  supplies one"*, and an encounter *"tells a careful reader considerably more
  about the witness than about the Levril"* — and the dreams entry renders
  membrane bleed *"as imagery where the archetype vocabulary is shared."* On
  that ground, noticing a low-salience Levril presence is interpretive work:
  a mind practised in contemplative attention (the Sisterhood's "first work
  is attention") and stocked with the vocabulary renders what a distracted
  mind files as an instrument anomaly or nothing at all. That reading costs
  no new physics and stays clear of the refused psi trope. The alternative —
  "sensitive" as a perceptual *capacity* correlated with spirituality —
  would be a new lawful coupling needing a Scope-of-Physical-Law-style
  specification before it could enter lore. ~~**Confirm which is meant.**~~
  — **confirmed by Dermot, 26 August 2026: "The rendering reading is
  correct."** No new physics, no new sense; the differential is rendering
  practice, bounded below enforcement salience by the Dock Seven precedent.
- **"Sensitive or deeply spiritual"** is read as one population under the
  rendering reading (trained attention, however acquired — contemplative,
  devotional, or simply disciplined), not two distinct traits. This reading
  rides with the rendering confirmation above; not separately ruled.

### What was done with it

- Captured here with the canon survey. **After the rendering confirmation
  (same day), a "Who Notices" section was drafted into
  `src/lore/levrils.md`** — an extension in the canon-stability sense (adds
  without altering). What it commits: below enforcement salience, noticing a
  Levril is rendering work by the witness; reports cluster among the
  contemplative, devout, and deeply attentive as a fact about *practice*,
  never a faculty ("this setting keeps no perceptive elect"); and the
  answered-prayer interpretation pattern is explained as a **selection
  effect** — the witnesses likeliest to notice are likeliest to hold a
  devotional frame — which the record files without adopting. A matching
  "choose to appear to the faithful" bullet added to Common
  Misinterpretations. Lore, so draft-and-stop — ~~on the branch, not
  merged, Dermot's to approve~~ **approved by Dermot, 26 August 2026.**
- ~~The **`canon_facts` rule** (no entry may assert a prayer was answered —
  the experience is the fact) remains proposed, not yet ruled~~ —
  **confirmed by Dermot, 26 August 2026** ("the canon_facts rule is
  confirmed"), and written into CLAUDE.md's content authoring conventions
  the same day.
- Readings indexed in `open-questions.md`.

## Fourth direction, same day — the overlay may consider

Dermot, verbatim:

> Church Space overlay may consider the possibility of answered prayer and
> spiritual voices.

### Canon check

- **This completes the structure the third direction set up.** Where an
  answered prayer may live now has three non-Archive homes, each holding it
  differently: the **Codex** carries it as a named author's account
  (canon-for-its-author); a **POV block** carries it as a character's
  experience (the experience canon, the interpretation the character's); and
  the **overlay** may hold it as a considered *possibility* — a lens across
  the whole record, opted into per domain, never load-bearing. The Archive's
  own voice remains what `what-the-record-refuses.md` says it is: prayers
  are not answered *by the cosmology*, and that line does not move.
- **The overlay's nature already accommodates this.** The church-space FAQ's
  standing position — same constants, same Cascade, disputing what the
  Archive thinks it has *proven* rather than any fact — is exactly the room
  "consider the possibility" occupies. And `ask-and-it-will-be-reviewed.md`
  already walks to the line's edge: Permitted Development ("most of what
  providence does in a life was never applied for") is providence considered
  without a single answered application on the register. The direction lets
  the overlay go one step past re-picturing the record's refusal: it may
  entertain that the refusal describes the cosmology, not the whole of what
  is.
- **"Spiritual voices" is the genuinely new element.** The record's refusals
  are firm on its own side: Kieme admits no dialogue; the prophetic dream is
  re-derived ("a dream is never evidence about who sent it: nothing did");
  gods who answer are refused outright. None of that stops a devotional
  layer considering that an experience *was* a voice — the same
  experience/interpretation split as prayer, applied to locution. What the
  record carries is that the character heard; whose voice it was is the
  overlay's to consider and never the record's to file.
- **No published page needs an edit, and structurally cannot get one.** The
  Levrils draft and `what-the-record-refuses.md` both say devotional
  readings live "in the Codex, attributed" — the overlay is now a further
  home, but a *private-thread* one, and the validator enforces exactly this:
  a public page may not link into the church-space thread (it caught the
  Levrils draft trying, earlier today). So the public record's framing stays
  as written, and the overlay's wider licence is visible only where the
  overlay itself is. The boundary does the bookkeeping.

### Reading taken, flagged for confirmation

- **"May consider the possibility"** is read as exactly that — the overlay
  may entertain, discuss, and read specific recorded events as
  possibly-answered or possibly-a-voice, but does not *assert* either as
  fact. The codex ruling's reader-test is taken to govern here too: after
  reading an overlay page, canon must be no less settled than before —
  the overlay holds the possibility open without closing it in either
  direction. ~~Confirm that the test transfers.~~ — **confirmed by Dermot,
  26 August 2026: "The reader test transfers."**

### What was done with it

- Captured here; **no overlay content drafted** — the direction is
  permissive ("may consider"), not an instruction to write. Future
  church-space overlay pages inherit the licence, within the bound above.
- The prayer entry in `open-questions.md` updated; the `canon_facts` rule
  from the third direction is unaffected and stays open.
