## 1. Fliade — CLEAR TO WRITE

**Status:** no collisions found. Anstolik and Pandoids are new to the repo.
**One flag:** *"Convergence Point"* is not an established term — it appears
nowhere in `src/` as a defined concept. It needs either a definition of its own
or a mapping onto existing canon (Concordant membranes, boundary zones, or
Lagrange fold points). It recurs in three separate blocks below, so it is worth
settling once.

**Ruled 18 August 2026 (Dermot): map it onto boundary zones. No new term.**

A Convergence Point is an **inter-membrane boundary zone** — a region where a
**flexure** in one membrane carries a patch of it toward its neighbour and the
[Interval](../src/glossary/interval.md) narrows over that patch. That is the
operational case in [`boundary-zones.md`](../src/lore/boundary-zones.md) and the
one everything on that page describes. The word *Convergence Point* stays out of
`src/`: it names a thing the record already has a name for. If it is ever wanted
as an in-world coinage — a popular or Codex-register word for a narrowing —
that is a separate decision needing a canonical-glossary row, and it is not this
one.

Four things the mapping settles, and they apply to every block below:

- **Degrees, yes — and the vocabulary already exists.** *"A **minor**
  Convergence Point"* is a **Boundary-State Class**: Class I (Stable Gradient),
  Class II (Oscillatory Interface), Class III (Shear Event). Name the class;
  drop *minor*.
- **A boundary zone has coordinates in an ordinary sky, and no distance to the
  thing it is a boundary with.** *"The system sits near one"* is fine. Any
  phrasing that measures how far something is *from the other universe* is not:
  the Interval is manifold separation across the Common Manifold, not a line a
  ship could fly.
- **It is not a fold.** A flexure is not a higher-dimensional fold — a fold is
  engineered transit inside a single Concordant — and *"nothing travels along a
  flexure and nobody makes one."* Any block calling this feature a fold between
  two universes needs the word changed.
- **What crosses is constrained.** Membrane Shadows are gravitational imprints
  with nothing crossing; Intermembrane Bleed is Etheric templates competing for
  admissibility; a **transient** gravity tunnel is the rare Class III case where
  matter crosses. Class III non-sapient **shadow beings** are separately canon
  and do physically enter material space, so a zone they cross at is ordinary.

### Block 1a — physical

> Fliade is a rocky, cold terrestrial planet orbiting at a distance that keeps it well below Earth-like temperatures across most of its surface. The equatorial band is the most hospitable zone, reaching cool temperate conditions at peak season, but even there snowfall is common and standing water freezes for long stretches of the year. The polar regions are permanently glaciated and largely uninhabitable by surface-dwelling life. Most of the planet's biodiversity, including its two dominant species, is concentrated in the vast underground cavern systems that honeycomb the crust. The Anstolik, giant reindeer-like herbivores, range across both the surface tundra and the upper cavern levels, fiercely defending feeding grounds and migration corridors. The Pandoids, powerfully built humanoid bear-like beings with a calm and reclusive nature, prefer the deeper cavern networks, where their strength and familiarity with the underground terrain give them a natural advantage. Fliade's position in the broader stellar neighborhood has drawn some scientific interest from human space, given that the planet's unusual geothermal activity and its proximity to a suspected Convergence Point may make it relevant to ongoing research into the multiverse's structure.

### Block 1b — sensory

> Fliade greets visitors with biting, thin air and a sky that rarely warms past a pale grey-blue, even at the equator. **Open question:** are the Pandoids sapient? "Calm and reclusive nature" reads
that way, but they are listed alongside the Anstolik as one of two "dominant
species", which reads ecological. That distinction decides whether Fliade is a
first-contact world or a wildlife one, and whether Frontier Transformation
Protocols' sapience clause applies.

**ANSWERED — Dermot, 16 August 2026:** *"Yes Pandoids are sapient and one of
very few humanoid like aliens."*

So **Fliade is a first-contact world**, and Frontier Transformation Protocols'
sapience clause applies to it. The Anstolik are unaffected by this ruling and
stay as written — giant reindeer-like herbivores, one of two dominant species in
the ecological sense.

Two things the ruling leaves live, neither blocking:

1. ~~**"Low-tech" is not confirmed**~~ — **#450, ruled 16 August 2026, and the

   can be given about the Pandoids or anyone else; there is no scale for it to

2. **It touches a published refusal — tracked as #449, and see below.** The

### What the Pandoids are, as ruled

**Dermot, 16 August 2026:** *"Pandoids are not space faring and rely on oral
traditions and cultural memories."*

Note what shape that answer has, immediately after the ladder ruling above: it
is **two specific bounded questions answered**, which is exactly what the record
says survives once the ranking is refused. *Not space faring* is a fact about
capability. *Oral tradition rather than written record* is a fact about how a
people keeps what it knows. Neither is a rung, and **neither is a deficiency** —
that reading is precisely what `what-the-record-refuses.md` now declines.

So the settled Pandoid facts are: **sapient**; descended from a **bear-like
alien ancestor** native to Fliade, not from anything terrestrial; **not space
faring**; and keeping their record **orally, in cultural memory**. Fliade is a
first-contact world under the FTP sapience clause.

**Two consequences worth having deliberately.**

**Contact is asymmetric, and that is the whole situation.** A sapient,
non-spacefaring people does not arrive; they are arrived at. Everything the FTP
sapience clause is *for* lands on this case — consent, disturbance, and the
question of what the Corps is doing there at all — and none of it is softened by
their being reachable only one way. Any Fliade prose is a story about that
asymmetry whether or not it says so.

**The Archive meets a record it cannot read the way it reads everything else.**
This record's whole epistemology is documentary — Sen's [Marked
Absences](../src/codex/marked-absences.md) doctrine, *mark what is confirmed,
mark what is assembled*, the migration map, the Survey Archive's filing. A
people whose record is spoken is a genuine test of that, and a good one.
**The precedent already exists and should be followed rather than reinvented:**
[Hymn of the Thin Places](../src/codex/hymn-of-the-thin-places.md) is an oral
tradition the Archive files as a **composite text** with a compiler's note — no
single archival master, verses varying by chapter and world, the refrain
invariant across every submitted copy, and the variance recorded as evidence
rather than corrected. That is the Archive's existing method for a spoken source,
and it is a Codex form, not a Lore one — which is where Pandoid material would
have to sit whenever it is written down in-universe.

**Left open, deliberately, because inventing it would cost more than it pays:**
whether Pandoid memory works as human memory does. The standing rule that a
species may carry its own systems — declared once in its lore entry and
registered, never improvised per scene — was written for pronouns and applies
here just as well. If their recall is ordinary, say nothing; if it is not, that
is a canon vocabulary decision for Dermot and not something to slip into a
scene. **Not** to be answered by assuming an oral culture must have prodigious
memory: that is the ladder again, wearing a compliment.

### The humanoid collision — needs a ruling before Fliade is written (#449)

[What the Record Refuses](../src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md) states, in
prose and again in its summary table, that **"the Krenyi are the only other
sapient humanoids"** in this record — and flags that refusal as *"the
load-bearing one"*, the whole point being that this setting is not a humanoid
galaxy. The 13 August species-design work was driven by that ruling; five
species got body plans because three portraits had quietly drifted humanoid.

Sapient, humanoid Pandoids are a second exception to a refusal whose published
wording admits exactly one. Nothing is broken *today* — Fliade is unwritten and
the Pandoids appear nowhere in `src/` — but the first page that describes them
would contradict a live canon page.

**RULED — Dermot, 16 August 2026: reading 3**, with a clarification that does
most of the work: *"Option 3 for the Pandoids is fine, but derived from a bear
like alien ancestor not actual terrestrial bears, despite the name."*

So the Pandoids descend from a **bear-like alien ancestor on Fliade**, not from
anything in Earth's biosphere. *Pandoid* and *bear-like* are the observers'
vocabulary — a first survey reaching for the nearest familiar shape — and they
record what the encounter resembled, never what the lineage is. That is not a
special case: [Cross-Biosphere Taxonomy](../src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/)
already holds that life began separately on each world and that nothing in this
record is a metazoan but us. The name was always going to mislead someone; the
ruling is that it misleads about descent and the record says so.

Drafted into `src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` — the humanoid refusal now
turns on the **human frame** rather than on posture, with the naming caution
stated alongside it. Fliade and the Pandoids are **not named on that page**:
they are unwritten, and a doctrine page is the wrong place for a species to
first enter canon. The distinction stands on its own and the named example can
follow when Fliade is written.

Three readings, the third now taken:

- **They are not humanoid in the refusal's sense.** The refusal targets people
  "with two arms, two legs and one head, differing from humans in colour, ridge
  and ear" — the rubber-forehead alien. A powerfully built ursine being is
  bipedal without being a human with a nose job, and Dermot's own phrasing was
  *humanoid **like***. On this reading the refusal stands untouched and Fliade
  prose simply writes them as bear-derived rather than man-shaped.
- **The refusal widens to two named exceptions.** Krenyi and Pandoids, both
  remarked on rather than typical. This is a canon change to a load-bearing
  published page and needs a PR he approves, plus a check of the summary table
  row and the 13 August species-design premise.
- **The tiers are distinct** — human-frame humanoid (Krenyi) versus
  bipedal-but-not-human-derived (Pandoids) — and the refusal gains a sentence
  distinguishing them. This is the *explain the complexity in-story* option: the
  distinction is more interesting than either flat answer, and the record has
  reason to draw it precisely.

**RESOLVED 18 August 2026 — drafted as `src/lore/umbral-moon.md`.** All three

### Block 2a — structure and position

> The Umbral Moon occupies a stable position within a higher-dimensional fold that creases the boundary between the Material Universe and the Shadow Universe. Unlike a wormhole, this fold does not connect two distant points in space. Instead, it bends the geometry of both universes inward along a shared axis, forming a crease where the two realms press against each other. Running through that crease is a gravity tunnel, a channel of concentrated gravitational force generated by the competing pulls of both universes, and the moon sits suspended within it, held in place rather than drifting fully into either realm. The fold sits in near-Earth space along the Earth-Moon L5 corridor, placing it within gravitational and dimensional reach of Eden Space Habitat in Low Earth Orbit and the Kingdom of the Four Islands on Earth's surface. Its position within the fold shifts slightly over time as the fold breathes, but the gravity tunnel keeps it from escaping the crease entirely. Because the moon straddles the boundary, shadow beings and other entities from the Shadow Universe can cross into the Material Universe here more easily than at any other known point in the solar system. New London Space Habitat, stationed at Earth-Moon L5, has been logging the Ethereal disturbances radiating outward from the fold's edges, though the moon itself remained undetected until Eden's AI research systems flagged the anomalous gravitational micro-pulses.
>
> The Umbral Moon is home to elusive shadow beings, which are entities native to the Shadow Universe. These beings possess a fluid, mutable form that shifts with the ambient energy patterns of their realm. While they lack a fixed physical structure, they manifest as dark silhouettes with glowing eyes of cold green or pale silver. On rare occasions, more substantial creatures cross into the Material Universe through the fold, appearing as ghostly apparitions that can manipulate shadows and subtly influence the environment around them. These shadow beings are drawn to the disturbances caused by magic and often interact with the inhabitants of Eden Space Habitat, either out of curiosity or with more enigmatic intentions.

### Block 2b — sensory

> The Umbral Moon is nearly impossible to see from Earth's surface or from Eden Space Habitat without sensitive instruments. Its crust absorbs incoming light rather than reflecting it, a property researchers attribute to the dimensional fold pressing against the rock and pulling photons partially into the Shadow Universe before they can bounce back. Through deep-field sensors, it appears as a dark smear against the star field, its edges occasionally flickering with the pale, shifting colors characteristic of the Shadow Universe: deep violets, cold greens, and sudden washes of silver that pulse and fade like a slow breath. Observers in pressurized vessels stationed nearby report a low, bone-deep hum that standard instruments cannot fully capture, as though the gravity tunnel running through the fold vibrates at a frequency just below the threshold of human hearing. The air inside those vessels carries a faint metallic taste, and shadows fall at wrong angles, pooling in corners where the light should reach. When the fold is active, the moon's silhouette shimmers and briefly doubles, as if a second version of it exists a half-step out of phase with the material world. Shadow beings crossing through the fold leave cold pressure waves that sensors read as gravitational micro-pulses, distinct from normal tidal forces.

### The diagnosis, retained (checked 18 August 2026)

**Checked against canon 18 August 2026, and filed as #472, and the heading above is the most
misleading thing in this file.** It is not two naming decisions. The ship's
central concept rests on a **category error**, and three refused tropes are
fitted to it. Read this section before drafting anything from the blocks below.

### The category error: the Overfold is not a place you travel through

**`src/glossary/overfold.md`: the Overfold is the [Fellowship of Light]'s
contemplative name for boundary-zone Etheric phenomena** — *"Three names, one
thing, in the order they were coined: **the Overfold** (contemplative, oldest),
**Etheric** (the formal cosmological layer), **Intermembrane Bleed** (the current
Survey Corps operational term)."* It is a **phenomenon**, and an old word for a
thin place. It is not a medium, not a network, and not a route.

The block treats it as a transit system in nearly every paragraph: *Overfold
corridor networks*, *Tier-3 Overfold Rating*, *Overfold turbulence shields*, an
*Overfold drift predictor*, *Overfold-sensitive crew*, and an *Emergency Node-Hop
Protocol allowing short-range Overfold jumps if a corridor collapses mid-transit*.
The ship's identity, its certification, its defensive suite and its class history
are all built on it.

**And it would be a third FTL channel.** [What the Record
Refuses](../src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md): *"Faster-than-light travel
exists on exactly two channels"* — the Quantum Space Harmonic Wave and fold
transit. There is no third.

**The original check was backwards, and this is the lesson worth keeping.** The
note below reads *"**Overfold** is already established canon (9 files)"* and
treats the hit count as reassurance. The word is established; it means something
else. Counting files is not reading definitions — the same failure `images.md`
records about reasoning from `image_alt` strings instead of opening the picture.
