## 3. Dr. Falkorian — foster cousin of Karla Wender, Chief Science Officer

New character, no occurrences in the repo. **The rank already exists and fits
exactly**, which makes this cheaper to add than it looks.

`src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md` establishes **Chief Science
Officer** as the duty title for a Chief on the research track, running a post or
vessel's science section — the same rank tier as **Chief Pilot**, the title
Wender holds at the start of Season 1. See also
`src/lore/star-rangers-science-corps.md`.

So the two of them start the series at **identical rank on different tracks**,
and are family. That is a good structural spine and it is free — no new rank,
no new institution.

**It also fills a real gap.** There are currently no science-track characters on
the roster at all; the only character page mentioning science is Tissadelle's,
in passing. The Science Corps exists in lore with nobody in it.

### Addendum — Falkorian refuses the label (Dermot, 13 August 2026)

Dermot, 13 August 2026: **Andrew Falkorian might or might not be a
high-functioning autistic, or else just very highly intelligent, but he refuses
to be defined by a label, a diagnosis or a stereotype.**

**Nothing in the repo touches this.** Checked 13 August: no occurrence of
*autistic*, *neurodivergent*, *diagnosis* or *high-functioning* anywhere in
`src/` or `story-bible/`. A first filling-in, not a revision.

**The thing to get right is whose ambiguity it is.** Written carelessly,
"might or might not be" reads as the narrator hedging — as though the record has
not worked it out yet and will tell us when it does. That is the opposite of the
fact. **The ambiguity is his position, held deliberately**, and the page has to
make that unmistakable or it will read as coyness.

The register already exists on the roster and is worth copying rather than
reinventing. `tissadelle-shepherd.md`'s Tobble section does this exactly: it
states the fact once, names the misreading people habitually make, records that
she *"corrects it once, precisely, and does not raise it again"*, and declines to
explain further to a room that has not asked. Same shape here — with the
difference that what Falkorian declines is not a misreading but the *question
itself*.

Two traps worth naming in advance:

- **Do not resolve it in the narration.** No canon fact establishing what he is;
  no doctor's opinion filed as settling it. If the record knows, he has lost the
  thing that characterises him.
- **Do not let the refusal become a coded confirmation.** A character who refuses
  a label *while the text nudges the reader toward it* is being labelled anyway,
  more cheaply. If the text is honest that it does not know, both readings must
  stay live — including the plain one, that he is simply very bright.

**How it sits with the Science Corps role.** He is the roster's first
science-track character, so he arrives carrying the genre's whole freight of
brilliant-and-strange scientists. The refusal is the answer to that freight
rather than an instance of it: the stereotype is precisely what he declines, and
the page can say so without ever naming a condition.

**What it does not license.** No inference about the other survivors, and none
about the rescue. `plural-minds`-adjacent care applies — this is his own account
of himself, and the record's job is to carry it, not to adjudicate it.

*Open, and his to settle:* whether anyone in the foster family has ever raised it
with him, and what he said. Karla, who learnt her own precision from a machine
(see §9's addendum), is the obvious person to have tried once.

### What still needs deciding

- **"Foster cousin" is now ambiguous, and the answer changes the texture.** If
  they were orphaned on the *same* voyage and rescued together, they read as
  foster *siblings*. Cousins implies they went to different but related
  households — or that they were already blood cousins and the fostering is
  what made the kinship stick. All three are good; they are not the same story.
- **Whose robots?** The setting's AI is Kernel-compliant, so these were not
  generic machines. The economical answer is that they were Eden's own, or a
  Kernel-compliant vessel system — and if the Eden Warden or a predecessor of it
  had any part in the rescue, then the AI that raised them is **still running
  the habitat**, and Wender's relationship with it is decades old and entirely
  unexplored. That is a large, cheap opportunity. It is also a trap if
  overplayed: the Warden is two personas sharing one system, not a parent.
- **Do Wender and Falkorian diverge or converge?** Her competence is judging
  what is *executable*; a Chief Science Officer's is judging what is *true*.
  Those conflict productively, and shared-orphan kinship means neither can
  retreat into rank when they do.
- **Is "Dr." academic or medical?** Decides what section he runs.
  *(Settled 2026-08-04: **an engineering doctorate** — consistent with his
  §7 role as one of the human specialists who maintain Xenon, and with a
  Chief Science Officer running a research section from the engineering
  side.)*
- **Season 6-7 role.** Rook-7 is the last purely instrumental character in that
  treatment. A Chief Science Officer with a family claim on Wender is the
  candidate to take that weight instead — the one person who can tell her that
  her doctrine is wrong and cannot be dismissed for it.

### One thing to be deliberate about rather than accidental

**All three principals now have Eden in their past.** Tissadelle sang for
[Slipwave](/star-rangers/glossary/slipwave/) on Eden's civilian arts circuit
before her cadet intake; Wender and Falkorian were raised there. That is either
a good convergence or too tidy, and it should be a decision rather than a
coincidence nobody noticed.

**Decided (Dermot, 4 August 2026): "Eden is the nexus where the characters
meet."** The convergence is design, not drift — and the corpus already

Causeway, and the frontier keep their own ground, and Eden is the place

scene sits. And the settlement raises the standing §3 opportunity a notch:
if Eden is the deliberate meeting ground, then the question of whether the
Warden (or a predecessor) had any part in the rescue — the AI that raised
them still running the habitat — is now load-bearing rather than
decorative, and worth settling on purpose when its story arrives. Note that Wender's page already states Shepherd was
"a cadet well below Wender's operational horizon" at Season 1's start, so no
prior acquaintance is implied and none is required.

New character. Half of this lands on existing canon and half requires a small

the rank, serving in a corps, not a new duty title. That needs nothing added.
My earlier note that "Chief Engineer is not established" was right about the
*duty title* only: the hierarchy's five Chief titles are Chief Ranger, Chief
Pilot, senior chief warden officer, Chief Provost and Chief Science Officer,
with no engineering entry. But the **Engineering Corps itself is well
established**, and richly:

- `src/lore/chthonari.md` — Chthonari are "disproportionately represented in the
  Star Rangers' Engineering Corps, specifically in fold-scaffold construction
  and maintenance", a career choice rather than a species quota, and their
  recruiters are tired of explaining the difference to visiting press.
- `src/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings.md` and
  `src/glossary/virtual-reality.md` both reference it as an existing institution
  with its own culture.

**The one gap:** there are lore files for the Frontier, Safety and Science
Corps but **none for the Engineering Corps** — it exists only inside other
entries. If Altarei is going to carry it, that file is the natural companion
piece, the same note as applies to Dr. Lekrin and the Medical Corps.

**Good material sitting there already.** A human Chief in a corps where
Chthonari dominate the prestige specialization is a specific place to stand, and
the Chthonari entry describes fold-scaffold work as reading "a structure under
gradient stress that must be read and corrected before it fails." That is
forensic reading. The bridge from engineer to Detective Inspector is not a
career change at all — it is the same act performed on a different object, and
the story should probably know that before Altarei does.

**Why the career change is the interesting part.** Both jobs are the same
skill pointed at different objects: reconstructing what happened from what is
left. An engineer reads a failed component; a detective reads a scene. The move
is lateral, not a demotion or a reinvention, and the story should probably know
that before Altarei does.

**Still needs from Dermot:** what ended the engineering career — chosen, or
made for him — and whether he serves at Threshold alongside Ashcombe or
somewhere else.

Dermot, 4 August 2026: **Lumbarian is too large in scale to repair Xenon if
needed, and not humanoid enough to inspire affection — but still highly
valued and respected.** Three consequences. First, it narrows the
peer-units question: Xenon's machine maintainers are survivors of the line

in the rescue family — refined the same day by Dermot: **"Lumbarian would

survivors' three machines map to three registers — Lucene holds kinship by
name, Xenon holds kinship by care, and Lumbarian holds **creature-love and
respect**: adored the way something enormous and gentle is adored, patted
rather than held, with no pretence of personhood-kinship behind it. Third,
the craft point, which is exactly the tone rule's register and should be
hinted rather than stated: **affection followed form, not merit — and form
decided not whether he was loved, but as what.** The machine whose EVA
capability is the reason anyone got off the wreck at all could not be made
a father or a second sister — too big, wrong shape, nothing to hold onto —
so the humans made him a beloved animal instead, and everyone who owes him
their life knows precisely what he is worth. A pet elephant is not a
lesser standing than family; it is the standing a body that size can
hold.

maintained by a ship's AI or by Xenon.** The asymmetry this creates

way — Xenon can keep Lumbarian running, and Lumbarian, too large for fine
work, can never return it. Even among the machines, Lumbarian receives
what he cannot reciprocate — the same shape as his standing with the
survivors, and consistent with it rather than a new fact. It also narrows
his whereabouts without naming them: wherever Lumbarian is, he needs
either a vessel-scale intelligence or his old colleague within reach.
Still open: where he is now and whether he is active.

  how much an answer will cost the asker, perform the sympathy flawlessly,
  and still give all of it. The performance shapes *delivery*, never
  *content*. That is the character in one sentence.
- **It gives the rescue trio a natural division of labour** (inference, not
  settled): Lumbarian could reach the wreck, Xenon could keep the machines
  running, and Lucene could hold the children — a machine that reads terror

- **How and when the name was made** (Dermot, same day): **"Lucy Anne" is a
  deliberate childish mispronunciation of *Lucene*, and Karla named her

## 8. Anne of Wender — first sheriff of a frontier world

Dermot, 28 July 2026: **Anne Wender settled on a frontier planet and became its
first local Sheriff**, and is known afterwards as **Anne of Wender**.

### This lands exactly on the Frontier Corps clause, with nothing bent

`src/lore/star-rangers-frontier-corps.md` already defines the swearing-in of a
world's first sheriff as the single event the entire Corps exists to bring
about — and to be ended by:

> The mandate ... is narrow, enumerated, and self-terminating: it ends
> automatically, without review, petition, or Ranger consent, at the moment the
> colony constitutes *any* local authority of its own. **One elected sheriff
> ends it.** ... The swearing-in of a world's first sheriff closes the Frontier
> Corps' file on that world, and the Corps' standing tradition is that the
> departing Ranger attends the ceremony — **as a civilian guest, unarmed, at
> the back.**

So Anne's swearing-in is not merely a career event. It is a **canonically
defined ending**: the moment a Star Rangers mandate extinguishes itself, with a
Ranger standing at the back of the room, disarmed, watching it happen. The
ceremony is already written. It just did not have a name in it until now.

### The resonance, which should never be stated on the page

Anne was **the oldest survivor** of a disaster in which every adult died — a
teenager who had to be the responsible one because there was nobody older left
to be it. She grows up to become **the first person a world ever formally swears
in to be responsible for it.**

That is the whole arc and it needs no commentary. Do not let any character
observe it aloud.

### "Anne of Wender" — the name is doing something

The shift from *Anne Wender* to *Anne of Wender* is toponymic in form: it reads
as *of the place called Wender*. The natural reading is that **the settlement
took her name**, which the Frontier Corps clause quietly permits — a world that
constitutes its own authority names its own institutions, and the Corps has no
say in it.

If that is right, it is a strong inversion worth keeping: **a child who lost her
home and her name's meaning in one night ends up being the name of a place.**
Worth confirming with Dermot rather than assuming, since the alternative — that
she is simply known by her family name in the old style — is also fine and much
plainer.

### Karla

Anne is Karla Wender's elder sister. The Corps tradition puts *a* departing
Ranger at the back of that ceremony, unarmed. It does not have to be Karla. But
Karla is a Star Ranger, and this is her sister, and the scene exists whether or
not anyone writes her into it.

**Resist making it neat.** If Karla is there, the interesting version is that
she attends as the tradition requires and not as a sister, and that the
distinction costs her something. If she is not there, the reason is worth
knowing.

### Still open

- Which world, and whether it has a name yet.
- Whether the settlement is named Wender, or she is simply "of" her family.
- Where this sits in the timeline relative to Karla's Chief Pilot rank at
  Season 1's start.
- Whether the Frontier Corps had a caretaker mandate on that world at all — the
  clause only applies to charter colonies that signed the standing invitation
  before landing. A world that never invited the Corps would have Anne as its
  first sheriff with no Ranger at the back, which is a quieter and possibly
  better story.

### Addendum — 13 August 2026

Dermot: **Anne joins a *commercial* frontier settlement and becomes the first
*elected* Sheriff of the new planet, with resistance from the corporation that
hired her, and the reluctant help of a Senior Officer in the Frontier Corps.**

This closes two of the open questions above and sharpens the third.

**The Corps is present.** There is a Senior Officer on that world, so the
caretaker mandate applies and the "no Ranger at the back" variant is off. The
swearing-in extinguishes his own posting.

**She is a corporate hire first, and sworn second.** The company brought her out
to a world it was developing — and then the colony elected her, which is not the
same job and answers to a different authority. The corporation's resistance
therefore needs no villainy to explain it: it hired a person and got an
institution. Per the cast principles, write the company as sincerely aggrieved
and partly right — it carried the cost of the settlement, it has obligations to
people who are not on that planet, and an elected officer it cannot direct is a
genuine loss of something it genuinely paid for.

**Why the Senior Officer is reluctant — and this is structural, not
temperamental.** The charter is explicit:

> The Star Rangers are neutral: they take no side in political disputes and
> cannot be used to lend legitimacy to any political claim.

A contested election between a settlement and the corporation that financed it
*is* a political dispute. Every form of help he can offer Anne is
indistinguishable, from the outside, from the Rangers putting their weight
behind one side of it. He is not reluctant because he doubts her. He is
reluctant because he is the one person on that world who is forbidden to want
her to win — and helping her at all is the closest thing to a charter breach he
will ever commit.

Which gives the two of them the same problem from opposite ends: **she needs the
legitimacy he is forbidden to lend, and he needs her sworn so that he can
leave.** That is the engine; nobody should say it.

Note also the neat cost: his help is what ends his mandate. He works his own
posting out of existence, on purpose, and attends the ceremony unarmed at the
back. See §8 above — the ceremony was already written.

**Still open after this:** the corporation's name and what it was actually
developing there; whether the election was contested by a company candidate or
simply obstructed; and whether the Senior Officer is someone we already have.

### Addendum — the corporation is the Cairn Trust (19 August 2026)

Dermot asked for the corporation to be named. Drafted as
`src/lore/cairn-trust.md`; the name is a proposal, the structure around it is
canon already published and merely assembled.

**Name: the Cairn Trust.** A cairn is stacked by people who have already passed,
for people who have not yet arrived, so the name is a claim about who the
concern is building for — and the whole of the storyline is the moment somebody
tests the claim. It is also the deliberate inverse of the *Hyperfold* precedent,
where the naming section establishes that a concern selecting a word which
cannot be held to a meaning has generally selected it on advice. This one picked
a word that can be, and gets held to it. Checked for collisions: *cairn* appears
nowhere in `src/`, and no organisation in the corpus uses *Trust* as its form.
Deliberately not a third **Combine** — Hyperfold and Halvern have that shape
already.

**What it was developing: the power base, not a commodity.** This inverts the
expected answer and is much better for it. `the-fusion-ceiling.md` already
establishes that a frontier world runs on *imported cores rationed across
essentials in the first years, local fabrication bootstrapped over a decade or
more, full generating capacity arriving a generation after the founding
landing*, and `star-rangers-frontier-corps.md` already establishes that a
colony is *institution-poor by sequencing, not failure* for exactly that reason.
So the Trust finances the interval: passage out, the first cores, the certified
crews, the ten-to-twenty-year buildout, recovered over decades against the
colony's own later output. It was not strip-mining the world. It was installing
the reactor, which means its interest and the settlers' interest are **the same
interest for the whole of the buildout** and diverge exactly once.

**Why it is sincerely aggrieved and partly right — and this is the load-bearing
choice.** The cast note said write it that way; making it a *trust* rather than
a company is what makes that structural instead of asserted. Its capital is held
for named beneficiaries — settlers on the register who have signed or paid for a
passage not yet taken, waiting on cores from a finite production run. A
shortfall on one world is a year added to somebody else's departure. So **a
company may forgive a debt whenever it likes; a trustee is forbidden to**, and a
trustee who does it anyway has moved a cost onto people with no seat at the
table. Every refusal has a person behind it, and they are never the person in
the room. That is a grievance no villainy is needed to explain, and it cannot be
resolved by anyone being nicer.

**What this does to the Sheriff storyline**, without settling anything the
Still-open list above still holds. The Frontier Corps clause is written about the
Rangers and lands on the Trust: for the whole buildout the Trust deals with a
compact it helped draft, a colony with no government, and a caretaker officer who
is neutral by charter and can take no side. Afterwards it deals with an
institution that owes its office to the settlers and owes the Trust nothing but
the debt. **The counterparty changes from a document into a constituency in one
afternoon, and the only authority present the whole time is obliged to leave.**
That is the same absence `what-nobody-certifies.md` names, met from the other
side.

**Why she was hired follows from the business and is worth taking.** A concern
installing a fusion core and running a certified buildout hires a site safety
officer, not an enforcer — which keeps the Trust clear of the Corps' own charge
sheet (*company enforcers whose contracts read better than they behave*) and
sharpens the addendum's best line. The colony elected the person who had been
keeping it alive. **The Trust's own good decision produced the institution that
now constrains it**, which is "it hired a person and got an institution" with the
knife in.

**Deliberately left open, and not touched by this:** which world and whether it
is named Wender (see §8's Still-open list and #444); whether the election was
contested by a company candidate or simply obstructed; and whether the Senior
Officer is someone the cast already has.

**Deliberately kept out of the published entry:** Anne, the election, the world,
and the Senior Officer. The entry is about the Trust's structure only, so it
seeds the storyline without spoiling an arc that is not written. The general
mechanism it cites — one elected sheriff ends the mandate — is already published
in `star-rangers-frontier-corps.md` and is not a spoiler.

## 9a. Karla learnt her manner from Lucene (Dermot, 13 August 2026)

Dermot, 13 August 2026: **Karla Wender can be very precise, formal and correct in
social situations. She learnt that from Lucene.**

**What this settles that was previously only an atmosphere.** Her page already
carries the manner — *"She does not perform authority. She carries it"*, and the
running note that Rangers who mistake quietness for uncertainty correct the
reading quickly. What it never had was a *source*. Now it has one, and the source
is a machine whose emotional register is performative by design (see the Lucene
addendum in §7).

**That is the interesting part, and it needs handling carefully.** The obvious
reading — *she learnt to perform feeling from something that only performs* — is
available and would be a cheap and rather cruel note to strike. The record should
not strike it. What a frightened child actually learnt from Lucene was that
**precision is a way of taking someone seriously**: the machine that stayed exact
while a ship came apart was the one that did not lie to her about what was
happening. Formality, learnt there, is not distance. It is the opposite.

It also resolves an apparent tension already sitting in the material. §7 records
that **Karla always calls her Lucene, never "Lucy Anne"** — the childhood name is
hers alone and unused. That could read as coldness toward a foster mother. Read
through this, it is the same lesson applied back: the correct form of address,
used every time, is how she was taught that care is shown.

**Where it must not go.** Nothing here makes her manner a symptom, an
impairment, or a machine-like affect. She is a person who was taught well by
someone unusual. The distinction matters more than usual on this roster, given
§3's addendum on Falkorian and the standing care around plural minds — the
record's habit is to describe how a person is, and to decline to diagnose it.

*Open:* whether she knows she learnt it there, and whether she would say so.

## 9. Xenon and Lumbarian serve on Karla's ship

Dermot, 28 July 2026. This closes the thread: **all three rescuers are
accounted for.** Lucene-9000 is at Eden in Oyelaran's bureau; **Xenon and
Lumbarian are members of Karla Wender's ship.**

### What this does

**The machines that pulled her off a dying ship as a child now serve under her
command.** She was helpless and they decided she could be reached; she is now
the officer who decides what they are ordered to do.

And her defining competence — already on her page, before any of this existed —
is *telling orders that can be executed from orders that only look executable on
a map.* She spends her career making exactly the judgement that was once made
about her, and she makes it about the same machines. That was a resonance when
it was only an origin. With Xenon and Lumbarian aboard it is a working
relationship she has to conduct every day.

**None of this should be said aloud by anyone.** It is structure, not dialogue.

### Both make plain operational sense, which is what stops it being twee

A starship complement wants a maintenance robot that keeps the other machines
running, and it wants something large that can work a hull in vacuum. Xenon and
Lumbarian would earn their berths on any ship. That they earned them on *hers*
is the part left unexplained — and it should probably stay unexplained for a
long time.

### The question worth asking

**Did she seek them out, or did they follow her?** Both are good and they are
not the same character:

- If Karla arranged it, she is someone who quietly assembled the remains of her
  own rescue around her, which sits oddly against a woman who does not perform
  anything and does not mythologise the work.
- If they went where she went, then two machines made a choice nobody ordered,
  and Xenon's inability to create new robots takes on another edge — it can
  keep what remains running, and what remains chose her.

### A real institutional question

Eden's precedent is explicit: mobile AI humanoids answer to their operational
superior for tasking, but to [the Eden Warden](/star-rangers/characters/eden-warden/)
on any question of their own standing, treatment, or fitness for continued
service — *"a line Oyelaran has never once had reason to test."*

**Who holds that line on a Star Rangers ship?** If it is the captain, then Karla
holds both halves for the machines that rescued her, and the separation Eden
maintains does not exist where it would matter most. If it is someone else, the
Corps has a welfare authority nobody has named yet. Either answer is worth
having deliberately.

### Still open

- Which ship, and from when — before or after she made High Captain.
- Whether Karla, Andrew or Anne have ever discussed it with any of the three.
- Whether Xenon and Lumbarian remember the rescue as Lucene-9000 evidently
  does, or whether that is a difference between them worth using.

## 10. Rulings on the remaining backstory decisions (19 August 2026)

Dermot asked for the outstanding #444 items to be ruled on. Working through the
file's Still-open blocks in one sitting, as the issue's cheapest-repair note
asks. **All of these are proposals.**

The first finding is that **the file had gone stale in the ordinary way**: four
of the listed questions were answered months or weeks ago by material that
landed elsewhere and never came back to update the blocks. Those are recorded as
closed rather than ruled, because nobody needs a ruling on something the corpus
already states.

### Already closed — corrections, not rulings

**Anne's surname is Wender, and it is published.** `s01e00c04` carries it in
`canon_facts` (*"made Anne Wender, her younger sister Karla, and Andrew

**Xenon and Lumbarian are active, and both are aboard Karla's ship.** Settled by

**The corporation is the Cairn Trust, developing the power base.** Closed

### Declined deliberately — these are not gaps to close

**What the disaster actually was.** The rescue chapter lists the ship's name,

and the reason is in the prose rather than in a note: *"Where the machines had
come from, and who had sent them, the machine did not volunteer and Anne did not

declines to explain it, and explaining it later would cost the line. **Leave it.**
The loss of the machines stays hinted per §7 and no page may state it.

**The "Lucy Anne" echo.** Marked *"Still open, deliberately"* in §7 with a craft

chapter did do: Anne's awareness is now *shown* rather than asserted — *"The name
had Anne's own name inside it. She noticed the way you notice a draught: once,
completely, and without the first idea of what to do about it."* Whether Karla
reached for it remains exactly as open as before, and so does why Anne abstains
from the name.

### Ruled

**1. Andrew and Lucene — the question no longer has the shape it was asked in.**
It presupposed that the machines delivered the children and left. The chapter has
Lucene not leaving: *"through the intake hall and past it, into the weeks on the
other side, until the escorting had turned into something no form had a line
for."* All three grew up with her. So all three know exactly who she is, and
there is nothing left to discover.

**What survives is the better question, and the ruling on it is that nobody has
asked her what happened — for three different reasons.** Anne decided on the
night that it was not the missing fact and has never revised the judgement. Karla
was five; she has the bond and the name, not an account. **Andrew is the sharp
one**: at eleven he asked Xenon everything and stopped at the one question with a
floor under it — *"some instinct that had grown in him over nine days told him
which questions had floors under them, and he did not ask the next one"* — and he
has kept that instinct for forty years. He is the one who knows there is a
question and has decided, repeatedly, not to put it. The unasked question stays
unspent, per §7.

**2. The settlement is named Wender — and Anne did not name it.** This is the
third option and it is better than either of the two the block offered.

A world that constitutes its own authority names its own institutions and the
Corps has no say in it. So the naming is done **by the colonists, at
constitution, for the person they have just elected**, and Anne neither proposed
it nor could refuse it. That preserves the inversion §8 wanted — a child who lost
her home and her name's meaning ends up being the name of a place — and improves
it, because she does not do it; it is done to her.

It also rhymes exactly with the intake hall, which is why it is the right answer:
*"It went into the record as plainly as that, and the record, the way records do,
set about making it true: one word, holding three people."* One word, holding a
world. **Anne has now been named into a family and named into a place, both times
by somebody else's pen, both times without being asked**, and that is the whole
of her character stated twice without a line of commentary.

*Anne of Wender* follows naturally afterwards and needs no explaining.

**3. Which world: the record does not fix a name, and the mechanism is the
answer.** A charter world sits on the register under its charter designation,
and the people who live there call it by the settlement's name. Both are true at
once and neither is a marked absence — this is simply how a settlement's name
becomes a world's name, and Corryn Prime is the precedent for the register form
outliving nobody's usage. So: **the settlement is Wender, and Wender is what the
world is called.** The registry designation exists and is not worth a page.

**4. Timeline: the settlement is founded in the mid-2810s and the election falls
in the series' present, after Season 1's start.** Deliberately a range and not a
year, because the arithmetic here is exactly where contradictions hide.

The constraints that fix it: Anne is about fifteen at the wreck in 2788, so about
fifty-three at Season 1's start in 2826. The [Cairn Trust](../src/lore/cairn-trust.md)
buildout runs ten to twenty years before a colony carries itself. And the harm of
placing it in backstory is that **the storyline would already be over** — an arc
whose ending has happened is background, not material.

So Anne goes out in her late thirties or early forties, the buildout runs its
decade-plus, and the swearing-in falls somewhere in the late 2820s. Karla is
Chief Pilot and rising through the whole of it. **Karla is therefore never the
departing Ranger at the back** — that officer is Frontier Corps and she is Pilot
Section — so if she attends she attends as a guest, which is §8's harder and
better version. The exact year is Dermot's to fix when the arc is written.

**5. The election was obstructed, not contested — and the obstruction was lawful,
procedural, and possibly right.** The Trust never ran a candidate.

A company candidate makes the Trust a political actor and turns the storyline
into an election thriller, which is exactly the villainy the cast note forbids.
What a trustee actually does is **test the thing that changes its counterparty**:
argue the compact's terms, question the roll, dispute whether the settlement had
yet reached the threshold to constitute an authority at all.

And the objection has to be one the Trust could be right about, or it is not
worth writing. **It is that the election was premature — that the colony could
not yet carry the cost of its own government.** That is a claim about the
schedule, which is the Trust's actual product and the thing it knows more about
than anyone on the planet. It may well have been true. Nobody will ever be able
to say, because the colony held the election and then had to be right.

**6. The Senior Officer is new — and [Galen Kane](../src/characters/galen-kane.md)
is who he calls.** Reusing Kane directly is the wrong shape, and finding out why
produced the better arrangement.

Kane is not reluctant to be extinguished; it is his service philosophy. *"Every
one of them is a world that no longer needs him, which is the only outcome the
caretaker clause was written to produce."* He is also on a desk now, and his
circuit days are written as past. So the officer on Wender is someone else.

But Kane's page already defines, exactly, the role this storyline needs filled:
he is *"the advisor she trusts most... he sits nowhere in her chain of command,
has no stake in any roster she controls and no career left that her favour could
advance"*, and what he checks is *"whether standing is still the job or has
quietly become the habit, and when leaving is the correct order that nobody on a
command deck wants to be first to say aloud."*

**That is the Senior Officer's question with one word changed.** He is not asking
when to leave. He is asking whether he may help her, so that he can — and the
help is the closest thing to a charter breach he will ever commit. Kane has
advised on a hundred departures and has never been asked this one. Put it to him
and the corps' most scrupulous man about which hat is speaking has no hat that
covers it.

**7. Which ship, and from when: they were aboard before she had any say, and
stayed with the hull.** Machines are posted to hulls, not to officers. Xenon and
Lumbarian joined the complement of a ship Karla served on, and were still aboard
as she rose — so *"Karla Wender's command"* on their pages became true **without
anybody doing anything**, and the phrase reads both ways on purpose. The hull's
name stays unfixed; both pages already work without one, and a chapter can name
it when a chapter needs it.

**8. Did she seek them out, or did they follow her? Neither, and that is the
point.** §9 offers two good answers and the record's answer is the third and
dullest: the Corps posts machines where machines are needed, and a fleet with few
of their generation left routes them to postings that can maintain them. Nobody
arranged anything.

**The record holds the posting orders, and posting orders do not record motive.**
So the question stays live for the characters and closed for the Archive, which
is where §9 wanted it — *"it should probably stay unexplained for a long time."*
Nobody has ever checked whether the dull answer is the whole answer, because
checking would mean asking.

**9. All three machines remember it, and the difference is not memory but
vantage.** §7 asks whether Xenon and Lumbarian remember the rescue *as Lucene
evidently does*. They do not, and neither does the phrasing hold: **they
remember a different event.**

- **Lucene held the children.** Her whole function is the register in which a
  rescue is a relationship, so hers is the account with people in it.
- **Xenon spent the passage going over five machines with nothing wrong with
  them**, under a light a boy was holding, beside a rack of empty cradles whose
  padding he had smoothed and whose latches he had closed. His account is of
  work, and of the count.
- **Lumbarian was outside for the whole of it** and did not see a child until
  Eden, when he lowered one manipulator flat to a deck and held it still while
  the smallest survivors patted it. His account has no compartment in it at all.

Three memories of one event, none of them the same event. That refuses the cheap
reading in both directions — machines that remember perfectly, machines that do
not remember at all — and it is the house's own three-way-reading instinct
applied to machine testimony.

**10. Karla knows she learnt her manner from Lucene, and would not say so.**
§9a's closing *Open* line. She knows; it is not the kind of thing a person
misses. She will not say it because **saying it would make Lucene an explanation
for her rather than a person she owes**, and the lesson itself forbids that: she
was taught that the correct form, used every time, is how care is shown. Filing
her foster mother as a source note is not the correct form. It is the same reason
she says *Lucene* and never *Lucy Anne*.
