## Third — Varn's arrival, drafted

The evening's ruling on the second section's open shape, Dermot verbatim:

> Varn's arrival, draft it

Shape (a) taken as recommended. Drafted the same evening as
`src/seasons/s09/e01/s09e01c01.md`, ***Nothing to Report*** — the first
chapter of Season 9 and of the `young-star-rangers` thread, and the first
chapter on young.fianilchruinne.com's own thread. Two POV blocks in one
scene, 720 and 774 words, inside the young-adult band: Deputy Officer Iklix
Varn arriving on Eden's ring with everything on the kit list, finding the
hatch between a door with a name on it and a door with a plant outside it,
knocking (wrong) and using the handle (right), hearing Smith's *the event
has not come in my tenure* and waiting for a rest of the sentence that does
not come, and standing the first shift one step too far from the desk; then
Field Officer Zoe Smith reading the fourteenth Deputy the way she reads all
of them, by small things — the posting order folded once and not smoothed,
the question not asked — and at the end of the shift sending the first log
entry back: *Nothing to report* is an opinion, and the Corps did not post
either of them to hold opinions in a compartment. The entry that replaces it
is what happened — six instruments agreed every ninety seconds, the hatch
opened once and closed once, the watch stood by two named officers, no
event — and every clause of it can be checked by someone who was not there.
The chapter ends on Varn not moving the step yet, and Smith writing nothing
down about that either.

What it commits the story to, in two canon facts: Varn's first shift on the
watch was in the early spring of 2828 UCSD under Smith, and its log records
no event; and on a watch of the Corps a shift in which nothing happens is
logged as what the instruments did and who stood it, *nothing to report*
being refused as an assessment rather than an observation. The second is an
extension of the decision doctrine's *say only what can be supported* to the
log's smallest entry, not a new rule. Everything else in the chapter is
already on the record: the six cross-correlated instruments and the
ninety-second agreement, the hatch the residents walk past, Smith's first-day
sentence and its register (*Sent for the Log*, Varn's page), the three-bar
tab seated by the quartermaster record (the uniform page), Deputies standing
one step too far from the desk, and Eden's spoke car and spin ring (the
Eden page). The certification office that will one day send a log with a
line too many is glanced at from Smith's block, two hundred metres around
the ring, as a thing she does not know yet — so Season 9 slots under Season
10 without touching it.

Readings taken while drafting, flagged for his eye:

1. **The day count.** *Sent for the Log* has the form on *the ninth day*.
   This chapter names no day; it is the first shift, and the Reading Plan's
   *nine days later* counts from it. If he would rather the chapter carry the
   date in its timestamp (*2828 UCSD, Early Spring, Day 1 of the posting*),
   that is a one-line change.
2. **The kit list is prose, not lore.** Service dress two sets, the tab, a
   reader, a torch, boots for grating, a printed posting order — written as
   what one Deputy carried, with no canon fact and no lore page, so the Corps'
   kit stays undescribed on the record. If a Deputy's kit is worth a
   paragraph on the uniform page, that is a separate extension.
3. **Varn's pronoun** is *they*, as on the character page; Smith's is *she*.
   Smith's block uses *they all did* of the fourteen Deputies generally,
   which is the plural, not a pronoun choice.
4. **The knock.** Varn knocks on the hatch, which Smith reads as *the
   Academy still taught manners and still did not teach hatches*. This says
   nothing about Academy curriculum the Academy page contradicts; it is her
   observation, in her head.
