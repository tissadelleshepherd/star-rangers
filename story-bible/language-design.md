# Language design — what the standing canon already forces about how a people speaks, and how translating it fails

Unpublished authorial note, the third sibling: `species-design.md` asked the
questions that decide a **body**, `mind-design.md` the ones that decide a
**mind**, and this one asks the questions that decide a **language** — and,
because the record refuses the universal translator, the ways its translation
goes wrong. Written 8 September 2026 at Dermot's line, verbatim:

> alien linguistic patterns and common failure modes for translation

Nothing here is new canon. Every claim below is derived from
`src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`, `src/lore/star-rangers-academy.md`
(the Xenolinguistics Branch), the species and planet pages it cites, and the
*Below the Roof* chapters, and says so; where the derivation runs out the line
says **open** and the decision is Dermot's. Written unasked, as a draft, on
the same footing as the mind note: a language is stated before it is
translated, so that a season needing one reaches for what the canon
generates rather than the stock shapes the trope default refuses.

Open questions raised here are indexed in `open-questions.md`, per the
standing convention.

## What is already fixed

Three rules, all published, and everything below is their consequence.

- **The universal translator is refused outright.** Language is learned, by
  xenolinguists with AI support, on an exact division of labour: the machine
  aligns corpora, proposes parses and loses no morpheme; the linguist decides
  what a word *means* and owns the error. A kernel-compliant intelligence
  will not claim a certainty about meaning it does not hold, so its output is
  candidate readings with confidence stated. First contact is measured in
  years (`what-the-record-refuses.md`, "Signal and Distance").
- **The original is the document; the conversion is the courtesy.** Filed in
  the author's own notation, converted beside it, the conversion versioned,
  attributed and revisable (`timekeeping-and-the-common-record.md`). The
  record applies this to language exactly as to clocks, and marks where it
  has converted: the Told's tellings, the Ovruhn's person-forms, Nerath's
  "water".
- **The interpretive layer never smooths.** On a cross-species call the layer
  renders environment, posture and signal into terms the other body would
  recognise, with confidence stated and the behaviour still visible behind
  the reading. It never redraws the speaker as a human or resolves an
  ambiguity to spare anyone the trouble. The officer decides; the officer
  owns the error.

## The questions that decide a language

The body note's rule was *a sensorium determines what a person can be wrong
about*; the mind note's was *a people thinks in the medium it perceives
best*. The language follows both: **a people speaks on the channel its body
can afford, and its grammar marks what its sensorium cannot help noticing.**
These are the questions, in the order they bite.

### 1. What is the channel, and what does it cost to use?

Every described people has a channel the record names, and the cost of the
channel shapes the language more than anything else about it.

- **Ilveth** — quiet click-speech for the day's work, and the *warm-write*:
  vascular signalling vanes flooded and starved deliberately, meaning moving
  across the body as glow. On a world where heat is the only wealth the
  channel is never free: *no thermal small talk exists, emphasis is
  expenditure*, and an Ilveth who warms a sentence for you has spent on you.
- **Ollune** — a coherent low-frequency signal carried in air; crying out is
  *free*, costs nothing, binds nothing. The two peoples are the record's
  opposite poles on cost, and every other difference between their manners
  follows from that one.
- **Cerebraun** — two simultaneous channels: speech or writing carrying the
  central brain's stated position, and skin-display carrying real-time
  affective and confirmatory signal from the limb-ganglia beneath it, plus a
  formal display-language for precise technical and diplomatic work.
- **Chthonari** — the Undersong, felt through the structure the speaker
  stands on; deaf to airborne sound. Communication, art and thought run on
  it, and it does not translate.
- **Ovruhn** — click-speech and an under-song in which a body's sound and a
  voice's statement are *one signal*; person-forms carried as much in the
  under-song as in the speech.
- **Meridian sapients** — claw-percussion on shell and structure, audible at
  range underwater, and a slower chemical channel diffused into the water
  column that Cerebraun administrators still need instrumentation to follow.
- **The Told** — a spoken record and no other; and, older than speech for the
  purpose of announcing oneself, *a stone moved*, so the ground says you are
  there before you are seen. Their first word to a stranger is not a word.

*The question a season should ask first:* what does it cost this people to
say a thing, and what is free? Everything in question 2 follows from it.

### 2. What is a statement, and what does it commit the speaker to?

- An Ollune alarm *commits no one to anything*; asking why someone raised
  one is asking why they exhaled. An Ilveth warm-written sentence is a
  spend, and a people for whom saying costs *have never once said a thing
  carelessly*. The same utterance — *the air has gone wrong* — is a reflex
  on Cirrane and a gift on Sardain.
- A Cerebraun's words can be courteous and non-committal while the cost of
  saying them is paid, in real time, on the skin. The statement is on both
  channels or it is not the statement.
- A Krenyi does not confront a claim that has stopped matching its stated
  terms; they *withdraw relevance* from it, quietly, and return an exchange
  to material terms by *naming the distortion aloud*. Silence from a Krenyi
  is a reading, not an absence of one.
- Among the Told *a telling is not yours until somebody has heard it*, and a
  claim is tested by *who was there*. A statement nobody heard has not been
  made.

### 3. What does the grammar mark that ours does not, and what does it drop?

- **Contact.** The Undersong marks a person by whether they are felt through
  shared structure or not — a distinction the record's page *lets go, since
  a reader on a page is in contact with no one*.
- **Position without a plane.** The Ollune never developed maps and did not
  fail to; their notation is *depth, bearing and phase* from the Long Eye.
  *Where* is a different kind of question in that grammar, and *up* is a
  bill being paid.
- **Kin by lineage of symbiont, not birthplace** (Verdani): a name says
  which thallenwort strain you carry before it says where you were born.
- **The grown name** (the Told): a name is the first true thing said of a
  person that others went on repeating, and it is *extended*, never
  replaced. Length is maturity. The record renders it in translation with
  hyphens and does not shorten it.
- **Whole vocabularies with no single-word rendering**: Mnemari clinical
  language for the exhaustion of carrying every year at full resolution;
  the Ollune's terms for the Chthonari arrangement, which Chthonari
  translators *have declined, politely, to render precisely*.
- **What is dropped**: thermal small talk (Ilveth); a word for the planet
  (Ollune have *Freyari*, the band of air, which is also their word for
  *everywhere*); a word for *not being able to get to people*, which a
  species whose neighbours are reachable six hours in eleven years does not
  find remarkable enough to need.

### 4. What leaks, and what are the manners about leakage?

The record's most productive pattern, because it runs both ways.

- Ilveth vanes leak — exhaustion, illness and fear flicker involuntarily —
  and Sardain's oldest courtesy exists for exactly this: *it is bad manners
  to answer what a body says instead of what a person says.* Warm-blooded
  visitors, by Ilveth lights, never stop leaking, and the Ilveth extend them
  the same courtesy.
- To an Ovruhn ear a human body is a continuous broadcast — heartbeat,
  breath, the tremor under a controlled voice — and none of it coheres with
  the words. A human's statement and a human's body are *two signals that
  disagree constantly*; a calm body that strikes *is not in their model*.
- Cerebraun skin-display is leakage made into a channel, and *a trained
  listener reads both*.

*The rule this forces:* every people has an involuntary channel and a
courtesy about it, and the courtesy is the thing contact most needs. A
season that gives a people a leak should give them their manners for it in
the same breath.

### 5. What is a record, and who may correct it?

- The Told keep a record that *cannot be seized, cannot be edited by a later
  hand, and does not survive the loss of the people who carry it*.
- The Kingdom of the Five Islands keeps the Long Accounting, a written
  record older than either institution can confirm, in a language every
  Solar System register had presumed extinct; the Abbess who keeps it
  corrects the delegation's errors *without visible impatience* and
  distinguishes, in every exchange, what the Accounting states from what
  court tradition assumes.
- A Mnemari *is* a record — a negotiator who sat at a table two centuries
  ago does not need the file pulled — and pays for it.
- The Archive's own answer is the archival rule above: original filed,
  conversion beside it, the conversion's revision history part of the
  record.

### 6. What does the people call the things the survey named first?

The record is explicit that its exonyms describe the observers. *Pandoid* is
the survey's coinage and is kept in every filing; the people are *the Told*.
*Ollune* renders roughly as *the held*; *Cirrane* is a body and *Freyari* is
a world, and Ollune translators are firm that the two words do not denote
the same object. The Ovruhn's name for humans is *the Loud People*, a plain
observation; an Ilveth surveyor's is *lanterns that cannot be put out*. The
house practice is to keep both names side by side and never let the
survey's stand in for the people's.

### 7. What is a name, and what is it for?

The Told's is a telling; the Verdani's is a lineage; the Ollune's word for a
rich individual is *heavy*, and is not a compliment. Human names, to all
three, are the odd case: a label given before anything true has been said of
the person, which the Told would not recognise as a name at all. **Open,
his:** whether any season ever has a non-human character remark on this. It
is the kind of line that lands once and should not be reached for twice.

## The failure modes the record already holds

Each of these is on a published page as a case, not as a rule. Named here
as patterns so a season can reach for the right one, with the case it comes
from and the strand most likely to need it next.

1. **Filed as noise.** A language logged as a phenomenon. Cirrane: the
   coherent low-frequency signal the survey filed for *several weeks* as
   atmospheric resonance had been discussing the probe since it entered the
   shell. Meridian: heavy claw-percussion filed as biological noise until a
   duty operator noticed *the address* — the percussion was answering the
   test pulses pattern for pattern. The failure is the instrument's
   question, not its accuracy; the doctrine paid for it is that *no evidence
   of sapience is a statement about the survey*. **Most at hand for the
   Told's next season**: the survey's unit records a rising sound at the
   end of every shift and logs it as a sound.
2. **The correct transcript and the wrong meeting.** The Cerebraun second
   channel, unread: *the words were courteous and non-committal, and the
   second channel — where the cost of saying them was being paid — was not
   read at all.* The single most-cited cause of misunderstanding in Hegemony
   records, and *rarely dramatic, which is why it persists*. Structure
   captured; meaning missed.
3. **Answering the body instead of the person.** Early contact records full
   of Ilveth solemnly answering human blushes as statements; Ovruhn stopping
   mid-negotiation to comfort a human whose words were confident and whose
   heart was hammering, and being experienced as intrusive. The same failure
   from the other side: a person speaking for an institution, saying words
   their body does not hold, is to an untrained Ovruhn *a patient, not a
   diplomat*. The remedy on record is a courtesy, not an instrument.
4. **Reading an economy as a temperament.** Ilveth thrift with heat read as
   coldness of manner; Krenyi stillness read as poise when *movement is
   noise in your own readings*; Cerebraun three-part answers filed as
   evasion *for a decade* before xenolinguists admitted they were simply how
   the species thinks. The Academy's own diagnosis: *failures of patience
   rather than of method*. A pattern of cost mistaken for a fact about
   character.
5. **The courtesy mistaken for the fact.** Nerath: *water, sea, brine,
   salinity* — every one a translation made early, for human convenience,
   and *the courtesy has been mistaken for the fact for most of a century*.
   Cirrane and Freyari collapsed into one referent by Federation
   cartography. *The hybrid people*, a press coinage the record has never
   quite recalled. The translation's own vocabulary becomes the culprit,
   and the archival rule is the only cure: the conversion, revisable,
   beside the original.
6. **The question that does not translate.** *Ask an Ollune why they cried
   out, and the question does not translate.* The Undersong *does not
   translate*, and the Five-O treatment's instruction is to let that stand
   rather than explain it away. Chthonari translators declining, politely,
   to render a thing precisely — *a sentence no universal translator has
   ever had the honesty to say*. The honest output is a declined rendering
   with its reason stated, never a smoothed one.
7. **Naming by resemblance.** *Pandoid* from a bear, *the hybrid people*
   from three Earth animals in one paragraph: a familiar animal in a
   species' name describes the observers who coined it (species-design's
   caution), and the name then licences the wrong body and, this note adds,
   the wrong mind — a *bear-like* people are assumed to growl, and the Told
   have never raised a voice. Keep exonym and endonym side by side and let
   neither stand in for the other.
8. **Confidence entered as certainty.** The delegation on Vellamar noticing
   that *Krilzat* does not sit in the same phonological company as the other
   island names and filing it *as a candidate reading at low confidence* —
   the right way; the Abbess who knows *how much a single mistranslation
   can cost a written archive's credibility*; the kernel's output as
   candidate readings with confidence stated. The failure is the confident
   entry; the remedy is the error bar and the translator's name on it.
9. **The part-translated page.** What a written record structurally cannot
   carry: the Undersong's contact-marking, dropped *since a reader on a page
   is in contact with no one*; Ovruhn person-forms that arrive at the text
   *already part-translated*; the record's *they* for an Ovruhn, a Chthonari
   or one of the Told, which is *its translation default, not a claim about
   a pronoun system of their own*. The house practice is to say the
   conversion was made and what it dropped.
10. **The rendered telling.** The Told: *no phonetic form*; the thread told
    from inside is *a telling rendered, not a transcription*; intervals
    converted into UCSD and marked. The failure this guards against is the
    trope's — an invented phonology, apostrophes, a word glossed in a
    footnote — and the record's answer is to translate everything and mark
    that it has, the way it converts a clock.

## What does translate — mathematics, within the regime

Dermot's question the same evening — *is mathematics universal (within each
Metawright regime)?* — has a canon answer, and it is the one thing the
translator's craft can lean on. The scope ladder
(`the-scope-of-physical-law.md`) makes *provability regime-wide*: a
Metawright settles a conceptual realm's axioms and what can be proved in
them, every universe authored beneath that Metawright shares them, and *a
proof carried between regimes arrives as an assertion*. Above the regime
only Conseleme's condition holds — what any mathematics must satisfy —
and number itself begins below the Upper Structure. So within a regime,
mathematics is the one vocabulary two peoples share before either has
learned a word of the other; between regimes it is not, and no instrument
can be carried to where the comparison would be made.

The record already uses this without naming it. The tell at Meridian was
*pattern for pattern*: percussion answering test pulses in what no
disturbed animal does. The plainmark's tally strokes carry number across
every language on a habitat because number is not a language. The
Chthonari reach the same theorems as Federation mathematicians through
harmonic intuition rather than symbols — *close enough to the same fact*
— which is the mind note's rule (a people thinks in the medium it perceives
best) meeting the scope rule from the other side: **different routes, the
same regime, the same proofs.** What a first contact can establish without
a translator is that the other side counts, and what it can prove; what it
cannot establish that way is what anything *means*, and the refusal stands
exactly there.

One caution the scope page itself supplies: *universal* has two lawful
senses in Archive voice, universe-wide and Ensemble-wide, and regime-wide
is neither. A page that says mathematics is *universal* is using the older
loose word; the exact one is *regime-wide*.

## Traps this note exists to close

- **No conlang.** The record has never printed a word of any non-human
  language in its own phonology and should not start; *rendered in
  translation* is the house register, and a footnoted alien word is the
  universal translator wearing a scholar's coat.
- **No weekend fluency.** First contact is *years of translation work by
  named people with error bars*. A character who *picks up* a language
  between chapters has the refused device in their head.
- **No telepathy as translation.** The Ovruhn hear intention; the Ilveth
  see heat; the Cerebraun read skin. None of them reads *meaning* without
  learning it, and each page says so in as many words.
- **No one language per people.** The Knarr Line keeps five human
  languages on purpose; the Celtic Union six. A species with one tongue is
  the one-hat species the trope default refuses, said in grammar.
- **No smoothing.** The interpretive layer's rule is the prose's rule: a
  reading arrives as a reading, the behaviour still visible behind it.

## What the record does not say — open, and his

- **What two peoples speak across a table.** No page names a Federation
  working language, a trade tongue or a standard, and the trope default
  would refuse *Galactic Standard* on sight. Two honest shapes: (a) every
  standing delegation works in the other side's language, with named
  translators and error bars, which is what the Four Islands and Vellamar
  pages already show; (b) a written interlingua on the plainmark's model —
  which the plainmark page itself argues *is only honest if it carries far
  less than language does*. The record's silence is consistent with (a)
  and has cost nothing yet. **Open.**
- **Whether a xenolinguist is on the roof at Fliade.** Two years, a sound
  channel, and nothing translated is consistent with the refusal — but the
  survey party is a survey party, and the next season may need to say
  whether the Branch has been asked for. **Open**, and it touches the
  shadows seed (`intake-2026-09-08.md`, sixteenth section).
- **Whether the Told's word is one word or a phrase.** Already open on the
  glossary page; stays open.
- **Whether a crew off-Belt keeps an Undersong** — open in `mind-design.md`,
  and a language question as much as a mind one.
- **Whether any non-human character ever remarks on human names** (question
  7 above).

## Where this goes

For the Told's next season, whichever shape the shadows seed takes: failure
modes 1 and 8 are the survey side's — a sound logged as a sound, and the
temptation to enter a reading of it confidently — and mode 10 is the deep
side's discipline, already kept. For the Chthonari strand of Five-O's second
season: modes 6 and 9 — the thing that does not translate, and the page that
cannot carry contact — with the treatment's instruction to let both stand.
When a people's crew is cast, each member's page can answer the seven
questions above in the character's own particular, as the mind note asks for
the head and the body note for the frame.
