# Uniforms and insignia — reference for portrait prompts

Working reference for generating character portraits that stay consistent with
each other and with canon. Three forces, deliberately built to read differently
at a glance.

**What is canon and what is proposed is marked throughout.** Anything under a
*Proposed* heading is new and Dermot's to accept, change or reject; everything
else is already in `src/lore/`.

---

# One conflict to resolve first

**"Garda Síochána of the Celtic Union" cannot exist as a Union-wide force.**

`celtic-union-of-planets.md` is explicit: the Union is *"deliberately weak at the
centre. There is no Union-wide executive. Each member world retains full
sovereignty over internal governance, language policy, and **law**."* The
confederal charter covers only a narrow set of shared obligations.

A Union police service would be exactly the central executive the founders left
Earth to avoid — they were cultural custodians who feared *"an institution which
treated ambiguity as something to be re-categorised or erased."*

**Three ways out, and the second is the one written up below:**

1. Make it Union-wide anyway, which means amending the Union's founding
   character. Expensive and undercuts the entry's whole argument.
2. **Make it a single world's force** — the Garda Síochána of **Tír na nÓg**, the
   Union's Irish-speaking flagship world. Costs nothing, and it fits: the name is
   Irish, so a Welsh or Breton world would have its own differently-named service
   anyway. Sovereignty over law is *why* each world has one.
3. Make it a treaty liaison body rather than a police force — officers seconded
   from member worlds to coordinate cross-world matters, with no arrest power of
   its own.

Everything below assumes **(2)**.

*Addendum, 10 September 2026:* a fourth uniformed body is now canon beside the
three — the **Garda an Uachtaráin**, the Union's presidential protection unit
(Dermot: *Celtic Union presidential protection unit Garda an Uchtarain*;
`intake-2026-09-10.md`, eighteenth section). It is not the Union police this
conflict note rules out: it protects the Uachtarán, the council's presiding
member, and polices nobody. No prompt yet; when one is written it should read
apart from the Highland Guard (ceremony) and the Gardaí (a world's civil
police) — protection, plain, and not a costume.

# 1. The Star Rangers

**Canon, from `rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`.** This is the most constrained of
the three and the least free to invent.

## The governing idea

The uniform was designed to *reject* the Military Space Command's display
culture. The MSC dressed officers in braid and star-count that told you who
outranked whom "and almost nothing about what that person was certified to do in
a boundary zone." The Rangers rebuilt it *from the certification up rather than
the hierarchy down*.

For a prompt, that means: **functional, matte, unornamented, and slightly
austere.** No braid, no epaulettes, no ribbon-bar clutter, nothing gold.

**The reason, in Dermot's words (10 September 2026, `intake-2026-09-10.md`,
fourth section): *Star Rangers have uniforms for the same reason as hospital
staff do, except there are no plain clothes ranks or roles.*** Read for role
by colleagues, built for the work, announcing a function and not a claim on
anyone — and worn by every rank and every role on duty, Provost and Liaison
included, with no undercover arm and no exemption for seniority. For a
prompt: **a Ranger on duty is never in civilian dress**, whatever the rank or
the corps; a Ranger in ordinary clothes is a Ranger off duty, and the prompt
should say so. *Aliens have uniform equivalents* (same direction): the
medium per species is open — `species-design.md` carries the shapes — so
prompt an alien Ranger in the Rangers' shell and tabs, and prompt an alien
in their own service with no invented insignia until a page has chosen one.

## Hard constraints — do not violate these

- **No faction crest, no world-of-origin flag, no national emblem of any kind.**
  The charter's neutrality clause bars the uniform from *looking* like it has a
  side. A Ranger must read as a Ranger to every party in a dispute.
- **Insignia is never sewn on.** Every mark is a **magnetically-seated tab or
  plate** — hard-edged, seated proud of the surface, visibly removable. This is
  the single most distinctive visual feature and the easiest to get wrong in a
  prompt. Say *plate* or *tab*, never *patch* or *embroidered*, except for
  certification marks.
- **Modular shell over a thermal-and-pressure liner**, not a tailored garment.
  It must fit Krenyi, Pelagene Littoral, Veyr Basaltborn and Serephine Dunekin
  bodies, and bolt to a robotic chassis like Rook-7, which wears no garment at
  all and mounts its marks directly on the hull.
- **Dress uniform differs in finish, not insignia** — the same marks, better
  materials. A Ranger should never have to relearn the system across settings.

## Reading rank at a glance

**Canon since 4 September 2026** — Dermot's "Agreed" on the three shapes put
to him after asking about a Deputy's insignia (`intake-2026-09-04.md`,
twenty-fourth message). The table below and the track colours further down
are now on `src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md` as *Reading Rank at a
Glance*, with the bar count made explicit (Novice one, Deputy three, Field
Officer four, Section Lead seven). Portraits are left as they are: a plain
tab is what canon specifies at portrait scale.

| Tier | Mark |
|---|---|
| Novice → Section Lead | plain **bar count**, one more bar per tier |
| **Chief** (the hinge rank) | **bar-and-ring**, with a track-colour insert |
| Starwarden → High Captain | **rings only**, one more per tier, no track insert |
| Council of High Captains, Triumvirate | a **seal**, not a tab — a seated body, not a rank |

Tab colour is keyed to **service track**, not corps. Above Chief there is no
track insert at all: the command ladder marks *what post someone holds*, not
what track they climbed.

**Three separate marks, never conflated** — this is the design's whole point:

1. **Rank tab** — what they are trusted to command
2. **Certification patch**, worn beside it — what they are trusted to be *right
   about*. The significant one is **certified boundary analyst**, issued only by
   the Safety Corps.
3. **Decorations**, worn below — what they have *done*

## Two colour systems, not one — and they do different jobs

Corps-coloured uniforms and track-coloured tabs are **not in conflict**, because
canon only ever constrains the *tab*:

> Rank is marked with a tab or plate, plain-finished, in a colour keyed to
> service track rather than corps loyalty — the Rangers wanted a mark that told
> you *what kind of authority this person holds*, not *which department they
> answer to*.

That rule is about the **rank mark**. It says nothing about the garment. So:

- **The shell carries the corps.** Which department you belong to — Survey,
  Safety, Science, Frontier, Engineering. Visible across a hangar.
- **The tab carries the track.** What kind of authority you hold. Legible at
  arm's length, by someone who needs to know whether you can order an evacuation.

Far from breaking the design, this sharpens it. The charter's framers objected to
insignia that let people *"assume the second from the first"* — so keeping the
two on separate carriers, in separate colour systems, is exactly the point. You
can be a Safety Corps officer on the piloting track, and the uniform says both
without implying either follows from the other.

## Proposed — corps colours (the shell)

Muted and industrial, so a hangar full of Rangers reads as one service:

- **Survey Corps** — pale grey-green
- **Safety Corps** — dark amber-ochre
- **Science Corps** — deep teal
- **Frontier Corps** — dust brown
- **Engineering Corps** — graphite with copper seam-piping

## ~~Proposed~~ Canon since 4 September 2026 — track colours (the tab insert)

*(The six colours below were adopted as canon with the bar count, same day,
and now appear on the rank page verbatim.)* Canon said tab colour is keyed to
track but until then never named the colours. The lore
entry's own cover art is *"a blue chevron insignia emblem in a ringed seal"*, so
blue is already associated with the Rangers generally. Suggested:

- **General operations** — slate blue
- **Survey** — pale grey-green
- **Boundary safety** — amber
- **Piloting and navigation** — deep indigo
- **Security / provost** — oxblood
- **Engineering** — copper

Muted throughout, so the tab reads as information rather than decoration.

## The uniform is a standard of marks, not a garment

This is the part most easily lost in a portrait prompt, and canon is emphatic
about it. The Rangers' service includes Krenyi, Pelagene Littoral surveyors,
Veyr Basaltborn specialists, Serephine Dunekin scouts and analytical chassis
like **Rook-7, which has no garment at all and mounts its marks directly on the
hull.**

> That is the entire reason insignia is never sewn on. Every rank and
> certification mark is a magnetically-seated tab or plate, sized and positioned
> per body plan by the wearer's own quartermaster record… A robotic chassis wears
> the same tab standard as a human sleeve; it is simply bolted to a different
> surface.

**What is constant is the mark. What varies is what it is mounted on.** So a
portrait of a non-human Ranger should never show a human uniform stretched onto
a strange body — that is the failure mode. It should show the *tab* in corps and
track colour, correctly placed for that anatomy, on whatever that species
actually wears or is.

Practical guidance per body plan:

- **Chthonari** — small, six-limbed, exoskeletal, built for negligible gravity
  and brief vacuum. A garment would fight the exoskeleton. Marks seat **directly
  on the carapace**, on the upper thorax where a human wears a chest tab. Any
  fabric is a pressurised sleeve for sustained work, not a tunic.
- **Prismeri** — winged, with load-bearing flight membranes on Full Wings adults
  and long motile sensory tendrils from the skull ridge. **Nothing may cross the
  wing root or foul the tendrils.** Marks go low on the thorax; the shell is cut
  away entirely across the back.
- **Veyr Basaltborn** — mineral-bodied. Marks bolt to the body itself, like
  Rook-7's. Corps colour appears as an inlaid band rather than cloth.
- **Serephine Dunekin** — desert-adapted scouts. Corps colour on a light layered
  wrap rather than a sealed shell, since the thermal problem runs the other way.
- **Pelagene Littoral** — marine. Corps colour on a hydration or pressure
  harness; the tab is sealed and seated where it stays legible wet.
- **Rook-7 and other chassis** — no garment whatsoever. Corps colour is a
  **painted hull band**. The tab is bolted on, and looks it.

**Prompt rule of thumb:** describe the *tab* and the *corps colour* in every
portrait, and describe the garment only where the species wears one. Adding a
tunic to a Chthonari or a jacket to a chassis is the most likely way to generate
something that contradicts the entry.

## Prompt block — human Ranger

> Standard field uniform of an interstellar rescue and survey service: a modular
> matte shell in dark slate-grey over a close-fitting thermal liner, sealed
> seams, utilitarian and unornamented. No braid, no epaulettes, no national
> flags, no crests of any kind. Rank is shown by a small hard-edged metal tab
> seated proud of the chest, marked with plain horizontal bars [or: bars and a
> ring / concentric rings], with a narrow coloured insert. A separate small
> fabric certification patch sits beside it, and a row of low-key service
> ribbons below. Practical, worn, credible — the uniform of people who work in
> hazardous places, not of an army.

# 2. The Highland Guard

**Canon, from `s05e02c01`** — one scene, but a precise one.

They are a **ceremonial escort** of Tír na nÓg's uplands, and — canon since
10 September 2026, Dermot: *Highland Guard is part of the ceremonial
military* — a body of the Celtic Union's mostly ceremonial military, beside
the Garda an Uachtaráin, which is that military's one standing
non-ceremonial formation. Union protocol assigns
a Highland Guard honour escort to any Star Ranger on personal leave within Union
territory, from arrival until Ranger-cleared transit off-world. The escort is
*"a courtesy owed the Union's own charter, not a comment on the individual
officer."*

In the scene: **four of them**, **ceremonial rifles at rest**, bringing Shepherd
down from the uplands *"in good order and better silence"*. The narration gives
the note to play exactly:

> the kind of correctness that read less like hostility than like a people who
> had decided, generations back, that correctness was the safest shape for a
> feeling they didn't have a better container for

Their farewell is *"precisely, briefly, with no more warmth than the occasion
strictly required and no less."*

**So the visual brief is: formal, archaic-leaning, immaculate, and emotionally
withheld.** They are not soldiers and not police. They are a courtesy made
visible.

## Proposed

The uplands are working sheep country — the Shepherd flock-holding runs five
generations back in the Boirinn Uplands. The Guard should look descended from
that, not from a barracks:

- **Heavy weatherproof wool** in upland colours — heather, wet slate, dark peat.
  Cloth that would genuinely keep the weather out at altitude, cut formally.
- **A muted check or twill**, not bright tartan. The Union is Irish, Scottish,
  Welsh, Breton, Cornish and Manx — a loud tartan would read as one strand of
  that claiming the whole.
- **A knotwork gorget or shoulder-clasp in worn gold**, echoing the Union's own
  emblem, *"a gold Celtic knotwork emblem in a ringed seal."* One piece of metal,
  polished, and nothing else.
- **Ceremonial rifles**, long and old-patterned, carried at rest.
- No body armour, no tactical webbing, nothing modern. The point is not force.

## Prompt block

> Four ceremonial guards on an upland track in cold grey weather, standing at
> rest. Heavy formal greatcoats of weatherproof wool in heather and wet slate,
> with a muted dark check; a single worn-gold clasp of Celtic knotwork at the
> throat. Long old-pattern ceremonial rifles held at rest, not raised. Bearing
> immaculate and expressionless — correct rather than hostile. No modern
> tactical gear, no body armour, no bright colour anywhere. Overcast highland
> light, wet grass, stone wall behind.

# 3. The Garda Síochána of Tír na nÓg

**Proposed in full** — nothing about a Celtic Union police service exists in
`src/lore/` yet. See the conflict note at the top: this is a **single world's**
civil police, not the Union's.

*Síochána* means peace, and the name should be doing the work: **guardians of the
peace**, not enforcers. Everything visual should follow from that.

## Proposed

- **Deliberately unmilitary.** Where the Rangers are austere-functional and the
  Highland Guard are austere-ceremonial, the Garda are ordinary. Soft caps, no
  helmets, no visible sidearm as standard.
- **Dark bottle-green or near-black**, the least martial colours available, in
  ordinary hard-wearing cloth.
- **Insignia sewn, not plated** — the deliberate opposite of the Rangers.
  Embroidered shoulder flash, a simple harp or knotwork device, in the world's
  own language.
- **Rank in cloth**: chevrons and bars, low-contrast, on the shoulder.
- A **high-visibility over-vest** for public order and traffic, which is the
  most ordinary thing imaginable and exactly the point.

## The Ranger interface, which is worth getting right

The Star Rangers charter gives them **no jurisdiction on planets** — they enter
by invitation and must leave when it is withdrawn. So the Garda and the Rangers
never overlap: the Garda cannot operate off-world, the Rangers cannot police
Tír na nÓg, and any contact between them is a **handoff at a gate**, exactly as
the Highland Guard hand Shepherd to Galen in `s05e02c01`.

That is a good, quiet piece of worldbuilding: three uniformed services in one
scene, none of whom may do each other's job.

## Prompt block

> A civil police officer of a rural planetary world: dark bottle-green tunic and
> soft peaked cap, no helmet, no body armour, no visible weapon. Embroidered
> cloth insignia on the shoulder — a simple knotwork device — with low-contrast
> cloth rank chevrons. Calm, approachable, unmilitary bearing. Small town street
> in soft overcast light.

# Making the three read apart

The distinction that carries at a glance, and the thing to preserve in any
regeneration:

| | Star Rangers | Highland Guard | Garda Síochána |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Register** | functional | ceremonial | ordinary |
| **Insignia** | **magnetic metal plates** | one gold knotwork clasp | **sewn cloth** |
| **Palette** | slate grey, muted track colour | heather, slate, peat, worn gold | bottle green, near-black |
| **Silhouette** | modular, sealed, technical | heavy formal greatcoat | soft, civilian |
| **Says** | *I am certified* | *you are owed a courtesy* | *I keep the peace* |

Rangers wear metal that can be unclipped. The Garda wear thread that cannot.
That is not decoration — it is the difference between an organisation that
moves people between body plans and postings, and one that belongs to a single
place.
