# Ecology design — what each world's life lives on, and what lives on that

Planning note; not rendered on the site. Written 20 October 2026 at Dermot's
direction, verbatim: *"Ecology (a): an ecology-design.md sibling in the story
bible, worlds the story leans on first"*. The sixth sibling of
`species-design.md`, `mind-design.md`, `language-design.md`,
`starship-design.md` and `civilian-dress.md`. Drafted on Opus. Every point is
marked **derived** (it follows from a page cited by path, or from real
physics or biology applied to one) or **proposed** (a choice that fits, his to
overrule). **Not established** means the record is silent and this note
leaves it so. Nothing here is canon, and nothing here is written to `src/`.

**Verify before trusting.** Each quotation names its page. A chapter count or
a quoted line can go stale as the record grows; check the page before a
chapter leans on this file.

## Purpose and rules

### Why this file exists

The record has a strong frame for alien life and almost no ecology. The frame
is settled: no other people is a metazoan, *animal* is a declared translation,
and nothing was seeded (`src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`); any life
holds a boundary, runs a metabolism, keeps a record that copies with error,
and organises parts into a whole (`src/lore/what-a-life-is-built-from.md`).
What the record lacks is the next question: *what does a world's life live
on, and what lives on that?* One page states a food web, Cirrane's, which runs
on photochemical haze. No page names a decomposer or builds a chain from
producer to apex to rot. Prismere comes closest, with a grazer, a filterer and
an apex hunter, and no recycler.

So when a chapter needs a meal, a carcass, a season or a smell, the writer has
nothing to stand on, and the generator will fill the gap with an Earth biome.
This file is the ground to stand on, built before a chapter needs it, for the
worlds the chapters already use.

### How a world's ecology is built: from the energy source outward

The order is the body note's order turned on a whole world. Start with what
pays for life, then what makes food from it, then what eats the food, then
what returns the remains. Never start from an animal.

1. **The energy source** (derived, real physics). A biosphere needs a
   disequilibrium it can tap. The record's worlds offer five:
   - **Starlight**, taken by producers that build food from light. Free
     oxygen at Earth-like levels is the signature: no known abiotic process
     keeps an atmosphere that oxidising for long, so a world with breathable
     oxygen has had light-driven producers for a very long time.
   - **Chemistry**: reduced matter meeting an oxidant, such as hydrogen,
     sulphur compounds or metallic iron meeting oxygen, water or other
     oxidised matter. Producers that live on this need no light at all.
   - **Geothermal heat.** Heat is a habitat, not a meal. Uniform warmth can
     keep a solvent liquid and speed every reaction, but no organism can eat
     it. What a warm spring offers as food is the chemistry its water
     carries up from below.
   - **Photochemical haze.** Ultraviolet light builds complex organic
     molecules in an upper atmosphere, and they fall. The food is made by the
     sky, so the base of the pyramid is a reaction, not an organism.
   - **Tides.** A large tidal pull heats a body's interior (and so feeds the
     geothermal and chemical routes) and, at a coast, mixes water and
     exposes ground on a rhythm. Tides add no food directly; they make the
     others more productive.

2. **Producers, then consumers, then recyclers** (derived, real ecology).
   - **The pyramid.** Roughly a tenth of the energy at one level reaches the
     next. On Earth the figure runs from a few per cent to about twenty,
     depending on who is eating whom. Three things follow on every world.
     A large hunter is rarer than its prey by an order of magnitude or more.
     A web on a poor energy source is short, two or three levels at most.
     And a large animal on a poor source needs a large range, or it must be
     few.
   - **The recycler.** Something must return the dead to the producers, or
     the producers run out of what they are built from. On Earth that is
     decomposition. On another world it may be an organism, fire, or the
     planet's own depths. Every section below names which.
   - **The limiting nutrient.** Whatever is scarcest of the things a
     producer is built from sets the whole world's productivity. On Earth it
     is usually nitrogen or phosphorus. Off Earth it is whatever that
     chemistry needs and the world withholds.
   - **Seasonality** comes from axial tilt, from an eccentric orbit, from
     more than one star, from a star's flares, or from weather that keeps no
     period. A world's season is whichever of these dominates, and several
     worlds below have no tilt season at all.

### The Earth-animal attractor rule, for named fauna and flora

Non-sapient creatures are **named by what they do, never after an Earth
animal, and built from their world's medium**. This is species-design's own
rule (`story-bible/species-design.md`, *Living with other life*, the method,
point 4: *"It is named by what it does, never after an Earth animal. If it is
ever drawn, the prompt negates the nearest Earth animal by name"*), resting on
the taxonomy page's *A Likeness Belongs to Whoever Sees It*: a likeness
*"describes how it looks to the observer and never says where it came from"*,
and *"a likeness is a hint, never a template"*. The image convention is
`story-bible/images.md`, line 61, 12 October 2026.

Three working consequences (derived):

- **Survey names already published stand.** *Anstolik* (named for reindeer),
  *driftjelly*, *umbral ray*, *grianmhór* (*big sun-cat*) and the
  *ridge-strider* are field names, and the pages that carry them already say
  whose likeness it is. This file adds no name of that kind.
- **Every name proposed here is a plain compound of function or place**, in
  the Archive's register of *glasswood*, *root-mat*, *thornback* and
  *lumenspire*, and contains no Earth animal and no Earth taxon (no worm,
  mite, moss, grass or fungus in a new name).
- **A generic Earth word for a role** (*grazer*, *filter-feeder*, *producer*,
  *recycler*) is a translation like *animal*: it says what the thing does and
  nothing about what it is made of.

### The Tír na nÓg exception

Tír na nÓg's near-Earth life is the record's singular exception, and it
stays one (`CLAUDE.md`, *A singular exception stays singular*; the taxonomy
page: *"There is one possible exception, and it is an exception of degree, not
of kind"*). Its section works out what an Earth-like web there *means*, for a
chapter set on a farm or a shore. It never says why the web exists. The record
leaves that open in six theories and a file no institution has closed
(`src/lore/planets/tir-na-nog.md`, *An Anomaly Nobody Has Closed the File On*),
and on Sen's doctrine an explained gap invites the record to defend a guess
forever. Nothing in any other section may become a second Earth-like web,
however close a likeness runs.

## Fliade

### What the record says

- *"Cavern systems honeycomb the crust on a scale the surface gives no hint
  of, warmed by geothermal activity and fed by subterranean rivers, and they
  hold self-contained ecosystems that evolved largely independent of the
  conditions above them."* (`src/lore/planets/fliade.md`)
- *"Bioluminescent fungi coat the walls in soft amber and blue-green, filling
  the underground with a dim, steady light that has no cycle to it."* (same)
- *"The oxygen is the surface biosphere's, since any ground the Anstolik graze
  has producers of its own … What the deep networks contribute to their own
  air, or draw from it, has not been measured."* (same)
- The **Anstolik**: *"large migratory herbivores that range across the
  surface plains and the upper cavern levels alike, and defend feeding grounds
  and migration corridors with more conviction than their build suggests."*
  (same)
- The surface is *"thin, dry and mineral-sharp"*; the poles *"support no
  surface-dwelling life worth the name"*. (same)
- In the Told's telling: *"The first warm seep, the long dark before the
  fungus came, the coming of the up-people"* (S11E01C01, again in S12E01C01);
  and *"The first warm seep is a real place … at the bottom of the warm, where
  the amber is thickest: a place in the rock where the warmth comes up out of
  the ground on its own … The people do not live right at it. It is too
  warm"* (S12E01C01).
- The runners *"graze the upper levels"*; *"A runner is born knowing where the
  grazing is"* (S11E01C01, C02).

What the record leaves unstated is the gap the direction named: **what the
cavern fungi live on**. Also unstated: what the Told eat.

### What it derives

- **The glow cannot be the base** (derived, thermodynamics). A light made by
  members of a web is paid for out of food the web has already eaten, so the
  web cannot live on it; at best a little is recaptured. And the record's word
  *fungus*, read as the translation it is, names a consumer of fixed carbon,
  not a maker of it. So the fungi live on something, and the glow is spent
  energy.
- **The energy base is the seep's chemistry** (derived, from the page's
  geothermal warmth and the chapters' seep, with real chemistry applied).
  Warmth alone feeds nothing; warm water rising through rock carries reduced
  matter up from below. Where it meets the oxygen that comes down from the
  roof, through *"the passages that open onto the plain"*, there is a
  standing chemical gradient, and producers can live on it without light.
  The chapters already place the life where this predicts it: *the amber is
  thickest* at the seep. The glow is densest where the food is richest.
- **A second, smaller base comes down from the roof** (derived). Rivers carry
  surface matter in, and the Anstolik bring dung and their own dead into the
  upper levels. That input is seasonal and thins with depth.
- **So the deep is self-contained in its producers and not in its air**
  (derived). The page says both, and both hold. The deep's food is made below;
  the oxidant that makes the richest of it comes from the surface producers,
  which is why the page's *largely* independent is the exact word. Richness
  should peak where seep water and roof air meet, and fall off below, where
  only slower chemistry that needs no oxygen can run.
- **The fungi are the recyclers** (derived). In a sealed cavern nothing
  carries the dead away. Every body and every leaving stays where it falls,
  and what the record calls fungi is exactly the role that breaks them down.
  So the walls that light the deep are also what keeps it from filling with
  its own dead. That answers the gap: the fungi live on the leavings of
  everything else in the warm, on the seep's own production, and on whatever
  the rivers and the runners bring.
- **The pyramid is short and the large are few** (derived). A chemical base in
  a few warm places feeds a short web. A large-bodied people at its top cannot
  be many in any one warm, and warms must be spaced by what each seep yields.
  The page's scattered, unjoined communities and its *calm and reclusive* are
  what this predicts.
- **The deep has a season after all** (derived). Light below has no cycle, but
  water does. Snowmelt on the plain pulses the rivers, and the Anstolik
  migration brings its dung and dead to the upper levels on the surface's
  calendar. The deep's season arrives by water and by runners, never by light.
- **What kills a large animal** (derived where marked). On the plain: the
  winter, which the page makes long and hard. In the upper levels: falls on
  *vertical broken ground* (page). Below: cold beyond the warm's edge.
  Whether anything hunts the Anstolik is **not established**: the corridors
  they defend so stoutly may be defended against each other.

### Proposed

A minimal web, with three new names. Each is proposed.

- **Seep-mat** (proposed). A film of chemical producers on the rock around a
  warm seep, thickest where seep water meets air from above. It is the deep's
  own base. It need not glow.
- **The fungi** (the record's word, kept). Recyclers and the light of the
  deep, living on the dead, on the leavings of every grazer, and on the edges
  of the seep-mat. The glow is proposed as paid-for advertising. In still air
  with no wind, something must carry spores along a passage, and light that
  draws a small grazer to it does that.
- **Spore-carriers** (proposed). Small grazers of the seep-mat and the
  wall-growth, drawn to the glow and carrying its spores between passages:
  the one animal level between the producers and anything large.
- **Frostcrust** (proposed). The low, cold-hardy producer of the equatorial
  plain that the Anstolik graze. It makes the oxygen the whole planet
  breathes, the deep included.
- **The Told's food** (proposed; a chapter's to fix). The manners say the
  runners' ground *"was the runners'"* (S11E01C01), and a people who cede
  the upper levels are unlikely to hunt their grazers. So the Told are best
  read as gatherers of the wall-growth and the seep-mat's edges, living in
  the warm a little above the seep, where it is not *too warm*. Nothing on the
  page says so, and this stays proposed until a chapter needs a meal.
- **The Told's telling** (proposed reading, never history). *The first warm
  seep, then the long dark before the fungus came* is the Told's account and
  valid as theirs. This ecology happens to fit it, since a seep-mat comes
  before the light, and the record states that fit as a fit and no further.

### Traps

- **The Earth cave.** Blind white swimmers, hanging fliers at roost, glowing
  threads on a ceiling: the cave biome the word *cavern* imports whole. The
  deep's animals come from a chemical base, in still air, with a light that
  has no cycle, and are drawn from those facts.
- **The fungus forest.** Giant capped stalks in a lit hall are a fantasy
  furnishing. The record's fungi coat walls; its image shows brackets and
  small caps on wet rock. Keep them a coating.
- **The bear in its den.** The Told's body has already had to be designed
  away from the bear once (`species-design.md`, *The Told — a second warm's
  body*). Their home must not bring it back: no den, no hibernation, no
  hunting the runners.
- **An animal behind the shapes.** The shapes at the seep and on the line in
  Season 12 have no warmth, move no stone and are marked, not explained.
  This file supplies no organism that could account for them, and none may be
  added later to do so (CLAUDE.md, *mark the absence; do not explain it*).
- **The Anstolik as reindeer.** The survey's name stays; the animal is
  Fliade's, and a picture of it negates *reindeer, caribou, deer, elk*.

### What a page would need

If a chapter ever calls for it, one clarification on `planets/fliade.md`'s
*Below the Roof*: that the warmth that rises at the seeps carries the
chemistry the deep lives on, and that the fungi live on the dead and the
leavings of the warm. That is enough to make a meal, a death or a seep
legible. The Told's food would go on `src/glossary/the-told.md` or in the
chapter itself, in their voice.

## The Undersong Belt

### What the record says

- *"Thousands of bodies ranging from loose rubble to habitat-scale
  planetoids, none large enough to hold on to an atmosphere"*
  (`src/lore/undersong-belt.md`).
- The Chthonari *"evolved to move, build, and communicate in rock and vacuum
  rather than open air"*. Their exoskeleton *"tolerates brief unsuited vacuum
  exposure"*, but *"sustained work and reproduction both require a sealed,
  pressurized burrow"* (`src/lore/chthonari.md`).
- *"The Undersong Belt is already made of metal"*; *"no folk memory of
  scarcity of the usual kind"* (same).
- The vessik: *"a burrow commensal … the vibration-sensing arrangement common
  to a great deal of Undersong life … Chthonari settlements tolerate vessik in
  the walls for the reason humans once tolerated cats in a granary"*; on Eden
  it *"comes out for warmth"* (`src/characters/thrum.md`).

### What it derives

- **The Belt is an inside world** (derived, real physics). With no atmosphere,
  a liquid can persist only where rock holds it under pressure, sealed, in
  pores and cavities. Unfiltered ultraviolet and vacuum sterilise the outer
  faces. So Undersong life lives in the rock, and the people's need for a
  *sealed, pressurized burrow* is the need of everything that lives there.
- **Light and heat cannot pay for it** (derived). Starlight reaches
  millimetres into rock. A small body loses its formation heat quickly and
  holds little radioactive heat over the life of a star. So the energy base is
  chemical.
- **The metal is the food** (derived from the page's own fact, with real
  chemistry). Metallic iron is a reduced fuel. Where it meets water or other
  oxidised matter, it corrodes and gives off hydrogen and energy, and
  organisms elsewhere live on exactly that. A belt of metal bodies and
  volatile-bearing bodies has, wherever the two meet, a chemical battery
  that does not run down for a very long time. *Already made of metal* is the
  people's industry and, on this reading, their world's food.
- **The scarce thing is the volatiles** (derived). Metal is everywhere.
  Water, carbon and nitrogen, held in a closed volume, are not, and nothing
  that escapes through a breach comes back. So recycling inside a sealed
  volume must be close to complete, and the limiting nutrient is whatever
  volatile a cavity holds least of.
- **No large animals** (derived). Negligible gravity places no ceiling on size,
  but a sealed cavity's volume and a battery's slow yield do. The Chthonari,
  forearm-to-torso length by the page, are among the largest things the Belt
  supports.
- **The season is a seam's life** (derived where marked). Tumbling small
  bodies have no tilt season that reaches inside. What changes is that a
  contact face exhausts its fresh metal over years to centuries (derived),
  and life must follow fresh faces (proposed).
- **What kills a large animal** (derived): a breach, which vents the volume
  and freezes it; a fracture that opens or shuts a passage; the cold that
  follows either. The vessik's stillness as a fracture alarm is the ecology's
  most important safety fact: here the hazard is structural, and Undersong
  life evolved to feel it.

### Proposed

Two new names.

- **Seam-crust** (proposed). Producers living on the contact where metal meets
  volatile-bearing rock, in sealed cavities. They are the Belt's base.
- **Wall-grazers** (proposed). Small grazers of seam-crust and, in a burrow,
  of stores. They are what a vessik eats, which is the granary reason the
  page gives.
- **A burrow is a garden** (proposed). A fresh-cut face exposes fresh metal,
  and seam-crust colonises it, so every excavation also makes new ground for
  food. Pressurising a burrow keeps that ground alive. Chthonari stores (the
  *granary*) would be cultivated seam-crust. On this reading excavation is
  composition, as the page says, and planting too.
- **Warmth marks where the chemistry runs** (proposed). Seam-crust and its
  grazers make a little heat, and a pressurised, occupied burrow makes more.
  An animal that *comes out for warmth* is going where the food is.
- **Recyclers** (proposed, unnamed). The film and the grazers' own leavings
  are broken down in place by the seam-crust's own community. No separate
  recycler needs a name until a chapter wants one.

### Traps

- **The hive.** *Insectoid* invites a queen, drones and a single colony mind.
  The record gives individual Rangers making individual career choices
  (`chthonari.md`); nothing in this ecology needs a hive.
- **Space creatures in the void.** Nothing here lives in vacuum. An animal
  that crosses vacuum briefly is crossing between sealed pockets, as the
  Chthonari body does, and never lives outside.
- **The asteroid-miner frontier.** Prospectors, claim-jumpers and ore rushes
  are the space western the record refuses (CLAUDE.md, *No guns-and-spaceships,
  no space western*). The Chthonari cut; they do not prospect.
- **A famine history.** The scarcity of volatiles is a physical fact of the
  ecology. Whether a Chthonari *feels* it as scarcity is the mind note's, and
  the species page's *no folk memory of scarcity of the usual kind* must
  stand. Write no hunger-age into the Belt.
- **The vessik as a cat.** It is in the walls for a granary reason, and the
  likeness is the page's analogy, not the animal.

### What a page would need

If a chapter set in a burrow needs food, a breach or a death, one paragraph on
`undersong-belt.md` under a heading such as *What Lives in the Rock*: the life
is inside, it lives on the metal's chemistry, and the volatiles are kept. The
solvent and the Belt's distance from Skarn stay **not established** until a
page needs a temperature.

## Tír na nÓg

### What the record says

- *"The only surveyed world on record classified as naturally
  human-compatible: breathable atmosphere, non-toxic biochemistry, and a
  carbon-based biosphere stable enough to support unmodified human
  agriculture within the first settlement generation"*; *"Native flora and
  fauna are not Earth-descended; they simply resemble it closely enough that
  early colonists mistook entire genera for transplanted species"*
  (`src/lore/planets/tir-na-nog.md`).
- Lowland and wetland flora *"heavily toward pale, wide-petalled flowering
  plants"*, so close that colonists *"kept their old common names for them"*;
  *"dark-feathered wading birds"*, *"indistinguishable from home at ten paces,
  and unmistakably not at three"*; smaller shorebirds on tidal flats (same).
- *"Axial tilt runs close enough to Earth's own"* for a four-season calendar;
  uplands turn *"to gold and copper canopy in the autumn months"*; bare
  treelines *"for a good third of the local year"* (same).
- Two moons, Donn and Cliona, *"work the tides between them"*; they rarely
  align, and the **Conjunction** is the hour they do (same).
- The Boirinn Uplands: *"stony, lichen-hung"*, *"low-canopy native trees"*,
  *"ground cover that turns rust-red"*, conifer stands on the ridgelines, and
  *"livestock descended from Currach Fleet stock"*, horses as often as flock
  animals (same).

### What it derives

- **Energy base: Mannán's light**, taken by light-driven producers (derived:
  breathable oxygen at Earth levels is their signature, as above).
- **The shape of the web is Earth's shape** (derived). Flowering producers,
  grazers, hunters of small aquatic animals at the reed margins, shore feeders
  on tidal flats, a deciduous turn and a winter: a temperate web of the
  pattern Earth's ecologists would recognise, in the colonists' own kept
  names. The record supplies this, and the section adds no organism.
- **Compatibility runs both ways** (derived from *non-toxic biochemistry*,
  *unmodified human agriculture* and the Boirinn holdings). On other worlds a
  colony's crops and stock live in a sealed or engineered system, or on ground
  whose native life fed nothing an ark carried (Winterbourne's bare downs;
  Aethelrock's land, which *fed nobody before landfall*). Here,
  stock *descended from Currach Fleet stock* graze native ground in the open,
  so Earth animals eat native producers and return their dung to native soil.
  The two biospheres share a recycling floor: native soil life breaks down
  what Earth life leaves, and Earth soil life breaks down native tissue, or
  the holdings could not have run for generations. That is the ecological
  meaning of the exception, and it belongs to no other world.
- **It follows that what crosses can cross further** (derived). A compatible
  web is open both ways to invasion, to grazing pressure from introduced
  stock, and in principle to disease. Whether any of this has happened is
  **not established**, and the record should not be made to say it has.
- **Tides of two periods** (derived, real physics). With two moons the tidal
  range beats between their periods, and is greatest when they pull in line.
  So the flats' widest exposure, the shore feeders' richest hours, cluster
  around alignments, and the rare Conjunction brings the largest range of all.
  The folklore of *a great wave heard before it arrives*, which Cliona's name
  anchors (`planets/tir-na-nog.md`), sits beside that and need not be
  explained by it.
- **Seasonality from tilt**, as on Earth (derived from the page).
- **What kills a large animal** (derived where marked). The large animals on
  the page are the Currach Fleet's. What kills them is winter on wet stony
  ground, falls in the escarpment chutes, the spate (*"the falls run loud
  enough in spate"*), and husbandry. Large *native* animals, and any native
  hunter of them, are **not established**.

### Proposed

No new organism. Names on Tír na nÓg are the colonists' kept common names, by
the page's own account, so the Earth-animal rule there is answered by the
record (proposed reading): a native organism a chapter needs is named as the
colonists name it, with the *ten paces, not three* difference shown, and never
filed as Earth stock. That is the same singular exception, not a second
one. One proposed habit for chapters: a farming scene on Tír na nÓg can show
the shared floor (native and Earth litter rotting together in one field), and
should never show the reason.

### Traps

- **Ireland with two moons.** The danger is not the likeness, which is
  canon, but forgetting the three paces. Every native organism is unmistakably
  not Earth's at close range: pigment chemistry, the autumn turn that *behaves
  as though independently evolved*. A description that closes the gap has
  turned the exception into a transplant, which the record corrected.
- **Explaining the anomaly through the web.** The web shows *what* the
  compatibility does. It must never be made to show *why* (a precursor's
  seeding, a lost gate, providence). The file stays open on the page and in
  the codex survey note.
- **A second Earth-like web.** No other world gets an ecology this close.
  Trígrian's sun-cat and Kernowek Reach's ridge-strider are likenesses of
  shape, convergent and chemically foreign; neither may borrow this section.
- **The Hollow as ecology.** The pale-barked trees in Boirinn's hollow are an
  Etheric-layer structure expressing through ordinary trees
  (`src/lore/dryadic-trees.md`). They are not a species, and no organism
  explains them.

### What a page would need

Nothing for now. If a Boirinn chapter turns on farming, the page could gain a
clarification that native and introduced life share the soil, as a fact of
husbandry, with no word on mechanism.

## Verdance

### What the record says

- Four biomes, *"four sharply distinct climate bands"* from *"axial tilt and
  irregular ocean distribution"*: the **canopy belt**, *"a multi-layered
  rainforest"*; the **fenlands**, *"a vast tidal marsh network fed by seasonal
  flood cycles"*; the **high terraces**, *"sparse but highly specialized
  alpine ecologies adapted to thin air and mineral-heavy soil"*; the
  **steppe**, *"migratory herding ecologies structured around seasonal grass
  and water cycles"* (`src/lore/planets/verdance.md`).
- Each biome reached *"its own separate equilibrium"* because the bands stayed
  stable (same).
- Thallenwort, an obligate photosynthetic symbiont, pays *"a measurable share
  of daily caloric intake"*; *"Light Is the Budget"*; strains are adapted to
  each biome's *"light quality, humidity and canopy density"*; Verdani are
  *"fully compatible with standard humanoid life-support and atmospheric
  standards"* (`src/lore/verdani.md`).
- **Ruled 14 October 2026** (`species-design.md`, *His choices*, item 3): what
  the Aurelin Herdlands herd stays unestablished until a chapter needs a herd.

### What it derives

- **Energy base: the Calyx star's light**, taken by light-driven producers
  (derived: an atmosphere within humanoid standards, and the thallenwort).
- **Four light regimes, not four Earth biomes** (derived, the species-design
  method). What separates the bands for a Verdani is light quality, humidity
  and canopy density, and so the producers of each band differ first in how
  they take light: filtered and layered in the canopy, misted and dim in the
  fens, intense and unfiltered on the terraces, seasonal on the steppe. The
  kinbed colours on the people's page (deep green, olive-brown, pale,
  reddish) are the same four answers carried by a person.
- **Partnership is the biosphere's habit** (derived from
  `what-a-life-is-built-from.md`, which calls kinbed bonding the household case
  of a heredity that passes a partner, and says *"the general form recurs
  elsewhere"*). On Verdance it is likely common far beyond the Verdani:
  consumers carrying producers. That is how a consumer stretches a thin
  budget, and it is why a Verdani's hunger, light and health are one question.
- **Productivity** (derived, real ecology): highest in the canopy and the fens
  (light and water both), lowest on the terraces (thin air, and the cold a
  highland implies), pulsed on
  the steppe. The fens' *tidal* marsh implies a tide-raiser the record has not
  named; **not established**.
- **Recyclers**: not established. The Understory Assembly's *older,
  slower-growing groves* on the forest floor imply a slow floor where the
  canopy's fall is returned, which is where a canopy's recyclers would be.
- **What kills a large animal**: not established. On the steppe, a failed
  water cycle is the derived candidate.

### Proposed

No new organism. The record's own words carry a four-band web, and the 14
October ruling holds the herd back for the reason it gave: a herd named early
is where the pastoral allegory gets in. If a chapter needs the herds, the
section's derived points are the starting place: build the herd from the light
budget and the water cycle, and decide first whether it carries a partner.

### Traps

- **Four Earth biomes by single word.** *Rainforest*, *marsh*, *alpine* and
  *steppe* each import an Earth whole (`species-design.md`, *The Earth smuggled
  in by a single word*). Read them as light regimes.
- **The forest people of legend.** A green, slow, forest-dwelling people
  invites the elder tree-folk and the planet-wide forest mind. Verdani are
  four nations with factions, and Verdance's forest is a biome, not a person.
- **The pastoral allegory.** As ruled.

### What a page would need

Nothing for now. Light quality per band is already on `verdani.md`.

## Prismere

### What the record says

- *"A genuine biosphere world … stable, self-sustaining, no life-support
  engineering required"*, on *"a silicate-carbon chemistry that builds glass
  and crystal into living tissue"*; forests that glow before sunset, and night
  light *"bright enough to read a datapad by"* (`src/lore/planets/prismere.md`).
- **Glasswood**, *"slow-growing, crystalline-barked trees whose translucent
  foliage doubles as a lens"*, re-emitting light; **lumenspires**, *"mineral
  analogues to fungal towers"*, each fed *"by a shared root-mat network"*;
  **glass spires** at elevation (same).
- **Driftjellies** filter *"airborne spore and particulate matter"*;
  **thornbacks** *"browse glasswood undergrowth and shelter in lumenspire
  root-shadow during the brightest hours"*, shells *"shed naturally during
  growth molts"*; the **umbral ray**, apex glider, hunts driftjelly colonies
  *"and, on rare confirmed occasions, unwary Prismeri"*, riding the same
  thermals; hence the dusk curfew (same).
- *"A permanent haze of suspended crystalline particulate, seeded continuously
  by its own biosphere"*, which makes the Lattice visible (same).
- Low gravity, dense atmosphere, seven moons (same).

### What it derives

- **Energy base: Iridess's light**, taken by glasswood (derived).
- **The page already holds two chains** (derived): glasswood → thornbacks;
  and glasswood's airborne spore and particulate → driftjellies → umbral rays.
  The second is the record's one aerial food chain with an apex.
- **The umbral ray's rarity is the pyramid's** (derived). An apex hunter two
  levels above an airborne filter-feeder is rare by an order of magnitude or
  more, and an attack on a Prismeri is rarer still. *"Rare confirmed
  occasions"* is the right frequency.
- **Dusk concentrates the hunt** (derived where marked). Thermals over open
  ground fade as the sun goes down (real physics); the ones that last rise
  from ground that has stored the day's heat. If the groves hold heat longest
  (proposed), the day's last thermals, and the driftjellies riding them,
  gather over the lumenspire stands where the Prismeri live, and the rays
  follow them there. The curfew is the people's reading of that.
- **The glow is affordable** (derived, with an Earth-like noon assumed). Light
  enough to read by is a few lux. A bright noon delivers on the order of a
  hundred thousand. Spending a small fraction of a day's capture on night
  light is cheap, so the glow needs a purpose but not a large one.
- **The root-mat is the recycler** (derived). *Fungal*, read as the
  translation it is, names the role: the network that feeds the lumenspires is
  the one that takes up the dead. Glass-built tissue makes recycling slow,
  since silica must be dissolved before it can be used again, so fallen
  glasswood lies a long time. The root-mat's line of light across standing
  water is the recycler made visible.
- **The thornback export is a small permanent loss** (derived). Shed shells
  taken off-world carry silica and whatever else is built into them out of the
  cycle. At present scale it is negligible; at trade scale it would not be.
- **What kills a large animal** (derived where marked): an umbral ray, for a
  driftjelly or a straying Prismeri; for a thornback, **not established**. The
  molt is the derived candidate (soft between shells).

### Proposed

One new name.

- **Glow-followers** (proposed). Small night fliers drawn to flowering
  glasswood's glowing blossoms (the page's image shows glasswood *in flower
  with faintly glowing amber and rose blossoms*), carrying its reproductive
  matter between trees. They give the night glow its purpose. They never come
  near a settlement: a kept or resident flier would trip the Prismeri facet
  band all night (`species-design.md`, Prismeri under *Living with other
  life*).
- **The haze is partly the forest's spore** (proposed). The particulate the
  driftjellies filter and the biogenic haze that shows the Lattice are, on
  this reading, partly the same thing. So the sky that reads the corridors is
  thick with the forest's reproduction. A season of heavy spore would show the
  Lattice best. This stays a reading, and the Lattice's physics is not touched.

### Traps

- **The crystal kingdom.** A glowing glass forest invites the enchanted realm
  of fantasy. Prismere's glow has a budget, a recycler and a hunter, and its
  architecture is shaped by people who notice what holds a load.
- **The names as templates.** *Driftjelly* and *umbral ray* are survey
  likenesses. A driftjelly is never drawn as a medusa (the Ollune lesson,
  `species-design.md`, *The jellyfish*), and an umbral ray is never drawn as a
  manta. Negate both by name.
- **The dragon.** The Prismeri have been drawn as dragons more than once, and a
  winged hunter in the same frame doubles the risk. Never picture an umbral ray
  with a Prismeri.
- **The monster.** The umbral ray is an animal at the top of a pyramid: rare,
  hungry and ordinary. It is not meta-dimensional, and the curfew is not a
  counter-move against a monster. It is a people's sense about thermals.

### What a page would need

Nothing for now. A chapter about the night forest could carry the
glow-followers in prose first.

## Cirrane

### What the record says

- *"The shell's food web runs on the haze — the photochemical organic aerosol
  Cirrane's upper atmosphere produces continuously in sunlight, drifting down
  through the shell as a slow permanent supply of edible complex molecules.
  Cirrane's ecology is filter-feeding almost from top to bottom, and its large
  fauna, the Ollune among them, are overwhelmingly soft, buoyant, and
  translucent"* (`src/lore/planets/cirrane.md`).
- The temperate shell is *"some tens of kilometres thick"*. Below it *"the
  descent is not survivable"*, and it is *"simply the direction in which things
  stop"* (same).
- Zonal bands sweep past one another; the shear layer is *"the most hazardous
  environment the Ollune routinely enter"*; the Long Eye is a persistent storm
  (same).
- An Ollune floats on warm gas *"by continuous metabolic effort, every hour it
  is alive"*; *"Anything an Ollune carries must be lifted for as long as they
  own it"* (`src/lore/ollune.md`).

### What it derives

- **Energy base: Vantine's ultraviolet, through the haze** (derived from the
  page). The base of the pyramid is not alive. Most biomass sits one level
  above the base, on filter-feeding, and the page's *almost* leaves room for a
  thin layer of hunters above that.
- **The recycler is the planet** (derived, gas-giant physics). Whatever dies
  sinks below the shell, into heat and pressure that break it down to simple
  gases. Convection returns those gases to the upper atmosphere, and sunlight
  builds them into haze again. No decomposer organism is needed to close the
  loop, though anything that feeds on the slow fall of the dead within the
  shell takes a share on the way.
- **The haze evens out the day** (derived). Haze is made only in sunlight but
  falls slowly through tens of kilometres, so the supply in the shell is close
  to constant. The day/night rhythm a filterer feels is in heat, which an
  Ollune buys in sunlight and pays for at night (`species-design.md`,
  *Ollune*), not in food.
- **Bands are separate populations** (derived). Two shoals in one band never
  meet, and neighbouring bands meet for hours at intervals of years. Any
  drifting organism is likewise held to its band.
- **What kills a large animal** (derived): heat lost, then lift lost, then the
  fall below the shell; the shear layer; the Long Eye's outer bands. Hunting is
  at most the *almost*.

### Proposed

One new name.

- **Haze-sifters** (proposed). The small drifting filterers of the shell, too
  small to matter one by one and most of its living mass together. The Ollune
  feed on the same haze at a larger scale, and nothing needs to feed on the
  sifters for the web to stand.
- **The hunters above** (not established). The page's *almost* names a gap,
  and this file leaves it one until a chapter needs a hunter.

### Traps

- **Sky whales and floating leviathans.** A gas giant's large life invites the
  vast singing beast of the clouds. Ollune are people, and the large fauna are
  soft, translucent and limited by lift.
- **The depths as underworld.** The page refuses it, and so does this ecology.
  The depths recycle; they are not a place.
- **Earth's ocean by analogy.** *Marine snow*, *plankton* and *krill* import a
  sea. The shell is air, with no surface below and no shore.

### What a page would need

Nothing; the page already states the web. One clause on the depths'
recycling could join *The Temperate Shell* if a chapter needs a death at
depth.

## Ilenne

### What the record says

- An old, quiet orange dwarf that has *"not flared on any instrument"*;
  gravity *"a little under nine-tenths of standard"*; air *"nitrogen and oxygen
  at about Earth's surface pressure, with somewhat less oxygen than Earth's and
  about seventy times as much carbon dioxide"* (`src/lore/planets/ilenne.md`).
- Rain in wet runs, then dry spells that *"have no period"*, from three years
  to most of a century (same).
- *"Its producers are low and long-lived, holding water and reserves through
  the dry spells and growing in the wet runs, so that a hillside can stand
  apparently dead for decades and green within a season of the rain coming
  back"*; large animals *"live by walking between such places"*; *"few of them
  are hunted, since a biosphere this sparse feeds few hunters"*; a large animal
  *"is killed far more often by the land than by anything living on it: thirst
  on the wrong route, a fall, cold"* (same).

### What it derives

- **Energy base: a dim orange star**, taken by light-driven producers
  (derived from the oxygen). Less light, and redder, means lower productivity
  than Earth's at the same water (derived).
- **The carbon dioxide helps the producers** (derived, real plant physiology).
  A producer that takes carbon from the air loses water every time it opens to
  do so. At seventy times Earth's carbon dioxide it can open less and lose less
  for the same carbon. On a world where water, not light, is the limit, that is
  a large advantage, and part of how a hillside can wait for decades.
- **Recycling comes in pulses** (derived, real ecology). Decomposition needs
  water and warmth. In a dry spell the dead accumulate almost unbroken; when
  the rain returns, decomposition wakes with it and releases a store of
  nutrients at once. The page's *green within a season* is that pulse.
- **The pyramid is short and thin at the top** (derived; the page states the
  consequence: *few hunters*).
- **Seasonality from weather, not tilt** (derived from the page). The year's
  tilt season is **not established**. The rain's run-and-failure is what
  governs.
- **What kills a large animal**: as the page says.

### Proposed

One new name.

- **Rain-wakers** (proposed). The recyclers of the uplands, dormant through a
  dry spell and active within days of the rain. A wet run's first season is
  theirs before it is the producers'. The smell of a hillside the morning
  after rain returns is what a Mnemari would remember of every spell.

### Traps

- **The elephant that never forgets.** The likeness a reader brings to a long-
  lived, long-memoried large walker. Ilenne's memory is a cost and the body
  is a tripod (`species-design.md`, *Mnemari*); negate *elephant* in any
  upland picture.
- **The Earth desert.** Dunes, camels and oases import a hot sand world.
  Ilenne's uplands are cool, high, worn and waiting.
- **The drought apocalypse.** A dry spell is the world's ordinary weather,
  not a catastrophe. No dystopia, and nothing in it that argues hope was
  foolish (CLAUDE.md, *Tone*).

### What a page would need

Nothing; the page already carries its web. *Rain-wakers* could enter a
Mnemari chapter as a remembered smell before it enters any page.

## Trígrian

### What the record says

- A circumbinary planet of a tight orange pair, with a smaller white companion
  on a wide eccentric orbit; insolation swings *"by double-digit percentages"*,
  and the companion adds a slower cycle; *"Trígrian's climate is not a pattern
  with seasons"* (`src/lore/planets/trigrian.md`).
- *"The land is grass because grass is what survives … deep-rooted,
  fast-seeding, fire-adapted, dormant within days when the heat spikes"*; herd
  species migrate *"across thousands of kilometres"*; *"Ash from the burning
  seasons and dust off the dry plains feed soils of exceptional depth"*; the
  recovery flushes are *"disproportionate"* (same).
- The triple sunset *"opens the burning season"*, with *"continent-scale fires"*
  (same).
- The **grianmhór**, apex grassland predator, in *"sororal colonies that share a
  single breeding matriarch"*, holding embryos *"in diapause through the burning
  seasons"*; hunts and evacuations coordinated *"across kilometre distances by
  means the survey has not yet fully characterised"*; the sapience clause
  applies *"with unusual force"* (same).

### What it derives

- **Energy base: three suns' light**, taken by light-driven producers
  (derived). Continent-scale fire proves free oxygen at roughly Earth's level:
  at Earth-like pressure, wildfire does not spread below about fifteen per
  cent.
- **Fire is the recycler** (derived from the page's ash). Fire returns a
  grassland's standing dead as ash in hours, not seasons, and the soils' depth
  is the record of it. Below ground, soil life does the rest.
- **Seasonality from orbit and three stars, not tilt** (derived from the
  page). The insolation maximum, three suns together, sets the burning season.
- **The pyramid** (derived): grass → herds → grianmhór. Apex colonies are few
  and range widely, which is what kilometre-scale coordination implies.
- **The diapause is the pyramid's answer to fire** (derived from the page). A
  predator that breeds only into a recovery flush breeds when the prey
  multiplies, so its numbers follow the grass at one remove.
- **What kills a large animal** (derived): the burning season above all; heat
  spikes and moving water; the grianmhór. The page's second report opened with
  a casualty list after a fire season, not after an attack.

### Proposed

No new organism. The page carries a complete grassland web, and its herds stay
unnamed until a chapter needs one, on the 14 October precedent for Verdance.

### Traps

- **The savanna documentary.** The page leans into the likeness on purpose:
  the grianmhór is the induction example that resemblance is a shared problem,
  not shared ancestry. The danger is a chapter taking the likeness as licence.
  At close range the grianmhór is a sororal colony with a single breeding
  matriarch and a diapause, and a description that shows a pride of lions has
  made the claim the record refuses. The page's photograph stands in at *any
  distance a field scope closes*, and a generated picture of the grianmhór
  would negate *lion, lioness, big cat*.
- **The man-eater.** The casualty list is the fire's. With the sapience
  question open, the grianmhór must never become a hazard to be shot.
- **A second breathable world.** Oxygen enough for fire is not human
  compatibility. The page does not say a human can breathe Trígrian's air, and
  Tír na nÓg stays the only world that asks nothing of a human first.

### What a page would need

Nothing; Trígrian's page already holds the record's most complete web.

## Where this goes

**Nothing is published from this file.** A world's ecology reaches its lore
page only when a chapter or a page needs it, and then by extension (adding to
what stood) or clarification (saying better what was already true), never by
rewriting. This is the Cerebraun and Mnemari pattern: the Cerebraun body
waited in `species-design.md` until a page needed it, and Ilenne's page took
from the Mnemari section only the world facts the body needed. Each section's
*What a page would need* names the smallest extension that would serve.

**For image prompts.** A fauna or flora prompt for a world starts from that
world's section here: its energy base, its medium, the name proposed, and
its traps, which become the standard negative (the nearest Earth animal or
biome, by name). A prompt for Tír na nÓg's life starts from the page's own
photographs and the *ten paces, not three* rule. A prompt for an organism this
file does not name waits until its section names it.
