::: pov tissadelle

She did the arithmetic once more to see whether it had changed, and it had.

Not worsened. Finished. That was the difference she had been keeping the
count for, through mornings past numbering that came up grey and then gold
and declined, as instructed, to resolve — and she checked the finding twice,
once for content, once for what it was for, and for the first time since
Dock Seven the two readings said the same thing in the same voice. The
becoming was done. What she was now was not a woman holding a door. It was a
door.

She had been taught — by her mother's kettle, by a well in Ireland, by a
levril whose entire vocabulary was the word *no* spoken gently at the edge
of everything — that the one unforgivable act left to her was to let someone
else say what this meant. Nobody else had. The account had stayed open,
true, untied. So the tying was hers, and she found, testing the knot the
way she had tested every load-bearing thing in a working life, that she had
no speech to make about it. It was the only executable order left. She had
never given one she couldn't defend, and she was not going to spoil the
record now.

There were farewells, and she made them properly, and they are not in this
record. What was said in each belonged to the one it was said to, and she
had never in her life volunteered what was not hers to spend. Some of those
she took leave of had crossed with the house and grown old in it in ways
the forms back home had never had boxes for; to each of them, whatever else
was said, she said that the holding would stand. And once — only once, at
the low wall, with the flock sound coming up from below — she named the ones
who had not crossed. Each of them. Fully. The way a debt is discharged, so
that nothing, in her or out of her, could go on feeding on a story left
vague. The grief came then, the whole of it, and she let it arrive and
observed that it was smaller than the fondness, and had the grace to know
itself outranked, and left.

Her mother was at the kettle. The made Wender stood by the door and did not
offer to be kinder than the real one, which was, as it had always been, the
kindness. You do not say goodbye to memory. Memory is not staying behind;
it goes with the house, and the house was no longer hers.

It had been his since he saved it. She had merely been living in it, the
way she had lived in the uniform, the way she had lived in the rank —
correctly, and on loan, and with the inventory kept current for the day it
had to be handed over clean.

The book lay on the table where it always lay. She had read it twice
through in the years of mornings, and there were pages in it smoothed over
the way water smooths a name from a stone, and she had never once asked
what was under them. Some things a person keeps without needing the reason
said aloud, and some things a friend keeps *for* you, and the not-asking
was the last gift she had that cost her anything to give.

Tobble sat in the middle of the standing part of everything, being a cat,
watching her the way he had watched her for thirty-eight years, which was
entirely, and without appearing to.

*You did the arithmetic,* he said.

*Twice,* she said. *It closes.*

*Yes,* he said. *I did it three times.*

The morning came up over the Boirinn Uplands, grey, then gold, and this
time it did not stop there. The edge at her back went from warm to
something there was no instrument word for, because no instrument had ever
been on this side of one: the feeling of a door that has spent six years
learning to be a door finally being *about* to be open, and of everything
on the far side of it being his, and new, and enough.

She put her hand down, and he pressed his head against it, once, hard, the
whole weight of him behind it, and neither of them dressed it up as
anything other than what it was.

*Happy New Universe, Tobble dear.*

::: pov tobble

He had been asked, and he had truly considered declining, because declining
was real — the one door in all of this that canon and courtesy alike held
open — and a thing is only chosen if the other thing could have been. He
had sat with it the way he sat with everything, somewhere warm, at length,
without visible effort. What decided it was not duty, which he had none of,
and not her, who had scrupulously refused to ask. It was the house. A house
wants living in. He had found the one part of it still standing, once, and
put something small and living inside, and it had not occurred to him until
much later that the manoeuvre worked in both directions.

His book was in order. It had been in order for years, which is the only
state a record is permitted, and the glosses were where he had set them —
some over pages that were nobody's business, hers least of all and the
record's never, and some over pages where, on review, he had figured too
well, and a book that admires its author is not a record, it is a statue.
He had smoothed those flat himself. She had never asked. He had watched her
not-ask twice through, at reading pace, and had filed the not-asking where
he filed the few things too large for any page.

Then her hand came down, and he put his head into it with the whole of
himself, and she said the thing she said.

He did not answer in words. There was no room in the moment for them, and
anyway the moment was not addressed to his vocabulary. He held still — he
was good at that; it was on the record — and the door finished being a
door, and the fondness went through it with her, all of it, at once,
and what remained on his side of everything was a holding, a morning, a
flock somewhere below, a book, and a universe, his, one second old.

The morning came up grey, and then gold. He sat in the middle of it, being
a cat, and waited to see what it would do, and what it did was stay.

*Good morning,* Tobble said, to somebody who could not hear it, now, ever
again — and said it anyway, in full, because thresholds get greetings, and
she had taught him that, or he had taught her, and the book, on review,
declined to establish which.

Later, in his own hand, he entered the day. The entry is one line long.

Then he glossed it.

::::: scene 2

::: pov wender

The watch had never been handed off. There were officers who could have
stood it — Sen had offered, in the manner of an archivist offering, which
is a filing proposal with kindness in it — and Wender had declined them
all, on the grounds she gave nobody, which were that the last order on the
file was hers, and that the order had been *let her tell us what she
wants*, and that an order is not discharged by delegating the listening.

So it came to her, as it was always going to, at four in the station's
morning: Rook-7's arrays, then Sen's confirmation, then her own eyes on
the raw phrasing before anyone had dressed it in an interpretation. Six
years of iteration — sustained, warm, patient as weather — and now one
final phrasing, complete, closed at every margin, and not repeated. She
read it twice, once for content, once for what it was for, because a dead
woman had taught her that and the woman had declined, for six years, to be
dead.

The content was short. The account, returned — unaltered, which from
Shepherd was a signature more binding than ink: *checked, found true,
nothing to correct*. The finding, completed at last in the flat voice the
whole Corps had learned to trust precisely because it never reached past
its evidence: *the marker states. It resolves.* And then the one sentence
that was an order, addressed to the station, to the Council, to the file,
and — she did not pretend otherwise — to her: *enough is enough. Close it.*

Under all of it, beneath the data where only a non-standard array would
think to listen, Rook-7 had logged a cadence. Structured, it said.
Non-informational, it said. Rook-7 was a fine analyst and wrong twice in
one line, and Wender, who had stood at the back of exactly one muster with
her collar loosened and heard the cohorts sing the fast one, recognised
the slow one — the companion piece, the one that circulates quietly and is
not sung, the one that was neither confirmed nor denied as hers in public
and now never would be, because the only person entitled to confirm it had
just spent it, privately, on a High Captain standing alone in a signals
room at four in the morning. It was not in the content. It was in the
phrasing. It was how the sentence was *carried*, and no line of it would
ever appear in any record Wender kept, which was, she understood, exactly
the point. Some things a person keeps without inventing the reason.

Then the warm edge went from warm to finished.

Not cold. Finished. The arrays caught the difference before she could have
said it aloud: a signature out of the manuals' back pages, the founding
profile, the one every boundary officer memorises and no one at Threshold
had ever seen outside the textbook — jurisdiction Telearch, standing
referred, classification not casualty. Somewhere past the edge of
everything the station could measure, something one second old had a
morning in it. The log wanted a word for the opposite of a death and the
log did not have one, because the forms had never had a box for her. They
were not going to start now.

The levril did not linger. She had grown used to it the way you grow used
to a lighthouse — present at every distance, asking nothing — and she
perceived its going the only way anyone perceives anything about them: as
a shape her own mind supplied, which this morning was an officer stepping
back from a door, watch complete, permitted at last to leave, and leaving
the way the good ones leave, without being thanked.

She closed the file herself. It took four minutes. The last sentence of
the accurate account was hers to write and she wrote it the way the whole
account had been written, exactly as long as the truth required and not
one clause longer: *Line Captain Tissadelle Shepherd, at her own order,
file closed complete — cause of closure: founding.* The Council would
misread it. The Codex would improve it. What she privately knew would go
on never quite reconciling with either, and she found she had stopped
minding, because the woman the three accounts disagreed about had signed
off on the disagreement herself.

"Goodnight, Captain," Wender said, to a signals room, at four in the
morning — one crack in a career of not doing that, entered into no record,
paid without complaint.

Then she filed it. Records don't need telling. They only need keeping, and
she meant to keep this one a very long time.
