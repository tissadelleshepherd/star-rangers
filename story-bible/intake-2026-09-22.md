# Intake, 22 September 2026

## The enigmatic quality named: the reader may assume nothing about what is real — a direction, given in chat

Given in chat while the Survey Archive's *Standing Claims* working paper was
being written, unprompted. Verbatim:

> So the mysterious enigmatic quality of Fian Ilchruinne is that the reader
> can never make assumptions about what is real and what reality consists
> of, even when the surroundings look familiar

### Read against what stands

- **Mysteries are shadow-shaped (2026-09-08)** says what a mystery *is* — a
  real thing seen through a gap that cannot be crossed, unsolved because
  unsolvable, marked rather than closed. It locates the enigma in the object.
- **Endings are recognitions, not reveals (2026-09-20)** says what the
  marking must do to the reader at the end of an arc. It locates the enigma
  in the shape of the telling.
- This direction locates it in a third place, and the one neither rule
  reaches: **the reader's own standing ground, at every moment, not only at
  the gap and not only at the end.** It is the register rule the other two
  were resting on unstated.

### The tension that has to be handled, not avoided

`src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` commits this record to hard science
fiction with one licensed deviation: real physics at the Material and
Physical layers, causality stable, no ansible, no time travel. That is a
promise the reader *can* assume, and it must not be read as withdrawn here.

The two are compatible, and the distinction is load-bearing:

- **The laws are stable, knowable, and promised.** A reader may rely on them
  and the record does not cheat them.
- **What a familiar surface is seated on is not assumable.** The same lawful
  cosmos supports ordinary noon light and a figure of borrowed daylight
  standing in it (`src/glossary/photographic-beings.md`); an ordinary human
  island kingdom and a cognate that puts an abbey under a steward it does
  not answer to; a habitat corridor and a Membrane Shadow's geometry.

So the rule is not *anything may be anything*. It is that **familiarity is
not evidence**: the ordinariness of a surrounding licenses no inference
about what it rests on, and the reader who reasons from resemblance is
making exactly the error the record's own institutions are built to catch.

### What the record already does about it, which is the reason this reads as a discovery rather than a new demand

The Archive is the reader's instrument for this, and has been. Sen's
doctrine of marked absences says a gap is marked and not explained. The
companion paper filed today, *Standing Claims*, grades what the record
*holds* on what each holding has survived — and its first line of doctrine
is the same sentence as this direction, addressed to an archivist instead of
a reader: a claim stands on what it has beaten, not on what it fits. A tidy
reading and a true one are the same shape from the shelf. Strozzi's *tribute*
is the worked case, and his question is the reader's as much as the
linguist's: *what would have to be true for this to be wrong, and did you
look.*

### The line drafted for `CLAUDE.md`, put for approval

> **Familiarity is not evidence: the reader may assume nothing about what is
> real** (Dermot's direction, 2026-09-22). The laws of this cosmos are
> stable, knowable and promised — `what-the-record-refuses.md` is not
> softened by this rule and nothing in it is withdrawn. What is never
> promised is that a familiar surrounding is seated on a familiar reality.
> An ordinary corridor, an ordinary noon, an ordinary human word may each
> rest on something the reader has no instrument for, and the record will
> not signal which. This is the third member of the mystery family, beneath
> the other two: the shadow-shaped rule says a mystery is unsolvable and
> marked; the ending test says the marking must change how the beginning
> reads; this says the reader is standing on unassumable ground the whole
> time, not only at the gap. It follows that the record never uses
> familiarity to reassure, never lets a resemblance carry an inference the
> prose has not earned, and never resolves an enigma by returning the reader
> to solid ground — the ground was never the thing that was solid. The
> reader's instrument is the Archive's own discipline, which grades what it
> holds by what that holding has survived (`src/codex/standing-claims.md`)
> and marks what it lacks without explaining it
> (`src/codex/marked-absences/`).

**Settled by Dermot the same day: the name is *Familiarity is not
evidence*.** The two alternatives put and set aside were *The ground is
never assumed* (truer to the feeling, vaguer as an instruction) and *No
inference from resemblance* (precise, and reads as a note to a linguist
rather than to a reader). Recorded in `CLAUDE.md` as the third member of the
mystery family, with the corollary and the two clauses below under it.

## The failure named: monsters that become ordinary and routine — the same direction, continued

Given in chat immediately after, as the worked example of what the rule
refuses. Verbatim:

> So for example, The Witcher fails as a magical fantasy, when the monsters
> become ordinary and routine, likewise with most Fantasy RPGs, in my
> opinion

### What the failure actually is

It is worth being exact, because the obvious diagnosis is wrong. The
monsters do not stop being strange because they are *explained* — this
record explains a great deal and loses nothing by it. They stop being
strange because they become **repeatable**: a class of thing acquires a
known counter-move, the counter-move works every time, and from that point
the creature is a procedure with a face on it. A bestiary entry is a
*predictive* classification with an answer attached — silver, oil, a
resistance table, a loot drop — and the second reading of it is
housekeeping. The RPG form makes this structural rather than incidental,
because a stat block is a promise that the same inputs will produce the same
outcome, which is exactly the promise a mystery cannot survive.

So the failure is not knowledge. It is **leverage that repeats**.

### Where this record already stands, which is better placed than it looks

- **The Corps' classification is descriptive and says so.**
  `src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md` splits the job into *frames*, which
  describe structure and claim no ancestry, and *lineages*, which claim
  ancestry and never leave one world — on the stated ground that across
  biospheres there is no tree, so a cross-species rank would be a claim about
  kinship where none exists. A bestiary's power is that it predicts things it
  was not built from; this scheme says in its own voice that it cannot, and
  that a Corps entry using an Earth word is a conversion, not the document.
  That is the anti-bestiary, already built and already load-bearing.
- **The one counter-move the record does name refuses repeatability in its
  wording.** The Unfounded are *countered, not addressed; starved, not
  destroyed; no chief* (`src/glossary/unfounded.md`). Nothing there is a
  procedure anyone can run twice.
- **The instruments measure without mastering.** A cross-membrane gravimeter
  registers a Photographic Being. Registering it is all it does. Detection
  that confers no leverage is the form knowledge has to take here.
- **The gaps are already filed as gaps.** *Marked Absences* carries a live
  absence — a specimen the record cannot classify despite a taxonomy built
  for organisms that defeat the usual one — and an unverifiable one, the
  number of Concordants, where the gap is in the reachable universe rather
  than in the record.

### His clarification: monsters are not refused, they are conditioned

Given in chat the same evening, and it answers the corollary rather than
qualifying it. Verbatim:

> In Fian Ilchuinne, monsters are rare exceptions, but valid under boundary
> conditions

This is consistent with what is already published and says it in one line
for the first time. `src/lore/meta-dimensional-beings.md` has boundary zones
as the most common entry point, with Class II/III instability at Concordant
edges permitting ingress, and Class III as boundary-state escalation whose
precursor conditions physical instruments can detect. The monsters are a
consequence of the one licensed deviation — the cosmology —
and `what-the-record-refuses.md` already names them in that breath.

**Why this matters for the corollary:** the record's protection against
routine monsters is **ontological rather than editorial**. A boundary
condition is not a place anyone can go to reliably, manufacture, schedule or
farm; it is an instability at an edge, rare by the cosmology's own terms.
So the Witcher failure is barred by the world before it is barred by a
writing rule. A monster-of-the-week season is not something this record
declines to write — it is something the record cannot supply the conditions
for.

The two halves then lock together: **rarity and boundary-dependence govern
how often a thing appears; the no-repeatable-answer corollary governs what
anyone can do about it when it does.** Either alone leaks. Frequent
encounters with no counter-move become a siege; rare encounters with a
reliable counter-move become a procedure that happens to be seldom used.
Both are needed and both are already the record's practice.

### The exposure, stated so it can be watched

One exposure is real and should be written down rather than argued away.
`src/lore/meta-dimensional-beings.md` lists monster *types* with feeding
behaviours — Beautiful Monsters, Threnes, Cute Predators, shadow-beings —
and a typed list of predators with named appetites is a bestiary's shape.
What keeps it from being a bestiary is that the list classifies by **what a
thing does** and nowhere attaches what anyone does back: no counter-move, no
material that works, no procedure. Class III precursor conditions are
detectable, which is **warning, not leverage** — the same discipline as the
gravimeter that registers a Photographic Being and does nothing else. The
page is one entry away from the other thing, and the entry it must never
gain is a remedy.

The record is not at risk from having a taxonomy. It is at risk on the day
any entry attaches a **reliable, repeatable answer** to a class of thing —
a procedure that works on all of them, every time, at a cost the record
treats as payable. That is the moment a Membrane Shadow becomes a drowner.
The test to apply to any future monster, protocol or protective measure:
*can a competent officer run this twice and expect the same result?* If yes,
the thing it is run on has stopped being a mystery, whatever the prose says
about it.

### Drafted as a clause of the same rule, put for approval

> **The corollary: nothing acquires a repeatable answer.** What kills an
> enigma is not explanation but leverage that repeats. A creature, boundary
> phenomenon or Unfounded manifestation may be described, measured,
> classified by frame and written up at length; it may never acquire a
> counter-move a competent officer can run twice and expect the same result.
> The Corps' taxonomy is safe here because it is honest about predicting
> nothing it was not built from; the Unfounded are safe because they are
> countered and starved rather than addressed and destroyed; the instruments
> are safe because they register without conferring leverage. The named
> failures are *The Witcher* and the fantasy RPG (Dermot, 2026-09-22), where
> the monster becomes ordinary at the moment its answer becomes routine.

**Settled by Dermot the same day: a clause of the *familiarity* rule, not a
fourth rule.** Both say a surface licenses no inference, so the Witcher test
and the familiarity test now sit where a session meets both at once, and the
mystery family stays at three members. The alternative put and set aside was
its own entry, on the argument that it bears on the trope default and the
tone guardrail as much as on the new rule.

## The science-fiction form of the same dilemma: aliens

Given in chat after the rule was settled, and it extends it rather than
reopening it. Verbatim:

> A similar dilemma for science fiction is aliens who become too familiar
> and too humanoid unless that is the point is the story, but aliens as
> space monsters is worse, in my opinion

### Two failures, and he ranks them

**Too familiar, too humanoid.** A people becomes a person in a costume. The
reader's own frame is never challenged, so the encounter stops being one —
which is the fantasy dilemma in a different coat: familiarity doing work it
has not earned. His exception matters and is not a loophole: *unless that is
the point of the story.* The human isolates are exactly that case. The
Kingdom of the Five Islands is human by descent and the point is that it is
human; [Strozzi's](/star-rangers/characters/ernesto-strozzi/) standing
position is that this makes the work harder rather than easier, and the
*tribute* nine months are the proof. Familiarity as the subject is honest.
Familiarity as a shortcut is the failure.

**Aliens as space monsters, which he ranks worse.** The first failure
flattens a people; the second deletes the category. A people becomes a
hazard — something to be countered rather than met — and that is two
breaches at once: the corollary's, because a hazard is the kind of thing
that acquires a counter-move; and the cast principles', which hold that
antagonists are misguided and never pure villains, that war is never assumed
of a people, and that minds are not ranked.

### What already holds the line, and must not be broken

- **No other people in this record is a metazoan**
  (`src/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`). The humanoid default is refused
  at the level of biology, not of description.
- **Peoples and monsters are filed in different places.** Peoples get
  *frames* and *lineages*; monsters are classified as meta-dimensional
  (`src/lore/meta-dimensional-beings.md`), where sapient monsters have no
  Cascade standing and no legitimate authority at any tier. Keeping those two
  filings apart is the structural reason *alien* cannot collapse into
  *monster* here — and the reason a page must never quietly move a people
  from one to the other.
- **The record says in its own voice that nothing prefers a humanoid** —
  `src/lore/arilon.md` on Champion selection, and the dryadic entry's flat
  statement that the Dryad is not a humanoid being either.
- **The built examples run the other way.** The Chthonari feel the world
  through rock and are six-limbed and exoskeletal; the Veyr are
  lattice-framed. *Humanoid* appears in the corpus as an off-worlder's
  yardstick, never as the shape a people happens to have.

So a new people is built from its world and its senses outward, and any
resemblance a reader finds is convergence — which the taxonomy already says
in its own voice, and which is the same sentence as the silhouette clause
above.

**Nothing open.** Recorded as a fourth clause of the *familiarity* rule in
`CLAUDE.md`, beside the silhouette clause it is the science-fiction half of.

## The middle rung, and faith — two statements of one principle

Two directions given the same evening, the second reaching further than the
first. Verbatim, in order:

> The middle rule: partial evidence leads to a valid belief but is not
> certainty

> Faith is neither blind credulous belief nor absolute certainty beyond
> doubt

### The first, and what it changed

The morning's work gave the middle grade its treacherous half and not its
honest one: *Standing Claims* called *consistent* the most dangerous genus
on the list, and the `applied-statistics-for-AI-engineers` card said only
that the first strength justifies acting. Read plainly, both make the
ordinary condition of knowledge sound like a failure — and most of what any
archive holds, or any evaluation establishes, will never stand at the first
grade.

The correction is now in both (star-rangers PR #842,
applied-statistics PR #23): **none of the grades is a reproach.** Partial
evidence earns a belief; a belief may be relied on in proportion — worked
from, planned around, submitted, taught — by anyone who knows what grade it
carries. What it does not earn is certainty, and **the error is never the
rung but filing one as the next one up.**

### The second, which is the same sentence about faith

Faith sits at the middle rung, and the two things it is refused as are the
two adjacent rungs claimed falsely:

- **Blind credulous belief** is not the middle grade at all. It is a claim
  filed without the question ever being asked — below even *sole*, which at
  least means someone looked and nobody offered a rival.
- **Absolute certainty beyond doubt** is the *verified* grade, which is
  earned only by surviving the rivals that were actually tried. Claiming it
  without that is the Strozzi failure: a high confidence band that is a claim
  about the claimer rather than about the claim.

So both refusals are one error in opposite directions, and the principle
holding them apart is the one already written into the Archive's discipline.
That is why this belongs in the record and not only in a conversation: the
gradings were built for translations and incident reports, and they turn out
to describe a religious epistemology without being bent to do it.

The in-record resonance is Strozzi's, and it is not about doubt: *the only
honest use of a high band is one you would still have filed knowing it would
sit uncorrected for a year.* A discipline of confidence, not a demand for
certainty.

### The caution that goes with it

The record's voice is institutional, and this must stay a statement about
**grade**, never an argument that anything in particular is true. The moment
a page uses the middle rung to argue *for* a belief it has become
apologetics, which is a different register and not this record's. The
church-space thread is one readership's overlay, gated to the contemplative
tier, and nothing here changes what any other tier reads.

**Settled by Dermot the same day: shape (b), a clause in the *familiarity*
rule's paragraph in `CLAUDE.md`.** The three put were (a) nothing published,
the principle governing from here; (b) the clause; and (c) a church-space
codex entry in the existing voice, the natural companion to *Ask, and It
Will Be Reviewed*. The reason for (b) over (c), and it stands as the reason
not to revisit it lightly: the principle is doing real work across three
repositories already, and a page written the same day would be the record
arguing rather than recording. (c) remains available later, if the prose
ever reaches it — which is the ordinary route for anything in this record.

## The technique observed: the silhouette that fires and is never confirmed — the same direction, continued

Given in chat shortly after, as a worked example of the effect rather than
the failure. Verbatim:

> The new Pendragon Rise of Merlin series on Netflix had an interesting
> twist when a distinct people almost looked like elves or Fae, but weren't
> and the other people almost looked to mythical Tolkien dwarves except for
> normal height. The effect was heighted by different styles of clothing and
> bearing, but made an interesting surreal quality.

(Recorded as described; the series postdates this assistant's knowledge and
nothing about it is asserted here beyond Dermot's account of it.)

His own qualification, given immediately after, and the reason this section
is about a technique rather than about a series:

> The Rise of Merlin has some elements of fantasy which confuses the issue

It does, and the confusion is instructive rather than a reason to drop the
example. **In a world that has magic in it, the near-miss cannot resolve.**
A reader who files a people as Fae has made a reading the setting can never
refute, and might quietly be right; the silhouette may have been declined,
or merely varied, and there is no way to tell. The effect is real but
provisional — it decays into ordinary worldbuilding the moment the reader
decides the world would allow it either way.

**This record has the opposite problem, which is to say none.** The hard-SF
floor is already promised and published: no elves, no Fae, no magic, one
licensed deviation that is a cosmology rather than a permission
(`src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md`). So the referent is *known to be
unavailable* before the reader meets the people at all, and the impression
can only ever be the reader's own — convergence, dress, bearing, register.
The technique is therefore **more stable here than in the work it was
observed in**, not less, and the thing to borrow is the technique and not
its setting.

That also sharpens the guard rail below: the record never has to deny the
impression, because it has already denied the category in general terms and
in its own voice. Denying it page by page would be answering a question the
reader was meant to keep holding; the standing refusal does the work once
and silently.

### What the technique is

A third thing, and neither of the two the trope default already governs.
Refusing a trope removes the shape; using a trope cashes it in. This
**lets the shape arrive and then declines the referent**: the reader's
recognition fires, files the people as elves or as dwarves, and nothing ever
confirms the filing or denies it. The surreal quality is the sound of a
classification that was made and never tested.

Two details in the account do the load-bearing work and should not be
mistaken for decoration:

- **The carriers are clothing and bearing, not physiology.** A pointed ear
  is a claim, and a claim can be checked and refuted, which collapses the
  effect either way. Dress, carriage, register and custom are *choices*, so
  the resemblance stays a resemblance and never becomes evidence for
  anything. The surreal quality survives precisely because nothing testable
  was offered.
- **The near-dwarves are of normal height.** The single feature that would
  have sorted the impression is absent — the discriminating check the reader
  never gets to run. That is today's *consistent* genus applied to the eye
  instead of the shelf: the reading fits, another reading fits equally, and
  nobody looked.

So this is not a fourth rule. It is the **reader-facing instrument** of the
*familiarity* rule: the way a page makes a reader feel the unassumable
ground rather than be told about it.

### Where it fits this record, and where it does not

- **Not the Veyr or the Chthonari.** A lattice-framed people and a
  six-limbed insectoid people are too far from any myth to almost-read as
  one; the eye files them as new and the effect never starts.
- **The human isolates are the natural vehicle**, and the record has several
  — the [Kingdom of the Five Islands](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/),
  the Celtic Union's worlds, Plainmark, the Generation Ark descendants. They
  are human, so a reader cannot retreat to *alien* and file the strangeness
  under biology. Everything odd then has to live in dress, bearing, custom
  and register, which is exactly where the observed technique puts it.
- **The in-universe licence already exists.** `cross-biosphere-taxonomy.md`
  holds that two species that look alike are convergent design or
  coincidence, **never branches of one line**. The record has already said,
  in its own institutional voice, that resemblance is not descent — which is
  the same sentence the technique asks the reader to feel.

### The line that keeps it legal, and the hazard worth writing down

The trope default refuses well-known tropes. This technique deliberately
invokes a silhouette, so the distinction has to be explicit: **the trope is
refused as a referent and used only as a reader-side effect that is never
confirmed.** Nothing in this record is elves. If any page ever confirms the
impression, the surreal quality collapses into fantasy with the serial
numbers filed off; if any page ever pointedly denies it, the record has
answered a question the reader was supposed to keep holding.

The hazard is nearer than it looks. This record already has a malign version
of *wearing a shape it has no claim to* —
[The Borrowed Silhouette](/star-rangers/codex/the-borrowed-silhouette/), on
the Unfounded wearing the archetypes of what they counterfeit. A reader who
knows that paper may read every benign near-miss as a counterfeit, which
would make an ordinary human people sinister for no reason the record
intends. The two need to be tellable apart, and the difference is available
without a word of explanation: an Unfounded's borrowed shape is doing
something to someone, and a people's dress and bearing are doing nothing at
all except being theirs.

**Nothing open here that blocks the rule** — this section records a technique
and its guard rails rather than a decision. If it is wanted as a written
practice rather than a note, the natural home is the *familiarity* rule's
paragraph in `CLAUDE.md`, one sentence, after the corollary.

## The register of each rung, named

Given in chat after the rule was settled, and it fills a gap the tier
principle had left open since 4 September. Verbatim:

> The child reader tier should be interesting and playful, the young adult
> tier should be more mysterious with a sense of exploration and adventure,
> the general tier should go deeper, and the contemplative tier is allowed
> to question everything

### What it fills

`CLAUDE.md`'s standing principle says the ladder *"selects by register,
protagonists and depth"*, and `src/tiers/index.md` defines each rung by the
threads it carries, whose body is in the room and which address serves it.
Neither ever said what the register **is**. Protagonists and depth were
specified; register was named and left empty. This is the missing third.

### The reading

Four registers, and they escalate in **permission** rather than in
seriousness — which is the distinction worth holding, because the obvious
misreading is a ladder from light to heavy:

- **Children — interesting and playful.** Short scenes, plain words, a story
  enjoyed rather than decoded. Delight is the measure, and a playful page is
  not a shallow one.
- **Young adult — mysterious, with exploration and adventure.** The reader
  goes out into the record and finds things. The mystery is an invitation
  here, where on the rungs above it is a weight.
- **General — deeper.** The same record with its reasons under it: the
  institutions, the cost of a decision, the Lore and Codex behind a chapter.
- **Contemplative — allowed to question everything.** Nothing is off the
  table, the record's own premises and `CLAUDE.md`'s own rules included. A
  questioning page is not a grim one.

The tone guardrail and the shadow-shaped rule hold at every rung; none of
this moves them.

### The line that keeps it from breaking the ladder

The invariant on the public page is that **nothing read on a lower rung is
withdrawn or contradicted on a higher one**, and *question everything* could
be read as licence to do exactly that. It is not, and the distinction is
one line: **questioning is not contradicting.** The contemplative tier may
ask anything and may leave the asking open; it may not withdraw or deny a
reading a lower rung was given. A question asked at the top is an addition
like every other thing that tier adds.

That also explains a pattern already in the corpus rather than inventing
one: the epistemics can surface explicitly at the top — the middle rung of
belief, the marked absences, Sen's gradings — while the rungs below simply
practise them without naming them.

**Nothing open.** Recorded in `CLAUDE.md` under the tiers principle, and as
a *How it reads* line on each of the four sections of
`src/tiers/index.md`, so a reader choosing a door sees it too.

## The revelation ledger read as an evaluation dataset — scoped and deferred to the confirmation pass

Not a direction about the story. It arose in chat while the applied
statistics course's exercises were being rewritten, and it is recorded here
rather than there because the one action it asks for falls on this
repository, during work nobody has scheduled yet. Open questions index:
`open-questions.md`.

**How it came up.** The course's fourteen applied exercises all opened on
the reader's own evaluation harness, so a reader who has not got one could
do none of them. They were rewritten on 22 September to offer a second route
through Reference Set 2, the 12,713 frames of Dermot's own photography with
his own keep verdicts, which he had ruled admissible on 21 September. He
then observed that his writing might serve as data too. It was scoped
against this corpus the same day.

**What the dataset would be.** Not prose. Two columns already exist here and
disagree with each other in an interesting way:

- `revealed_by`, on an Archive page, is a **declared label**: a human
  judgement that the prose has established that page's subject for a reader.
- `scripts/revelation-ledger.js` prints beside each page **the first chapter
  whose body names the page's title**, which is an automatic guess at the
  same thing.

A declared label and a machine predictor over the same items is exactly the
shape an evaluation has, and nothing else in the course carries it: Reference
Set 2 has ground truth but no system output, because nothing scored those
frames.

**What it measures, taken at `16dc671` on 22 September.** Over the 328 titled
pages of `src/lore/`, `src/glossary/`, `src/codex/` and `src/timeline/`,
against the 64 chapters in story order:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| declared, and the rule finds a chapter | 24 |
| declared, rule finds none | 3 |
| not declared, rule finds a chapter | 5 |
| not declared, rule finds none | 296 |
| precision / recall / F1 | 0.828 / 0.889 / 0.857 |
| accuracy | 0.976 |
| recall's 95% interval on 27 positives | 0.72 to 0.96 |

Where the rule and a declaration both name a chapter they agree on which
chapter 23 times in 24. The exception is `krenyi.md`, declared `s01e01c01`
and found at `s01e00c04`.

**Why it would teach well.** Three things in it are real rather than staged.
The accuracy is 0.976 and means nothing, while the class that matters rests
on twenty-seven rows and spans a quarter of the scale. The rule fires on
21.7% of pages with titles of one or two words and on none at all of the 124
with five or more, and short titles are disproportionately what has been
declared, so its apparent precision is partly an artefact of which pages were
labelled. And the declaration rate splits hard by section — glossary 18.9%,
lore 7.7%, codex and timeline zero of 85 pages between them.

**What it cannot serve.** A paired comparison needs two rules that disagree;
two variants differ on three pages, which is too few to test and is only an
illustration of the lesson's own advice to read the rows instead. A
calibration table needs a score to bin, and the rule is binary.

**Why it waits, which is the substance of this note.** The twenty-seven
labels are a first pass for confirmation, not a settled verdict — that is
what `16dc671` says of itself. So the absence of a label on the other 301
pages does not mean the prose never named them; it means nobody has declared
yet. A confusion matrix that treats every blank as a true negative is
therefore wrong, and the table above does exactly that. Dermot, asked
whether the labels were stable enough to build on: *They might not be
stable*, and then *leave it until the confirmation pass*.

**Waiting costs nothing, and gains the better example.** The present state is
already pinned: every one of the twenty-seven declarations is recoverable
from `16dc671` for as long as the history exists, so there is no snapshot to
take and no window closing. And the course's test-set governance lesson
currently teaches re-adjudication from an invented example — five hundred
shared rows, sixty added, one label changed. Two versions of this set,
derived from two commits with the labels re-adjudicated between them and the
scorer untouched, would replace that with a real one. One version cannot,
and shipping a single frozen set whose labels are known to be provisional is
a weak reason to publish anything.

**The one thing the confirmation pass should do, and it is free at the time.**
Record in the commit message what moved and why — which pages gained a
label, which lost one, which had the chapter corrected, and on what grounds.
That is the adjudication log, it is the half that cannot be reconstructed
from the diff afterwards, and without it the second version is a changed
number with no account of itself, which is the precise failure the lesson
exists to name.

**Two constraints, both already resolved, so neither needs revisiting.** The
dataset needs no titles: page slug, section, title word count, declared flag,
declared chapter, rule's chapter. No story text crosses into a differently
licensed repository, and the question of whether a title index is a
derivative work never has to be answered. And because nothing readable
ships, the list of pages the prose has not yet reached becomes a column of
booleans against opaque slugs rather than a map of where the story is going.

**Open.** Whether to build it at all once the labels settle, and whether it
is then a third reference set or a second version of an existing one. Both
wait on the confirmation pass.
