# Intake, 23 September 2026

## *Dragon* keeps one meaning, and *meta-dragon* becomes *meta-Levril* — a ruling, given in chat

Asked in chat, verbatim:

> Is Dragon the best name for ordinary lesser Levrils in Fian Ilchruinne?

The answer put was to keep *Dragon*: canon needs the word (every witness at
Dock Seven recorded dragons, *The Borrowed Silhouette*'s argument is that
dragons are real, and the page answers the trope rather than repeating it),
and an invented word or a functional name like *Frontier Levril* would lose
all three (*Frontier* is also taken, by the Frontier Corps). What needed fixing
was that *dragon* carried three meanings in the record:

1. every Levril (the glossary's *"commonly called dragons and their kin"*, the
   lore page's *"Levrils are dragons and dragon-kin"*);
2. the lower rank only (the subclass list, *The Borrowed Silhouette*);
3. *meta-dragons*, a Concordant-span property that cuts across rank.

His ruling, verbatim:

> Yes meaning 2 and meta-levrils

### What it changes

- *Dragon* means the lower rank and nothing else in Archive voice. The loose
  use for every Levril is recorded as common speech, with its reason: Dragons
  are the Levrils people see, because their work is high-energy and high
  salience, and at high salience every witness renders a dragon.
- *Meta-dragon* is renamed *meta-Levril*: a pure rename, no change of meaning.
  The property stays rank-independent.
- Witness accounts (Dock Seven, *nine of nine used the word dragons*) are
  untouched. They report what was seen, and what was seen was Dragons.

search found no fantasy creature, game or novel of that name. Realized as a
pure rename with no change of meaning, across the meta-dimensional beings
page, the Levril and Unfounded pages and the canonical guide, with a
migration row. The canonical guide's *unclean spirit* register rule is
rewritten to say it: it had recorded *"Demon is different… that use stands"*

in devotional voice, where it means the whole Unfounded class.

## Which Cascade strata are ranked within — a direction, given in chat

Verbatim:

> Celestials and Levrils (and maybe champions or prophets) are the only
> Cascade strata with an internal ranking and hierarchy needed

### Read against what stands

- Levrils already had two ranks, Higher Levrils and Dragons, by seniority.
- No stratum above the Celestials had grades. Mediarchs differ by
  force-domain, and the matrix branches by function. So for those strata the
  direction confirms canon: a **clarification**.
- The Celestials had no internal order in canon, so for them it is an
  **extension**. The trap is the retired *High Celestials* and *higher
  celestials*, which must not come back as grade names.
- *Prophet* is not a tier. The Arilon page files it as one culture's word
  for a Champion.

Three shapes were put for the Celestials: (a) record the grading and leave
its shape a marked absence; (b) two ranks by seniority, after the Levrils;
(c) ranks by the reach of the boundary kept. On Champions the recommendation
was to leave them unranked. His ruling, verbatim:

> 1 (a) for Celestials

He then asked why (c) would be a problem. The answer: it conflicts with the
Celestial page's own *"Scope attaches to the process, not to the persons"*,
and with the Levril ranks being *"by seniority rather than raw dimensional
reach"*. A version that works was put: grade by the breadth of delegation
the Telearchs entrust, which is authority, not reach. His ruling, verbatim:

> delegation version of (c)

and, a moment later, verbatim:

> (c) would be delegation from the Mediarchs

Read against what stands: the Celestial glossary said Celestials act
*"within the scope delegated to them by Telearchs"*, and the Mediarch page
says *"A Mediarch is a Telearch acting by proxy inside a universe"*. So this
is a **clarification**, not a reversal. The Telearchs allocate enforcement
scope across Concordants, and inside a universe the Mediarchs hand it to the
Celestials. The glossary sentence is reworded to say so.

Realized as an *Internal Order* section on the Cascade page's Celestials,
a line in the glossary, a classification rule in the canonical guide, and a
*Ranked within, and flat within* paragraph under the Matrix Exceptions.
Champions are recorded as unranked, which is the recommendation he did not
dispute. It stays open to reverse if he meant otherwise by *maybe*.

## The Good People get an official name, and Court-Fae is renamed — rulings, given in chat

Asked in chat, verbatim:

> any issues with the usage of fae or elves in Tir na nOg?

The issues put: *fae* belonged to the predators (Court-Fae) while the Irish
*na daoine maithe*, the Good People, is itself the euphemism for the fairies;
*Court-Fae* carried the Seelie/Unseelie trope, and *court* claimed a
hierarchy the Unfounded cannot have; *elves* brought Tolkien with it; and the
Court-Fae glossary entry carried a remedy line (*"Counter: clarity"*) of the
kind the 22 September corollary says a monster entry must never gain. His
rulings, verbatim, in order:

> 1 (a) for Celestials, rename the Good People and review the Court-Fae

> (A) Lingerers, Good People are also known as Freyari in the official
> records

Both names failed vetting: *Freyari* is already canon (the Ollune's word for
the band of habitable air around Cirrane), and *Lingerers* echoes the
*fey lingerer*, an undead fey monster in Dungeons & Dragons 4th edition.

> rename good people again and find an alternative term for court-fae

Candidates put, vetted: *Caomhari* (from Irish *caomh*, gentle, kind;
recommended) or *Harmonic Presences*; *Obligers* (recommended) or
*Unsettlers*. *Tairngiri* was dropped because modern Irish *tairngire* also
means prophet, and *Debt-Keepers* because its nearest web match is a fae-debt
novel. His ruling, verbatim:

> Yes both recommendations accepted

And, while the sweep was running, verbatim:

> Rewriting already published narrative prose is optional if the usage of
> Court-Fae is simply a word chosen by the character rather than the official
> canonical name in lore.

### What it changes

  to `/glossary/obligers/`, and the old URL redirects. Its remedy line is

- **Published prose:** `canon_facts`, the agency's formal classification

Two further notes of his, verbatim, while the PR was in CI:

## Where a noögenic universe's energy comes from — a question and a draft, same evening

Asked in chat, verbatim:

> do noogenic universes violate any conservation of energy at higher
> dimensions or is there an abundant source?

> the same hidden energy that drives spatial expansion, for example?

Canon was silent: it says universes replicate by structure, not mass, and
that a parent owes *"a start, not an author"*. The answer put follows real
cosmology: energy conservation is local, not global, in an expanding
spacetime; a universe can sum to zero total energy (matter against
gravity); and a baby universe inflates in its own spacetime, so the parent
pays only for the seed. A fence keeps the refusal of limitless energy intact:
the child's energy is in a spacetime nobody in the parent can reach. His
answer, verbatim:

> Yes please draft it

Drafted as *Where the Energy Comes From* on
`src/glossary/noogenic-protouniverse.md`. An **extension** with no new
deviation from real physics. Held for his review of the wording.

His answer to the draft, verbatim: *Yes please add the real world notes and
attributions in the Journal.* Realized as *The Free Lunch and the Fence*,
merged with the section. Two further notes, verbatim:

> however it is fine to name real physicists and citations in the codex if
> relevant and not violating any copyright or privacy issues

> the link to plural systems is highly speculative, of course

Both recorded as standing conventions in `CLAUDE.md`. For the second, two
in-world clarifications: `seed-mind-lifecycle.md`'s note on how a plural mind
fractures now says it is a reading drawn from the one Dock Seven case, not a
