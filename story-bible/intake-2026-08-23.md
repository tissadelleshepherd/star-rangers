Open questions raised here are indexed in `open-questions.md`, per the standing
convention.

Dermot's direction, given verbatim in the session that drafted Suvra Kel,
alongside the instruction to draft a scene with her:

> In most jurisdictions a Plural System is jointly and collectively
> responsible for every action of their host body, no matter who was fronting
> or confronting at the time. The only exception would be a dysfunctional
> Plural System with a psychiatric diagnosis and all the issues and
> restrictions that go with such a diagnosis.

## Canon check

- **`src/glossary/plural-minds.md`** holds the personhood side — plural
  registration, the paperwork-not-the-people correction, the Five Layers
  coupling note — and says nothing about responsibility. The direction is an
  extension, not a contradiction.
- **"Host body"** is consistent with the glossary's own phrasing ("a single
  body hosting more than one person").
- **Existing plural people**: Sen Lovax (Cael, Wyn, Sen), Tissadelle Shepherd
  and Tobble, the Eden Warden (synthetic case, expressly unsettled). The
  s05e02c03 `canon_facts` entry about catastrophic paradox load fracturing a
  plural mind *unevenly* — the load-bearing member first — operates at the
  physical layer and does not conflict with joint legal responsibility; if
  anything the chapter's own material (Tobble declining to let "not holding
  anything" be called a virtue) sits exactly on this rule's ground.
- **"In most jurisdictions"** is kept as given — deliberately not all, leaving
  room for jurisdictional variance the record has not mapped.

- **The exception's trigger** ("a dysfunctional Plural System with a

## Second direction, same day — the shape of alien lives

Dermot, verbatim:

> Aliens may have gender, but do now have DNA of the same kind as terrestrial
> life forms, and do not need to grow and develop through sexual reproduction
> or natural selection. The human experience of birth, growth, adulthood,
> decline and death is not experienced by all sapient civilizations. Some no
> not have individual personhood or identity in the same way.

### Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

Both **confirmed by Dermot, 23 August 2026** ("do now and confronting were
typos; no not was do not"):

- ~~**"do now have DNA"** is read as **"do not have DNA"**~~ — confirmed:
  typo for *do not*. Consistent with settled canon (Cross-Biosphere
  Taxonomy: DNA "one solution, found once, on one world"; panspermia
  refused).
- ~~**"Some no not have"** is read as **"Some do not have"**~~ — confirmed.

### Canon check

- **No conflicts; mostly generalisation of standing instances.** The record
  already holds the arc broken piecewise: Mnemari (no natural bound on
  lifespan, no memory decay — decline and death), Prismeri (two adult forms —
  one adulthood), Krenyi (centuries without degradation), Verdani (kinbed
  bonding "passes a partner rather than a gene" — heredity), plural minds
  (one-to-one personhood already broken in one direction). The direction
  makes the doctrine explicit and adds three genuinely new assertions:
  **gender decoupled from terrestrial reproductive machinery**, **development
  without sexual reproduction or natural selection as a permitted origin**,
  and **civilizations without individual personhood in the Concord's sense**.
- **"Do not need to"** is permissive, not retroactive: species pages that
  describe an "evolutionary history" (Serephine, and the survey's working
  language generally) are unaffected — selection is a mechanism some worlds
  used, not a law all obeyed. The Cascade grounds this: life and sapience are
  formtypes; no tier specifies the mechanism by which a world reaches them,
  and noogenic seeding already exists as a non-Darwinian route to minds.
- **No named example is invented** for the no-individual-personhood case —
  per "explain the epistemology, never invent the fact", the doctrine entry
  states that the record has met such civilizations and translates them
  poorly, and stops. Whether a named example species should be worked up is
  left to Dermot.

### What was done with it

- **`src/lore/the-shape-of-a-life.md`** drafted — doctrine entry in the
  Habitable-for-Whom register: gender without our machinery; no shared
  letters (pointing at Cross-Biosphere Taxonomy rather than duplicating it);
  origins otherwise than by winnowing; the human arc as one shape among
  several; persons counted otherwise, with the charter's invitation problem
  and the plural-responsibility section as the worked domestic case.
  Draft-and-stop, same PR as the rest of today's work.
