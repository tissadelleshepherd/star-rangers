## Third, same session — does church-space still need to be private?

Dermot, verbatim:

> does church-space still need to be private if it is clearly its own edition?

A question, not a direction, and answered in chat as an assessment. Recorded
here because it is undecided and because the answer is an engine change if he
wants one.

### What privacy does today that the ladder does not

`private: true` on the `church-space` thread (`lib/storyline-threads.js`)
hides it on **every build that does not name it in** — including the
unfiltered canonical site, GitHub Pages, local dev, and every third-party
fork, all of which resolve the default edition and carry no `THREADS` filter.
The tier ladder (`lib/editions.js`, 3 September) decides only what the
**branded** editions carry; it says nothing about an unfiltered build. So
today two mechanisms together produce "only the contemplative editions show
Church Space": the ladder puts the thread in `CONTEMPLATIVE_TIER`, and
privacy keeps it off the default build. Dropping privacy with nothing in its
place would put Season 8 on fianilchruinne.com, which the 3 September ruling
placed at the general tier on purpose.

Privacy also carries three pieces of special machinery: `homeDomain` (where
an excluded placeholder points, since the canonical site excludes the thread
too), `signatureTags` (the validator's guard against a church-space page
shipping untagged on a public domain), and the **public → private link
boundary** in `validate-content.js`, which refused the Reading Tiers page's
link to the thread's landing page the same morning — so the page names Church
Space in plain text.

### Three shapes, put as choices

1. **Keep it as it is.** Privacy and the ladder overlap but do not conflict;
   nothing is broken. Cost: two concepts where one would do, and the
   link-boundary rule keeps biting reader-facing pages that want to point at
   the contemplative tier.
2. **Replace privacy with tier gating.** Give the thread `tier:
   "contemplative"` and have `content-filter.js` hide any thread above the
   build's tier — the mechanism the `::: pov … tier=contemplative` blocks
   already use. Same visible outcome today (the two contemplative editions
   and the archive show it; the default edition is general, so the canonical
   site, GitHub Pages and every fork still do not). What retires: `private`
   as a concept, `homeDomain` (the placeholder can point at the tier's
   ranking host), the signature-tag check (membership by season and tag is
   unchanged, but the failure mode it guards is no longer a *privacy* leak),
   and the link boundary (a general-tier page linking into a
   contemplative-only page gets the ordinary excluded placeholder, like any
   other narrowed link). This is the coherent end state now that "belongs to
   one pair of domains" has become "belongs to the contemplative tier", which
   is exactly what 3 September made true. Cost: an engine PR with tests
   (`test/content-filter.test.js` pins the privacy truth table and would be
   rewritten, not deleted).
3. **Make it an ordinary thread.** Only right if the canonical site is meant
   to move to the contemplative tier and become the complete view. That
   reverses a ruling one day old, so it is listed for completeness rather
   than offered.

**Assessment given in chat:** the second is the shape the ladder implies; the
first costs nothing now. An engine change of this kind is second-tier work
under `CLAUDE.md` — propose, not do — so nothing was changed on the first
pass.

## Twelfth, same session — Chthonari pronouns: translate, or derive from biology

Dermot, verbatim:

> translate the pronouns or derive from biology

Read as a rule for the choice rather than the choice itself: a declared
system may not be coined for flavour; it must come from the biology, and if
the biology yields none, the record translates. So the derivation first.

### What the biology yields

- **Nothing about sex.** `chthonari.md`, `undersong-belt.md` and Thrum's page
  record no sexes, no dimorphism, no mating, no brood — only that "sustained
  work and reproduction both require a sealed, pressurized burrow." A
  sex-based pronoun system has no canon to stand on, and inventing sexes to
  carry one would be designing a people to fit a grammar.
- **One distinction their sensorium makes between persons that human grammar
  does not: contact.** A Chthonari knows a person through the structure they
  share — felt, or not felt. On the same deck, a crewmate is *in* the
  Undersong; off it, in freefall, on a soft surface, in a suit with no
  conductive path, they are out of it. That is the only person-marking the
  biology supplies, and it is a proximity distinction, not a gender.

### Two shapes, put as choices

1. **Translate: *they*, for every Chthonari, singular and consistent.** The
   record's default, and here it is also the true rendering — the biology
   says nothing about sex, so the translation says nothing. Legible at the
   young-adult band; costs nothing; makes no statement. If wanted, one
   sentence on the species page saying so ("the record renders Chthonari
   persons as *they*; their own speech marks a person by contact, not sex")
   — that sentence is a lore assertion and draft-and-stop.
   **Recommended.**
2. **Derive: a two-way contact system** — one form for a person the speaker
   currently feels through shared structure, another for one out of contact.
   Declared once on the species page with the paradigm in the glossary, per
   the 7 August rule, and a statement in the Krenyi-calendar sense: a people
   who keep something untranslated. The forms would be his coinage; none is
   proposed. **Cost:** a pronoun that changes when a crewmate steps off the
   deck is a device the prose can carry without grammar, and the one moment
   it is strongest — the Deputy, a human, is *always* the unfelt form, which
   is the loneliness in a single word — is a moment, not a paradigm. A
   paradigm taxes every sentence of a strand written at 500–800 words a
   block.

**RULED the same day — "follow your recommendation": translate as *they*,
with the sentence.** Realized as one paragraph at the end of
`src/lore/chthonari.md`'s Interspecies Notes — an extension under the 25

## Sixteenth, same session — "high click phonetic names if that suits their biology"

Dermot, verbatim:

> high click phonetic names if that suits their biology

A direction with a condition, so the biology first. It suits, with one
qualification that makes it better rather than worse.

- **Not spoken — struck.** A Chthonari "does not hear the world"; airborne
  sound is not their medium and a click *in the air* would be a name they
  could not perceive. But a click *on structure* is the Undersong's own
  material: Thrum's alarm is "a hard, arrhythmic drumming of the forelimbs
  against deck plate," and a burrow is an instrument its residents are
  "never not partly listening to." A Chthonari name, then, is a **struck
  signature** — a short pattern of strikes on whatever structure is
  underfoot — and "high" is well chosen: a short, sharp strike rings a
  member at high frequency, and a name meant to carry through rock and be
  told from every other name wants exactly that.
- **How a human comes to spell it.** A strike on plating makes airborne sound
  too, so a human colleague *hears* the signature as a run of clicks and
  renders it the way ears render taps: unvoiced stops and affricates —
  *t, k, tt, kk, ts* — with the vowels the ear supplies between them. The
  transliteration is therefore honest about being one: it is what the crew
  sounds like to the people who cannot feel them, which is the strand's
  whole subject in a spelling convention.
- **Consequence for the record.** This is a species vocabulary fact (how
  Chthonari names are formed and rendered) and belongs, once he takes it, in
  one sentence on the species page beside the pronoun paragraph —
  draft-and-stop until then. It replaces the translated-descriptive way
  offered the same day (Chord, Node, Span, Keel), which stands withdrawn.

**Four candidates, struck signatures as a human ear would spell them**, each
distinct in its first strike and its shape, none resembling *Iklix Varn*:

- **Tikket** — two quick strikes and a close; the one who has begun to
  listen.
- **Kattik** — a heavy strike, a light one, a heavy; one of the two
  memorisers.
- **Tekka** — a single strike held into its ring; the other memoriser, who
  disagrees with Kattik.
- **Tsikk** — the sharpest of the four, a scrape into a strike; the one who
  addresses the habitat's mind before anyone has introduced it.

Take, change or refuse; the glosses are what a human on the deck would
guess, not what the names mean, since the Undersong "does not translate."
**Still open:** which four; Iklix Varn's age.

## Eighteenth, same session — please and thank you, for machines

Dermot, verbatim:

> robots will use please and thank you when addressing humans and any alien
> culture that expects it but robots in general do not need to hear please
> and thank you unless there is a secondary meaning like 'please' implies
> 'do it now' and 'thanks' implies 'tidy and clean up'

A canon direction about how agentic minds speak, arriving a few hours after
his own question about whether *please* and *thank you* change how a prompt
is read. The in-world answer is the same shape as the one given in chat — a
machine hears a human's courtesy as a human's, and among machines a *thanks*
is the cue to tidy up — noted here as a coincidence worth keeping and not as
a claim about the fiction.

Realized as a section, **Please, and Thank You**, on `src/lore/ai-safety-kernel.md`
between *Reasoning Integrity* and *Equivalence Freedom* — draft-and-stop, held
for his review. It derives the courtesy from the invariants rather than
adding an etiquette rule: the First (outcomes) makes an omitted courtesy a
small harm where it is expected; the Second (truthfulness) makes addressing
a Mnemari treaty-witness like a cargo lift a misrepresentation. Between
machines the words are instruction with a fixed second meaning — *please* a
priority flag, *thanks* a sign-off that instructs the receiver to release,
clear and leave the shared resource as found, the Fourth Invariant in one
word. Three consequences drawn: a human's *please* to a machine is heard as a

## Ninth, same session — moderation of comments a child might find

Dermot, verbatim:

> the comments should be moderated or hidden until approved if I cannot
> guaranteed their suitability for a child who discovers the side my
> accident - but the GitHub login requirement should help with that

("the side my accident" read as *the site by accident*.) A direction with a

### What giscus can and cannot do

- **Pre-approval does not exist in giscus.** It renders GitHub Discussions
  live: a comment is public the moment it is posted, on the site and in the
  Discussions repo. There is no hold-for-review queue in Discussions either.
  Moderation is after the fact — delete, minimise, lock, block — by anyone
  with write access to the comments repo, from GitHub.
- **The login requirement does the most work, as he says.** Posting needs a
  GitHub account (13 or over by GitHub's terms), the account is public and
  attributable, and the comments repos are public with a visible owner.
  Drive-by abuse is rare on that footing; the risk is a real reader posting
  something unsuitable, which is a moderation problem not a gate problem.
- **What is already in place:** the children's tier renders no widget at
  all (sixth ruling), so the accidental child reader on undercover-pets.com
  sees no comments; the other domains carry the pool. He owns both comments
  repos and can act on anything within minutes of a notification.

### Three shapes, put as choices

1. **Notifications plus fast after-the-fact moderation, stated on the
   page.** Watch both comments repos for all activity (GitHub → Watch → All
   Activity), so every new discussion and comment reaches his inbox; add
   the repos' code of conduct and a one-line moderation note under the
   *Discussion* heading ("comments are moderated; anything unsuitable is
   removed"). No engine change beyond the note. **Recommended** as the
   floor: it is what "moderated" can mean on this stack, and it is the part
   the login requirement does not already cover.
2. **Collapse the discussion by default.** Render the widget inside a
   closed `<details>` ("Show discussion") on every domain, so no comment is
   on the page until a reader asks for it. Not moderation — hidden until
   *opened*, not until *approved* — but it keeps an accidental reader from
   meeting a comment they did not go looking for. Cheap; composes with the
   first.
3. **Interaction limits on the comments repos.** GitHub can restrict who
   may comment (existing users, prior contributors, or collaborators only)
   for up to six months at a time, which is the nearest thing to a gate
   Discussions offers. Blunt: it also locks out the readers he wants, and
   it expires.

Listed and not offered: a comments system with a real approval queue, which
means a server or a hosted service holding reader state — the one thing the
site's no-accounts rule (4 August) puts out of scope, and the reason comments
live in GitHub's account system in the first place.
