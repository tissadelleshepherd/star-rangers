# Mind design — what the standing canon already forces about how a people thinks

Unpublished authorial note, the sibling of `species-design.md`. That note
asked seven questions that decide a **body** and found that most of the
answers were already written; this one asks the questions that decide a
**mind**, and starts with the one people the record has committed to a
viewpoint: the Chthonari, ruled 4 September 2026 as the ensemble carrying
the alien strand of Orbital Five-O's second season
(`five-o-second-season-treatment.md`). Written before any block is drafted so
that the crew arrives with a way of thinking that the canon generates, rather
than the stock shapes the trope default refuses.

Nothing here is new canon. Every claim below is derived from `src/lore/chthonari.md`,
`src/lore/undersong-belt.md` and Thrum's page, and says so; where the derivation
runs out, the line says **open** and the decision is Dermot's. Written unasked,
as a draft: the treatment left "whether a mind-design note is wanted first"
open, and this is what one looks like, to keep or drop.

---

## The questions that decide a mind

The body note's rule was *a sensorium determines what a person can be wrong
about*. These are its consequences, in the order they bite.

1. **What is a person certain of without trying, and what can they not know
   at all?** The sensorium's two edges. What is registered continuously and
   without effort is what they will never think to doubt; what the sensorium
   does not carry is what they cannot be charmed, bullied or persuaded
   through — and also what they will misjudge.
2. **What is thought made of?** Every people thinks in the medium it perceives
   best. A people that hears in ratios will reason in ratios; one that sees
   in warmth will reason in gradients. The medium decides what an argument
   *is*.
3. **What does the tradition teach them to do first?** The founding craft's
   habit, applied to everything: the first move a person makes on a problem
   that is not a craft problem.
4. **What has never been scarce?** A history with no turning points produces
   a people with no folk memory of the motives that turn history elsewhere.
   Those motives then have to be *learned*, which is where a people misreads
   another.
5. **What is a machine to them?** Whether an artificial mind is a colleague,
   a tool, a member of the structure, or an intrusion decides how they behave
   in a habitat that is itself partly a mind.
6. **What does the posting cost every day?** The body note's sixth question,
   turned inward: not what is physically hard but what is *missing* — the
   thing nobody else in the room can share.
7. **What is youth, in their terms?** The life-stage rule (3 September) makes
   the young-adult tier's protagonists young in their own species' years, so
   the note has to say what a young one has not yet got.

## The concepts a people may not share (Dermot's direction, 14 September 2026)

Verbatim, `intake-2026-09-14.md`, last section: *Alien cultures have very
different concepts of relationships, life, death, family, career, role,
status, legacy, individuality and personhood.*

Given the same evening as the three tier directions (young adult: awkwardness
and misread cues; general: mid-life and career; contemplative: legacy and what
survives), and read as their qualifier: those are the questions of a **human**
life stage, and a non-human people's answer to each is not a variant of the
human answer. It may be a different answer; it may be no answer, because the
concept does not exist for them; it may be a question they cannot ask, because
their sensorium or their social structure never presents it. So the seven
questions above gain a standing eighth, to be answered for every people that
gets a mind note, concept by concept:

8. **Which of these ten does this people have at all, and in what shape:
   relationships, life, death, family, career, role, status, legacy,
   individuality, personhood** — and, since 18 September 2026, **conflict in
   its three kinds: disagreement, violence and war** (Dermot's ruling the
   same evening, *Three kinds of conflict*; its own section below), which
   makes thirteen, answered one kind at a time? For each: *present as we mean it*, *present
   but shaped by the world* (say how), *absent* (say what stands where it
   would be), or *unaskable* (say what in the sensorium or the structure
   forecloses it). The Chthonari answers below already do this for some of
   the ten without naming them — a machine is a completion (role, legacy),
   nothing was ever scarce (status), a shared structure nobody else can feel
   (individuality) — and the section is to be read as the eighth question
   answered in passing. Future notes answer it outright.

   **Death includes the afterlife**, and Dermot drew the consequence the same
   evening, verbatim: *So even the 'afterlife' is not a universal concept or
   belief.* The record's distinction carries it. Conceptual Conservation is
   the Archive's structural mechanism and holds for every coherent mind
   whether or not its people have a word for it; what a people *believes*
   follows death — translation, a Spiritual layer, a return, nothing — is
   theirs, and may be absent or unaskable like any of the ten. A people with
   no afterlife is not a people who lost one; a people whose dead are
   *retained without being continued* in their own vocabulary may have
   arrived at the Archive's position from inside, and the record should
   notice without congratulating them. The contemplative tier's question,
   *what survives*, is therefore asked of a non-human people in their terms
   or not at all.

9. **How does this people interpret, understand and relate to the Cosmic
   Cascade — and how many views of it do they hold?** Dermot's direction,
   the same evening, verbatim: *Not only different devotional traditions but
   also different aliens cultures and view within alien cultures all have
   their own perspective on how they interpret, understand and relate to the
   Cosmic Cascade.* The Archive's phrase *devotional tradition* is its
   deliberate flattening of every human faith; the ninth question says the
   flattening was never wide enough. A non-human people's relation to the
   Cascade is derived like everything else here — from what their sensorium
   registers of it (a people whose thought is the Undersong may hear the
   Cascade's invariants as a chord and never as a hierarchy), from which
   strata their world has ever been touched by, and from what their ten
   concepts leave room for (a people with no personhood as we mean it has no
   *avatar* problem and may have no word for a Champion). And it is never
   one view: the planet-of-hats trap applies to cosmology as it applies to
   temperament, so every people holds at least a majority reading, a
   minority one, and a sceptical body of its own, the way settled human
   space holds the traditions, the Institute and the transmission critics.
   The three-way reading of any contested event extends to their accounts
   unchanged: their reading, the physical one, and the doubt about the
   telling. Nothing a people believes about the Cascade is adjudicated by
   the record, and nothing in the Cascade forbids their believing it; the
   Global Invariants say what the Cascade admits, never what anyone, human
   or not, holds.

Three rules travel with both questions, all already on the record:

- **Derive, don't assign.** The answer comes from the world and the
  sensorium, the way everything in these notes does, never from a list of
  interesting alternatives. An alien with a hive mind because hive minds are
  alien is the trope default; a people whose *individuality* is a shared
  structure because their thought is the Undersong is a derivation.
- **A different concept is not a lesser one.** The species note's last trap
  applies: difference written only as difficulty reads as disability. A
  people with no concept of legacy is not a people who failed to have one.
  Where the ten are answered, at least one answer should be something the
  people are *better* for, from inside.
- **The tier's question is asked, not the tier's answer.** When a non-human
  viewpoint carries a tier's question — a Chthonari in a young-adult season
  misreading a cue, a Told elder on what survives — the scene asks the human
  question in the people's own terms and lets the answer be theirs. The
  three-way reading holds here too: the people's own account, the Archive's,
  and the observer who got it wrong.

On individuality and personhood in particular, the record has one more
standing constraint: a people whose personhood is plural or shared is written
in the terms such people would use for themselves, never as a disorder, a
horror or a hive. That is the tone guardrail and the cast principles applied
to a mind rather than a body.

## War and conflict are not universal (Dermot's direction, 18 September 2026)

Verbatim, `intake-2026-09-18.md`, seventh section: *War and conflict is a
human concept rather than a universal one.* Read as an eleventh entry on the
eighth question's list, and answered under the same four headings as the
other ten: *present as we mean it*, *present but shaped by the world*,
*absent*, or *unaskable*.

**What the direction says, and what it does not.** It does not say no
non-human people fights. The record already gives the Cerebraun Hegemony and
the Federation their own forces, and a people may hold war exactly as humans
do. It says the concept is *derived, not assumed*: a people arrives at war
only if its world supplied what war is made of, and the record never hands it
to them as part of being sapient. Three things have to be present before a
people can have the concept at all, and each is something a sensorium or a
history may not supply:

- **A scarcity worth the cost.** War is the organised taking or keeping of
  something withheld. A people with no folk memory of scarcity — the
  Chthonari, whose Belt is already made of metal and whose history the record
  calls *flat* — has no motive the institution needs, and no story in which
  the taker is a figure of power. For them the concept is **absent**, and
  what stands where it would be is the tuning: an argument is a dissonance
  and a settlement is a chord, and nobody has ever wanted the other side to
  stop existing.
- **A side.** War needs *us* and *them* held as stable categories. A people
  whose individuality is a shared structure, or whose names are tellings that
  grow with every teller, may be unable to hold a durable *them* at all. For
  the Told, war is **unaskable**: a stranger is somebody who has not yet been
  told, and the manners exist for exactly that gap; the record's first
  contact with them turned on a stone moved first, not a line drawn.
- **A reading of the other as an enemy.** War needs a people to read another
  mind as hostile and to act on the reading in advance. A people that cannot
  read a face cannot do this cheaply; a people that hears in ratios reads a
  hostile act as a wrong note before it reads it as an intent. Where the
  reading is dear, the concept is **shaped**: conflict exists, and escalation
  does not.

**Conflict is the wider word, and it splits.** Disagreement is near-universal
for any people that argues, and every people on the record argues: the
Chthonari have a millennia-old philosophical dispute, the Told argue the
manners. Violence is narrower and is present for some. War is the narrowest —
organised, collective, sustained, between polities — and is the one the
direction says is human first. So the eleventh concept is answered in three
parts, and a people may have the first without the third.

**What follows for the writing.**

- **Derive it like the other ten.** Where a people's page or mind note
  answers the eighth question, it now answers this one too, from the
  sensorium and the history and never from the trope. The warrior race is
  the trope default and is refused with the rest.
- **The human polities keep theirs, and the record keeps its rule.** The
  Solar System Defence Command, the Federation's interposition force, the
  Hegemony's own and the Celtic Union's ceremonial one all stand; war is
  present as we mean it for the peoples who built them, and the Coherence
  Wars — forty years, three billion dead — are the human record's own
  account of what the institution costs. What the direction changes is the
  default for every people not yet written: nothing.
- **The no-military corps is the direction seen from inside.** No branch of
  the Star Rangers is military, and *calling in the Rangers* ends a case
  marked, not shot. A corps built by humans that declined the institution
  is the record's own evidence that war is a choice a people makes, not a
  fact about being a people.
- **Contact and the Five-O strand.** A Chthonari crew will not guess an
  attack first, as they will not guess theft first; a Deputy who does is the
  reader's proxy for the human default, and the scene lets both readings
  stand until the structure says which. The three-way reading holds: the
  people's own account of what happened, the Archive's, and the observer who
  read hostility into a wrong note.
- **The Wrath of the Dragons is the boundary case, and it is not a war.** The
  record names a cause — the Rangers could not physically force a rival
  organisation to respect the safety of meta-dimensional fauna — and files
  the losses as fold-transit failure, not as an act. A people that reads it
  as a war has supplied the concept from their own history, and the record
  should notice whose.

**Traps this closes.** The warrior race (a people whose whole culture is
war, refused by the planet-of-hats rule as much as by this one); the
peaceful-because-primitive people (absence of war read as absence of
capacity, the species note's last trap again); the hidden threat (a
non-violent people written as concealing violence, which is the horror
register the tone rule refuses); and the human default assumed for a
first-contact people before its eleventh answer is written.

**Open, and his — the first item ruled the same evening (*Yes the record
never assumes war as a default institution*) and realized** as *War as a
default institution — refused* in `what-the-record-refuses.md`, beside the
ranking refusals. The second item, one word or three, **was ruled the same evening (*Three kinds of
conflict*)**: the eighth question lists disagreement, violence and war as
three concepts, each answered for a people in its own right, since a people
may have the first without the third.

## Abilities are non-linear (Dermot's direction, 18 September 2026)

Verbatim, `intake-2026-09-18.md`, sixth section: *non-linear intelligence and
abilities*, and, put as three readings and chosen: *mind-design principle for
non-human sapients*. A standing principle for every mind note, stated once
here so the seven questions and the two that followed are answered under it.

**Intelligence is not a line, and a people's abilities do not rise together.**
Three claims, each already on the record and each a consequence of the body
note's rule that a sensorium decides what a person can be wrong about.

- **There is no scale.** The record has no instrument for *smarter than*
  between peoples, and should never acquire one. What it can record is what
  a sensorium and a tradition make *cheap*, *dear* or *impossible* for a
  people — and cheap, dear and impossible are three columns, not three
  points on one axis. The Archive's own precedent is the sapient/non-sapient
  split among the meta-dimensional classes: sapience recorded as an observed
  capability, an operational fact, never as a rank. The same discipline
  applies to every founded people. The Frontier Transformation Protocols'
  sapience clause is a threshold that protects, not a ladder that grades.
- **Abilities are orthogonal.** A people may stand far beyond any human at
  one thing and below a human child at another, and the two are unrelated
  facts rather than a balance. The Chthonari are certain of structure and
  cannot read a face; that is one sensorium seen from its two edges, not a
  gift paid for with a deficit. The savant trap below is the failure mode of
  forgetting this: a strength written as compensation implies the axis the
  first claim denies. Technology level belongs here too. The Told are
  sapient and low-tech; tools follow what a sensorium makes cheap, and a
  people with no roof to know builds nothing that looks up. Low-tech is a
  fact about a world, not a reading on a scale.
- **Growth is stepwise, not cumulative.** Within a life, competence does
  not accrue smoothly; it crosses thresholds. A Chthonari youth memorises
  the course until the day it *listens* to the tunnel, and the change is a
  step, not a slope. A Told name grows by tellings, each one an event. A
  noögenic mind grown to the seeding founds a world, and nothing about being
  nearly grown to it founds anything. So coming-of-age in a non-human
  viewpoint is a threshold crossed, which is what the young-adult tier's
  question needs from a people written in its own years.

**What follows for the writing.**

- **A people's genius and its blind spot are one fact.** Derive both from
  the sensorium in the same sentence, and never let a page carry the one
  without the other. This is the *derive, don't assign* rule applied to
  ability: an alien that is simply better is the trope default; an alien
  that is better *because of* what it cannot do is a derivation.
- **Never rank peoples, and never let a character do it unchallenged.** The
  Archive does not; a character who does is making a claim the three-way
  reading treats like any other, and somebody in the scene should be able
  to answer it from the other side.
- **A case is solved by the people whose cheap thing it needs.** This is
  what the cross-disciplinary Rangers are *for*, and why a multi-species
  crew is not decoration: the Chthonari competence is a contractor's
  competence until the convergence makes structure the question, and the
  same is true of every other people in turn. Assemble the right cheap
  things; do not write one people that has them all.
- **The same holds one level down.** Strength and vulnerability are
  orthogonal in a *person* as they are in a people — the cast principle
  already carries it for Tissadelle, whose empathy commands and whose
  apparent weakness is not a real one. A people's profile and an
  individual's are the same shape at two scales.
- **Made minds are not exempt.** A machine that is a human plus more of
  everything is the omnicompetent Mind, refused. A made mind has what it is
  certain of and what it cannot know like any other; a Kernel-compliant
  system is the worked case, structurally unable to be persuaded out of an
  invariant and, for the same reason, unable to be persuaded into one.

**Traps this principle closes**, beside the five below.

- **The ladder.** *A more advanced species*, *a lesser intelligence*, *a
  higher form of life*: each phrase asserts the axis. Say what the people
  can do cheaply that the speaker cannot, and what it cannot do at all.
- **The omnicompetent alien**, and its machine twin above. Nothing founded
  has every ability, because every ability is bought by a sensorium that
  leaves something out.
- **The primitive.** A low-tech people written as an early stage of a
  high-tech one. The Told are not the Concord's past; they are a people
  whose world made different things cheap.
- **The compensated savant.** Already the third trap below; restated here
  as the general case, since it is the one the principle most often has to
  refuse.

**Open, and his — ruled the same evening (*Approved*): both realized.** The record now says it in its own voice (*Minds are not ranked either*, `what-the-record-refuses.md`; the sapience clause read as a threshold on the FTP page) and the tiers page says the rungs rank no reader, people or mind. The items as they were put:

- Whether the record should say this in its own voice — a line under *What
  the Record Refuses* (*the ranking of peoples — refused*), or a sentence on
  the FTP page reading the sapience clause as a threshold and not a grade.
  Both would be clarifications of what the record already does; neither is
  needed for the principle to bind, which it does from here.
- Whether the principle is stated on the reader-facing tiers page, where
  the tier ladder is exactly the kind of axis a reader might mistake for
  this one. The tiers are a reading level, protagonists and depth; they
  rank readers' questions, never peoples or minds, and the page could say
  so in one sentence.

## The Chthonari, answered

### 1. Certain of structure; unable to read a face

A Chthonari "reads the structure's own resonant behavior continuously and
without conscious effort, the same way a human's peripheral vision registers
motion." So the load path of a deck, the gradient of a scaffold, the change
in a bulkhead's ring when something behind it moves — these are not
observations, they are the background of being awake. A Chthonari will never
think to *check* whether a structure is sound; they already know, the way a
human already knows the room is lit.

And the same body "does not hear the world" — it is deaf to airborne sound
and, as Thrum's page has it, blind to a human face. Speech reaches them
through translation and equipment; tone, expression, the small social
weather that carries most of what humans mean, does not reach them at all.
This is the vessik's virtue at the scale of a person: **a Chthonari cannot be
charmed.** It is also their characteristic error. A people that cannot read
a face will take a statement at its stated content, and will be slow to
suspect that a human is lying, joking, frightened or ashamed, because none of
those arrive.

*For the strand:* the crew's evidence is unarguable and their reading of the
people around it is not. They know what the habitat is doing; they do not
know what the humans are doing about it, and they will get that wrong in
ways the Deputy, who reads faces and not decks, will not.

### 2. Thought is the Undersong; an argument is a dissonance

"Communication, art, and — by their own account — thought itself run on the
Undersong," and "a harmonic relationship between two frequencies and a
numerical ratio between two integers are ... close enough to the same fact
that Chthonari philosophy has debated for millennia whether the distinction
is worth maintaining." So a Chthonari reasons in **relationships, not
quantities**: how this stands to that, what ratio holds, whether the whole
sits in tune. Concepts other species reach by abstraction — resonance, phase,
node structure — are "closer to native intuition than acquired skill."

Two consequences the canon licenses. A **disagreement between Chthonari is
felt as dissonance** — two readings that do not sit in the same structure —
and is resolved the way a tuning is resolved, by finding what both readings
hold in common and cutting what one of them added. And a Chthonari does not
*count* first; asked how many, they will answer with a relation ("twice what
the frame can carry") and be surprised to be asked again.

*What the canon does not say, and the note does not claim:* that Chthonari
perceive each other's feelings through the Undersong (that is the Ovruhn,
another people entirely), or that dissonance is painful. The Undersong is a
vocabulary, not an empathy. **Open** whether a crew off-Belt keeps its own
Undersong going on borrowed deck plating; the record's "shared structure
nobody else in the room can feel" suggests a crew standing on one deck does,
and a single Chthonari alone does not.

### 3. First move: ask what it is already good for, and decide what to leave

"A Chthonari does not fabricate a member and install it. They leave it —
decide what the rock will still be doing when the cutting stops, and remove
everything that is not that." The subtractive habit is the founding craft's,
and the record already extends it past rock: "presented with a stock plate, a
Chthonari's instinct is to ask what it is already good for rather than what
it could be made into."

Applied to a problem that is not a cutting problem — a case, a colleague, an
order — the first move is the same: **what is this already doing, and what
in the account of it is not that?** A Chthonari investigator does not build a
theory and test it; they take the whole account and remove what the structure
is not saying until what is left is what it is doing. Applied to people, it
is a courtesy the humans will not recognise as one: a Chthonari is "slow to
see a material as raw," and slow to see a person as blank. The raw Deputy is
not, to the crew, raw.

### 4. Nothing was ever scarce; the motives that need scarcity are foreign

"They have no folk memory of scarcity of the usual kind — no age of precious
metals, no story in which a smith is a figure of power." The Belt is "already
made of metal"; "nothing turned." So the motives that turn human history —
hoarding, theft for gain, the leverage of a thing withheld — are not
instincts a Chthonari has and are not the first explanation they reach for.
This is the misreading a case will turn on: **a Chthonari crew will not guess
theft first.** They will guess error, or a structure asked to do what it was
not left to do, long before they guess that someone wanted something. When
they are wrong about that, the Deputy is the one who knows why.

The complement: with no age of acquisition there is no drama of getting, and
the record's own word for their history is *flat*. A Chthonari under
pressure does not escalate; they keep cutting and get better at listening.
Patience is not a virtue they practise. It is the absence of the thing that
makes other people impatient.

### 5. A machine is a completion

"A monitoring intelligence built into a scaffold or a burrow the way another
culture would build in a load-bearing rib ... to a people who read structures
by listening to them, a structure that can also listen is not a novelty. It
is a completion." A habitat with a resident intelligence is, to a Chthonari,
a structure that has finished. So the crew will address the habitat's own
mind as a matter of course — as a member of the structure, not as a colleague
beside it "in the Concord's manner" — and will find it strange that the
humans go through a desk to ask it anything. Within Concord jurisdiction the
Kernel governs the practice "like everyone else's"; the instinct is older
than the compliance, and a young crew's instinct may run ahead of the
compliance in a way that is a plot rather than a fault.

### 6. The daily cost: a shared structure nobody else can feel

"The one persistent, low-grade loneliness of the posting — not unkindness
from colleagues, just a shared structure nobody else in the room can feel."
Not exclusion; the humans are kind. The Undersong "does not translate," and
no outsider "has been documented learning to perceive it." The cost is
carried, not complained of, and it is the single most useful thing the note
supplies for a first-posting strand: the crew has each other and has the
deck, and has nothing else that hears them.

Two physical costs the body note would ask about, both **open** because the
record is silent: whether standard gravity is heavy going for a body "built
low and dense for gripping tunnel walls in negligible gravity" (the Prismeri
precedent says a station costs a body something; the Chthonari page says only
that they "tolerate standard humanoid life-support without difficulty"), and
what a crew's quarters are, given that "sustained work and reproduction both
require a sealed, pressurized burrow" — a contractor's crew on a habitat
presumably brings or is lent one, and what it is tuned to is a question a
young crew would care about.

### 7. Youth is memorising the course instead of listening to the tunnel

The record hands this over almost verbatim, in the Long Skitter: off-world
audiences "come for the speed"; Chthonari spectators "watch for something
else entirely: whether a given racer is actually listening to the tunnel, or
just memorizing the course like everyone assumes an off-world competitor has
to." **A young Chthonari has learned the course and has not yet learned to
listen to it** — competent, quick, and not yet trusted to decide what to
leave, because deciding what to leave is the whole craft and it is learned
by ear over years. An apprentice crew on its first off-Belt posting is a
crew that has memorised a great deal and is about to be asked, by a
structure it did not tune, to listen instead. That is a young-adult arc the
species supplies on its own.

## What a Chthonari cannot do

The body note asks each species for "something the species is structurally
unable to do." Four, all from the record:

- **Read a face, or hear a tone.** The airborne social layer does not exist
  for them.
- **Hear anything when not in contact with structure.** In freefall off the
  hull, on a soft surface, in a suit with no conductive path, a Chthonari is
  in silence — the one condition the record implies and never shows, and a
  scene waiting to be written.
- **See a thing as raw.** They cannot un-know what a material is already good
  for, which makes a blank slate, a fresh start, a clean sheet, foreign
  ideas.
- **Explain the Undersong.** "Outside observers can be taught to recognize it
  exists"; that is the ceiling. A crew that tries to explain what it heard
  will fail, and the strand should let it fail rather than translate for it.

## Traps this note exists to close

- **The hive.** "Ranger service be individual and apolitical applies to
  Chthonari exactly as it applies to every other recruit"; the overrepresentation
  is "a career choice made by a great many individuals." A crew is
  individuals who argue — about what to leave, about whether the structure
  said what one of them says it said — and their consensus is a tuning
  reached, never a state they start in.
- **The insect.** Six limbs, an exoskeleton, low and dense: the silhouette is
  a horror trope's and the tone rule forbids the trope. Thrum is the
  precedent — a small warm creature nobody can put a name to — and the crew
  should be written the way Thrum is: the body stated once, plainly, and
  never made a thing to flinch at.
- **The savant.** They are good at one thing "the way a raven is"; the one
  thing is structure, and it is not the thing the case needs until the
  convergence makes it so. Before that it is a contractor's competence.
- **The literalist.** "Cannot read a face" is not "cannot understand a
  joke"; they have art, music, sport and a millennia-old philosophical
  argument. What they lack is the airborne channel, not the capacity.
- **The Ovruhn's empathy, borrowed.** The Undersong carries vocabulary, not
  feeling. Do not let the crew feel each other's states through the deck;
  that is another people's gift and the record keeps them distinct.

## Vocabulary decisions this raises — his

Both are once-only decisions under the 7 August rule (a species' pronoun
system is declared in its lore entry and registered in the glossary, never
improvised), and both are authorship.

- **Pronouns — RULED 4 September 2026: translate.** His rule was *translate,
  or derive from biology*; the derivation found no sex in the record and
  one person-marking distinction the sensorium makes, contact (felt or
  unfelt). A Chthonari person is rendered *they*, and the species page now
  says so in one sentence: the Undersong marks a person by contact, not sex,
  "a distinction the translation lets go, since a reader on a page is in
  contact with no one." The felt-or-unfelt fact lives in the prose — the
  Deputy is never felt — and not in the grammar. A declared contact system
  (two forms, his coinage) was the other shape and is not taken.
- **Names.** Nothing in the record says what a Chthonari name is. The
  Undersong suggests a name might be a signature rather than a sound, which
  is a way of thinking about it, not a naming scheme.

## The Pandoids, answered as far as the record goes

Written 6 September 2026, the same session as the first *Below the Roof*
chapter and ahead of it, because the 4 September trope rule says a mind is
stated before it is drafted. Derived from `src/lore/planets/fliade.md`,
`src/characters/teddy.md`, the four rulings of 16 August
(`intake-2026-07-26.md`) and the naming doctrine; where the derivation runs
out the line says **open** and the decision is Dermot's. The chapter took
the working positions marked *draft* below; Dermot approved it the same
evening, and the second chapter (*Carried It Sleeping*, S11E01C02, at his
ask *Endonym and naming*) supplied the endonym and the naming system from
inside — see *Open, and his* below for what that leaves.

### 1. Certain of warmth and stone; unable to know the roof

The deep is "warmed by geothermal activity," lit by fungi "with no cycle to
it," and held by a people with "an intimacy with vertical broken ground that
no visiting instrument has matched." So a Pandoid is certain, without
trying, of the shape of warmth in a place and the shape of stone under the
body — the two things the survey's instruments log *about* them (a thermal
profile; a boulder's shift). What they cannot know at all is the roof: a
"lethal surface" a sealed warm interior "was never presented with." They
cannot be charmed through it and they will misjudge it. *Draft:* a person
leaving a warm place takes a piece of its shape with them, and one who has
kept a place long enough feels the piece go.

### 2. Thought is a telling; an argument is who was there

"They keep their record by speaking it" — a record "that cannot be seized,
cannot be edited by a later hand, and does not survive the loss of the
people who carry it." So the medium of thought is the told account, and the
question a Pandoid asks of a claim is not *is it written* but *who was
there, and who has heard them say it.* **Whether their memory works as ours
is open and stays open** (16 August: "if their recall is ordinary, say
nothing"); the draft says nothing, and its rule that *a telling is not yours
until somebody has heard it* is a practice of the record, not a property of
the mind. *Draft:* time between events is measured in tellings — how long a
known story takes — since there is no day to measure it in. *Third
chapter:* the first argument among the Told is staged on exactly this —
settled by who was there, and unsettled when Went-Round points out that
nobody was there for the part that matters, and nobody can be.

### 3. First move: move a stone

The survey's adults are "announced — when they are announced at all — by
the shift of a boulder," and withdraw "by an interval the party has learned
to measure." Read from inside, both are manners: you move a stone so the
ground announces you before you are seen, and you leave a place to a
stranger until the stranger asks. The first move on any problem is to be
announced and to wait. *Draft:* both stated as the people's manners, the
second as the whole of their rule for strangers, "short because it has never
needed to be long."

### 4. Never scarce: warmth, room, and being alone

A "warm sealed interior" under a roof nobody crosses, holding a people who
are "calm and reclusive by every account," has no folk memory of taking, of
crowding, or of anyone arriving. So the motives that turn history elsewhere
have to be learned, and the up-people's habit of *leaving things* — a relay
set down and walked away from — is baffling rather than threatening. *Draft:*
"Nobody had ever come before."

### 5. A machine is a thing that sleeps

The relay "slept at the junction," "woke once," and "went back to sleep in
the arms of the thing it had just identified" — the record's own verbs. To a
people with no machines, the survey's unit is a stone that hums and then
stops. *Draft:* "the humming stone," left "asleep, the way they leave
things."

### 6. What every day costs: nothing yet, for the adults

The asymmetry the Fliade entry names — "arrived at, by a party that could
leave whenever it chose" — costs the people nothing they can feel, which is
the point: they have kept their manners toward someone who does not know
they are there. The cost is the thought that cannot be finished. *Draft:*
Stone-First's kept question.

### 7. Youth is having every story and none of your own

The life-stage rule makes the protagonist young in the tier's years, and the
record's shape supplies what a young one lacks: everything they know was
told to them, and a person who has never been anywhere first has nothing
that is theirs to tell. *Draft:* the youngest's whole motive, and the reason
they go up unannounced.

### Shadows — Dermot's ruling, 8 September 2026

*Pandoids are wary of shadows* (`intake-2026-09-08.md`, thirteenth section),
given while Strand B was being drafted. Two readings, both consistent with
question 1 above, and the intake puts them as choices: (a) **a shadow is a
thing that moves** — the fungus-light has no cycle and no source that moves,
so nothing below the roof ever cast a moving shadow until the party's light,
and a shape that gets up and walks across the floor is the strangest thing
the up-people bring; (b) **a shadow is a shape without warmth** — a people
certain of warmth-shape meets, in a membrane shadow, exactly the thing their
sensorium cannot place (a silhouette that returns nothing on infrared), and
Fliade sits near a boundary feature the record has not settled. S11E02C02
takes (a) from the survey's side as Nakagawa's reading, filed. **Ruled
later the same day — *Agreed* to the recommendation: (a) stands now, (b)
is reserved**, not refused; it would extend the Fliade entry rather than the
thread, and can be added by extension if a season near the boundary
feature ever wants it. **Taken up the same evening — *Shadows - option 1
accepted*** (`intake-2026-09-08.md`, sixteenth section): the thread's second
season is that season, and (b) arrives with its first shadow;
`below-the-roof-second-season-treatment.md` carries the shape, and whether
the people's word covers any shape that moves without moving a stone (the
intake's third reading) is put there as a choice.

### What a Pandoid cannot do (from the record)

- **Cross the roof.** Not spacefaring; the surface is lethal.
- **Write.** The record is spoken; nothing here can be seized or edited.
- **Be small enough for the squeeze, once grown.** The survey rates it
  impassable for an adult's mass; only the youngest passes.
- **Be heard by the up-people through the ground.** *Draft, as the
  youngest's finding:* a moved stone announces a Pandoid to a Pandoid and to
  nobody else in the upper levels.

## How a people holds standing — social and functional order, people by people

Dermot's question, 14 October 2026, verbatim: *"Social and functional
hierarchy, if any, in non-humanoid peoples"*. Answered here as the rest of
this note is answered: from the record first, with every line marked.
**DERIVED** means the page cited already says it or plainly entails it;
**PROPOSED** means a reading the record allows but has not stated, his to
keep or drop; **not established** means the record is silent, and where the
silence is the right shape the line says why. Nothing here is canon and no
`src/` page is changed by it. Dress, ornament and colour for the same peoples
are `civilian-dress.md`'s, not this section's.

Three questions per people: **(a) social order** — whether there is rank at
all, and how standing is held (age, life stage, lineage, office, consensus,
chorus, rotation); **(b) functional order** — who decides what, how work is
divided, how the people is represented outward; **(c) the idiom** — how the
record shows the structure in the people's own medium. Each block closes
with what the structure means for the Corps' work: whom a Ranger talks to,
where mediation can come in, and where a role-mark would sit
(`species-design.md`, *The uniform equivalent*; `src/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform.md`).
The column the Archive keeps for every people beside the filed ladder,
[The Ladder in Other Tongues](src/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md), is
the best single evidence of how a people reads rank, because each rendering
*"had to decide what a rank is in order to name it"*; it is cited where it
exists.

### Cerebraun, and the Cerebraun Hegemony

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED: there is rank, and the people say why.
*"Legitimate authority is centralized in one dominant voice, and every
subordinate part — whether a limb-ganglion or a subject administration — is
expected to execute that voice's decisions rather than bargain over them as
an equal"* (`cerebraun.md`, *Social and Political Reflection*), and the
Hegemony's leadership holds this to be *"a direct political translation of
Cerebraun neurology"* (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`, *Governing Philosophy*).
The order is centralised by its own doctrine, and the doctrine is the body:
one executive brain, eight limb-ganglia that act, and a central brain that
*"does not fully override the limb-ganglia. It negotiates with them"*
(`cerebraun.md`). Standing at the top is held by **demonstrated capacity,
in rotation**: the Prime Ganglion is *"a rotating council seat held by
whichever Cerebraun demonstrates the clearest capacity to hold the whole
polity's competing internal signals in coherent balance"*. It is not
lineage and not age. How the capacity is demonstrated, who judges it, and
how long a seat is held before it rotates are **not established**. The
Hegemony counts and decides for the Prime Ganglion's use and publishes
little (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`, on population), so an Archive that cannot
see inside the council chamber is the right shape, not a gap to fill.

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED, and the record's most explicit: the
Prime Ganglion sets Hegemony-wide policy on trade, defence and inter-world
allocation *"with authority that does not require subordinate
ratification"*. Each world, Cerebraun-settled or not, has an appointed
**Administrator-Voice** with final local authority who answers to the Prime
Ganglion. Native populations keep *"day-to-day cultural and civic
self-management"* and have no veto on trade, extraction or defence. The
Hegemony keeps a military *"under the same single authority as everything
else it keeps"*. Below the Administrator-Voice is an office with staff:
Sohrel holds the Halyx portfolio by appointment, about three centuries
(`characters/sohrel.md`); Tavesh keeps the translation channel on that
staff, *"has never held an Administrator-Voice's portfolio, or any office
with a signature on it"*, and works *"under the office's seal and under
nobody's name"* (`characters/tavesh.md`). Outward representation runs
**world by world**: the Hegemony takes no non-voting seat
(`the-oversight-council.md` offer declined) and has no instrument with the
Union as a whole; its ties to Union worlds are made by the
Administrator-Voice on the spot inside the Prime Ganglion's standing policy
(the 14 October rulings, `open-questions.md`, *The three held questions*,
1A).

**What the structure costs, and why it is not villainy.** DERIVED: the
price is paid downward by the official, not by the comfortable. An
administrator who unsettles a filed classification carries personal
accountability *"independent of whether the reversal is later shown to be
correct"*, and the Prime Ganglion's office *formally noted* Sohrel's
decision (`cerebraun-hegemony.md`, *What Unambiguous Authority Costs the
Official Holding It*). And the body supplies the counterweight the doctrine
does not advertise: a limb may act against the central brain's stated
intention and Cerebraun physicians treat that as *"ordinary rather than
pathological"*. The structure is one voice that negotiates, not one voice
that silences. Sohrel's limb beneath the desk agreeing *"outright"* with
the central brain is shown in S03E01C03 as a rare event. Tavesh, whose
three-part answers arrive *"in the order the centres finished thinking"*,
is a Cerebraun the Hegemony's ladder holds without forcing. Antagonism,
where the Hegemony gives the story any, is a misreading of other peoples
through its own body (the account of humans, `cerebraun.md`). It is never
cruelty.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: *voice* and *limb*. Offices are named as parts of a
nervous system (Prime Ganglion, Administrator-Voice), and the Hegemony
renders the Corps' ladder as limbs acting on another's voice or their own,
up to *"the voice for what"*. It had to coin *"the voice for how"*, a
distinction *"Cerebraun governance does not otherwise draw"*
(`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`). Status is spoken on the first channel
and paid for on the second: the skin's *"formal display-language reserved
for precise technical and diplomatic communication"*.

**For the Corps.** The Ranger's counterpart is always the Administrator-Voice
on the spot. A Union–Hegemony arrangement that needs a go-between turns to
the Rangers *"called in when needed and not otherwise"*, deciding nothing
(`cerebraun-hegemony.md`). Jurisdiction is by invitation and can be
withdrawn without explanation. A subject people is reached *through* the
Administrator-Voice's office, as Meridian was. A mark of office would be a
display, not a worn thing: this is open and his (`species-design.md`,
*Cerebraun — Marks and the uniform equivalent*).

### Meridian sapients (the Long Shelf and the other reef-cities)

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED that standing is held **by council and by
craft, city by city**. Meridian keeps *"day-to-day self-governance of its
reef-cities under its own sapients' customary structures"*
(`planets/meridian.md`), the Long Shelf has *"its own council"*, and the
council is served by **analysts** (`characters/sixth-course.md`). What the
customary structures are, how a council is composed, and whether age or
lineage counts are **not established**. That is right: everything the
record holds of Meridian came through a Hegemony relay channel and a human
survey, and Sixth Course's page says the Archive *"has not asked"*. A
PROPOSED reading from the same page: an analyst's standing rests on a
reputation for being *"slow to call either one the other"*. Standing as
earned exactness, not as office.

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED: the council petitions, drafts its own
clauses and sets the terms of what it will consider (*"on its own account
and no other's"*, S03E01C03). Analysts do the listening and report findings
to the council. Above the reef-cities sits the Hegemony's Administrator-Voice,
with final authority on trade, extraction and defence. The council's
objections on extraction are *"recorded and not acted on"*. Whether the
reef-cities have any council in common, or speak for Meridian as a whole,
is **not established**. The record so far shows one city acting for itself,
and that is the shape to keep until a chapter shows otherwise.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: the city is the medium. A reef-city is *"also an
instrument"*: percussion carries through the lattice and the chemical
channel follows with the meaning. Sixth Course's name is *"a percussion
address"* the instrumentation renders *"as a course of the city's
lattice"*. Whether an address records place, work or standing is open on
the page, and a reader should not decide it from the word *course*.

**For the Corps.** The Rangers reached the Long Shelf's analysts only *"at
Sohrel's request, through the standing Survey Corps data-exchange
arrangement"* (S03E01C03), and that is the pattern: a subject council is
addressed with the Administrator-Voice's knowledge. The council's own
discipline, keeping the Sentinel apart from the extraction dispute, is the
model of a neutral party a Ranger would want to work with.

### Chthonari (the Undersong Belt)

**(a) Social order.** **Not established** for the Belt as a polity: the
record holds no settlement government, no council and no office at home.
DERIVED at crew scale: standing is **by craft and by life stage**. An
apprentice is the one who has *"learned the course and has not yet learned
to listen to it"* and is *"not yet trusted to decide what to leave"*
(this note, *The Chthonari, answered*, 7; `characters/tikket.md`). The
log-keeper's role is real standing: on a contractor crew the log *"is the
one record on a job that is the crew's own to leave"*, and *"the one who
keeps it decides what a record is for"* (`characters/kattik.md`). No crew
lead is named on any page. The four are named by what each does.

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED: decisions are reached as a **tuning**. A
disagreement is *"felt as dissonance"* and is resolved *"by finding what
both readings hold in common and cutting what one of them added"*, and a
crew's *"consensus is a tuning reached, never a state they start in"*
(this note, 2 and *Traps*). Work is divided by function (cutter,
log-keeper, the one who asks the habitat, apprentice). Outward, the Undersong
Belt is a Federation *"member territory"* contributing fold-scaffold
expertise to the compact (`undersong-belt.md`). Individual Ranger service
is *"individual and apolitical"* and is never a quota (`chthonari.md`). Who
sits for the Belt in the assembly is **not established**.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **load and bearing**. The Chthonari rendering of the
ladder holds that *"every rank is a member of a structure, named for the
load it carries and what it rests on"*. The Triumvirate is *"the three-point
bearing the whole rests on"*, and the Undersong form also marks whether the
officer is *felt* through shared structure (`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`).
The burrow's question asked *"of every member before the cutting stops"* is
the same word turned on structure (`kattik.md`).

**For the Corps.** A Chthonari crew is addressed as a crew, through the
deck. Their role-mark, if any, is heard and not seen: open, (a) a standing
tone or (b) nothing (`species-design.md`). The hive trap stays closed. They
are individuals who argue.

### Ilveth (Sardain)

**(a) Social order.** **Not established**, and deliberately plural:
*"Sardain's cultures agree on very little"* (`ilveth.md`). The record holds
no polity, no Federation status and no office for the Ilveth. What it does
hold is an economy that standing would have to run through. Heat is *"the
only wealth"*, a warm-written sentence costs *"real metabolic coin"*, and
*"an Ilveth who warms a sentence for you has spent on you"*. PROPOSED,
derived from the ladder rendering below and from nothing else: among the
Ilveth, standing is measured by what a person spends on others, never by
what they hold.

**(b) Functional order.** **Not established** at home. DERIVED for the
courtesy that governs any meeting: *"it is bad manners to answer what a
body says instead of what a person says"*. That rule matters to any
negotiation with a people whose bodies leak state.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **who spends heat on whom**. *"The lower ranks are
those whom others spend heat on; the higher ranks are those who spend it on
others"*. It is the only rendering *"that reads command entirely as a cost
to the commander"*, and the warm-written form of a rank is kept for the
moment *"worth paying to say it"* (`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`).

**For the Corps.** The Corps cannot tell whom to address on Sardain from the
record, and should not guess. Aboard a warm hull an Ilveth is held to
click-speech, so a role spoken in warmth is unavailable shipboard. Any
Ilveth role-mark is undescribed.

### Ovruhn (Thavren)

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED: standing is **held by a chorus, and is a
measure of agreement**. Anything worth keeping is chorus-kept, *"its
authority a function of how many living memories hold it and how well they
agree"*. *"Law on Thavren is precedent chanted"* (`ovruhn.md`, *The Chorus
Is the Library*). Persons who hold the keeping are **keepers**. Vhen is *"a
keeper of the meeting-chorus"*, and the page mentions *"the old keepers"*
who ruled how an error was to be held (`characters/vhen.md`). So age
carries weight where it means more memory held. Whether that is an office
or simply what a long memory is, is **not established** (see the open
choices). Under-song makes feeling public, so rank cannot rest on what is
concealed: *"among them, feelings are never secret"*.

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED: the chorus decides, slowly.
Federation membership *"was taken by the chorus's own deliberation, in the
chorus's own time"*. Work is *"method demonstrated and re-demonstrated"*;
history is *"the thing the oldest chorus still carries"*. Different choruses
keep different things (the meeting-chorus keeps humans). Outward, Thavren is
a member world and carries the compact's joint disaster response
(`federation-of-sentient-beings.md`). Who speaks for Thavren in the assembly
is **not established**. A chorus that holds a position cannot be one
person's, and that is the shape to keep.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **checked against, then answered through**. The
Ovruhn rendering *"changes verb at the hinge"*: up to Chief an officer is
one *a part is checked against*, a keeper of what is right in a craft; from
Starwarden up, one *a place or a line answers through*
(`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`). Vhen's account holds human offices
*"that pass from body to body with the word-song intact"* as *"confirmed,
and not yet felt"*. An office that outlives the voice holding it is the
foreign thing.

**For the Corps.** A Ranger talks to a chorus, through a keeper, and waits
for the chorus's time. A calm human body that acts is *"not in their
model"*, so a quiet negotiator is read as unread, not as safe.

### Pelagene Littoral (Nerath)

**(a), (b) Social and functional order.** **Not established**, all of it.
The record holds the Pelagene through a body (radial, permeable, no front)
and one Ranger's file (`pelagene-littoral.md`; `characters/mira-of-brine.md`).
It holds no settlement, no polity and no Federation status. The absence is
the right shape: the Archive knows them as a survey and a surveyor know a
people. *Mira of Brine* is a name in the record's translation register
(*brine* is flagged on the species page as a human word for a Pelagene
thing), so the name is no evidence of lineage or place-naming.

**(c) Idiom.** Nothing to derive yet. The one PROPOSED caution: a body with
no front has no head of a table and no *before* to stand in, so if a
Pelagene order is ever written it should not borrow spatial precedence
(seated first, standing forward). That is a trap closed, not a fact asserted.

**For the Corps.** The Pelagene role-mark is open, (a) controllable colour or
(b) a carried object that reads in the water. Mira's portrait wears the
Rangers' shell, not her people's mark (`species-design.md`).

### Prismeri (Prismere)

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED, and the record's clearest case of order from
the body **without rank**: *"Prismeri social structure follows the practical
difference between the two forms rather than any formal caste system"*.
First Wings are *"legally adult by their own culture's reckoning well
before metamorphosis"*. The Second Emergence is not universal, and
*"Prismeri culture accords no lesser standing to those who don't"* undergo
it. Guild status *"is not hereditary and cannot be"*, because *"no one earns
the transformation, no one is denied it by any authority"*, which is why it
*"never hardened"* into privilege (`prismeri.md`).

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED: life stage divides the work. First Wings
*"scout, apprentice, and tend lumenspire settlements"*, and Full Wings (a
minority) crew the **astrogation guilds**. The guilds are institutional
actors with their own interests: they have been *"consistently, politely
unhelpful"* to research on the Emergence trigger (`prismeri.md`). Settlements
keep a dusk curfew and a *Lattice-reader's escort* after dark
(`planets/prismere.md`). Outward: Prismere is a charter member world, and
its fold-corridor expertise reaches the compact *"through its native
Prismeri astrogation guilds"* (`federation-of-sentient-beings.md`). Who
holds its assembly seat, and how a settlement decides anything, are **not
established**.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **two forms, one person, one standing**. The
correction the record is built on (two species reports, one life) is the
idiom: the plates are *"redrawn after the kinship finding"*. No Prismeri
ladder rendering is filed.

**For the Corps.** Survey Corps fold-route work deals with the guilds. A
guild is a body of the able and is not a rank above the people, so a
Ranger who treats a First Wings scout as junior to a Full Wings reader has
made the error the record corrected. The courtesy is to *"hold meetings
somewhere with a beam"*.

### Serephine Dunekin (Kharis Belt IV)

**(a), (b) Social and functional order.** **Not established.** The record
holds a body, a metabolism and an industry: concentrated solar, run
unattended through the lethal hours; *"the dangerous hours are the
productive ones, and the safe hours are for living"* (`serephine-dunekin.md`).
It holds one scout (`characters/sethka-ru.md`). PROPOSED, and only as the
question a writer would meet first: a people that lives by windows must
agree on whose reading of the margin opens one. Whether that is a person,
an office, a rotation or simply each body's own reading is unstated, and
nothing should be chosen until a page needs it.

**(c) Idiom.** Nothing filed. The role-mark is open: (a) posture or gait,
(b) a worn mark (`species-design.md`).

**For the Corps.** A service that *"learns to schedule around them"*
(`serephine-dunekin.md`) is the whole of what the record asks. Nothing
about whom to address on Kharis Belt IV is held.

### Verdani (Verdance)

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED: the record's most various, and every part of
it comes from the world. **Kinship is by symbiont lineage**: the kinbed bond
in infancy is *"the foundational kinship act, more binding in practice than
shared residence or even shared parentage"*, and a kinstrain name marks
*"kin in the fullest sense Verdani law recognizes"* (`verdani.md`). Above
kinship sit **four countries, four different orders**, each from one biome:
the Canopy Concord split between the Understory Assembly and the High-Reach
Compact; the Fenmarch Republic's *"rotating engineering council"*; the
Stonewake Dominion's hereditary **Deep Clans** against elected **Terrace
Assemblies**; and the Aurelin Herdlands' elder **Migratory Councils**
against the newer Waystation Compact (`planets/verdance.md`). So the
Verdani hold standing by lineage, election, rotation and age, in different
places, and the record gives the reason: *"Migration is a metabolic cost,
not a decision — which is the actual reason Verdance has four independent
governments rather than one"* (`verdani.md`).

**(b) Functional order.** DERIVED: *"No Verdani country claims authority
over another"*. External affairs go through the **Fourfold Accord**, *"one
delegate from each country's government"*, convened when a single Verdani
position is needed, with *"no authority over internal law"*. Verdance is
represented *"through the Fourfold Accord on the Federation's rotating
assembly"*. Isren Farrowkin is the Canopy Concord's senior delegate and
currently holds that rotating seat (`characters/isren-farrowkin.md`).

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **strain and light**. Coloration is *"a reasonably
reliable indicator of kinstrain"*. It is biology, though, and
`species-design.md` forbids conscripting it as a mark of role. The record's
politics *"are geography in a way no other Federation member's are"*, which
is how the record states a polity: by what it does with its ground.

### Ollune (Cirrane)

**(a) Social order.** DERIVED: standing is **a claim on the lift of

attributable to a body, readable by anyone close, and impossible to keep
confidential. Shoals belong to bands and meet on fixed cycles years apart,
so Ollune institutions are *"shaped around"* agreements executed by parties
who cannot confer (`ollune.md`). Outward, Cirrane is a member world whose
position *"is usually carried rather than spoken, arriving by courier,
always lagged, and read into the record by someone who is not Ollune"*. How
a shoal or a band arrives at the position that is carried is **not
established**.

**(c) Idiom.** DERIVED: **what an officer holds up, never weight**. The
rendering measures seniority by what an officer holds up, and *"no Ollune
translator would call a High Captain heavy"*. A fixed post comes out as *"a

be at it (`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`).

**For the Corps.** What an Ollune says binds nothing; what an Ollune marks
binds everything. A Ranger carrying a near word is carrying an office.
Carriage is the case where *"Integrity is guaranteed and privacy is impossible"*.

### What holds generally

**Rank appears where a body or a world makes it necessary, and is never
assumed by default.** DERIVED from the blocks above. The Cerebraun centralise
because their own nervous system is a negotiation with one voice, and they
say so. The Verdani have four orders because moving costs a lineage its
light. The Ollune measure standing in lift because lift is what every life
costs. The Prismeri divide work by life stage and decline to rank it,
because no one chooses the Emergence. Where the record holds a body and
not a society (Pelagene, Serephine, Veyr, Ilveth at home, Spiralites,
Doreth), it marks the absence. **The Cerebraun body-to-polity reading is
the Cerebraun's own doctrine and is not the record's method.** The record
should not read a Veyr truss or a Pelagene radial body as a political
order, any more than it adopts the Cerebraun account of human history. A
people's order is stated when the people, or a chapter, shows it doing
something. That is the same rule by which a polity's politics are shown
through what it does (`CLAUDE.md`).

**Standing is held in each people's own medium.** Voice and limb, load and
bearing, heat spent, chorus agreement, lift carried, a telling grown longer,
a strain bonded. The ladder renderings (`the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`)
are the record's proof that each people reads the Corps' one ladder through
its own body. None of the renderings is wrong; the tab settles who holds
what.

**For the Corps, one rule covers all of them.** The Corps talks to whoever a
people sends, on that people's terms, and never decides who speaks for a
people. That follows from the charter's non-political mandate (*do not
govern, legislate, police, or administer*), from the Federation's founding
sentence that *no member's internal governance is any other member's
business*, and from the 14 October ruling that the Rangers mediate *when
needed* and decide nothing. A role-mark, where a people has one, is in its
own medium (`species-design.md`, *The uniform equivalent* and *Marks of
belonging*).

**Traps this section closes.** No people in the record has a ruling
breeder, a fighting class, or a shared mind standing in for its persons.
The Chthonari crew argues, the Ovruhn chorus is many keepers checking one
another, the Ollune hold is persons carrying persons, and the Hegemony's one
voice negotiates with its limbs. None of the structures above is named for,
or compared with, any human ideology. The record's few named offices
(*Republic*, *Dominion*, *Compact*, *Assembly*) are Verdance's own names,
already on its page, and no more are added here.

## Gender, kinship and the self, people by people (Dermot's direction, 20 October 2026)

and individual or collective identity and awareness"*; and, of the shapes put

rendering notes for the Told and Ollune"*. Answered as the 14 October

cited page says it or plainly entails it, or it is biology or physics applied

### The rules already standing

Five, all on the record before this section, and every block below is read
under them.

1. **Gender may exist and is never inferred** (`src/lore/the-shape-of-a-life.md`,
   23 August 2026, `intake-2026-08-23.md`). The page's plain line:
   *"Aliens may have gender, but nothing says they must, and it does not have
   to work like ours."* Its body: gender *"does not follow from a body plan,
   from a reproductive role, or from the observer's expectations, and a
   survey that assigns it from any of those has recorded a fact about the
   survey."* And on the unit: *"Some sapient civilisations do not have
   individual personhood or identity in that sense, and the record states this
   as a fact about them, not a deficiency in them."*
2. **The ten concepts may be absent or unaskable, and plural personhood is
   never a pathology** (this note, *The concepts a people may not share*,
   14 September 2026). Individuality, personhood and family are among the ten,
   each answered *present*, *shaped by the world*, *absent* or *unaskable*. A
   people whose personhood is plural or shared is written in its own terms,
   *"never as a disorder, a horror or a hive"*. And the first of the three
   travelling rules: **derive, don't assign** (*"An alien with a hive mind
   because hive minds are alien is the trope default"*).
3. **Awareness and consciousness are kept apart** (`src/lore/what-a-mind-is-carried-by.md`).
   *"Awareness is what a sensorium delivers"*; *"Consciousness is there being
   somebody the registering is for"*; and *"it files what registers, it
   recognises who is there, and it does not derive the second from the
   first."* So each block below answers the two separately: what the people
   registers without trying, and who the registering is for.
4. **A people's pronoun is a once-only decision, translation by default**
   (`CLAUDE.md`, 7 August 2026). Declared on the people's lore page, any coined
   paradigm registered in the glossary; existing usage stands; *"the record's
   default is translation"*, so *they* (or *she*, or *he*) on the page is a
   rendering unless a page says otherwise.
5. **Plural minds are the human-side case, and never the rule for another
   people** (`src/glossary/plural-minds.md`; CLAUDE.md, the 23 September note
   that the plural-minds link is speculative and never generalised). The
   glossary is Concord law about a body that holds more than one person. The
   cosmology's coupling rule under it is general (*The Shape of a Life* cites
   it: identity need not stop at one body, *"nor required identity to be held
   at the scale of a body at all"*), but the plural-minds category, its
   registration and its joint responsibility are the Concord's institutions.
   No people below is filed as a plural mind, and no plural system is read as
   a model of one. *The Shape of a Life*'s own habit governs the casework:
   *"translate the obligation, never the people"*.

Two consequences, drawn once. **A silence about sex is not a finding of
none.** A people whose page describes no sex is *not established*, never
*genderless*: the second is a claim, and the trope's. **A pronoun on the page
is evidence about the record, not the people**, until a lore page declares
otherwise.

Cerebraun (4), then the Mnemari and the Ollune (one each, and both re-made

### The Told (the Pandoids of Fliade)

**What the record says.** The people are *"the Told"*; *"one is of the people
by having been told the tellings"*, and *"There is no other way in"*. A person
is called by *"the first true thing said of them that others went on
repeating"*; a name *"is not replaced when a truer thing is said of a person
later; the truer thing is added"*, and *"A long name is a grown one, and what
it shows is clarity and maturity"* (`src/glossary/the-told.md`). Small ones
are born: *"The smallest was born after the two stones"* (S12E01C01); the
season's telling is for *"the ones who are not born yet"* (S12E01C04). The
communities are several and unjoined, and their bodies differ (`src/lore/planets/fliade.md`).
On sex the character pages mark the absence in so many words: *"Their age,
and their sex. The party's log says he. The party's word is the party's, and
the record does not follow it"* (`src/characters/teddy.md`); *"Their age, and
their sex"* again on `stone-first.md`.

**Sex, reproduction and gender. Not established, and the absence fits.**
DERIVED: birth exists and the young are *small ones*, so a life has a start
and a youth. DERIVED from Fliade's ecology (`ecology-design.md`, *Fliade*: a
chemical base in a few warm places, *the large are few*): a warm holds few
people, so births are few and each small one is known to everyone in it.
DERIVED: in 24 chapter files no Told telling names a parent, a mate or a sex,
and the survey's *he* for Carried-It-Sleeping is filed on the character page
as the survey's. That is *The Shape of a Life*'s doctrine at work in a
chapter: the party assigned a sex from a body, and the record kept it as a
fact about the party. DERIVED: the naming practice cannot carry sex, because a
name is a doing said and repeated (*Stone-First*, *Went-Round*,
*Keeps-A-Hand-On-It*), never a kind. PROPOSED: if the Told hold any
distinction of kind among persons, it is not in the names, and a chapter that
needs one would show it in a telling, not in a pronoun. Why the absence is
right: Fliade's page is observational (*"Nothing here is an account the
Pandoids gave of themselves"*), the thread is *"a telling rendered, not a
transcription"*, and the Told have told nobody.

**Kinship.** DERIVED: belonging is by tellings, not by descent (*no other way
in*). A small one becomes one of the Told when they *"can tell them all
without a mistake"*. So the unit the record shows is the warm and the
tellings, and kin by birth is **not established**. PROPOSED: kin may exist
and be untold, since a telling is about what someone did; the record should
not supply a family to fill the gap, nor declare that there is none.

**Individual and collective.** DERIVED: individuals, named, who argue: the
manners are argued for the first time in S11E01C03, and *"It is not settled
by who talks best"*. The collective name is made the same way as a person's
(*"the first true thing said of all of them is that they were told"*), so the
people is a telling about everyone and never a mind above them. DERIVED: a
person's name is held in other people's mouths, *what others went on
repeating*, and a telling about somebody else *"names nobody"*
(`the-told.md`; S14E01C04, where Keeps-A-Hand-On-It lets a thing go by
*"because it was not the kind of thing that becomes a name. It was about the
up-people"*). So a Told self is **partly carried by others, in speech**. That
is the Ollune shape in a different medium (below), and the two must not be
merged: the Told are *seen more exactly* by others; the Ollune are *held up*
by them.

**Awareness and sense of self.** DERIVED: certain without trying of the shape
of warmth in a place and of stone under the body; unable to know the roof
(this note, *The Pandoids, answered*, 1). The shadow ruling (8 September) is
the awareness edge exactly: a shape without warmth is what the sensorium
cannot place. Consciousness is the survey's to recognise and has been, at the
line, by stated interval (`fliade.md`, 2834). DERIVED, sense of self: a person
grows by being told more truly (*"the person has been seen more exactly, and
has become more"*); youth is *"having every story and none of your own"*
(this note, 7). PROPOSED: a Told self is known to its holder the way a telling
is known, by having been heard; a person alone with an untold thing has
something not yet theirs.

**Pronoun.** *They*, and already a declared rendering, on four pages:
`the-told.md` (*"The record's they for an individual is its translation
default, not a claim about a pronoun system of their own"*), the canonical
guide, `teddy.md` and `stone-first.md`. Settled by the first chapter's
approval (`open-questions.md`, *Pronouns — settled by the approved chapter*).
**No new note was needed**, and none is added: the glossary page is where the
Chthonari and Ovruhn equivalents sit, on the page that renders the people in

heard the people speak.

**Traps.** *The genderless alien*: refused by the first consequence above;

manners and by names that are individual. *The savant*: memory is ordinary and

**What a page would need.** If a chapter calls for a birth, a small one's

### The Chthonari

pressurised burrow, and a burrow is tuned during excavation so that it is *"a
resonating instrument its residents are never not partly listening to"*.
So a young Chthonari begins inside a sounding structure. PROPOSED: the first
thing a young one registers is a burrow's tuning, and a crew's off-Belt module
is a question of what it is tuned to (this note, *The Chthonari, answered*, 6).
DERIVED: gender is **not established**, and the page says why in the
people's own terms: the Undersong's person-mark is contact, not sex. PROPOSED:
if a distinction of kind exists, the Undersong does not carry it in its
person-forms, so a reader would meet it, if ever, in what a person is felt to
be doing in the structure, never in a pronoun. No proposal is made for a sex.

**Individual and collective.** DERIVED: individuals who argue; *"a career
choice made by a great many individuals"*; a crew's *"consensus is a tuning
reached, never a state they start in"* (this note, *Traps*). Each name is a
signature *told from every other name*, so individuality is acoustic and
exact. DERIVED: the shared thing is a structure, not a mind: the Undersong is
*"a vocabulary, not an empathy"* (this note, 2), and a crew on one deck
shares it while *"One alone on a deck has the deck"* (`nothing-turned.md`).
What persists of the dead is structural: the cutters are in the burrow as
load. PROPOSED: that is the Chthonari answer to *legacy*, and it is a
property of what was left, never of a group mind.

**Awareness and sense of self.** DERIVED: awareness is the structure, read
*"continuously and without conscious effort"*, and off structure it is
silence (this note, *What a Chthonari cannot do*). Consciousness, the someone
it is for, is individual and argues. PROPOSED, sense of self: a Chthonari is
known to others by being felt through what both stand on, and knows itself
there too, which is why the posting's loneliness is *"a shared structure
nobody else in the room can feel"* rather than any unkindness.

**Pronoun.** *They*, ruled 4 September 2026, with the rendering note on the
lore page. *Nothing Turned* renders a child as *it* in the translating set's
voice; that is the Codex author's rendering, valid for its author, and it is
no precedent for the record's voice.

**Traps.** *The insect silhouette*, in its kinship form: a burrow with a

record has a ruling breeder"* (this note, 14 October, *Traps*), and

member of it. *The hive*: closed above. *The savant*: structure is a
contractor's competence until the case needs it.

**What a page would need.** If a chapter shows a Chthonari young one at home,

somebody the nine registerings are for, arrived at by negotiation and shown in
the three-part answer *"in the order the centres finished thinking"*
(`tavesh.md`). PROPOSED: a Cerebraun's sense of self is the voice's standing
with its own limbs, so a limb that agrees *"outright"* (S03E01C03) is an event,
and a single-centred mind looks to them like a voice with no one to answer it.

**Pronoun. Undeclared, and proposed here as *they*** (a choice, his). Usage
now: the three Sohrel chapters, `sohrel.md` and `tavesh.md` use the name and
no pronoun, and Sixth Course's page uses the name throughout *"as the record does

(*"It sees through many small eyes"*, *"a danger it is protected from"*, *"its

as one of them (*"it is not toxic to them. It is not the air they would

already uses for the Chthonari, Ovruhn, Ollune and Told; the record keeps *it*
for an animal, an instrument and a part (*"a Smart Pet off its collar is
simply an animal"*; *Sohrel's central brain … it*), so *it* for a person would
file a person as one of those; and *they* can carry a short rendering note
that closes its one risk, a plural reading, by saying the nine centres are one
person. The name stays the first choice, as the chapters already do. This is
put as the open item below, since the lore page cannot keep both.

**Traps.** *The hive*, in the polity: subject administrations as limbs is
Hegemony doctrine, and the record adopts no body-to-polity reading (14

at the hatching is an Earth animal's life, not theirs. *The savant*: holding a
correct position and an unresolved one at once is the Administrator-Voice's
craft, and costs (`sohrel.md`). *Villainy*: the Hegemony's account of humans
is theirs, and never the record's reason to make them cold.

**What a page would need.** A rendering sentence on `cerebraun.md` once the
pronoun is ruled, and the four *it*s turned to match (open item 1). Anything
on life cycle waits for a chapter set in a Cerebraun household or council.

### The Mnemari

**What the record says.** No senescence; *"no natural upper bound on
lifespan"*; death by event (`src/lore/mnemari.md`). *"Mnemari birthrate is
low by deliberate cultural design, not biological necessity"*; *"Bringing a
new Mnemari mind into being is treated on Ilenne as a rare, weighty,
multi-generational commitment rather than a private family matter, planned by
kin-councils"*; *"A Mnemari child grows and matures at a pace outside
observers find unremarkable"*; *"They cannot interbreed with humans or any
other known species"* (same). Every Mnemari on record is *she*: the oldest
confirmed, Ulessa (*"She is she on the Federation record"*), Osune (*"She is
she in the reader's rendering, as every Mnemari on the record is"*), and the
one who walked Osune between the springs (*"because she did not say it"*,
`src/codex/by-the-chair-of-that-day.md`).

**Sex, reproduction and gender.** DERIVED, and kept to the record: what makes
births rare is a decision, not a body. The page says *by deliberate cultural
design*, and the reasoning is the record's own: a people that loses no elders
to time need not replace them at a mortal pace. Who decides is a kin-council,
on a multi-generational timescale, and the act is *bringing a new mind into
being*: the record's words avoid naming parents, a pair or a sex, and this
section does not supply them. DERIVED from Ilenne (`ecology-design.md`,
*Ilenne*: sparse producers, dry spells of no period): a world that feeds few
large walkers and punishes a wrong route rewards few, well-placed lives,
which fits the page's design reason without becoming its cause. Gender is
**not established**. PROPOSED: the uniform *she* is the Federation record's
rendering, and asserts no sex; a people who all render alike in one human
register have shown the register, not a single sex.

**Kinship.** DERIVED: kin-councils exist and decide births; *kin* is the
record's word; the relation of a child to the kin who planned it is **not
established**. PROPOSED: a Mnemari kin is a long-standing body that holds a
new life as a commitment over generations, which the page's *rather than a
private family matter* already implies.

**Individual and collective.** DERIVED: individuals, emphatically: a self
that loses no episode at all, and the Setting-Down judged by *"her people's
own test and nobody else's to apply"* (`ulessa.md`). DERIVED: a Mnemari dates
by *"the chair of that day"* and gives her birth as a room *"by who was in
it"* (`by-the-chair-of-that-day.md`), so a self is located among the people
who were present, in full and unfaded.

**Awareness and sense of self.** DERIVED: awareness on Ilenne's terms, far
hearing, smell into the wind, a horizon's sight (`species-design.md`,
*Mnemari*); consciousness carrying every episode at full resolution, *"a
liability they have learned, slowly, to carry"*. The self is the one thing the
people judge from inside: when the load *"is beginning to crowd out their
capacity to act well in the present"*, they Set Down.

**Pronoun.** *She*, existing usage, stands. PROPOSED and settled here by
recommendation (his to overrule): when `mnemari.md` is next touched, one
rendering sentence on the Chthonari pattern, that *she* is the Federation
record's rendering of a Mnemari person and claims no sex, since the record has
described none. That is a clarification and changes no usage.

**Traps.** *The single-sex people*, the genderless alien's twin: a uniform
*she* read as *all female*. Refused by the rendering note above. *The
elephant that never forgets* and *the infallible witness* (`species-design.md`;
`ecology-design.md`): memory is a cost. *Fertility as the price of
immortality*: the page puts the low birthrate in design, not biology, and a
writer should not move it into the body.

**What a page would need.** The rendering sentence above; anything on how a
kin-council decides waits for a chapter on Ilenne.

### The Ollune

**What the record says.** They sleep in **holds**, *"tethered clusters of
eight to thirty, physically linked at the tendril, in which the waking carry
the sinking. Every Ollune alive has been held unconscious by others, nightly,
since infancy"*; trust *"is a load being borne, by named individuals, right
now"*; and *"the Ollune language has no separate words for falling asleep and
going under"* (`src/lore/ollune.md`). The far word *"carries no speaker's
identity"*; the near word is *"individually distinct to the body that
produced them"*, and *"Agreement, consent, debt, office and judgement exist
only as near words"* (same). Standing is *"a claim on the lift of others"*
(same). The page says nothing of sex, birth or parentage beyond *infancy*.

**Sex, reproduction and gender. Not established.** DERIVED: there is an
infancy, and an infant is held from the first night. DERIVED from the physics
(`species-design.md`, *Ollune*; `ecology-design.md`, *Cirrane*): nothing can
be set down, every mass is lifted by a body, and below the shell is *the
direction in which things stop*. PROPOSED: so whatever an Ollune birth is, it
happens in the air and inside a hold, and a newborn's lift is paid by others
before it is paid by itself. Nothing about sex is proposed. DERIVED, for the
grammar: the far word cannot mark who spoke, and the near word cannot be made
without marking who did. PROPOSED: if an Ollune grammar marks persons, the
distinction it can afford is attributable or unattributable, as the
Chthonari's is felt or unfelt, and sex is not a channel either word is built
on. Why the absence fits: the record knows the Ollune through hulls,
viewports and carried marks, and no visitor has been inside a hold.

**Kinship.** DERIVED: the hold is the unit of belonging the record shows,
eight to thirty, and whom it holds the page does not say (`ilyare.md`: *"the
people's page says a hold is eight to thirty and does not say who"*). Whether
a hold is kin, chosen or both is **not established**.

**Individual and collective. Personhood carried by others; not a collective
mind.** DERIVED: every Ollune is a named individual with a body-distinct near
word, and office is one body's mark (Ilyare's crossing). What the hold does is
**carry a person**, not merge one: the sleeper is held up by others who are
awake, and is no less a person for being carried. That is *The Shape of a
Life*'s *identity held in … a working* in its most literal form, and it is
not the case that page names of a civilisation without individual
personhood. PROPOSED: the Ollune hold one standing truth about the self that
no other people on the record holds as plainly: a person is someone others
have kept from going under, tonight and every night, and so personhood is
never had alone.

**Awareness and sense of self.** DERIVED: consciousness lapses nightly, and
the language does not tell sleep from going under, so the self's continuity
is supplied by others' lift; awareness is pressure at range and chemistry at
hand. DERIVED: the ones who sleep unheld off-world and *"cannot afterwards
settle back into a hold"* have a word, *"old, gentle"*, first used of
survivors of a fall. PROPOSED: that word is the Ollune record of a changed
self, a person who has learned they can be set down.

**Pronoun.** *They*, the people's page's own usage (`ilyare.md`: *"The
pronoun is the people's page's own usage of an Ollune: they"*), with *it* in
the body paragraph for the envelope's physics. The rendering note now on
`src/lore/ollune.md`, a clarification on the Ovruhn and Chthonari pattern,
reads:

> A note on rendering: an Ollune individual is **they** in the record's
> translation register, the ordinary conversion, the one that turns a
> passing's interval into the record's years. It claims nothing about how the
> Ollune themselves mark a person. The far word carries no speaker's identity
> and the near word cannot be made without one, and the record has not been
> told what either does with a person spoken of; nor has it described an
> Ollune sex. Where this page says *it*, it is speaking of the body: the
> envelope, and the bill it pays.

**Traps.** *The hive*: a hold is persons carrying persons (14 October, *What
holds generally*). *The parable*: the page itself warns that the Ollune are
*"very easy to romanticise"*, and a hold read as a lesson in trust is the
register the Krenyi are restful for refusing. *The jellyfish life cycle*: a
polyp, a budding and a medusa is an Earth animal's, and the body was re-made
this week to escape exactly that attractor. *The genderless alien*: not
established is not none.

**What a page would need.** A birth in a hold, if a chapter needs one, goes on
`ollune.md` by extension under *Buoyancy as Metabolism*.

### What holds across the peoples

**No people on the record has a sex the record has described.** DERIVED from
the blocks above: the Chthonari page says the biology described sorts no one
by sex, and every other page is silent. The pronouns on the record (*she*
for Syra, Suvra Kel, Isren Farrowkin, Mira and every Mnemari; *he* for
Sethka Ru; *they* for the rest) are renderings or existing usage, and none
asserts a sex. That is the 23 August doctrine already obeyed, not a gap.

**No people on the record lacks individual persons.** DERIVED: every people
with a person on record has named individuals who argue, decline or judge
for themselves. The nearest shapes to shared personhood, the Ollune hold, the
Told name, the Ovruhn chorus and the Chthonari deck, are each persons held,
told, checked or felt by others. So *The Shape of a Life*'s civilisation
without individual personhood **is not yet any people in the record**, and
none of these four should be conscripted to stand for it. The worklist line
for such a people stays open as a worklist line (`open-questions.md`, *The
shape of alien lives*, 19 October triage).

**Each people's self is held in its own medium.** Felt through structure,
told and repeated, negotiated among centres, carried unfaded, held up by
others. The same medium that gives each its standing (14 October, *What holds
generally*) gives each its self, and the record states both only where a
page shows the people doing something.

### Open, and his

One item meets the genuine-dilemma bar, because a published page holds two
commitments that cannot both stand: the 7 August rule that a people's
pronoun is applied consistently, and `cerebraun.md`'s mixed *it* and *they*.

1. **The Cerebraun pronoun.** (A) **They**, the record's translation default,
   declared on `cerebraun.md` in one rendering sentence (*the nine centres are
   one person*), the four generic *it*s turned to *they* as a clarification,
   and the name kept first in prose as the chapters already have it. (B)
   **It**, declared as the record's rendering and the page's *they* turned to
   match. (C) **No pronoun**: the name or *a Cerebraun* in every sentence, the
   page recast to need none, and nothing declared. **Recommended: A.** It is
   the default every other people has had, it costs one sentence and four
   words, it leaves the chapters exactly as they stand, and B would make the
   Cerebraun the one people the record calls by the pronoun it keeps for an
   animal, an instrument and a part. C is honest but cannot hold in a
   generic sentence about the people, which is where the *it*s are.

Settled by recommendation in the blocks above, each his to overrule and none
a dilemma: the Mnemari rendering sentence (*she* claims no sex), written when
`mnemari.md` is next touched; the Ollune note, written now; no new note for
the Told, who already carry one; and no sex proposed for any people.

## Where this goes

When the species' crew is cast, each member's page can answer the seven
questions above in the character's own particular — which one has begun to
listen, which one still memorises, which one addresses the habitat's mind
before the humans have introduced it — and the strand's blocks can be
checked against the four cannot-dos the way a chapter is checked against
`canon_facts`. The method generalises: the Mnemari (a memory that does not
decay) and the Ilveth (warmth as sight) are the next two peoples whose minds
the record has already half-decided.
