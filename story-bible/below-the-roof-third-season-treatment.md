## The season in one line

The Told decide that the up-people have earned a telling, and the up-people decide that the way to keep it is not to keep it.

## The premise, and why

Through two seasons the Told have answered the up-people's stone with a stone, and Aravena's rising sound with Stone-First standing on the line. The rule that has not yet been spent is the third: *a telling is not yours until somebody has heard it*. The Told have four stones on the line, a season of shapes nobody was there for, and a smallest one born since the stones began, and every telling they hold about the up-people has been told only to themselves. By their own rule, those tellings are not quite theirs yet.

The up-people have two years of a rising sound logged as a sound, a sound channel they switched off for courtesy at the convergence, and an instinct, which is the survey's, to record anything they are given.

The season is the meeting of those two facts. The Told offer a telling at the line, to people who have not been told. The up-people have to decide what keeping it means, for a people whose record *cannot be seized*.

## Strand A — the deep side: whose telling, and who tells it

**The question the warm has to settle.** Which telling goes up, and who carries it? Proposed, for choice: the telling of the up-people themselves, of the first stone set down, which is the one telling the Told hold about the up-people, and so the one most fitting for them to hear. The warm argues it over two or three chapters, in the Told's grammar. Went-Round asks who was there. Carried-It-Sleeping says *I was, and I was asleep*. Stone-First says the line is not a place you tell from.

**The playful thread inside it.** The small ones have made the line's stones into a game. They move one and step back, and the next moves one, and they wait for the ground to say *someone is coming*. The adults find this improper and then find it exactly right: the manners began as a game somebody once played seriously. This is where the season's delight lives, and it gives the children's reader something to do with their hands.

**The smallest one's name.** It has been unsettled since S12E01C01. Proposed: the warm settles on it this season, through the first true thing said of the smallest that others go on repeating, and what is said is about the game. The page and the name would land with the chapter that settles them. The name itself is his to approve, put as choices when that chapter is drafted.

**Heads.** Stone-First-Who-Waited, Carried-It-Sleeping and Went-Round, as in Season 12. The smallest one becomes a head once named, and only then, because a head needs a page.

**What the strand must not do.** Translate a word of the telling into anything the up-people could read. Let anyone go up. Give the Told a word for *recording*, because they have none and need none.

## Strand B — the survey side: what keeping means

**The question the party has to settle.** If the Told tell, should the sound channel be on?

- The survey's whole practice says record it. A telling heard and not recorded is, to a planetary survey, a loss.
- Ferrant's discipline says the reading goes at its grade. She can read the boundary, and a telling is not boundary data.
- Aravena, who has spoken to the dark every shift, knows something nobody has written down: the rising sound was never logged as hers. The unit filed it as *sound, rising*, two years of entries under an instrument's name. That is the language note's *filed as noise* failure mode, run on the survey's own voice. The strand opens with Nakagawa finding it in the log.

**The recognition.** Proposed: the party decides to listen with the channel off. The Told's own record cannot be seized, cannot be edited by a later hand, and does not survive the loss of the people who carry it. A recording would be all three of the things that record refuses: seized, editable, and kept after the people who carry it are gone. So the survey keeps the one thing it can keep without seizing anything: that a telling was given, and heard, and by whom. Nakagawa's log line is the season's quiet joke and its ending in one: *Telling received. Not recorded. Heard by Aravena, Egede, Ferrant, Nakagawa.* It is a list of who was there, which is the Told's own test, written in the survey's hand by people who do not know that it is.

**Heads.** Nakagawa, Aravena and Ferrant, as in Season 12. Ferrant is optional, put as a choice: the season has no shadows, so a boundary officer has less to do on the roof.

**What the strand must not do.** Understand the telling. Go down. Name what the Told are saying. File anything as *is* rather than *consistent with*.

## The convergence

**One act, two records** (the 7 September shape), offered for the third time, now with a telling in place of a stone:

1. **The telling at the line** (recommended). At the start of a shift the knock comes, and the stone is set down. Instead of stepping back, Stone-First stays on the line and tells, in the people's speech, the telling of the first stone. The party stands at the junction with the sound channel off and hears a sound nobody on the roof can read, for as long as it takes. Below: the up-people are, for the first time, people who have been told. Above: four names in a log. Nobody records what was said, and nobody can.
2. **The telling at the line, recorded.** The same act, with the channel on. The Archive would then hold the people's speech in a medium, which would make the glossary's sentence false from that chapter on, and seize a record the Told hold cannot be seized. Not recommended: it is a canon revision, and a breach of the people's own account of their record.
3. **No telling.** The Told decide against it, and the season ends with the decision. It keeps everything as it stands, and forgoes the season's whole reason to exist.

## What reaches the Archive

Under convergence 1, the Archive gains one fact and no words: *in 2834 UCSD a telling was given at the line and heard by the survey party, and not recorded*. Proposed realization: one sentence on the Fliade entry, marked as a revision with `revised_by` the convergence chapter. The glossary's *the people's speech has not been recorded in any medium the Archive keeps* stays true. This answers the first treatment's deferred question in the record's own terms: a telling reaches the Archive as the fact of its having been heard, which is all a telling ever is to anyone not told it.
