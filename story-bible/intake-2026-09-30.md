## The Krenyi glossary entry and the word enslavement

His correction, verbatim: *"Krenyi glossary entry wrongly names enslavement
when it meant the opposite"*.

### Canon check

`src/glossary/krenyi.md` stated the trait twice as a resistance: *structural
resistance to fear, fury, enslavement, mythification, and grief-predation* in
the summary, and *resistant to glamour, enslavement, and mythification* in the
body. The lore page's bullet said *Unenslavable: no physical, social, or
metaphysical mechanism for enslaving them exists within the story's
cosmology*, and its Levril section says *they are unenslavable in the strict
sense the cosmology gives that word*. S01E01C01's canon fact says *cannot be
enslaved or mythified*. The 27 September intake noted that *slavery* appears
in canon only of the Krenyi. So the record's meaning was never in doubt; the
glossary's phrasing named the thing by its absence and, in a list, invited
the opposite reading.

### Realized, as a clarification

The glossary's summary and body now state the freedom itself: structural
rather than won, no mechanism in the cosmology able to own or compel one,
cooperation only ever extended (the lore page's own floor). The lore bullet
says the same in place of *Unenslavable* and names the older filing; the
Levril section's sentence stands, since it defines the word as it uses it.
The chapter's canon fact is untouched.

### Put as choices — his

The wording, since the correction names the fault and not the replacement:

1. **Freedom stated as structure** (realized, recommended): *a freedom that
   is structural rather than won — no mechanism in the cosmology can own or
   compel one*.
2. **Autonomy as the defining word**: *structural autonomy* in the summary's
   list, with the same explanatory sentence in the body.
3. **Drop the item from the summary altogether** and leave the body's
   sentence to carry it, on the ground that a glossary summary should not
   list what a people cannot be subjected to at all.

### Ruled, the same day

Verbatim: *"Option 1"*. The wording stands as realized: freedom stated as
structure. Nothing open; PR #898 merged on green.

His note after ruling, verbatim: *"I simply misread it, but the
clarification is even better."* So the fault was a misreading the phrasing
invited rather than an error in the record's meaning, and the clarification
stands on its own merit.

A section on `src/lore/krenyi.md`, *The Nearest Frame Is the Strangest*,

as a familiar kind, so every difference arrives as a correction; they could

than inspection and never resting on a claim, since they use no social
deception; and survey teaching trains cadets on the nearest frame. One
sentence on the glossary entry. A dated paragraph in `species-design.md`.

### Readings, flagged

1. **Strangest is the observer's experience, not a rank of the peoples.**

2. **Passing is possible and never attempted.** The record says the Krenyi use

3. **Survey teaching trains cadets on the nearest frame.** New in-world
   practice, stated in one sentence and asserted of no page. Confirm or cut.
4. **The direction is not a licence for a second near-human people.** The
   species-design note says so. Confirm.

### Direction check

A clarification of the 22 September exception and the 13 August ruling; the
18 August correction that the Arilon pattern is species-neutral is untouched,
since the passing here is a fact about the Krenyi and not about the pattern.
The converse of the silhouette instrument is worth naming here and not on the

silhouette fires and is refuted, by descent, by blood and by the calendar.

## Follow-up, the same day — the Oversight Council's composition, and its standing beyond the Solar System

Dermot's question, verbatim: *"What is the Civilian Oversight Council of
the Star Rangers and how is it elected or composed?"*, and then *"What about
the rest of the galaxy?"*. Both answered from the record; neither ruled.
His answer to the shapes put: *"Not sure"*. Recorded here so the question
is not lost, and indexed in `open-questions.md`.

### What the record establishes

The Council is the civilian body the 2723 charter makes the Corps
accountable to, and the only one (`src/lore/formation-of-star-rangers.md`,
preamble; `src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`, the independence
clause). It descends from the 2722 Consolidation Accords' *explicit civilian
oversight*; the 2719 Hearing already had a *civilian oversight panel*, and
the record does not say whether the two are one body. It holds the
Triumvirate accountable for the Corps' conduct (*the three the Oversight
Council asks*, in `the-ladder-in-other-tongues.md`); it cannot defund the
Corps (`planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters.md`); it exists because *a
three-seat command body is still a command body*. In the story it drafts the
Dock Seven citation Wender will not sign, runs the inquiry with a liaison
officer on Threshold and a press office that answers in the Corps' place,
and holds Shepherd's funeral *as it was entitled to* (S06E01C02, S06E02C01,
the funeral address).

Beyond the Solar System no polity holds the Corps to account. The Union,
the Federation and the Hegemony *recognise* the institution
(`shore-leave-and-neutrality.md`: *no single government grants this*);
recognition can be refused to a person, and each polity keeps the power to
expel an individual Ranger. The Federation's compact *does not buy
influence over Ranger conduct*; the Hegemony sees *no reason to fund an
institution they do not command*; the Union has an information-sharing
agreement with the Survey Corps only. A Ranger's conduct anywhere is the
Provost track's, so the chain is the same in Hegemony space as at
Threshold: officer, Provost, Triumvirate, Council.

### What it does not establish, and the shapes put

No page names the Council's seats, term, electorate, appointing authority,
chair or size; no page says whether any polity outside the Solar System has
ratified or formally acknowledged the charter, or whether the Council's
writ is understood there as binding the Corps in their space. Under the
marked-absences doctrine this is an absence, not a gap: the Corps' standing
outside the Solar System rests on recognition each polity may withdraw,
which is the shape the framers wanted.

Composition, three shapes: (a) a Concord standards-board style body of
delegates from subscribing polities; (b) a body seated by the habitats and
outer stations whose escalation gap the charter was written to close;
(c) a mixed panel descended directly from the 2719 Hearing's civilian
oversight panel. Galactic standing, three shapes: (1) purely Solar System,
the other polities' recognition the whole of their relationship — the
reading closest to the text and asserting nothing new; (2) observer seats
or standing liaisons for each recognising polity, no vote — an extension
needing a line on three polity pages; (3) constituted by the recognising
polities — a larger change, awkward against the Consolidation Accords
origin, needing a genuine dilemma to license it.

**Recommendation, if and when it is wanted:** (b) and (1). The habitats
and outer stations are the parties on the record as having forced the
jurisdiction clause in and refused to ratify without it, so a Council
seated by them is the one shape the founding already argues for; and (1)
is what every polity page already says. Neither is needed by any chapter,
and nothing is asserted until one is.

### Not ruled

Verbatim: *"Not sure"*. Left open; nothing asserted. Nothing in `src/`
changed.

### Follow-up, the same day — not human-centric; ruled *Option 2 but it gradually evolves towards Option 3*

Dermot's observation, verbatim: *"Strangely the Star Rangers are neutral
and independent rather than human-centric."* Read as the tension under the

ruling already states as a usage rule. The record never explained the step
between the two. Three shapes put: (1) leave it a marked strangeness;
(2) write the mechanism into lore as a clarification — a neutrality that
admits one exception forfeits the recognition it is made of, so the clause
generalised the day the first non-human polity recognised the Corps;
(3) let the Council's composition answer it by seating recognising
polities. Recommended (2).

**Ruled, verbatim *"Option 2 but it gradually evolves towards Option 3"*.**

Realized as a new lore page, `src/lore/the-oversight-council.md`, which
also answers the morning's question of what the Council is. Option 2 is
the page's third section: the clause as written is parochial; recognition
is the whole of a Ranger's standing and is granted on the understanding the
Corps has no privilege; *no side, except our own species* would end that
recognition in the breath that named it; so the clause generalised by
structure, not amendment. Stated as the mechanism beside the formation
page's older reason (the Fellowship's vocation, *Older Than the Charter*),
so the two pages do not compete. The 28 September usage rule is restated

Option 3's gradual evolution is the fourth section, and it runs on the
same mechanism: a council that holds the Corps to account on behalf of one
polity holds it *as* one polity's institution, which is what every other
polity's recognition is granted on the understanding the Corps is not. The
Council answered by seat: a recognising polity may hold a non-voting seat

the Accords' seats vote, the added ones do not. The Federation holds the
longest-standing one, filled by an assembly appointee replaced at each
compact renewal; the Hegemony declined (*a seat without a vote is an
arrangement it sees no reason to occupy*), the offer renewed at intervals;
the Union has not answered, the offer filed beside its refusal of SSDC
integration. A non-binding Council resolution names the direction (*ought
in the end to be seated by those who recognise it*) and not the
destination. One sentence each on the three polity pages; a clarifying
sentence in the formation page's neutrality clause; links from the
shore-leave, rank and 2723 timeline pages; a guide entry.

**Direction check:** an *extension* of the 28 September usage rule (humans
not privileged), which it grounds without altering; an *extension* of the
shore-leave page's recognition doctrine, applied to the institution as it
was already applied to the person; consistent with the Federation page's
seam (*the Rangers are not the Concord*), which the drift strengthens. The
morning's *Not sure* on the Council's galactic standing is now ruled in
this direction; its composition question is narrowed, not answered — the
Accords' original seating stays open.
