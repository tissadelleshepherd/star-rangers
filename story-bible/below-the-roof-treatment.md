# Below the Roof — the survey-side strand

Unpublished planning note, the shape of the thread's second strand before any
of it is drafted. Dermot's rulings, 7 September 2026 (`intake-2026-09-07.md`,
third and eighth sections): **"two or more strands"** and, on the three
shapes put for where they meet, **"one act, two records."** This is the
treatment those rulings ask for: what is already fixed, what the two strands
are, where they meet, and the choices still his. Nothing here is prose and
nothing here is canon until a chapter carries it.

Open questions raised here are indexed in `open-questions.md`, per the
standing convention.

## The season in one line

Two peoples keep the same line from opposite sides, and the story is over the
moment one of them does the other's manners.

## Strand A — the deep side (the Told)

**As published.** Three chapters, three heads (Stone-First,
Carried-It-Sleeping, Went-Round): nobody looked up; the stone carried
sleeping; the manners argued and the margin kept in the open. The strand is
complete in the sense the rules require — a child who reads only these has a
whole story — and it ends on Stone-First standing on the line.

**RULED 8 September 2026 — "A forth and fifth chapter"** (`intake-2026-09-08.md`,
tenth section). Strand A runs to five. **C04 is the waiting**: the line kept,
the small ones kept from the squeeze, the sound at the end of each working
time heard and not answered. **C05 is the convergence from the deep side**:
Stone-First, on the line, hearing the ground announce a stranger — the
Told's record of the one act. **C04 drafted and approved 8 September
(#748); C05 drafted the same day as a pair with Strand B's third**
(`intake-2026-09-08.md`, fifteenth section).

## Strand B — the survey side (the up-people)

**To draft, at Dermot's ask.** Its own POV cast — the surface party, from
inside — and no scene shared with Strand A. What it is built on is already
on the page: Nakagawa's lead, Egede's *unasked, which is worse*, the relay
found upright at the party's own furthest mark, lifted, and not replaced. Its
question is the mirror of the Told's: not *why do they never ask* but *how
does one ask a people who leave the ground to you until you do.*

Three things the strand must do, and one it must not:

- **Read the same acts from above.** The relay found on the line (C02's
  stone, from the party's side). The one who speaks to the dark at the end
  of each working time — from inside, so that the reader knows what was
  asked and of whom, and the Told's strand goes on not knowing.
- **Stay in the children's band and the children's register**: the
  up-people are people here, with names the survey uses, and their
  bewilderment is written plainly. Egede's codex note is an adult document
  and is not quoted; its feeling is.
- **Keep the survey's naming doctrine visible.** The party says *Pandoid*
  and *Teddy*; the strand never corrects it and never explains it. A child
  who has read Strand A knows better, which is the whole pleasure.
- **It must not explain the Told.** No scene in Strand B may know what
  Stone-First is doing on the line. The up-people see someone standing in
  the open where nobody stood before, and that is all they get.
- **It must not make the Pandoids cute.** Agreed 8 September 2026
  (`intake-2026-09-08.md`, third section), after Dermot asked whether they
  were too cute for aliens on the children's tier. The cuteness is the
  survey's misreading and stays theirs: the log says *Individual Four*,
  *Teddy* is speech only, and the party's fondness never becomes the
  narrator's. The facts that answer it — mass, range, eleven minutes, the
  manners — are Strand A's and Strand B does not soften them by never
  seeing them. The cute-alien-companion pattern is a trope and refused,
  and Dermot named the instance: **they should not look like, or ever be
  confused with, the Ewoks of Star Wars** — in prose and in image prompts
  alike. What keeps them clear of it is already canon: adult mass, grey,
  upright, an alien face, and a people who argue manners and move stones,
  not a tribe of small furred forest creatures who adopt the visitors.

Who the POV heads are is his. The shape needs at least the one who speaks to
the dark; the ruled convergence names that person *the smallest of the
three, the one who knelt to the humming stone*, and **they are not Egede**
(ruled 8 September 2026, *The smallest one*, `intake-2026-09-08.md`, second
section). The survey side names them for the first time; the name is his.

## The convergence

**RULED 7 September 2026 — "one act, two records."** The survey side ends
with an up-person putting a stone down first at the line — the people's
manners, learned by watching; the deep side ends with Stone-First, on the
line, hearing the ground announce a stranger. Each strand records the act
from its side. Neither records what was said, if anything was.

What the ruling commits the season to:

- **The up-people learn the manners by watching**, not by being told. Nobody
  crosses the line to teach them; the Told's strand cannot show the learning
  and does not try.
- **The act is a stone moved first.** The same act the youngest performed in
  C02, from the other side of the line — the story's one gesture, made
  twice.
- **The convergence merges information, not the cast.** The Told do not go
  up; the up-people do not come down. The line stays where Stone-First put
  it. No conversation is recorded, and the season does not need one.
- **What the up-people do next is settled**: they ask, in the only grammar
  that reaches the ground. The survey's later question — whether a telling
  reaches the Archive, and how — is a different season's, and the Fliade
  entry's observational sentence stays as it is until then.

The three shapes are kept in the intake as the record of the choice.
