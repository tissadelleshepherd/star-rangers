# Intake, 20 September 2026

## Mysteries, endings, and two that fell flat — a direction, given in chat

Given in chat after the day's work on the photography games, unprompted.
Verbatim:

> Most detective stories seem ordinary, almost mundane. I prefer something
> more mysterious, existential and enigmatic. I thought the Hydrogen
> Sonata, by Iain M. Banks, fell flat at the end as did the The Lady in
> Lake as the end of the Witcher series.

### Read against what stands

- **The shadow-shaped rule (2026-09-08)** already says what a mystery in
  this record is: a real thing seen through a gap that cannot be crossed,
  unsolved because unsolvable, marked and kept rather than closed. It says
  nothing yet about endings.
- **The influences note** already holds that Banks's Minds work and his
  endings do not (2026-09-08). This names the ending.

### The reading put in chat

Both named endings fail the same way: the mystery is answered and the
answer is smaller than the question. In *The Hydrogen Sonata* the
founding lie is found, confirmed, and nothing turns on it. In *The Lady
of the Lake* the ambiguity is real but arrives by exhaustion, the threads
cut rather than tied or deliberately left, the register shifting at the
close. An ordinary detective story is a machine for reducing mystery to
information; what is wanted is a story where the question stays larger
than any answer and the ending is a recognition rather than a reveal.
Three marks of the books that manage it: the mystery is a condition, not
a puzzle (*Solaris*, *Roadside Picnic*); the ending changes how the
beginning reads (*The Book of the New Sun*, *The Affirmation*); the cost
is paid on the page (*The Dispossessed*). Touchstones offered, not his.

### Offered, and his word

An ending test for the story bible was offered, to be drafted for his
approval. **Ruled the same night, verbatim *Approved*.** Realized as the
bullet *Endings are recognitions, not reveals* in `CLAUDE.md`, beside the
shadow-shaped rule, with the test in one sentence: **the ending must
change how the beginning reads.**

## The Archive: a spoiler, or an additional point of view? — a question, later the same night

Given in chat after the ending test merged. Verbatim:

> How do I keep track of progressive revelation in the narrative, compared
> with lore? Should certain pages be hidden until revealed by the reading
> the narrative or does that complicate the reading structure or invite
> assumptions - in other words is the Archive a spoiler or an additional
> POV?

### Read against what stands

- `story-bible/` is the only place spoilers live (settled 2026-07-25) and
  the publication test is *does this tell a reader something the story has
  not told them yet?*; `scripts/list-canon-facts.js` is the spoiler test's
  instrument, informational, never a gate.
- Sen's doctrine marks absences rather than filling them; the shadow-shaped
  rule keeps the unsolvable unsolved; the reading tiers are not gates of
  protection and a visibility-by-progress gate would be a new kind.
- The Archive is a public record in-universe, and the Cascade notes hold
  that it refuses to narrow its readers.

### The reading put in chat

The Archive is an additional point of view, not a spoiler, as long as it
knows only what the record's own institutions could know at the record's
present. Revelation is of two kinds: what the Archive holds and a character
does not (dramatic irony, welcome), and what nobody in the record holds
until the prose makes it (shadow-shaped, kept in the story bible until the
chapter lands). Hiding pages until read was argued against on four counts:
it leaks through feeds, sitemap and the LLM export; the hidden set is
itself a map of the plot; it breaks the Archive's premise as a public
record; and it adds a second gate axis across the tiers.

### Three shapes put, and his word

1. **Keep the Archive open and track revelation as revision** — `revealed_by`
   and `revised_by` on Archive pages, a ledger derived from the corpus, a
   check that no declared chapter is missing. Recommended.
2. A reader-side read-order line, nothing hidden. An add-on if readers ask.
3. Hard gating by chapters read. Refused, for the four counts above.

**Ruled the same night, verbatim: *First shape approved, eventually all
lore will be revealed by the prose.*** Realized as the rule *The Archive is
open, and knows only what the record knows* in `CLAUDE.md`, the two fields
in `lib/content-schema.js` on lore, glossary and codex,
`scripts/revelation-ledger.js` (its `--check` in `npm test`), and this
record. No page carries `revealed_by` yet: declaring which chapter first
establishes each of the 308 Archive pages is authorship, and the ledger's
unrevealed list is the worklist his second clause names.
