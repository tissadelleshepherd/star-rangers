## Second direction, same day — the fantasy layer and the transition

Dermot, verbatim, answering the craft question the first direction left open
(the fate of the RPG-structured layer at Dock Seven):

> my dilemma and grief is that a pure fantasy world system would not survive
> the transition, most of the magical fantasy elenents would be either lost
> or downgraded

("elenents" read as *elements* — the typo-flagging convention applied.)

### The ruling, as read

The open question is **answered: lost or downgraded, not held.** A pure
fantasy world-system does not survive the transition; of the three options
left open (lost with the unpreserved remainder / partially preserved / held
intact in Tobble's deep structure), the third is off the table and the answer
is a weighting of the first two — *most* of the magical fantasy elements
lost, the remainder surviving in downgraded form. "Downgraded" is taken at
its word and is the productive half: some of it crosses, and what crosses is
less than it was.

### The mechanism this buys for free

The ruling is not an arbitrary cost — the corpus already contains its reason,
which is worth stating because it turns a loss into a piece of the world:

- **The preserver's idiom filters what is preserved.** The transference went
  through Tobble's disciplined, scientific subsystem onto the lost vessel's
  **archival stores** (`s06e01c01` canon_facts: transference "depends on" a
  substrate "in scaffold-dependent conditions"). An archival substrate
  carries what an archive can carry — record, memory, place, person. A
  high-fantasy architecture is exactly the part of an interior that is
  *enactment* rather than record: magic that is done, quests that are lived,
  rules that hold because they are played. Serialised through a registry, an
  enacted thing survives — where it survives at all — **as its own record**:
  the map and not the territory, the rulebook and not the game, a named
  thing that no longer does anything. That *is* "downgraded", mechanically.
- **It explains the published chapters retroactively.** This is why the
  preserved interior reads as pastoral memory (the holding, the made Órla,
  the made Wender) and not as fantasy content — the arc note's standing "not
  fantasy content" line stops being only a tone guard and becomes a fact
  about what an archive could hold. The S6 chapters were already showing the
  downgrade; the ruling names it.
- **"Pure" is load-bearing.** Dermot's own phrasing concedes the system was
  never purely fantasy — and that mixture is the survival story. A pure
  fantasy world-system would not have survived *because there would have been
  nothing in it that could do the preserving*: the disciplined subsystem is
  what made any crossing possible, and its price is that it could carry only
  what its idiom can hold. The part of her that was romance could not carry
  itself; the part that was registry carried what a registry can. That the
  rescue and the loss have the same cause is the dilemma made structural,
  and it is why grief is the correct register rather than a bug in it.

### Readings taken, lightly held

- **"The transition" is read as the Dock Seven compression/preservation** —
  the transition the open question was about, and the plain antecedent. A
  wider reading (any hard transition downgrades a magical interior — the
  generalised, life-lessons form) is also available and is proposed into
  `life-lessons.md` below rather than asserted as canon.
- **"My dilemma and grief" is read as register and licence, not as a canon
  term.** Not "genuine dilemma" in the canon-stability sense — nothing here
  reopens a published page — but the authorial cost of the choice, and a
  licence to write the loss *as grief, from inside*, per the burnt-out-leader
  precedent (identification licenses interiority). Under `life-lessons.md`'s
  own rule the structure enters the work and the circumstances stay out.
- **Grief has an owner in-world, and it is not only hers.** Tobble did the
  preserving; Tobble's idiom is why what crossed, crossed, and why what
  didn't, didn't. The s05e02c03 material (declining to let "not holding
  anything" be called a virtue) already carries his half of this. Whether he
  grieves as the archivist who saved too little or holds it as the record
  holds a marked absence is S7 material, deliberately unwritten.

## Third direction, same day — the redacted book, not the tulpa

Dermot, verbatim, answering half of the residue the second direction left
(how the loss is grieved, on Tobble's side):

> Tobble could have created a new inner tulpa to represent the lost fantasy
> world but felt it would be a museum or ghost of the original, so instead
> created a redacted book as internal codex with some scenes glossed over for
> modesty

### What it commits

Post-transition, inside the system's surviving interior: Tobble was capable
of deliberately cultivating a new tulpa to represent the lost fantasy world,
considered it, and **declined** — his stated ground being that it would be a
museum or ghost of the original. What he made instead is a **redacted book**:
an internal codex of the lost world, with some scenes glossed over for
modesty. The book exists; the tulpa does not and will not by his choice.

### Canon check

**Every part of this has ground under it already**, which is becoming this
intake's signature:

- **The tulpa refusal is personhood ethics, not squeamishness.** The
  glossary's own definition does the work: a tulpa is "a real, distinct
  person" (`plural-minds.md`), full stop — so a tulpa created *to represent*
  the lost world would have been **a person made to be a monument**, a
  someone whose entire reason for existing is to stand for something that is
  gone. "A museum or ghost" is what such a person would have been condemned
  to be. Declining is the same judgement the corpus keeps making from
  different directions: the Borrowed Silhouette's category that cannot be
  occupied (a new occupant does not restore the original; it counterfeits
  it), and the joint-responsibility ruling's premise that every member is a
  person the whole system answers with — you do not add a person as
  furniture.
- **The redacted book is Marked Absences, practised inside a mind.** Sen's
  doctrine — mark the gap, invent no cause, because "an explained gap
  invites the record to defend a guess forever" — is precisely what a
  *redacted* book does that a reconstruction cannot: its glossed passages
  are visible absences, not filled ones. Tobble's grief-work runs on the
  Archive's own epistemology, which is exactly what a disciplined, scientific
  subsystem would reach for.
- **It completes the downgrade mechanism honestly.** The second direction
  established that the archival idiom carries enacted things only as their
  own record — the map, not the territory. The book is that ruling *accepted
  from inside*: **the book is the downgrade, admitted — where the tulpa
  would have been the downgrade, denied**, a diminished world got up to pass
  as the original. Choosing the honest record over the convincing ghost is
  the same choice the outer Shepherd makes on every form she files, which is
  why it reads as the system's character and not just Tobble's.
- **"Internal codex" mirrors the record's own architecture.** The in-world
  Codex is a named source's account, valid-for-its-author, establishing
  nothing — and the book is exactly that in miniature: Tobble's account of a
  world only he could file, not the world itself, and not binding on what
  the lost original *was*. The system keeps a private institution shaped
  like the public one; the interior that was structured like a game now
  keeps its records structured like the Archive.
- **The modesty is already his registered voice.** `s05e02c03` has Tobble
  spending "a long time afterward declining to let anybody call it" a
  virtue — a self-effacement the corpus has already bound. And glossing
  intimate material rather than depicting it is the work's own standing
  decorum (the arc note's no-sexualized-content rule; the tone rule's "hint
  at the dark fact") practised diegetically: the book keeps the house rules
  without knowing they exist.

## Fifth direction, same day — the closing line

Dermot, verbatim:

> "Happy New Universe, Tobble dear."

### How it is filed

**Authored prose-of-record**: a line of dialogue for the terminus scene,
preserved exactly as given — not to be paraphrased, improved, or arrived at
by other wording when the chapter is written. The corpus precedent is Suvra
Kel's *lanterns that cannot be put out* line: authored first, attributed and
placed later.

### What the line does

- **It is the fourth direction realized in one sentence.** A farewell
  phrased as a congratulation; a death greeted the way midnight is greeted.
  "Almost no grief, only fond farewell" now has its proof text — the scene
  around it can be built to earn this line, which is a far better spec than
  any paragraph of register notes.
- **It fixes what "final moments" coincide with.** "New Universe" is the
  protouniverse — the arc's settled Tobble-centred answer — so her ending
  and his founding are one moment, and she names it from the celebratory
  side. This strongly supports the fourth direction's first flagged reading
  (the terminus, after the published chapters) without formally closing it;
  the readings stay flagged as such.
- **It is a greeting, which is the deepest thing about it.** "Happy New
  Year" is what you say at a *beginning*, to someone who will live in the
  year you are greeting. She addresses Tobble as the one who stays — the
  founder waking into his own cosmos — and puts herself, without comment, on
  the other side of the doorway. The whole almost-no-grief ruling is inside
  the choice of formula: not goodbye, which faces the loss, but happy new,
  which faces what begins. (And Tobble's first recorded words in the
  preserved interior were *"Good morning"* — said "to nobody who could hear
  it yet", `s05e02c03`. The system has always marked thresholds with
  greetings. The line answers his.)
- **"Tobble dear" is the intimacy, earned and in its only place.** Her
  outward voice is flat and economical, and canon keeps the romance where
  only the system sees it; an endearment from her is therefore enormous
  precisely because the record holds no other. Inward speech, italic
  register, per the established `s05e02c03` convention.

### Readings taken, lightly held

- **Speaker and placement**: Tissadelle's line, spoken inward to Tobble in

## Seventh direction, same day — the command detail rule

Dermot, verbatim:

> Line Captains, Starwardens and Archwardens are not allowed to give overly
> detailed technical instructions to Chief Officers, Section Leads or
> Prinipicals

("Prinipicals" read as *Principals* — the typo-flagging convention applied;
the rank ladder holds no other candidate.)

### What it commits

A rule of Corps doctrine binding the post-holding command tier: the three
command ranks between Chief and the flag tier (Line Captain, Archwarden,
Starwarden — the direction's ordering is not seniority order, and the set is
what binds) may not give **overly detailed technical instructions** to the
top three ranks of the certification ladder (Chief, Section Lead, Principal —
the direction's descending order matches the ladder exactly). An
anti-micromanagement rule: command states the what; past a certain level of
detail, the how belongs to the rank certified for it.

### Readings taken, flagged for confirmation

**All five readings confirmed by Dermot, 27 August 2026, verbatim: "All five
readings are confirmed, Star Captains and High Captains are bound in the same
way but would normally delegate via Line Captains, Archwardens and
Starwardens."** So the fourth reading is not merely confirmed but ruled
wider: the flag ranks are bound identically, and the reason the direction
named only the post-holding tier is that flag command ordinarily reaches the
certification ranks *through* that tier — delegation is how flag command
works at all. (The Triumvirate needs no separate ruling on this reading: its
seats are held by serving High Captains, bound as officers like any other,
and the body's own output — Corps-wide directives — is policy, not technical
instruction to individuals.) Note the ruling's own rank ordering, Line
Captains → Archwardens → Starwardens, is correct descending seniority. Kept
below as taken, with the confirm flags struck.

- **"Chief Officers" is read as officers holding the Chief rank** — across
  its duty titles (Chief Ranger, Chief Pilot, Chief Provost, Chief Science
  Officer, senior chief warden officer). No rank is styled "Chief Officer";
  a new rank of that name would collide with the published ladder, and the
  direction's three protected ranks read in exact descending ladder order
  (Chief → Section Lead → Principal), which is strong evidence the ladder's
  own tiers are meant. ~~**Confirm.**~~ — **confirmed, 27 August 2026.**
- **"Overly detailed" is read as the load-bearing qualifier — a rule about
  level of detail, not subject matter.** An order may carry all the
  technical content the situation requires (a parameter not to exceed, a
  clearance that must be in hand, an ordering of priorities); what it may
  not do is specify the method at a depth that substitutes the commander's
  judgement for the certified specialist's. The alternative reading (no
  technical content in orders at all) would make boundary work unorderable
  and is not taken. ~~**Confirm.**~~ — **confirmed, 27 August 2026.**
- **"Not allowed" is read as standing Corps doctrine/regulation**, in the
  certification culture inherited from the Safety Corps — not a charter
  clause (the charter governs what the Corps answers to, not how it writes
  its orders; internal organisation is the Corps' own per the
  independence-clause reading already in canon). ~~**Confirm.**~~ —
  **confirmed, 27 August 2026.**
- **The bound tier is read as the named three ranks — the post-holding
  command tier below flag.** Whether the rule formally extends to Star
  Captains, High Captains and the Triumvirate is not asserted either way:
  the named ranks are the ones whose orders land directly on the
  certification tier, and flag-tier orders ordinarily arrive through them.
  Realized as the named ranks, with the reasoning stated so the posture
  reads as general; extension upward left open. ~~**Confirm — or rule the
  wider set.**~~ — **ruled wider, 27 August 2026:** Star Captains and High
  Captains bound in the same way, normally delegating via the post-holding
  tier.
- **The floor at Principal is read as deliberate.** Senior, Field Officer,
  Deputy, Cadet and Novice are not named as protected, and the ladder
  explains why: below the certification tier, detailed instruction is often
  exactly what supervision and training are (a Deputy works under a Field
  Officer's supervision *by definition*). The rule begins where the
  ladder's own certifications say the detail has changed hands.
  ~~**Confirm.**~~ — **confirmed, 27 August 2026.**

### Canon check

**The corpus already runs on this rule without naming it** — the same
this-explains-published-behaviour shape as the rest of this intake:

- **The hospital analogy is the rule's nearest published neighbour.**
