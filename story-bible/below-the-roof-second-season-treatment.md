  next — whether a telling reaches the Archive, and how — is a different

  arriving; the line stays where it is and no telling goes up.
- **One cost, named in the intake and accepted with the ruling.** A
  Membrane Shadow exists only at Class III (`membrane-shadows.md`), so the
  boundary feature near Fliade now reaches Class III in brief windows and
  falls back. That is a new fact about the feature and not a name for it:
  shadows are telemetry about the Interval, never about what lies across
  it, and the Fliade entry's *what that feature is has not been settled*
  stands. The windows are also why the mysteries are *several* — one
  window, one shadow, one telling.
- **First instance of the standing direction.** No culprit, because a
  Membrane Shadow is not a being; nothing solved, because nothing crossed
  and nobody was there for the part that matters; every case marked and
  kept on Sen's doctrine rather than closed. The detective refinement of
  the same direction (eighteenth section) does not touch this season — there
  is no bureau on Fliade and the up-people already are the Rangers — except
  in one shape put as a choice below: a planetary survey that has to call in
  the Corps' own boundary side.
- **Tier and tone.** Children's tier with the thread; tiers are reading
  level, not content ratings (4 September), and the Told's own test for a
  claim — *who was there* — hands the child reader the exact tool the
  season uses. A wariness, never a terror; hint, do not show. The shapes
  are never described past what a sensorium built of warmth and stone can
  register, which is the guardrail doing the season's work for it.
- **The register.** Dermot, the same evening (nineteenth section): *the
  mystery and enigmatic quality of Sapphire & Steel without the darker
  elements of horror*. Taken: the confined ordinary setting, the small
  stable wrongness, the anomaly never explained, the sparing outsider who
  is called in, stillness as texture. Left: the menace aimed at the people
  in the room, and the anomaly as an enemy — a shadow is not a being and
  wants nothing. The Told's tellings and the survey's log are both that
  kind of room.
- **No guns-and-spaceships, no space western** (twentieth section). The
  boundary officer, if called in, comes with a gravimeter and a question;
  nobody on either side of the line carries a weapon, and no chapter ends
  because something was shot. The season's only action is a stone set down.
- **The trope default.** An anthology of unsolved cases is a known shape.
  Here the cases stay unsolved because they are unsolvable, which is the
  record's position and not a withholding; no chapter may hint that an
  answer is being kept from the reader.

## The season in one line

Several shapes come to the warm that have no warmth and move no stone, and
both peoples keep a record of what they could not settle.

## Strand A — the deep side (the Told)

**What the shadows are to the Told.** The two things a Pandoid is certain of
without trying — the shape of warmth in a place, the shape of stone under
the body — fail at once. A shape stands in the warm and has no warmth. It
moves and moves no stone. By the people's own rules it is not a stranger,
because a stranger is announced by the ground, and it is not a person,
because it never asks; and the manners have no third case. Reading (a) is
already theirs (a shadow is a thing that moves); reading (b) arrives with
the first one (a shape without warmth); and the fullest form the intake
named — the people's word covers any shape that moves without moving a
stone — is put below as a choice.

**The season's shape: several tellings, one per window.** Each chapter is
one shadow and the telling of it, and each telling ends where Went-Round's
finding in *Someone to Ask* puts it: unsettled, because nobody was there for
the part that matters and nobody can be. The record keeps the readings and
settles none. That is not a trick played on the reader; it is the Told's
own discipline and the Archive's, and the child who has read Season 11
already knows the rule.

**Candidate shadows** — authorship, his; five offered so that three to five
can be chosen, and none of them is required:

1. **The shape at the seep.** A shape stands where the first warm seep is
   told to be — the place every telling begins — and it is not warm. It does
   not answer a stone. When the working time ends it is not there, and no
   stone was moved by its going.
2. **The stone that moved with nobody there.** The ground announces a
   stranger, as the manners require, and nobody comes. Canon supplies the
   mechanism without the Told ever learning it: the gradient near a strong
   shadow loads structure, and a loaded rock at the edge of the silt can
   shift. To the people it is the manners done by nobody, which is the most
   unsettling thing the season can say and needs no picture.
3. **The shape that was somebody.** A small one says the shape was
   Stone-First-Who-Waited, standing on the line, while Stone-First-Who-Waited
   is in the warm telling. The record's own position governs: a resemblance
   is the witness's pattern-matching first, and never a double. The Told
   settle it by *who was there*, and the answer is that the small one was,
   and Stone-First was not, and both are true.
4. **The shape that was not dark.** A brightening, in the place where the
   light has no cycle — the benevolent form the record allows. It is the
   one the people are least wary of and the one the season should be most
   careful with: not a visitor, not a blessing, a neighbour's imprint,
   received by a people who have no word for a neighbour.
5. **The shape that went through the squeeze.** It passes where no adult
   can, without passing — a shadow needs no gap. The Told's one certainty
   about the roof (it cannot be reached) meets a thing for which the roof
   is not there.

**Heads.** Stone-First-Who-Waited, Carried-It-Sleeping (older by the
season's own count of tellings), Went-Round; and, put as a choice, a new
small one for whom the shadows are the first thing they were not told —
the youth rule (mind note, question 7) applied a second time. His.

**What the strand must not do.** Explain the shadows. Name the Interval,
the membrane, the neighbour. Let a shape be described past what warmth and
stone register. Let anyone go up.

## Strand B — the survey side (the up-people)

**What the shadows are to the survey.** On the imager, a silhouette with an
optical signature and no thermal return; on the sound channel, nothing; on
the mass channel, nothing. The record says that combination *is* a Membrane
Shadow almost by definition — but only to a reader who knows the boundary
literature, and Nakagawa's party is a planetary survey. The language note's
two failure modes are the strand's: **filed as noise** (an imager fault, for
weeks), and **confidence entered as certainty** (the temptation, once
someone has read the right page, to write the answer into the log). Nakagawa
has already refused the second once, on the third reading; the strand lets
that habit hold.

**The instrument — a choice, his.** The boundary-proximity requirements are
in force on Fliade and the Fliade entry says nothing about instruments.
(a) The party carries a cross-membrane gravimeter under those requirements,
dormant in a rack, and its log has been reading a small stable wrongness
for months before anyone reads the log — the Forty-Second Discrepancy's
shape, on a roof; *recommended*, because it is the pattern the record
already owns and it puts the survey's blindness where Season 11 put it, in
the file nobody reads. (b) No gravimeter, and the party cannot know what it
is looking at until someone who can is on the roof.

**Calling in the Corps' own boundary side — a choice, his.** Either way,
what stands on the imager is Threshold's business and not a planetary
survey's, and the Survey Corps has a boundary side that reads Class III
for a living. (a) A boundary officer is called in and comes to the roof —
the standing direction's *calling in the Rangers* beat, here inside the
Rangers, as a survey lead asking for the one colleague whose page this is;
the officer is a new head, and the strand's second question becomes *how
much of what this person knows may be written in a log the Told will never
read.* (b) Nobody is called in; Nakagawa reads the literature alone, and
the season keeps the party at three. *Recommended: (a)*, because it gives
the survey side a person who knows what a shadow is and cannot say what
this one is, which is the season's whole discipline in one character.

**What the strand must not do.** Go down. See the deep's shapes; it sees
only what reaches the line and the unit. Explain the Told. Decide what the
feature is — the officer's reading, whatever it is, is filed as a reading
with confidence stated, and the Fliade entry's sentence does not change.

## The convergence

**One thing, two records** — the 7 September shape applied to a non-event.
Put as choices:

1. **A shadow at the line, seen from both sides.** The party knocks at the
   start of a shift; the imager shows a shape standing on the line with no
   thermal return; below, Stone-First-Who-Waited is on the line and the
   shape is beside them and is not warm. The party's stone is answered —
   Stone-First moves a rock for the up-people, as before — and the shape
   answers nothing and moves nothing. Both records carry the same facts and
   agree that nothing crossed. *Recommended*: it makes the season's one
   gesture a third time, and the thing it is made beside is the thing that
   cannot make it.
2. **No convergence.** The season is an anthology; the strands never meet;
   each ends on its own unsettled telling. Cheaper, and it forgoes the
   thread's one proven move.
3. **The officer's arrival, read from below.** The convergence is a new
   up-person at the line — the boundary officer — and the Told's record of
   a fourth up-person who knocks like the others and stands longer.
