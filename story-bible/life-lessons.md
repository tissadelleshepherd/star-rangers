## Life Lessons To Explore In Fiction

**How real experience is allowed to enter the work: generalise, then fictionalise.**
Take the *structure* of what happened — the mechanism, the asymmetry, the cost —
and leave the circumstances behind.

---

- Every virtue has a liability point.
- Wisdom is resource allocation under uncertainty.
- Intelligence without judgment is dangerous.
- Some problems are not communication problems.
- Energy is more precious than time.
- Discernment comes from experience, not advice.
- Humility can be confidence that needs no external validation, and distrusts flattery.
- Leadership fails by depletion more often than by corruption.
- Most of what was magical does not survive a hard transition; it is lost, or arrives downgraded.
- Better an honest record with gaps than a reconstruction pretending to be the thing.
- A life laid down well ends in farewell; the grief belongs to those who stay.
- A bound worth having comes from outside the thing it bounds; self-restraint alone hardens or is captured.
- Passion for one's work is a recent expectation; the older virtues are diligence, fidelity and craft, and the watch is kept on the days nothing is wrong.
- The work can be contracted for; the feeling cannot. Tiredness is ordinary and depletion is evidence about the role, so the honest answers to a role that drains past recovery are to redesign it or to leave it.
- A clarification is the same judge explaining; an appeal needs a different judge with the power to move the outcome, the record shown to the subject, and a recorded result. Commentary never moves standing, and a process that offers the first and calls it the second lets a finding carry a grade it has not survived.
- What does not kill someone makes them stronger only if recovery is possible. The same injury can leave a weakness that recurs, an immunity that holds, or both at once; which it is depends on whether there was time and ground to recover on, not on the injury. Two corollaries: the strength is specific, against the thing that nearly killed, and is paid for somewhere else; and the weakness that persists is most often not in the tissue but in the record of it, recurring as doubt. Not a human lesson: it holds for anything that survives with its continuity intact, repairs itself from finite means and carries a record of the harm forward (a body, a committee, a people, a soil, a hull, a mind), and it stops below the threshold of a self-record. What varies between peoples is the record: a human carries the harm as a retold doubt, a made mind as a filed and unforgetting error, the Told as a telling that may become a name, the Krenyi as something the centuries may wear away.
- A shared thing ages at the rate of whatever it was built to stand in for. What stands in for a fact that holds keeps its place; what stands in for a convenience of its moment is worked around within a few years, and the kindest end is to mark it superseded, with the reason, rather than let the next person find out.
