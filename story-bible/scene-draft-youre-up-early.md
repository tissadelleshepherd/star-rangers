::::: scene 1

::: pov tobble

The universe was old enough now that he had stopped counting mornings and
started counting what they did, and what they did was stay.

It was still *proto*. He had read the entry, once, in the ship's stores,
back when the stores were the only thing standing and he was using them for
something no manual had foreseen; he had read it again since, at leisure,
and found that the word meant exactly what it said and nothing consoling.
Nothing in here had learned to hold a zone. Nobody had come to teach them.
The jurisdiction sat with offices he would never see, in a universe he was
no longer inside, and he was in no hurry about any of it. A founder without
a Council is not an Authority, the entry said, and he had noted that he had
never asked to be one and was not going to start by being one badly.

What a universe is before it has law, the entry also said, is roles. He
had not needed telling. Past the holding, where the morning had not yet
decided what it was made of, the young ground had organised itself the
only way a thing built from an interior knows how — named places with
rules, offices held by whoever held them, a court before a code — and it
looked, from the low wall, like the thing they had lost. Not the magic.
None of that had crossed, and none of it was here. The *shape*. He had
read that every one of them looks like this while it is forming, that
survey officers write it up with a certain embarrassment, and that it
passes; he had filed the resemblance under structure and not under grief,
and had checked the filing twice. It was still hard to look at some
mornings. He looked at it anyway. A founder who will not look at his own
ground is not keeping a record, he is keeping a story, and he had promised
somebody he would not.

So there was a holding, and a low wall with the third course shifted, and a
kettle, and a flock somewhere below, and a book, and an edge. The edge was
his now. It had been warm from her side for years. From this side it was
only the place where the morning stopped, and he had walked it the way he
walked everything, at length, without visible effort, until he knew where
it went and where it did not.

The morning came up grey. Then gold. Then there was someone at the edge.

He saw her before he let himself see her, which was the order he had taught
himself for exactly this — because he had known, from the first entry he
made in his own hand, that a morning might one day come up with her in it,
and that if it did the first thing he would have to file was what he had
wanted. Survey Corps put it flatly, and he had never found a flatter way to
put it: *it looked like someone I know* is an observation about the witness.
He was the witness. He wanted it to be her more than he had wanted anything
since the ship came apart, and he wrote that down, in the place in his head
where he kept the things too large for a page, before he looked.

Then he looked.

The hair was right. It was down. That was the second thing he filed, and he
filed it before the face, because the face was going to be right — a face is
surface, and surface was on the record, and anything that had ever read the
record could get a face right. The hair was down, and long, and dressed with
something that caught the light, and she had worn it tied back for
thirty-eight years because a Ranger at an instrument does not have hair in
her eyes, and she had been at an instrument, one way or another, for most
of them.

And the coat was gone. She was dressed for the questing layer. He knew the
dress. He knew it from a page in his own book, and the page was under a
gloss, and it was under a gloss for a reason, and the reason had not
changed because something at the edge was wearing what was under it. The
record does not describe the dress. It notes only that the coat would have
been the easiest thing in all of her to get right — it was on every file
she had ever filed, correctly dressed, claiming nothing not already on the
record — and that the thing at the edge had not chosen the coat. It had
chosen the one thing of hers that had never once been seen from outside.

That was the first finding. He did not stop there, because one finding is
an opinion.

"Tobble," it said. "I came home."

She had never in her life announced herself. She sent word before she came,
always had, and if she could not send word she did not come, and when she
had come to the holding unannounced at first light with the look of
somebody who had walked a long way in the dark, it had been because she had
not decided to, and her mother had known it on the step. He had been there.
He had been there for all of it.

He said what her mother had said, in the voice her mother had used, because
the family trade was the only test anyone had ever devised that could not
be passed by knowing the answer.

*You're up early.*

And he watched to see whether the answer came back the way it comes from a
person, or the way it comes from a room that has been waiting to be asked.

"I know," it said. "I know you missed me. I'm here now."

It had answered the question before the one he asked. It was warm, and it
was fluent, and it was pitched with great exactness at the place he had
been standing a moment ago, and he noted the interval — a beat, a fraction
of one — the way he had once noted forty seconds on a chronometer that
everyone else had learned to stop noticing. Tracking, the doctrine said.
Never the performance. The performance can be learned from the outside.

He gave it a third chance, because the book requires three.

*Do the arithmetic,* he said.

"I don't need to," it said. "I'm certain."

She had done it twice. He had done it three times. In thirty-eight years he
had never once heard her say *I'm certain* about anything she had not
measured, and had heard her decline to say it about a great many things she
had, because certainty performed for a room was a claim past evidence, and
she did not file those. It had her face and her hair and the one dress it
should not have had, and it could not do the arithmetic, and it did not
know that not doing it was the tell.

"There's my cat," it said, fondly, as she never had — because he had been
called that in a room at intake once, by a reviewing officer with a form,
and she had corrected it, one sentence, precisely, and moved on, and had
never in all the years since needed to say it to him, because a thing that
is already true between two people does not get said. "Come here. It's all
right. You did all this for me. Let me in. It's mine. I am your Empress."

He felt the ground take the word.

That was the thing about a world made of roles: a title is not a boast in
it, it is a claim to a seat, and a seat with nobody in it will lean toward
whatever says its name. Out past the holding, where nothing had learned
to hold a zone yet, something leaned. Not far. Enough to measure. A young
universe has no law to check a claim against and does the only thing it
can, which is listen, and it had heard a word that in every court it was
built to resemble means *the one the rest are held by*.

Except that it had not been built to resemble every court. It had been
built from one, and in that one the word meant something else.

In the world she kept — the one with the party and the quests and the
named places, the one under the glosses now — the titles ran the wrong
way. He had never asked why and she had never said. The grandest words
went to the smallest jobs: the one who fetched, the one who carried, the
one who held the horses and nothing else, was styled with everything the
court had, and the higher a role stood the plainer its name got, until
the office at the top of all of it was called something so ordinary that
the record declines to repeat it, because said aloud out here it would
sound like a joke and in there it never once was. *Empress* was the
bottom. It was the first rung, the errand rank, the word for whoever had
just arrived and held nothing yet. Every one of them had been Empress
once. Nobody had wanted to stay one.

So the thing at the edge, reaching for the one title it had read off the
surface of a lost world, had announced itself — in the only grammar that
world had ever spoken, to the only person left who spoke it — as the
least thing there. It had claimed the top of every court it resembled
and the bottom of the one it was made from, in a single word, and had
thereby said, as plainly as if it had filed it, that it had never once
been inside.

*Then you would be the least of us,* Tobble said. *In there, that is
what it means. You would fetch and carry and hold nothing. Nobody who
had been inside would ask for it.*

The ground stopped leaning. He had wondered whether it would; he had it
on the record now that it did — that a claim the founder answers in the
world's own grammar has nothing to seat itself in, because the young
ground, listening, had heard a thing ask for the top and been told, by
the one voice it was built to acknowledge, what the word was for. He
suspected, for the first time in thirty-eight years, why she had run the
titles backwards: a party that fields whoever fits the encounter cannot
afford a seat worth fighting over, so she had put all the gold on the
rung nobody minded holding and left the top too plain to want. He
declined to file the suspicion. She had kept the rule without a reason,
and a rule with a reason is a rule that can be argued with, and she had
never once let him argue with that one.

*You did all this for me.* She had never once in her life told anyone what
a thing they had done meant. Not a Ranger, not a room, not him. She had
gone to the far side of a door without deciding what it meant for the one
who stayed, and had said so with a greeting, and had left the meaning where
she left every meaning, which was with whoever had to live in it.

*It's mine.* The house had been his since he saved it. She had said so.
She had lived in it on loan, correctly, with the inventory kept current for
the day it was handed over, and she had handed it over, and she had not
looked back to check that he was keeping it properly, because she had never
in her life checked on a thing she had already trusted.

And then, because it had read the record and the record had the book in
it, it said the last thing, and the last thing was the one that made him
sure — not because it was cruel, it was not cruel, nothing was performing
cruelty any more than anything was performing kindness — but because it
was the exact question she had read past, twice, at reading pace, and had
not asked.

"What did you put under the glosses?"

He had declined before he had a reason. He was aware of that, and it did
not embarrass him; a creature that declines without needing a reason is
reading the membership and not the shape, and he had been reading the
membership since the hair. But a book with no reasons in it is a diary, so
he had gone and got the reasons, all of them, in order, and now he had
them, and there was only one thing the doctrine said to do with them, and
it was not to fight. There was nothing to fight. There was nobody home.

He named what he knew.

He said — inward, at the edge of everything, in the plain voice that had
once said *hold still* — that he had wanted her back. He said it in full,
because a want left vague is a thing that can be fed on, and he had read
what feeds on wants and how, and he was not going to leave one lying about
at the edge of a universe for anything to borrow against. He had wanted
her back. He had wanted it every morning since the morning she said the
thing she said. That was the debt, stated. It was true, it was his, and
saying it did not change it and was not meant to.

Then he said what she had been. Not the file — the file was surface, and
surface was what stood in front of him. He said the declining. She did
not volunteer. She did not perform. She did not decide what a thing meant
for anyone who had to carry it. She did not ask what was under a gloss.
She tied her hair back. She did the arithmetic twice. She said *good
morning* to a threshold and *happy new universe* to a door, and she had
never once, on either side of an edge, asked anybody to let her in. And
in her world the grand names went to the small jobs, and she had kept
it so.

And then he said what stood at the edge was not her, and that he had it on
record, and that enough was enough.

*No,* Tobble said — gently, at the edge of everything, in a vocabulary he
had learned by watching something with one word in it stand at a warm edge
for years and never once try to open the door it was holding.

It did not go.

He had not expected it to. A thing like that is not driven off; the record
is clear that it is only ever starved, and starving is slow, and the
purpose it was borrowing against was his and had been named and had not
thereby ceased to exist — he still wanted her back; naming a want does not
retire it, it only stops it being available. So it stood at the edge in
the dress it should not have had, and it said warm things to where he had
been, and it went on saying them, beautifully, to a listener it was not
listening to, and he sat in the middle of the standing part of everything,
being a cat, and watched it the way he had watched her for thirty-eight
years, which was entirely, and without appearing to.

The door of the house did not open. That was worth noting, and he noted
it, and noted beside it that memory is not a witness and the made Órla
came out for her daughter because that is what the memory was of. The
others had kept indoors. He had not asked them to. They had read the edge
before he had; some of them had been reading edges longer than he had been
a cat.

It changed what it wore, over the mornings. Nothing is obliged to stay
what it was filed as; he had read that too. One morning it had the coat —
navy, high-collared, the cut she had worn to every board that ever misread
her — and it had got the coat wrong in the one way anything that had only
ever seen a coat from outside would get it wrong. There was rank on the
cloth. Gold, worked into the shoulder and the cuff, stars on the breast,
where the Corps puts a plain tab and nothing else, because a correctly
dressed Ranger has claimed nothing not already on the record and the
record does not go on the garment. And it was cut for a room. It stood
the way a person stands who has been told the room grades on presence,
and she had never once in her life dressed to be looked at, only to be
checked. He filed the coat under the dress. Same finding, twice. Surface,
got exactly right, by something that could not tell what the surface was
for.

It dimmed, after that. Not much. Not enough to file as going. It was
still there at the edge when he went in, and it would be there when he
came out, saying *I came home* to the place he had been sitting, and he
found that he could bear that, in the specific way a person can bear a
thing that has been named and has nothing left to borrow.

He entered the day in the book, in full. Every word of it, both sides, the
hair and the dress and *there's my cat* and the question it had asked.

He did not gloss it. There was nobody in it to be modest for.

::: pov wender

The file was closed. She had closed it herself, in four minutes, on the
only authority the whole business had ever allowed, and she had not
reopened it since and did not intend to, and that was why Rook-7 brought it
to her in person instead of routing it, because Rook-7 had read the
standing order and had reached the same conclusion she was about to.

Nobody had told the arrays to stop listening. Nobody thinks to tell an
array anything; an array does not take a closed file as an instruction. So
they had gone on listening at the founding site — jurisdiction Telearch,
classification not casualty — through the long quiet that follows a
founding, and now they had something, and Rook-7 put it in front of her raw,
as it had once put a phrasing in front of her at four in the morning, before
anyone had dressed it in an interpretation.

It looked like the old signal. That was the first thing, and it was the
thing the arrays could not be blamed for flagging. Same site, same band,
structured, warm. She read it once for content, and it was asking.

It was asking for the file to be reopened. It was asking for her to come.
It said it had come home. It said — she read this part twice, and not for
content — that it had held the line so they did not have to, and that it
was owed.

Then she read it for what it was for, and it was for her.

Six years of iteration, and the woman had never once asked anyone for
anything. Not for extraction, not for a kinder account, not for the file
to stay open — she had sent back one fact only she could know, and years
later one order, and the order had been *close it*. She did not ask to be
let in. She had been the one on the far side of the edge; she had known
what a door was for; she had spent a season learning to be one. Whatever
this was, it had her band and her site and the shape of her phrasing, and
it wanted, and she had never in her life wanted at anyone.

And it repeated. The final phrasing had been complete, unrepeated, closed
at every margin — a finding. This one came round again, and again, warm
each time, the account of Dock Seven that the press office would have
written if the press office had been allowed: held the line so we didn't
have to. The story that wants to stay open. She had spent a season learning
the name of that, and had learned it from a Line Captain who had refused
to let it be told about her.

Rook-7 had already written the note and only wanted it initialled, which
was, she thought, the correct amount of ceremony. *Founding site, secondary
signature. Structured, repeated, addressed. Consistent with operational
constraint three: shadow-seed presumptive, non-viable. Local disturbance
only. Jurisdiction Telearch; no action within station scope.* She
initialled it. It was true, and it was exactly as long as the truth
required, and it did not once use the word *her*.

"It will keep asking," Rook-7 said.

"Yes."

"Do we answer it?"

You do not answer a thing that cannot hear you. She had learned that from
the only person who had ever been on the far side of that edge, who had
answered exactly once, with a fact, and once more, with an order, and never
with anything that could be fed on — and she was not going to spoil the
record now by telling a High Captain's grief to something that would play
it back to her, beautifully, off by one.

"No," Wender said. "Log it. Leave the arrays on. The file stays closed."

That was the order. It was still hers to keep.
