## Syra does not look like Lucene-9000 — RECORDED; nothing open

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> Syra as a Kyeni does not look like the gynoid Lucene 9000, despite the
> stillness, symmetry and composure

*Kyeni* is read as *Krenyi*.

**Direction check.** A clarification of the morning's image reading (*"Syra
should look both human and somewhat alien at the same time"*, `images.md` § 1),
not a change to it, and no canon change: `src/lore/krenyi.md` and
`src/characters/lucene-9000.md` are both untouched. The three things he names
as shared stay shared.

**What separated them, and what had stopped separating them.** Lucene-9000 is
ruled *a real woman whose only tell is perfection* (11 October): a made thing,
built to look as human as a machine can, whose tell is the finish of its
surface. The morning's Syra prompt gave her the same tell under another name,
skin of one perfectly even tone with no flush, freckle or vein and a face *as if
drawn from an average rather than born*, and the frame also shared Lucene's
staging (hair drawn back, a table, hands clasped).

**The reading taken: born, not made.** A Krenyi's strangeness is in her
proportions, her eyes and her stillness, a living body that is the instrument;
a gynoid's is in the finish. So Syra keeps the symmetry, the eye set and the
open pupils, and loses the perfected skin: living skin, a freckle, loose hairs,
hands flat and apart, the gynoid and the perfected face negated by name. Toval's
queued re-roll takes the two remaining carriers, not even skin. Settled at
Opus as a render fix by the 11 October Lucene ruling; nothing put to him.
Re-rolled and filed the same evening (`image-prompts.md`).

## Pages for members of a plural system — RULED (*Option B makes sense*)

Dermot asked whether Tissadelle's headmates get their own character pages or
something different. Until today every system shared one page: Tobble and
Asteria were sections of Shepherd's page, and Cael, Wyn and Sen share Sen's.
Tobble already held a POV in a published chapter (S05E02C03), so his `tobble`
POV id resolved to no page. Three shapes were put to him: A, one page per
system; B, a member gets a page once they hold a POV in a published chapter;
C, every named member gets a page now. He chose **B**.

**The rule.** A member of a plural system gets their own character page once
they hold a POV in a published chapter. The page is written only from what
the record and the system's own account hold, with the rest marked absent.
The system's page keeps its sections and links the member's page.

**The portrait, ruled the same session**, verbatim: *"Non-physical headmates
get fantasy-style codex art portraits instead of the photorealistic portraits
for the host body of the plural system"*. A member who has no body of their
own is never photographed, because there is nothing for a camera to take. Their
page's `image:` is a painted Codex-art impression in the fantasy register
of Asteria's (the dress and light he named as his taste the same day). Like
Asteria's, it is made from the system's own description and asserts nothing
about a face. The host body keeps its photographic portrait on the system's
page. This extends the 7 October rule (*a character with a body gets a
portrait and a mind does not*) for this one case: a non-physical member gets a
painting, never a photograph. Asteria's existing impression is her portrait
when her page comes. Tobble's is queued. Sen's three stay on Sen's page until one of them holds a
POV of their own.

**Direction check.** An extension. *The record ranks no character* (10 October) is
served by it, not strained: the rule is a fact about published POVs, not a
grade. *A character with a body gets a portrait and a mind does not* (7 October)
already covers the image.

**Realized the same session:** `src/characters/tobble.md`, linked from the

## Inner worlds: the only place magic or time travel can seem to happen — RECORDED; nothing open

Dermot's direction, verbatim:

> Inner Worlds are the only place where magical fantasy or time travel can
> seem to happen

**Direction check.** A clarification. *What the Record Refuses* refuses time
travel outright and magic as a category, and both refusals stand: they are
about the world. An inner world is inside a mind, and the record already
treats what happens in a mind as an experience, never as an event in the
world. That is the 26 August rule for answered prayer and heard voices, and
the treatment magic already had in a POV block. Tissadelle's inner world is
already *structured like a high-fantasy role-playing game* on her page.
The direction names the one place where fantasy and time travel may appear,
and the word *seem* keeps the refusals whole.

**Canon checks.**

- Nothing crosses outward. No device, ritual or boundary makes the seeming
  real, and no account from inside an inner world is evidence about the world.
  The prophetic-dream and Membrane Shadow re-derivations are unchanged.
- *Only*: magic and time travel cannot seem to happen anywhere else. A
  character outside an inner world may still mistake a lawful thing for one
  (the page's Psionics and Ghosts entries), which is a misreading and not a
  seeming of this kind.
- A forming noögenic protouniverse is not licensed by this. *Roles before
  law* in its Formation stage is the record's own physics, already written.
  This direction concerns the interior a mind keeps.
- **The transition, the same session**, verbatim: *"however those magical
  fantasy elements were lost in the transition to a noogenic seed universe"*.
  This restates the 27 August ruling (*most of the magical fantasy elements
  would be either lost or downgraded*; answered *lost or downgraded, not held*)
  and agrees with the Formation section of `src/glossary/noogenic-protouniverse.md`
  (*There is no magic in a forming universe*). So the seeming stops at the
  crossing: an interior that becomes a seed universe has become a world, the
  refusals bind it, and what crossed carries its enchantments only as their own
  record (the 27 August mechanism of *downgraded*). **Sharpened minutes
  later**, verbatim: *"downgraded and normalized rather than destroyed"*. This
  re-grounds the 27 August *most … lost*: the magic is what goes, and the
  elements themselves survive. An enchanted thing crosses as its lawful
  counterpart (a working as a craft, a wonder as a phenomenon with a cause)
  and keeps its enchantment only as a record of having had it. *Lost*, in his
  earlier line of today, is read as the magic lost, not the things. The public
  pages say this in general terms. How Tissadelle's own loss is shown, and who grieves it,
  stays Season 7 material as ruled on 27 August, and nothing here draws on it.
- **The private repo's line *Not allowed: out-of-universe fantasy elements***
  is read as barring fantasy imported from outside this setting (another
  work's creatures, names or devices), not magic inside the inner world, which
  the RPG structure and this direction allow. That reading is recorded there
  too. Settled by precedent, since the RPG-structured inner world has been
  canon since 27 August.

**Realized:** a paragraph and a summary row in
`src/lore/what-the-record-refuses.md` (*The Fantasy Side*), and a paragraph
in the Journal entry *What the Refusals Cost*, which now counts four kinds of
story that moved and notes that an inner world can seem to offer the undo
without giving it.
