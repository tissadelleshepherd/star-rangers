## The Inspectorate of the Star Rangers — APPROVED same day (*Sounds good*)

Dermot's direction, verbatim: *"The Inspectorate of the Star Rangers
under the Civilian Oversight Council. A Chief Inspector, for example, is
rank-equivalent to a Chief Star Ranger, and the Inspector-General to a
High Captain."*

**What the record held.** No Inspectorate anywhere. The Oversight
Council (`src/lore/the-oversight-council.md`, settled 30 September) *can
ask, and the Corps must answer*: it convenes inquiries, holds the
Triumvirate to account, and cannot command a Ranger, direct a Safety
Corps finding or defund the Corps. Nothing said who does the asking on
the Council's behalf. Three bodies already put questions to Rangers, all
from inside the Corps: the Safety Corps (incidents, certification; its
own *Investigation Sits Outside the Branches* section), the Provost track
(a Ranger's conduct), and a major incident inquiry convened from the
command ladder. The rank-insignia page already imagined the outside
reader the tab is built for — *an inspector, a port authority, a party to
a dispute* — without naming an office. *Inspector* in the corpus
otherwise means a habitat bureau's Detective Inspector (Ashcombe at
Threshold, Albercombe on Eden).

**Direction check:** an **extension** of the 30 September Council
ruling. It adds the Council's instrument and alters nothing the Council
page said; the *can ask, must answer* line is the Inspectorate's whole
authority. One name point: the canonical guide (same ruling) says *the
Oversight Council*, never *the Civilian Oversight Council* as a proper
name, *civilian* being the charter's description. His direction uses the
description; the pages keep the title, and nothing is reversed.

**Readings taken, each following from the record and flagged for his
eye:**

1. ***Chief Star Ranger* is the Chief rank.** The ladder has no rank of
   that name; Chief is the hinge, and *Chief Ranger* is a Chief's duty
   title on a general section. Read as the rank, so a Chief Inspector is
   equivalent to any Chief whatever their track. The alternative — the
   duty title only — would make the equivalence depend on which section
   the Chief happens to command, which no schedule of precedence does.
2. **The Inspectorate inspects the Corps; it is not made of it.** *Of
   the Star Rangers* read as the thing inspected (as *Inspectorate of
   Constabulary* names what it inspects), not the membership. Under the
   Council, so its officers are not Rangers and hold no Corps rank. The
   equivalence itself argues for this: a body of serving Rangers would
   hold ranks and need no schedule.
3. **It does the Council's asking.** The office's function is the
   Council's existing powers exercised through standing officers: the
   inquiries it convenes, the record it holds between them. Nothing new
   is granted; the Inspectorate can command nobody, and *rank-equivalent*
   is read as reception, precedence and the scope of what a grade may
   ask.
4. **The Inspector-General is one High Captain's equal and no more** —
   below the Council of High Captains and the Triumvirate. Taken as the
   point of his second example rather than an accident of it: the body
   that inspects the Corps is outranked by the body it inspects, and
   accountability stays with the Council. Written up as such on the
   Council page.

**Realized as:** a new *The Inspectorate* section on the Council page
(after *What the Charter Made It*) and a line in its *What Is Not
Established*; a short *Rank-Equivalent, Not Rank* section at the end of
`src/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy.md`; and a guide entry, *The
Inspectorate of the Star Rangers (settled 2026-10-09)*, with the name
rules and the three bodies it is kept apart from. No new page: an
institution's page would want an emblem card, and the Inspectorate is the
Council's office, so it lives on the Council's page. Merged on green
under his 6 October standing approval of what follows naturally from
existing directions; the four readings above are what to overturn if any
was wrong.

**Two extensions offered, neither asserted, both his:**

- **The schedule entire.** Only the two rungs he gave are on record. (a)
  Leave it so, recommended — the Archive quotes the two rungs the Corps
  most often has to read and marks the rest as not kept here, which is
  the Council page's own habit with its seat count; (b) a full ladder of
  Inspectorate grades, each set against a rung, drafted for his ruling
  (something like Inspector, Senior Inspector, Chief Inspector, Principal
  Inspector, Deputy Inspector-General, Inspector-General — offered only as
  a shape, not a proposal).
- **Former Rangers as inspectors.** The record is silent. (a) Leave it
  unestablished, recommended; (b) rule it permitted with a bar on
  inspecting one's own former chain; (c) rule it barred, the Inspectorate
  being the one reader that must come from outside. The Liaison Corps
  page already gives departing Rangers a third path, so (b) or (c) would
  touch that page too. **Answered the same day by the direction below,
  near shape (c): recruited from outside the Corps.** Whether a former
  Ranger has ever been taken stays marked as not asked.

## Recruitment, a legal division and an audit bureau — APPROVED same day (*Sounds good*)

Dermot's second direction, the same day, while the first PR's checks
were running, verbatim: *"The Inspectorate is often recruited from
retired police officer and military police or equivalent from outside
the Star Rangers. It also has a legal division and financial audit
bureau."* *Retired police officer* read as the plural.

**What the record held.** The 10 September rule: no branch of the Corps
is military, none but the Frontier Corps is police, none but the Provost
Corps does the work of military police. The polities keep the police and
the militaries — habitat bureaus, Five-O, the Solar System Defence
Command, the Federation's contingent force, the Hegemony's own, the
Union's ceremonial one with the Garda an Uachtaráin. The Council cannot
defund the Corps; pay and pension come from data licensing and fold-route
revenue. A Ranger's conduct is the Provost track's jurisdiction under the
immunity; a polity can only expel.

**Direction check:** an **extension** of the morning's Inspectorate
section; it also answers the former-Rangers item offered above, so that
item is struck in `open-questions.md`.

**Readings taken, flagged:**

1. ***Often* is a habit, not a bar.** The page says *often recruited
   from* and does not say *only*; the absence of a former Ranger is kept
   as a marked absence, not ruled.
2. ***From outside the Star Rangers* excludes the Provost track.** The
   Provost Corps is the one body in the Corps that does police work, and
   the phrase is read as naming exactly that exclusion. Written so.
3. ***Or equivalent* reaches the non-human polities.** Whatever a
   recognising people keeps that does the same work; no people's body is
   named, since none is described.
4. **The divisions hold what the Council holds and nothing more.** The
   legal division reads the charter as law, advises, drafts findings and
   stands as counsel; it tries nobody and amends nothing. The audit
   bureau reads the books and reports; it has no purse and cannot
   withhold a credit. Both follow from *can ask, must answer* and from
   *cannot defund*.
5. **Why those recruits.** Written as the Council's reason: people who
   held police and military-police authority and set it down, who know an
   institution that answers to someone from the inside. This is the
   page's inference, offered as the obvious one, and is the sentence to
   cut if he reads the habit as convenience rather than design.

**Realized as:** two further paragraphs in the Council page's *The
Inspectorate* section, a clause on the rank page, the guide entry
extended, the *What Is Not Established* line re-cut. Whether the two
divisions keep grades on the schedule of precedence: not established,
marked on the page.

**Approved, his *Sounds good*, after both sections were quoted back with
the nine readings and the one open choice (merged as #1035).** The nine
readings stand as taken. The open choice — the schedule entire, and the
divisions' grades — is ruled on the recommendation: left unquoted, with
the absence marked on the page. Nothing of the Inspectorate is open.

## The present's name, and galactic years — RULED (*Option 1 for both*)

Dermot asked what the contemporary era of 2826 UCSD would be called, by
analogy with *modern* and *post-modern* for 2026 AD, and how galactic years
would be recorded and measured. Three shapes each; his *Option 1 for both*.

**The present.** The Archive names no era for its own present: the
retrospective names it carries (the Founding Era, the Imperium period, the
charter era, the Concord period) were given afterwards, and *the Concord
period* already runs to the present without describing it. A coined name
for the present is the *modern* mistake. A people may name the present in
its own voice and the record files the name beside the date.

**Galactic years.** None, for two reasons that are one: the astronomer's
galactic year is two hundred million of anyone's years, and a civil
galactic calendar would need a galactic present, which no signal faster
than a hull supplies. A year is always somebody's year, converted beside
UCSD and never replaced. The one galaxy-wide reference is a thing to read,
not to count by: the Survey Corps' pulsar ephemeris, a shared coordinate
and not a calendar, phrased so as not to contradict *Count and
Calibration*'s paper timescale.

**Realized as:** two sections on `src/lore/timekeeping-and-the-common-record.md`,
*No Galactic Calendar* and *Naming the Present*, and a rule in CLAUDE.md
beside the dates rule. Extensions of the 5 August timekeeping settlement.
