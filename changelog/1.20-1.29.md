## [1.28.0] — 2026-09-01

A small release finishing what 1.26.0 started. The four lore and codex
entries that arrived in the 29 August direction set without title cards now
have them, and the tool that drew them — `scripts/make-cards.js`, a
cross-platform port of the two PowerShell card generators — means a card can

dressed, and the page now says why that is the opposite of a performance.
MINOR: all additive, backward-compatible.

### Added

- **`scripts/make-cards.js` — a cross-platform port of the card generators** (2026-09-01). Draws the landscape emblem cards of `make-emblem-card.ps1` and the designed-card mode of `make-codex-cover.ps1` in Node with `sharp`, so a title card can be made from a session that is not on Windows. A local authoring tool like its two predecessors: not in the build, not in `npm test`, not in CI, and not a package.json dependency — `sharp` is installed on demand with `npm install --no-save sharp`. Two deliberate fidelity gaps are stated in the file header rather than left to be discovered: Georgia and Consolas are unavailable on Linux, so the port sets Liberation Serif and Liberation Mono and matches the layouts rather than the letterforms; and the star fields are seeded by a plain LCG rather than `System.Random`, so the dots are deterministic but sit elsewhere. A card that has to match a GDI+ card exactly still comes from the `.ps1` on Dermot's machine.
- **Title cards for the four 29 August entries that shipped without one** (2026-09-01): [The Ovruhn](src/lore/ovruhn.md), [Sapience Beyond the Human Measure](src/lore/sapience-beyond-the-human-measure.md), [The Maker's Shape](src/lore/the-makers-shape.md) and [The Two Songs of the Loud People](src/codex/the-two-songs-of-the-loud-people.md). Designed cards in the house palettes, drawn by `make-cards.js`; prompts of record in `story-bible/image-prompts.md` per the standing convention. The three lore entries take landscape emblem cards and the codex entry takes the square navy title card, as the split between the two generators requires.
- **Tissadelle Shepherd is always formally dressed, and never more so than on duty** (2026-09-01, Dermot's direction). Her character page carried the uniform as a present fact — "what she notices on her own family's hillside once the uniform is off", the Gardaí training as "nearly a different uniform" — without ever ruling how she dresses. A paragraph in Character Notes fills the gap and reads the formality as the opposite of the performance she withholds: she treats the title as a job description, and the uniform is that description worn where anybody can check it, claiming nothing that is not already on the record. What "formally dressed" amounts to off duty, and whether the Corps requires it or she does, are deliberately left open.

- **`-Underlay` on `scripts/make-codex-cover.ps1`** (2026-08-20) — the card compositor. A photograph is centre-cropped to fill the square and scrimmed in the template's navy, **heaviest at the two bands the lettering occupies** (the category line and the title block) and lightest through the middle, then the existing text pass runs over it. **The point is spelling**: an image generator cannot letter a card — the supplied set produced *Healng*, *ooroting*, *aa' Star Rrarles* and *superical* — so the picture is generated wordless and the words are set by the font engine, correct by construction. `-Scrim` is a percentage (default 62) and an underlay turns the motif off unless `-Motif` is passed explicitly, since a motif drawn over a photograph fights it. Underlays should be generated **square**: the crop is centred, so a 16:9 source loses a third of its width and loses it from the edges. Parses clean and the crop and scrim arithmetic are verified against five aspect ratios; **not render-tested**, since the tool is Windows-only and CI has no GDI+.

## [1.22.0] — 2026-08-16

A release about the machinery for noticing things. Two doctrines that looked
opposed turned out to govern different objects, and stating the boundary
between them produced a named review process, a public backlog to file its
findings in, and the first three findings themselves. Alongside that, the
Safety Corps gained an internal structure, Kieme gained three titles it can be
translated by, and one domain's deploy stopped failing silently.

The through-line, if there is one, is that **silence is the expensive failure**
— a cron that reports nothing, a gap nobody marked, an untidiness filed as a
fault. Most of what is below is an attempt to make one or another kind of
silence legible.

- **The Safety Corps gets branches, and the UL triad moves down a level** (2026-08-15). To Dermot's direction: the Corps is divided by **hazard domain** into branches — **Fold Transit Safety** and **AI Safety** named as the two largest — and the inherited Underwriters Laboratories split of research, standards and certification (**Solutions**) no longer describes the top level, surviving instead as subdivisions *inside each branch*. The vocabulary turned out to be already canon rather than new: the command-hierarchy entry has the Academy "organised as a college per corps and a **school per branch**", which the arrangement now explains. The Corps' stated reason puts function beneath domain — *the method is common property and can be taught to anyone; what cannot be taught quickly is the specific way a domain goes wrong.*

  **The AI Safety Branch does not hold the gate**, and the page is careful about it, because canon already assigns commissioning authority over agentic systems to the AI Governance Commission under the Concord Accords. The Branch certifies something narrower and entirely its own: whether a lawfully commissioned mind is fit to be **deployed** where the Rangers propose to deploy it. That is the hull-and-route division the Corps already lives with at a fold gate, applied to minds — *a mind may be lawful and unsuited; the Commission answers the first question and has never claimed the second.*

  **Incident investigation sits outside the branches**, because a branch that certified a hull cannot investigate that hull's failure without becoming its own reviewer. Ordinary investigation reports to the corps president. **A major, cross-disciplinary investigation is led from the command ladder by a Starwarden or above** — which reads at first like a straight contradiction of two existing lines ("none of them answerable to a Starwarden"; "a Starwarden cannot override a Safety Corps finding") and resolves in the word *cross-disciplinary*: a fold-transit loss with casualties is a Safety question about the hull, a Navigation question about the route, a Medical question about what the crew survived, and no corps president holds authority inside another corps. The only office that can convene all of them is on the command ladder. The charter line then holds underneath, unchanged: **command decides what is investigated and does not decide what any corps concludes** — the Starwarden directs the inquiry and cannot amend a single finding brought to it, including a finding that command's own tasking caused the loss.

  **What an investigation produces depends entirely on who is being answered.** Inside the Rangers a finding is not a suggestion: the Corps' standards bind the whole institution, and where an investigation concludes a standard was wrong the Corps amends it and the amended standard binds from the moment it issues, no approval sought. Outside them it produces a recommendation, **referred for review to whichever government actually holds the authority** — the polity that registered the vessel, the habitat administration, the world whose citizens died — which adopts, dilutes, defers or files it as it chooses; where it is declined, the Corps publishes that it was made and declined. *Absolute authority over its own house, none whatsoever over anyone else's* — and the Corps' answer to being asked whether that frustrates it: *a body whose recommendations were binding would be a body worth capturing.*

  **Boundary Safety is named as a third branch and the oldest**, descending directly from the USIS boundary-proximate programme and predating the Star Rangers — which is where the Corps' character was formed, and which finally gives the **certified boundary analyst** designation a branch to live in. **"Solutions" is retired**: the commercial coinage did not survive the body's charter as a public-interest institution, and the arm is called Certification, because *a body whose entire claim is that it has nothing to sell should not name the arm that issues its certificates after a product line.* That leaves the Corps declining two words from opposite directions — *regulator*, which claims a power it does not have, and *Solutions*, which claimed a relationship it did not want. Governance gains a second axis: a **chapter leader** holds a post, a **guild leader** holds a branch, neither outranks the other, and both answer to the corps president — all three held **on secondment from regular duties**, keeping the substantive rank and post and returning to both, which the Corps treats as load-bearing rather than administrative: *the ones who have to live with a standard are the ones who elected the person writing it, and that person has to come back and work to it.* Corps President is confirmed as a guild office and keeps its existing name; **Guild President was descriptive, not a rename**, so no terminology migration is needed. And **a Starwarden convening a major investigation holds nothing outside the Rangers** — where a Ranger appears in someone else's inquiry it is by invitation, as **neutral mediator, arbiter or observer**, a role conferred by the inviting party and revocable by them, *which is exactly why it is worth having*. The [Academy entry](src/lore/star-rangers-academy.md) turned out to corroborate the whole structure independently, being organised as a school per branch precisely because certifying "boundary analysts, ship's pilots, field surgeons, and investigators against a single standard" is "not a hard problem so much as an incoherent one".

  Two further tiers of who is bound, both to Dermot's direction. **Suppliers, subcontractors and their entire supply chain are bound by contract**, on pain of disqualification from future work — a certified assembly containing an uncertified part is not a certified assembly, so the condition flows all the way down, and foundries that will never sell a Ranger hull hold Ranger certification because the yard they sell to must. This is the nearest the institution comes to the power its charter denies it, and the page is precise about the difference: **it binds who the Rangers buy from, not who exists.** A firm that declines has lost a customer, not a licence. **The Dock Seven gap is deliberately left open** — procurement reaches only those who want the business, and the Hyperfold Yield Combine sold to hull operators and wanted nothing from the Rangers. **Donors and supporters are bound by none of it**, holding no certification and asked for nothing but the money; what is examined is whether the money may be taken at all, support being accepted only from legitimate, bona-fide, law-abiding citizens and organisations. That test exists for the same reason the Rangers are self-funding: *a body that can be embarrassed by its donors can be steered by the threat of it.*

The pairing puts both crossings of the hinge rank side by side — Wender came to command from the technical ceiling, Shepherd because rotating through everything left her no other ladder — and the hardest clause is the one that chapter demonstrates: **the mentorship gives a senior officer no standing to ask questions the command relationship would not already justify.** Shepherd's page keeps [Galen Kane](src/characters/galen-kane.md) alongside, and the two are not redundant: one adviser she is required to take seriously, one whose judgement costs her nothing to hear.

- **Leaving and coming back: early retirement, career breaks, extended sabbaticals** (2026-08-13). Negotiated rather than granted, because no schedule written in advance could anticipate what a particular Ranger's service has cost them. **A stress-related injury is an injury** — an institution that certifies competence for a living has never found it coherent to rule a person unfit and then treat the finding as a character judgement. On medical ground the Medical Corps' finding binds command, on the same guild-independence rule that stops a Starwarden revising a boundary standard. Two consequences stated: a record with a gap in it is an ordinary record, and coming back is not resumption — the ones who have done it generally say the first ordinary morning back was harder than anything in the incident that sent them away. This also closes an open question the day's other arithmetic had raised, since Brother Fintan's thirty-one years and departure before pension age is now a documented route rather than an anomaly.

- **The three-stage career structure past active service, and planetary representation as a third ladder** (2026-08-13). Representative standing opens at about **55**; from 55 to about **75** the officer is on the **reserve list** — left active service, not retired, still recallable; full pension is available from **75**, at the Ranger's own choosing. And it is a genuine non-command career path with its own progression — deepening specializations, certifications and decorations in place of promotion — which is what makes a High Captain and a Chief who transfer to the same world start level: not because the standing is honorary, but because the path has its own ladder and rank does not transfer onto it. The reserve stage sharpens a tension `planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters.md` already raised: under 75 she is not a private citizen with a service record, she is **a serving Ranger living at home, on a world the charter forbids the Corps to enter.** Nobody in-universe has made that argument formally.

- **The Krenyi present tense, and the gap that is not a withheld answer** (2026-08-13). The Quiet-Built attend to what is here and what is coming; the past is neither sacred nor suppressed, simply not where they look unless looking returns something usable — and most of what they are known for falls out of that single orientation, including grief that resolves and a state-marker meaning *still relevant here*. The page also names the cost, which they would name themselves: **they are poor keepers of anything whose value has not yet been demonstrated.** Alongside it, a third standing editorial rule in the story bible for a case the existing spoiler rule cannot cover — **some gaps are the answer and never resolve.** The Krenyi origin has no reveal behind it, no chapter discloses it, and no future session should treat the empty entry as a to-do; an unexplained gap reads to any competent drafter as an unfinished job, and that instinct is the failure mode. The test for spotting another one: if a page argues for *why* a gap exists, the gap is load-bearing.

- **Galahad Thorne left a difficult marriage before the Threshold posting** (2026-08-13). Written to the cast principles rather than around them: **there is no version of the account in which someone was a villain**, and he does not offer one. The twelve years were long frontier missions — six stations, years at a time — and a marriage conducted across that distance is a thing two people either build deliberately or lose by degrees while both of them are being reasonable. He does not describe her at all, which, given how precisely he describes everything else, is the most deliberate silence on his record. He treats it as neither a wound nor a lesson, which are the two things people usually make of a marriage that ended; he treats it as something that happened and finished. The page names the tempting inference and refuses it — that this is what made him ready for Elvira. He was not made ready by it. He arrived at the Marsh Causeway as a man who had already found out, once, what it costs to be away.

- **Asteria mentored Karla Wender, and the dates permit exactly one version of it** (2026-08-13). Asked with the condition attached — *"if the timelines fit"* — so they were checked rather than assumed. Asteria was already long retired when Galahad Thorne met her on a frontier posting inside his twelve unbroken Survey Corps years, which puts her departure in the early 2820s at the latest, at about a hundred, holding Star Captain. Wender was climbing the certification ladder through precisely that period: a Novice around the turn of the century, a Chief by the early 2820s, and not a Starwarden until after Season 1 opens. **The two careers overlap for the whole of Wender's ascent and not for a day of her command** — so Asteria was never her assigned mentor, because the formal pairing begins at Starwarden and by then Asteria was on a quiet planet running a chapter house. What she gave, she gave to a junior officer on the examined half of the ladder with no policy requiring it and nothing on either record to show for it. That closes a loop the corpus had already half-drawn: Wender now holds the *assigned* version of the duty for Shepherd, and had been discharging it unassigned for years before anyone wrote it down — which is not what the policy asks for and is exactly what she was taught. Asteria's two mentorships now bracket her retirement, one on either side, neither ever an assignment.

- **The Liaison Corps has a name, a second route in, and a ceiling** (2026-08-13). Planetary representation is a **specialist corps** — the Star Ranger Liaison Corps, whose members are **Planetary Representatives** — and its people are serving Rangers on a posting, paid by the Corps, not veterans on an honorarium. The nearest civilian comparison is a police officer moved off dangerous front-line duty and kept on the force, which is also its **second route in**: an injury that does not mend, a condition that will not certify, or a stress injury ends boundary-proximate work for people, and this is where the Corps puts them rather than losing them to a medical finding. Deliberately not a soft landing — the work is needed, hard in its own way, and performed under the officer's own name on a world that judges her by it; treating a liaison posting as somewhere people are put out to is the kind of remark seniors are expected to have grown out of. Being a corps, it elects its own chapter leaders and president, holds its own certification standards, and runs its own Academy college. The entry is retitled from *Retired Rangers: Liaisons, Representatives, and Recruiters* — the word *retired* was wrong in both directions, since they are not retired for the first twenty years and many never retire at all — and the **URL is deliberately unchanged** so that nothing linking there breaks; all nine inbound references are updated to the new name.

- **Corrected: an officer leaving at Star Captain or above cannot join the Liaison Corps** (2026-08-13). Earlier the same day this changelog and two lore pages claimed that "a High Captain and a Chief who take it up on the same world start at the same standing." That is wrong. The corps' senior post is **Chief Liaison Officer** — a Chief's duty title beside Chief Ranger, Chief Pilot, Chief Provost and Chief Science Officer — which places the whole corps on the certification half of the ladder and gives it a real upper bound: **a flag-tier officer is over-ranked for a Chief's berth**, and the Corps declines to invent a grander title to accommodate a large career, because a post inflated for an individual is how a ladder stops meaning anything. So the third path absorbs a great deal of the Corps and is closed at the top of it. Below the bound the original point survives: two Chiefs on the same world start level, and what separates them later was earned in front of that world. [Asteria](src/characters/asteria-the-sage.md) is the worked example of the closed door — she left at Star Captain for a Fellowship of Light chapter house, an institution the Rangers do not run, whose standing rests on having renounced the interests everyone else keeps. Her page needed no change of rank, only the naming of the route she did not take.

- **The maximum retirement age is about 110, and it is the one age nobody negotiates** (2026-08-13). Three ages now bound a career: the Liaison Corps floor at about **55**, a full pension available at about **75** by choice, and a hard stop at about **110** — set where the eleventh decade's borrowed capability runs out, and deliberately *below* the ~125 lifespan ceiling, so a Ranger who serves to the limit still has years in front of her and the Corps simply stops being where she spends them. The stretch between 75 and 110 is the longest optional period in the service and officers use it: Asteria left at about a hundred, a quarter of a century after a full pension first became hers to take.

- **Two more refused tropes: the interfertile alien, and the safe cross-species transfusion** (2026-08-13). `what-the-record-refuses.md` gains a **Bodies and Species** section and two summary-table rows. **No sapient species in this record crosses with another** — no half-human hybrids, no children of two peoples, no breakthrough pending. Not squeamishness: two lineages sharing no common ancestor share no chromosome count, no gene regulation, no developmental sequence, and nothing a gamete could do with another gamete. A trope that treats reproductive compatibility as the default outcome of two people liking each other is not being romantic about biology; it is declining to have any. What the refusal costs is a stock plot and what it buys is better — contact stories that have to find something else for two peoples to make together, of which the Four Islands relationship (years of translation, one standing delegation, nothing else) is already the worked example. The corollary: **cross-species attraction, where it happens, is a fact about a person and not a plot mechanism.** It resolves nothing and bridges nothing, and the universe does not rearrange itself to accommodate it.

- **The Krenyi are demisexual, and their immunity is why the transfusion question has a wrong intuition** (2026-08-13). Attraction arises only through an established bond — the whole species, not a distribution within it — and the page's existing "no flirtation or small talk, straight to material terms" turns out to have been describing that without the word: a Krenyi is not declining to flirt, there is simply no stage of a Krenyi relationship at which flirting would be doing anything. **They do not form romantic attachments to humans and will not consent to interbreed**, stated once and not elaborated, because *why not* asked of a Krenyi is very often the same question as *what are you, really*. Humans do form attachments the other way, and a Krenyi meeting one is generally meeting the mythification problem in its most personal register — a person wanting the Quiet-Built rather than wanting *them*. On the biology, the asymmetry is the part that costs: **Krenyi immunity destroys foreign genetic material outright**, so human DNA entering a Krenyi never persists, which is why "cannot interbreed with any other species" is a flat fact rather than an untested assumption — and running the other way, **Krenyi blood is hazardous to a human**, because transfused antibodies meet a body composed entirely of foreign genetic material and do exactly what they exist to do. The human survives and is seriously unwell for several days at minimum. The clinical file exists because somebody once reasoned, not unreasonably from a species that is honest and rarely sick, that Krenyi blood would do in a shortage.

- **Journal: *The Wrong Century*** (2026-08-13). On putting a chapter a hundred and three years from where it belonged, and being caught not by proofreading but by an unrelated sentence written hours later — the argument for constraints as a machine that checks what you have already written, for free, including the parts you were not thinking about. The entry's spine is the sceptical turn rather than the anecdote: when four further decisions all turned out to be anticipated by existing text, that feeling is *exactly what confirmation bias feels like from the inside*, since a large enough corpus appears to anticipate almost any rule. So it sorts them by whether the earlier writing **predicted** the new rule or merely **permitted** it — a clause that fits a dozen arrangements equally well confirms nothing, while a chapter that opens "back on duty" and never explains where she had been is a hole with exactly one shape. Two predictions and two permissions out of four, and the honest count rather than the flattering one. It closes on **diligence rather than bookkeeping** — bookkeeping being what you do to an account after the fact, diligence a way of working — and on the **harmonic balance between creativity and correctness**, which are not two budgets competing for the same hours: a world too loose to contradict you offers not freedom but the absence of resistance, so nothing pushes back and nothing is discovered, while a world held too tight admits nothing new. In balance, the correctness is not the price of the creativity but the thing that makes it load-bearing.

- **A failed `cpanel-autopull.sh` run now emails `ADMIN_EMAIL` instead of disappearing** (2026-08-13). The question that prompted this was "the photo site autopull should have run at 2am" — and answering it meant reading a deploy email, because nothing else could say whether the run had happened. The run had, and had succeeded; the point is that a *failed* run would have looked exactly the same. `err()` writes to stderr, and cron delivers stderr to the cPanel account's own system mailbox rather than to `ADMIN_EMAIL`, so "nothing to do" and "the pull failed" were both silence. Every deploy outcome was already mailed properly by `cpanel-deploy.sh`; the blind spot was precisely the window before the handoff, the one stretch with no mailer wired up. Exits 1 and 3 now mail directly, with the failure, the checkout's state and the tail of the decision log. **Exit 2 deliberately does not** — on a ten-minute cron a multi-domain deploy routinely outlasts the interval, so a held lock is the expected state, and mailing it would train the alert to be ignored, which is the failure this exists to prevent. No send is ever allowed to change an exit code.

- **`scripts/mail-lib.sh`: the mail transport, extracted so it has exactly one copy** (2026-08-13). The `mail(1)` → `/usr/sbin/sendmail` fallback lived inside `deploy-lib.sh`, which autopull cannot use: sourcing `deploy-lib.sh` runs `mktemp` and `git describe` as import side effects, and autopull sources its libraries on *every* cron run, including the overwhelmingly common one that finds nothing and exits in under a second. So the new file is side-effect-free on source, and that property is load-bearing rather than tidiness. `deploy_lib_notify()` now calls it and keeps its subject format and `MAIL_OK` bookkeeping unchanged. Two copies of a mailer is how one of them silently stops working.

- **`AUTOPULL_MAX_GAP_HOURS` (optional, `deploy.conf`) and a `lastrun` stamp** (2026-08-13). Every run now records that it *fired* — including runs that find nothing and runs that exit on a held lock — because "is cron still firing?" is a different question from "what is deployed?", and only the first can tell you the schedule itself has stopped. `--status` prints both, plus the resolved notification address. Set the new key and a run finding the previous one longer ago than that mails a "runs were missed" alert; unset by default and deliberately so, since the interval lives in cPanel → Cron Jobs and the repo has no basis for guessing one. Its limit is stated in the script, the sample config and the docs rather than left to be discovered: **it can only report a gap on the next run that actually happens.** A cron that stops firing altogether cannot report its own absence, and nothing in this repo can change that.
