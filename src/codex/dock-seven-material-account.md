*A note on what this document is. It is a research paper by a body that declines to use the [Cosmic Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/) and says so on its first page. It is filed here because it is the account most people in settled space actually hold, because it is careful, and because it is right about a great deal. It is not the Archive's account, and where the two differ the Archive has not been able to persuade the Institute and has largely stopped trying.*

---

## 1. Summary of finding

An aperture generated by commercial fold equipment registered to the Hyperfold Yield Combine was held open, past transit and against design, for approximately four months, at a site already exhibiting a long-period chronometric anomaly. The equipment was in service well beyond its rated duty cycle and was out of calibration on at least three axes for which we have recovered maintenance telemetry.

The result was progressive strain accumulation along the boundary gradient, terminating in a shear event of the class the models have predicted since they were first written and which no operator has previously been reckless enough to produce at this scale.

**Nothing about the failure requires explanation beyond the equipment and the duration.** We wish to be understood as saying the strong version of this: not that a material account is available alongside others, but that the material account is complete, and that the event is a maintenance failure of unusual magnitude.

## 2. On the reported shapes

Fifty-one witness statements describe animate forms at the perimeter of the event. Nine of these, taken from the outer gallery at Dock Seven, are unusually consistent and were given within the hour.

We do not doubt the witnesses and we do not think them foolish. Structured discharge along a strain gradient is directional, it is bright, it persists on the order of minutes, and it takes forms that the visual system resolves into figures — this is not a novel claim and the laboratory literature on it is sixty years old. The gallery crews observed a genuine phenomenon through a viewport with no instrumentation, at the end of a shift, while people they knew were being evacuated. They reported it with the first word available to them.

That the word was *dragons* is a fact about the vocabulary settled space has been supplied with, and we consider its uniformity across the nine statements evidence of a shared culture rather than a shared observation.

## 3. On the survival of the Star Rangers personnel

Every Star Rangers crewman present survived, as did three chartered courier hulls and every shuttle in the water. This asymmetry has attracted a quantity of interpretation we regard as unearned.

The explanation is preparation. The Star Rangers maintain the most rigorous boundary-proximate evacuation protocols in settled space, drill them at a frequency no commercial operator would tolerate, and have accumulated across decades of undesirable postings a practical working knowledge of boundary conditions that exists nowhere else. The officer commanding recognised an unrecoverable configuration early, committed to evacuation rather than salvage, and executed it in forty-one minutes.

People who know what they are doing survive things that kill people who do not. We are aware this is a less satisfying account than the alternative. We submit that it is the correct one, and that it does considerably more credit to the Corps than the alternative does.

## 4. Null results and unresolved observations

*Per house rule, everything we could not account for is printed here at the same length as the finding.*

**4.1** We cannot model the perimeter's formation time. The non-certified array logs at Threshold Station record a closed figure established in under one second across a volume of that size. No discharge process we can construct propagates a boundary that fast. We have not been able to fault the instrument and we have not been able to reproduce the figure. **This is the most serious unresolved item in this paper and we decline to minimise it.**

**4.2** We cannot account for the eleven-minute standoff. The same arrays log four discrete sources holding position outside the figure, without entering it, for eleven minutes, before the event concluded. Discharge does not wait.

**4.3** We cannot account for the selectivity by any strain-gradient model. Every hull registered to the operator ceased returning a signature, to a radius of some nine hundred thousand kilometres, including vessels far outside any plausible strain volume; every hull not so registered was untouched, including two within the volume. Registry is not a physical property. **We have no model in which registry is a physical property, and we state plainly that our account of §3 does not survive this observation unassisted** — preparation explains why well-drilled crews evacuate successfully; it does not explain why a Combine tender at nine hundred thousand kilometres, crewed by people doing nothing at all, stopped returning a signature.

**4.4** We cannot account for the residual signature at the failure point, which is structured, warm, and has not decayed on any schedule our models produce. It is still there.

**4.5** We note, without drawing any conclusion, that a Star Rangers field analyst logged §4.3 within the hour, offered an account of it that does not require registry to be a physical property, and was not asked for it again.

## 5. Conclusion

The Dock Seven event was caused by the sustained operation of unauthorised, overused and miscalibrated fold equipment against a boundary that was already anomalous, and the loss of the Star Rangers vessel and its commanding officer is a consequence of that operation and of nothing else.

Sections 4.1 through 4.5 remain open. We expect them to be closed by better instruments.
