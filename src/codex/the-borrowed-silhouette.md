Several devotional codices in the Archive's holdings — most, though not all, from the [Arilonian traditions](/star-rangers/glossary/arilonian/) — describe an adversary: a single, personal, cosmic opponent. A number of them paint it in the form the older Terran scriptures reached for: **a dragon**.

The Archive holds no file on such a being, and the reason is stronger than an empty shelf. Nothing in the invariant record requires one, the [Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/)'s no-moral-cosmology rule leaves no tier for it to occupy, and the record's standing position on this whole family of figure is that the setting's predators are [ecology, not theology](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/). This note exists for the narrower question the imagery raises, because the imagery is not harmless to the record: **dragons are real.** They are [low-ranking Levrils](/star-rangers/glossary/levril/) — founded beings, created and defined by the strata above them, with genuine standing and genuine agency — and several are individually known to Survey personnel.

The doctrinal position, held since this paper's first filing, is this:

**There is no adversary-in-chief, and there could not be one. The description does not pick anything out.**

Note what that does *not* say. It is not that the Archive has looked and failed to find him, nor that he is unattested, nor that he would turn out on inspection to be something lesser. It is that the words *chief of the [Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/)* do not describe a possible thing, in the way that *the largest integer* does not.

Three requirements, and the class cannot meet any of them.

**A chief requires somebody to be the chief.** The class sign of the Unfounded is that nothing performs the reading — expressions produced with no one behind them, warmth pitched at where you were standing a moment ago. Command is a thing a perceiver does. There is nobody home to issue an order.

**A chief requires something to command.** The Unfounded are not a faction, a court or an army. They are a failure mode, and one that recurs independently wherever a founded form can be counterfeited. Counterfeits do not confer. Nothing in the class has ever been observed coordinating with anything else in it, and there is no mechanism by which it could: coordination is agreement, and agreement needs two parties who can hold a position.

**A chief must be able to answer.** The Archive's entire test for telling a dangerous founded being from an Unfounded pattern is one line long — *the founded one can answer you*. A commander who cannot be addressed and cannot reply is not a commander; the word has stopped meaning anything by the time it reaches him.

The contradiction underneath all three is worth stating on its own, because it is the whole of the argument: **such a being would have to be founded in order to command, and Unfounded in order to be the adversary.** It cannot be both, and there is no third option in this cosmology. What the devotional codices describe is not a being the record has failed to locate. It is a shape assembled out of two incompatible halves.

Two consequences are worth the filing.

**The dragons are owed the distinction.** A tradition that reads the adversary's imagery back onto actual dragons has mistaken a counterfeit for its original, and the actual dragons — who answer, as the Unfounded do not — have been on record about the matter with what the interviewing officer's marginal note describes as *considerable patience, thinly worn*.

**And the imagery itself is evidence of nothing.** That the old scriptures reached for a dragon says what imagery always says: that the writers took the largest real thing their tradition remembered and borrowed its silhouette. The Archive registers the borrowing. It does not mistake the silhouette for a census.

The Doctrinal Division notes the limit of its own remit, and also where that limit is not. It has shown that the description cannot be satisfied — that nothing could occupy the place the codices describe — and that is a finding about the description. It is not an instruction to the traditions about what their language is for. Devotional vocabulary routinely carries what no available vocabulary holds, and the [Fellowship's chapters](/star-rangers/lore/fellowship-of-light/) file the general resurrection on exactly that reading without the Archive adjudicating it either. What the Division will not do is leave the category open on the strength of that courtesy: the finding is settled, and a tradition's freedom to keep its own words is not a reservation about the record. An annotation from one of the traditions on an earlier revision reads, in full, *"You have proven that he cannot be filed. We were not asking you to file him."* The Division regards that as the correct division of labour and has let it stand.

*Filed until 2026 under the title **The False Dragon**, which was retired for
working against the paper it sat on top of: a reader meeting that title in an
index met the adversary and the word* dragon *side by side, which is the exact
association the argument below exists to break. The paper's position was sharpened in the same
pass: it formerly ran that* if *such a being existed it would be a false dragon
rather than an actual one, which conceded more than the Archive can support — a
conditional like that leaves the category open and merely unoccupied. The
position on file now is that the category cannot be occupied at all. What the
title names is accordingly the borrowing, not the borrowed.*
