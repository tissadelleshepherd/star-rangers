Among the parables the older Terran scriptures preserve, one describes two builders. Each builds a house, and the text is careful about what it does not say: it does not say one house was smaller, or meaner, or worse made. The houses differ in nothing a visitor could inspect. One stands on rock and one stands on sand, and then the same weather arrives for both — *the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew* — and one house stands, and the other falls, *and great was the fall of it.*

The Doctrinal Division files this note because the parable states, more compactly than the Archive has ever managed, the ontology of the [Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/).

**The difference between the houses is not a property of the houses.** Inventory the sand-house room by room and nothing is missing. A foundation is not a component; it is a relation to something beneath the structure, and it cannot be found by inspecting the structure. This is the Archive's whole difficulty with the predatory patterns, stated a millennium and more before the taxonomy: an Unfounded thing is not a defective specimen of the kind it resembles. Feature by feature it may lack nothing. What it lacks is not a feature — the shape is accurate, and nothing underwrites the shape.

**Sand is not a poorer rock.** The parable resists the comfortable reading that the second builder economised on his footings. Sand is not a cheaper grade of foundation; it is what building looks like where no foundation is to be had. The Unfounded are likewise not founded badly, or founded on something inferior, or founded and then orphaned. They are patterns with no origin condition at all — recorded at the Conceptual layer, underwritten by nothing — and the record's care on this point is the parable's care: the failure is not one of quality but of category.

**Both houses stand in fair weather.** The text is honest about this, and the honesty is the part a field officer most needs. The sand-house is a house. It stands; it shelters; it can be entered, furnished, lived in for years. The Unfounded likewise persist, recur, and are catalogued — persistence is the one thing the record grants them freely. The parable's discipline is that standing is not the test. Weather is. A counterfeit holds its shape exactly as long as nothing leans on the membership it does not have, which is why the Archive's one field test for the founded — *the founded one can answer you* — is a load test and not an inspection. The flood is a question the sand-house cannot answer.

**And the fall is total.** *Great was the fall of it* — not damage, not subsidence, no ruin left standing to shore up. The Division reads this as the scriptures' own statement of the doctrine that evil is a shape collapse takes: what has no ground does not degrade toward the ground, having none to come to rest on. When the pattern fails it fails entirely, and what is left is not a worse house but no house — which is what it had been all along, wearing weathertight form.

The Division registers, as it must, the criterion the parable itself puts first and this note has put last: the builders differ in that one *hears the words and does them*, and the other hears only. The founded house is founded on practice — on the doing, not the assent. The Division cannot adopt the moral frame; it notes without comment that its own files describe the Unfounded in exactly these terms: words without doing, shape without practice, the performance of a kind with no member behind the performance.

One of the Arilonian annotations on the working copy reads: *"It was not written to classify your monsters. It was addressed to the builders, so that you would look to your own foundations."*

The Division has entered no reply. The annotation is correct, and a doctrine office that could not recognise a correct annotation would be building on sand.
