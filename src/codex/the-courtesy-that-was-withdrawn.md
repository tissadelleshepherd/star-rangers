**REGISTRY OF ROLLS AND HOLDINGS, TÍR NA NÓG — MINUTED REPLY**
**Document:** Reply of the Keeper of the Rolls to the petition for the institution of courtesy recognition of ancestral titles and chiefly names
**Occasion:** The petition returns roughly once a tenure, respectfully drafted and differently signed. This reply was minuted so that the next Keeper need not compose one.
**Standing:** Conveyed to the Survey delegation under the Union's records courtesy; filed as received.

---

The petitioners are careful to ask for nothing. That is the first thing to say about the petition, and the first thing to respect in it: it does not seek legal standing, precedence, a seat, or an acre. It asks only that the Registry keep, beside the rolls it already keeps, a courtesy list — the old names, the carried titles, acknowledged in the Registry's hand as being what their families say they are. No claim adjudicated. No right conferred. A line of ink and a little politeness.

I am declining it, as my predecessors declined it, and I want the reason on the minute in full, because the reason is not the constitution. The petitioners know the constitution as well as I do — the [founders](/star-rangers/lore/celtic-union-of-planets/) carried it out with the language, and it says the state confers no titles and takes none away. But the petitioners have read it closely enough to ask for courtesy rather than conferral, and a close reading deserves a better answer than a citation. The better answer is a piece of history that came out on the same ships.

The old republic on Earth kept, for some sixty years, exactly the practice this petition describes. Its heralds extended courtesy recognition to the old chiefly names: no legal standing, the state's polite acknowledgment, a letter and a listing, nothing more. The office did the work in good faith and with real scholarship, and the practice ended in a single season, when one of the recognised pedigrees was shown to have been written backwards — composed from its conclusion, by a hand that understood precisely what a state's politeness is worth. By then the recognition had been traded on for years, in introductions, in honours, in money. Nobody who relied on it had been careless. That was the discovery. They had relied on a courtesy, and a courtesy written down by a state is, at the point where anyone uses it, indistinguishable from an endorsement. The republic did not reform the practice, narrow it, or hedge it with disclaimers. It stopped, and it never resumed, and its silence afterward is the policy we inherited — not a doctrine first, but a burn.

The petitioners say the Registry is trusted, and that its trust would cost nothing to lend. They are half right. The Rolls are believed — that is all a register is, and the whole of what I keep. But every line I hold is checkable against ground someone can walk: a boundary, a harvest, a billet, a grave. A pedigree is the one claim of its kind that walks nowhere. The Registry cannot verify it, and so could only ever believe it, and a register that writes down what it merely believes has adopted it, and must defend it forever or be diminished by withdrawing it. The courtesy costs nothing until it is believed. It is only wanted because it will be believed.

What a family keeps in its own hand is its own, and handsome. The Registry will not notice it, will not dispute it, and will not record it — the three refusals are one refusal, and the old republic paid sixty years of scholarship and one bad season to learn which one it is. The Registry declines, with respect, and expects to decline again.

*Minuted for the Rolls. The next Keeper need only sign.*

---

*Archivist's note.* The practice the Keeper describes survives in the Solar System record substantially as she gives it; the terminating minute in the Earth-side file says less than she does, and nothing different. Her reply is filed beside the Union charter materials, and readers comparing traditions may set her final paragraph next to [Marked Absences](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/) — two registries, unacquainted for centuries, arriving at the same refusal to let a record adopt a guess.
