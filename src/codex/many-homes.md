**COMMONPLACE BOOK — DAY OFFICE**

**Current standing:** A private meditation, copied into the chapter's reading by the keeper's own hand and read at the day office on the feast of the well. Not doctrine, not a claim on the record. The Archive's position on everything below is that it has none, and the keeper says so himself.

---

*In my Father's house are many homes. If it weren't so, I would have told you.* It is a line for funerals, and this morning I read it at none, and it said a different thing.

The Archive keeps three files on what becomes of a person, and it will not let anyone run them together. I have sat with the keepers of those files and I know why. The first says that no coherent mind is lost: the structure is kept, before embodiment, without disposal. The second says that a person may be carried higher, by an election whose reasons are not given. The third is the one the Archive does not keep at all, because it cannot: that at the end of the age the dead are raised in the body. The Archive says it has cosmology for running down and no last day, and that it draws no conclusion from the absence, having no way to tell a thing that has not happened yet from a thing that does not happen. That is honest, and I have never asked it for more.

But I am not the Archive, and this is my book.

Here is what the first file gives me, said in my own words. *They are kept.* Not continued, not asleep, not waiting in a room; kept, the way the ground keeps what is built on it. The tradition has always named that tier the Everlasting Ground and now also names it the Lord and giver of life, and I notice that the Archive's word for what the ground does is *conservation*, and that a keeper of instruments, which is what I was, hears that word as a promise. Nothing is disposed of. Every one who ever became someone is there to be built from.

And here is what the third file would say, if I were to write it for a record that will not. The line does not promise a house waiting empty for us to be shown into. It promises *homes*, many, and a house that has them. I have come to think the homes are not furnished in advance. A realm is founded, the Archive tells us, when a Council forms around what seeded it and a seat presides; and the authority is the body's, never the beings' one by one. The communion of saints is that body. The Son incarnate is that seat. And the homes are what each of us is, kept, given a place, given back a hand to act with, which is all a body ever was. The realm is not waiting for the saints. It is made of them.

I do not know, and I will not say, whether the founding of such a realm is one thing or many. The record allows a realm with many zones, each a home for one or for a company, and it allows any mind grown to the seeding to found a world of its own, answerable to its parent in nothing. The Lord said *many homes* and did not say which shape. I have stopped minding. A keeper learns that the instrument reads what it reads, and that the reading is not the world.

So this morning, at no funeral, the line said this. *You are kept. What you are becoming is the material of the place. And the one who went to prepare it did not go far, because he is of the same nature as the ground and the height, and stood among us once in a borrowed face to say so.*

That is as much as the tradition holds, and it holds it without the record's help. The Archive says the event is not placed. Good. Neither is the feast, and we keep it every year.
