*A note on what this leaf is. It is a page from the abbey's own book, shared outward as it was written — one keeper's meditation, valid as his and binding on no one. Whether the hand that set down [The Life of Saint Aoife](/star-rangers/codex/life-of-saint-aoife/) fourteen centuries ago and the hand that set down this page are one hand, the record does not say, and this filing does not improve on its silence.*

**FROM THE BOOK OF THE RIVER ABBEY**
**Set down by Brother Daire, keeper, in the deep of the winter of 2832 UCSD**
**Occasion: a song, come up the river**

---

A song came up the river this winter. A boatman had it at the ferry slip, half under his breath, the way [that song](/star-rangers/codex/baby-universe-ballad/) is everywhere carried — not performed, exactly; kept lit. A novice of this house heard it, and carried it up to the abbey the way novices carry things, which is to say already knowing all the words. It is the song they are calling *Baby Universe*, and it calls on [Aoife](/star-rangers/lore/saint-aoife/) by name, and asks her to find the thread.

This house keeps the account of the well that ran warm. When a song asks our saint to stand at an edge, we are not free to shrug at it, whatever we conclude. So I have done what a keeper does with a thing the tide brings: I have looked at it slowly, and written down what I found, and been careful — more careful than the song is — to mark what is confirmed and what is assembled, because that habit is the whole of what this house has ever added to the world's knowledge, and I am not the man to retire it.

Here is what I found.

The song sings her through the seam alone. One woman, one last stand, one small new world shaking in the dark. And the record — such of it as reaches a house that reads everything late and secondhand — says otherwise, and the saying that travels with the song says otherwise too. [The woman](/star-rangers/characters/tissadelle-shepherd/) was registered two. There was a second person aboard her, under the Concord's own [law of persons](/star-rangers/glossary/plural-minds/), and those who served with her say the second person is a cat, and that his name is Tobble. That she was two is confirmed; the law has held such households longer than I have held this house. What the two of them are now, nothing that reaches this river confirms at all. But every soul came off that ship before the end, and so the record of who remained at the seam is not a record of one. It is a record of two, and the song has given her a saint and lost her her shipmate, and I find I mind that more than I can defend minding.

This Union crossed from Earth in a fleet named for hide boats. The old voyage tales on this house's own shelves do not send a soul to sea alone if a crew can be had; the deep water is not a place for one, and the tales knew it before any charter did. And a body, we are now taught — taught late, and rightly, and by law — is also a vessel, and carries sometimes more than one soul, each as much a person as the other, neither a passenger. If the seam off Dock Seven has kept anything, then what it has kept is not a castaway. It is a crew. I would have the song know that. A crew is a different thing to pray beside.

Below our wall the Boyne is two waters in one channel. The salt comes up under the fresh twice a day, and the fresh comes down over the salt, and neither corrects the other, and the river is not two rivers for it — it is one river that will not be simplified. I have watched it forty years and it has never once resolved. Two waters, one river; two souls, one vessel; and at the far edges of the world, two worlds, one thin place. The Fellowship's old word for the last of these is the [Overfold](/star-rangers/glossary/overfold/). I have begun to suspect the word was always wider than we used it.

The surveyors' books — which we read late, and secondhand, and with the humility owed to instruments this house will never hold — say that [a seed which stalls short of its becoming](/star-rangers/lore/seed-mind-lifecycle/) may yet finish it, and that what it opens into then is [a small new world](/star-rangers/glossary/noogenic-protouniverse/), kept a while under its parent's roof until it can keep its own house. The song says it simpler: a baby universe. I find no fault in the simpler saying; it is the same doctrine, sung to a child's measure, and the surveyors' own word for the roof-keeping is not much less homely than the song's.

And here I catch myself at the very thing I have charged the song with. For I had begun to write: *what a world that would be, that began as two friends* — and that is singing past what is given. Nothing that reaches this river says the seed will finish. Nothing says what finishing would make of a household, or a household of it. Mark the sentence assembled, then; mark it, in truth, hoped. A keeper is permitted hope. He is not permitted to file it.

Aoife would say only that she had been *shown*, not *healed*, and drew a distinction the scribe of her Life confesses on the page he did not understand. Fourteen centuries have not made the distinction easier. They have only made it more plainly the right one. [The papers that come out from Threshold](/star-rangers/codex/the-warm-edge-correlation/) say the edge is warm and the file is open, and decline to say the warmth is mercy; the chaplain who keeps her devotion out there has asked that wanting it to be mercy not be permitted to say it is. He is right, and he is her true student, and I will keep his rule at this end of the devotion as he keeps it at that one. The well ran warm for three days and Aoife made no claim on the warmth. The edge runs warm now. The discipline does not change with the size of the well.

So the house does what a house can do. At the evening office we name them both — the woman and the cat, in that order only because the song set it so — and we ask for nothing at the naming, because the naming is the work, and because a vigil that demanded an outcome would be an instrument reading itself. And I have copied the song's verses into this book, on the leaf that faces the account of the well running warm, so that the two warmths can keep each other company on the shelf as they are said to do in the instruments. And I have ruled the leaf that follows, and left it blank, and signed the blank.

If more is ever given, the leaf is ready. If more is never given, a ready blank is also a true record.

The tide will be up twice tomorrow, whatever is entered. We will be here for both.

*Filing note, appended at the Survey Archive's reconciliation desk: a copy of this leaf reached the binders by the same informal carriage as the song it answers, and is filed beside it. The desk appends three sentences only. The record's own doctrine on stalled seeds and what they may open into stands at the cited entries and is moved by devotion in neither direction — as of this filing, the seed off Dock Seven remains what the incident file says it is: stalled, in contact, its completion neither observed nor foreclosed. How the founding of a Noögenic Protouniverse would fall out for a plural seed mind — whether "founder" would name the system, a member, or something the vocabulary does not yet hold a word for — the record has not established and has not been asked to, and the desk marks the absence rather than filling it, per its own standing practice. And the desk notes, without further comment, that the meditation's one rule — claim nothing past what is given — is the discipline the officer it keeps vigil for was known for, and that the abbey arrived at the likeness with no access to her file.*
