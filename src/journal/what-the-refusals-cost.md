A refusal is usually written up as a virtue. [What the Record Refuses](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/) lists every shortcut this setting declines and the physics it keeps instead, and [Special Pleading](/star-rangers/journal/special-pleading/) says why I hold the line on magic and petitionary prayer: an exception made on request is the one thing the whole system is built to refuse. Both pages are about what the rules are for. Neither asks what they cost. That is the other column of the ledger, and a writer who never fills it in has not really chosen anything, only preferred something.

So, plainly: two refusals, each of which closed off a whole family of stories.

## What time travel would have given

The record refuses time travel outright. Causality flexes marginally and never breaks; nothing reaches backward; and in the [Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/), *the past is static*, one settled sequence admitting no revision by anyone. Four kinds of story go with it.

**The undo.** Going back to prevent the thing is one of the oldest engines in the genre, because it turns regret into a plan. Here regret stays regret. Nothing that happened in this record can be made not to have happened, by anyone, at any tier.

**The past as a place.** No one in this record can stand beside Saint Aoife at the thorn well and watch what she saw. The past reaches the present only as a document, and every document has someone behind it.

**The loop.** The self-causing object, the message from a future self, the plot that closes on its own beginning: all of that structure is unavailable. When I ask an ending to change how the beginning reads, it has to do so in the reader's understanding, because it cannot do so in the events.

**The mixed era.** A knight, a pilot and a machine from three different centuries, in one room, arguing. A good deal of the genre's comedy and much of its pathos come from that room, and it cannot be built here.

## What magic would have given

Magic is refused as a category. The governing sentence closes the [physics comparison](/star-rangers/lore/physics-comparison/): *the difference from the real cosmos is not magic. It is architecture.* Three things go with it.

**A will that acts directly on the world.** The spell, the curse, the blessing that lands. In fantasy, wanting something badly enough, with the right words, is a way of changing things. No one in this record can do that.

**The bargain.** Much of the best fantasy is about what power costs the one who uses it: the price named in advance and paid at the worst moment. Without the power there is no price of that kind to pay.

**The inner made visible.** Magic lets a story show guilt as a curse that can be seen and grace as a real change in the world. Here the experience can be a fact, and what it was an experience *of* cannot. A character may hear an answer to a prayer; the record will not say the prayer was answered.

And a fourth thing, less often counted: a readership. Some readers come for exactly these stories and will not stay for the substitutes, however well made.

## Where some of it went

A refusal moves a story more often than it deletes one, and four of these have moved rather than gone.

**The past came back as the record.** If the past cannot be visited, it has to be kept, and keeping became the setting's central institution. [The Archive](/star-rangers/lore/the-archive/) is what a world builds when nobody can go back and check. The Cascade page says it exactly: what changes about the past *is how it is remembered, interpreted and understood*, and that changes in the present. Saint Aoife reaches the present twice, as [a Life](/star-rangers/codex/life-of-saint-aoife/) and [a legend](/star-rangers/codex/the-legend-of-saint-aoife/), each with its author attached, and the gap between them is the story. The time-travel story becomes a story about evidence.

**The will came back as character.** A [point of view](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/#through-not-as) may hold an experience the narration does not endorse, and that is where prayer, vision and the answered call now live. What reaches the world from above it does so through structure, design, avatars, Champions and prophets, all of them bounded. The spell is gone, and in its place are people who keep going without a guarantee, which turns out to be a story about faith in the exact sense, a belief held in proportion to what supports it.

**The impossible came back inside minds.** An inner world is the one place in this record where magic and time travel can seem to happen. Tissadelle's is built like a high-fantasy role-playing game, and in it a working can seem to work and a past can seem to be walked back into. Nothing leaks out of it: what happens there is filed as the experience of the people whose interior it is, never as an event in the world. And the address does not travel: an inner world that becomes a universe of its own becomes a world, and its magic is downgraded and normalized in the crossing: the enchanted things arrive as their ordinary counterparts, not destroyed but no longer enchanted. It means the fantasy story was not refused outright. It was given an address, and the address has an edge.

**The marvel came back as physics.** The mythic register stayed and the mechanism changed. A dragon may fly where the air is dense and the gravity low, and owes nobody an apology; it also has a diet. Strangeness is allowed as long as someone can say how it works.

## What does not come back

Two losses do not move anywhere, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

The first is the undo itself. Nothing in this record substitutes for a second chance at the past: an inner world can seem to offer one, and the world outside is unchanged by it. Relativity would still let a traveller's clock run slow against home, so the record keeps forward travel of a kind, but forward is the only direction, and it is no help with what has already happened.

The second is the will acting directly. Nothing replaces wanting something hard enough that the world changes. This is the larger loss of the two, and the one a fantasy reader will feel first.

## What it bought

The refusals pay for themselves in three ways, and only the first was planned.

A loss that cannot be reversed has weight. The endings I care about most are recognitions, and a recognition is worth something only because the thing recognised cannot now be changed.

A single past makes a single record possible. There are no rival timelines, so the Archive does not have to arbitrate between versions of what happened, only between accounts of it.

And a mystery with no remedy stays a mystery. Magic would have given every shadow in this record a counter-spell, and the shadows would have become problems. As it is, they stay [marked](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/) and not solved.

That is the trade, stated as a ledger rather than a defence. It makes for a quieter book than either genre usually allows. I think it is the book I meant to write. But the cost was real, and it belongs on the page beside the reasons.
