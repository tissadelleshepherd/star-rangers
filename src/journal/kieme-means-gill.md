*Kieme* is the ordinary German noun for a gill. The fish organ. It is not obscure, not archaic, and not the sort of thing you need a dictionary to reach — it is a word German children learn in a biology lesson.

It is also the name of the second tier of the Cosmic Cascade, the one the devotional traditions call the Holy Triumvirate. So the most theologically weighted noun in this work is, to a German reader, a bit of a fish.

I already knew this. I checked it on the fourth of August, wrote down the collisions in full, and kept the name deliberately. The reasoning is still on file and I still agree with it: a title is a front door and gets searched bare, so it needs to survive a search engine; a glossary term is met in context, on the page, by someone already reading. Nobody arrives at *Kieme* by typing it into a search box. And chasing a coinage that collides with nothing in no language is a game you cannot win — every pronounceable string is a word somewhere or a small brand somewhere else. I had proved that to myself the hard way, some weeks earlier, with a graveyard of invented names that all turned out to be taken.

So when I came back to it this week, I expected to confirm the same verdict and move on.

What I found instead was that I had been wrong about which kind of problem it was.

The problem is not that *Kieme* means gill in German. The problem is structural, and it was sitting one entry away the whole time. **Conseleme — the third tier — has a living devotional name: the Everlasting Ground.** Kieme's equivalent, the Holy Triumvirate, is marked *legacy*. Which means that of the three tiers of the Upper Structure, exactly one had no current name that survives being translated. That one is Kieme, and that is why German lands on the fish. There was nothing else for it to land on.

Once it is put that way the repair is obvious, and it is not a rename.

**Titles translate. Coinages transliterate.** That is the whole of it. A translator meeting *the Everlasting Ground* renders the sense and it arrives intact in any language you like. A translator meeting *Kieme* has nothing to render, so it arrives as a sound — and a sound is at the mercy of whatever that sound already means where it lands. Renaming trades one collision for an unknown one. Adding a title removes the exposure entirely, in every language at once, and costs nothing that already exists.

So Kieme keeps its name and gains three titles, in three registers. **The Silent Origin** is the devotional one, the direct counterpart to the Everlasting Ground, and it takes the constraint for the name: the tier does not speak, has never spoken, and the traditions called it after what they met rather than what they wanted. **The Undivided** is the formal title, standing where the Most Holy Triumvirate stood — it refuses the count without refusing the picture, which matters, because one and three and many are rival images here and not rival answers. And **the First Condition** is what the Archive says when it declines both of those, deliberately cool and undevotional.

That is the tidy part. The part I did not expect was better.

Having three names for one thing looks like untidiness. It looks like the sort of accumulation you clean up when you notice it, and my instinct was to reduce it. But when I tried to write down *why* the tier has several names, the answer turned out to be the most interesting sentence in the entry.

Nothing there has ever spoken. It permits no dialogue with anything time-bound, no intervention that would identify it, and it can only be inferred from what holds true everywhere. **So nobody was ever told what to call it.** Every tradition that arrived at it named it from what it could see, from where it happened to be standing — and the record holds the results side by side rather than picking a winner. A tier with exactly one settled name would be a tier that somebody had been told about.

Which extends further than I first wrote it. These are all *human* names. Every species that has inferred the Cascade has named it out of its own psychology, its own social forms and its own language, none of which are ours. What survives translation is the striking bit: the names agree about almost nothing, and the constraints agree almost exactly. That is what you would expect of something real that has never spoken, and I have let the Archive say so and then decline to draw anything warmer from it.

I have spent a fair amount of this year tidying up after myself — retiring names, sweeping terminology, reconciling entries that had drifted apart. It is mostly good work. But this one was a reminder that the reflex has a failure mode, and the failure mode is expensive: I went in to clean up a mess and the mess turned out to be the evidence.

The untidiness was the clue. I had very nearly filed it as the fault.

*Postscript, 18 September 2026.* The tradition no longer calls this tier the Holy Triumvirate. On the mapping ruled that evening it calls Kieme the Eternal Son and, in its own register, [the First Word](/star-rangers/glossary/kieme/) — set against the Silent Origin rather than beside it. The gill stays, for the reasons above, and one more: a word that carries two meanings is the ordinary condition of a name, not a fault in one.
