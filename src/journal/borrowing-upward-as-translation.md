*This entry follows a day's decisions about derivative work — what a good reader comment could become, and where a television or streaming adaptation would sit — and records the principle that Dermot drew from them: that borrowing from a derivative work into the story is, in principle, the same shape as a translation the cosmology already describes. It is written for the peer author and the student of the writing, and it makes no claim about the story's mechanism.*

The licence for this work has a rung between fan fiction and canon. A **chartered work** is a derivative work by another hand that has been individually approved: endorsed, registered here rather than hosted here, and governed by one rule, *canon binds it; it does not bind canon*. Since today the rung is also a ceiling. Any adaptation for television, a streaming service or cinema is chartered at most, whatever its scale or the author's involvement, and never canon. The screen never becomes the record, and the record never adjusts to the screen.

Stated that way, the rule looks like a wall. It is not, and the interesting part is why. The rule stops an obligation, not a choice. Nothing an adaptation does can oblige the published story to follow it. But if the author sees something in an adaptation and chooses to write it into the story, that is an ordinary act of writing canon, and the adaptation has bound nothing. Borrowing upward is permitted. Being bound is not. The question for the craft is what governs the borrowing, and it turns out the cosmology had already answered it, in its own words, for a different case.

## What conservation keeps

The record's word for what happens to a mind when it stops is **Conceptual Conservation**: no coherent mind is lost, because any identity structure that once cohered remains an admissible state at the Conceptual layer, retained without being continued. The two halves of that sentence are the whole doctrine. Nothing is deleted. Nothing goes on. A conserved mind does not act, does not initiate and cannot be addressed, because that layer permits no direct action without coupling to the layers below.

A chartered work is kept in exactly that register. It is kept as filed, attributed to its author, never continued by the record and never load-bearing for it. If the published story later contradicts it, the chartered work is superseded rather than withdrawn: it is still there, still whole, and no longer current. *Canon binds it; it does not bind canon* is the same asymmetry as a retained structure that cannot act on the layers beneath it. Conservation is the register a chartered work lives in. That is the first correspondence, and it is the one that makes the other two possible, because a thing that is fully kept can be drawn on without being consumed.

## What translation carries

The cosmology also holds a second, separate idea, which the glossary is careful never to run together with the first. Many traditions in the record hold that **Kieme**, the second tier of the Cascade, may elect to translate an essence into the Spiritual layer after death, choosing which aspects are carried. Conservation is universal, unelected and structural; translation, where it is held to happen at all, is elective and selective. One keeps everything by default. The other carries something, on a decision, and carries aspects rather than the whole.

This is the shape of borrowing upward. It is the author's act, on the author's election, and it carries aspects, never the work. A design, a line of dialogue, a way a scene was staged, a reading of a character that the page had not made explicit: any of these can be carried into the story, and the chartered work they came from stays where it was, whole, in the register it was kept in. The translation does not diminish what was conserved. It never could, because conservation was never conditional on anything, least of all on whether something was later carried up.

## What is left behind

The third correspondence is the useful one, because it supplies the test. The record's collective name for its predatory pattern-entities is the **Unfounded**, and the glossary defines the class by membership rather than by shape. Every founded thing is created and defined by the strata above it; something decided what it would be, and that decision is what gives a kind its membership. An Unfounded thing wears the shape of a founded kind without being a member of it. It is underwritten by no origin condition, so there is no decision underneath for a category to record. The shape is accurate. The membership is not.

Read as craft, that is a precise description of the trope default: the stock beat that arrives first and wears the world's clothes. A showdown in a Ranger uniform, a chase where the problem ends when something is shot, a fantasy mechanism sitting in hard-science furniture, a species that is a hood and a silhouette. Each has the world's shape. None has an origin condition in the record, because nothing in the cosmology or the published story decided it. And that is exactly the aspect a translation declines. What can be carried up is what is founded, what has a decision underneath it in the world's own terms. What is left behind is what only looked as if it belonged.

So the test before borrowing is one question, and it is the same question the record asks of anything that wants to be canon: *what is the origin condition?* If the borrowed aspect can be grounded in the cosmology and the published story as they stand, it is founded, and carrying it up is writing canon in the ordinary way. If it cannot, it is Unfounded however well it played on screen, and it stays in the register it came from, kept and never continued.

## The boundary

One boundary keeps the principle honest, and it is the same line the record draws everywhere it touches the devotional. Kiemeic translation is what traditions hold. The Archive's own position is narrower: no instrument reaches the Conceptual or Spiritual layer, Conceptual Conservation is the only mechanism it writes, and it declines to rule on what the devotional words name. The craft rule borrows the picture, not the claim. Whether translation is real in the world is a question the record leaves where it found it, and the principle here works either way, because what it needs from the analogy is the shape and not the fact: kept by default, carried on election, and only what is founded.

That is also why this entry lives here and not in the glossary. It is a rule about how the work is made. The cosmology supplied the vocabulary, which is what a cosmology built by specification is for, and the vocabulary turned out to describe the author's own position toward derivative work better than the licence's plainer words did. Conservation says nothing is lost. Translation says the author chooses. The Unfounded says what the choosing leaves behind.
