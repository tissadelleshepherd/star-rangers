An invented cosmology is a confession. Nobody encodes rules they don't believe
into a universe they control completely — there is no editor to appease and no
tradition to defer to, so whatever laws survive are the ones you actually hold.
I have spent months building this setting's laws, and this week, reading them
back, I finally saw what they had been saying about me all along.

I don't believe in prayer or magic — *as special pleading*. The qualifier is
doing the precise work. What I reject is the transactional form: asking the
lawful order to make a local exception on my behalf. And that is exactly what
this setting refuses, everywhere, at every level. [Kieme](/star-rangers/glossary/kieme/)
admits no dialogue and permits no selective intervention; the Archive's whole
method is built on the absence of exceptions. Magic gets the same verdict for
the same reason — magic is special pleading against physics — which is why the
house rule says every deviation must be lawful, proposed through the glossary,
never improvised in prose. It only now strikes me that anti-special-pleading
is literally one of my craft laws: an exception improvised in the moment,
whether by a character or by the author, is the one thing the whole system is
designed to refuse. The writing discipline and the belief turn out to be the
same commitment.

But look at what I didn't refuse, because it's the more interesting half. The
[Unfounded](/star-rangers/glossary/unfounded/) doctrine is faith in ultimate
goodness stated as ontology: in this universe, **good is founded and evil is
not**. Evil has no origin condition, no design behind it, no membership in any
kind — counterfeit all the way down, grounded in nothing, dissolving the
moment it stops being tolerated. That is a very old position wearing new
clothes — the privation theory, evil as parasitic on being rather than a rival
to it — and I arrived at it by worldbuilding rather than by doctrine, which is
perhaps the most honest way to arrive anywhere. Add the
[Cosmic Limitation on Evil](/star-rangers/codex/cosmic-limitation-on-evil/) —
every debt repaid, on a schedule no one may predict — and what you have is
providence at the level of *structure* rather than *events*: goodness
guaranteed ultimately, never locally, never on request.

And notice what the setting's traditions keep once petition is ruled out. They
still name the tier. They still keep the shamrock. Prayer survives here not as
requisition but as orientation — witness and naming toward something that will
never answer and is still worth facing. I don't think that's a consolation
prize. It may be the only form of prayer that survives intellectual honesty,
and I find I built a cosmology that strikes the transactional kind and
preserves this one carefully, without ever having decided to.

One question stays open, in the canon and, I suspect, in me: whether the
ultimate goodness is a *someone*. Constitutive or discretionary; office or
officeholder. The record files that question
[exactly where invariant-only inference cannot reach](/star-rangers/codex/the-better-side-of-intervention/),
which is where it lives for everyone, on either side of the page. Faith in
ultimate goodness without certainty about a chooser behind it is not a halfway
position. It is the position the evidence supports from inside time — and I
appear to have built an entire fictional Archive that agrees with me.

The repository's own description has carried the creed from the start: *the
stars call us forward with hope; to protect what is good and to see what is
true.* The rest of the setting is just the working-out.
