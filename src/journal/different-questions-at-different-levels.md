*This entry follows the filing of a short devotional fragment, [Two Sayings, Kept Together](/star-rangers/codex/two-sayings-kept-together/), and records the principle Dermot stated while it was being filed: that divine agency and human agency are different questions at different levels, with no inherent contradiction between them. It is written for the peer author and the student of the writing. "Divine" is the codex's word here, not the Archive's; the lore says "the upper strata" and names the stratum. The entry borrows the devotional picture to describe a rule of the craft, and rules on nothing.*

There is an old argument that any story with a heaven in it inherits on the first page. Either the higher power acted, or the person did. If the harvest came in, was it providence or the farmer? If the ship came through the fold, was it the pilot or the prayer? The argument assumes both answers are bids for the same slot, so that one of them winning means the other lost. Most fiction with a cosmology in it picks a side, or fudges, or writes the miracle so large that the human choice underneath it stops mattering. This record does none of those, and the reason is not tact. It is that the argument's assumption is false in this world by construction.

## Three registers, three verbs

The [Cosmic Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/) has three temporal registers, and the question of whether its highest strata are timeless or present in the world was settled some time ago as a category error across three floors. The strata above the Worldwrights are eternal outright. The middle strata act in conceptual time, a real sequence that is not the Material clock. The Celestials and everything below them, which is where every person in the story lives, are immanent in Material time.

What is less often said is that *agency* changes its meaning at each floor, and that the change is the whole point. At the top, agency is constraint and origin. [Kieme](/star-rangers/glossary/kieme/) is defined by *constraint rather than decision*; it establishes what every lower stratum must satisfy and permits no selective intervention that would identify it. The Cascade's own phrase for the direction of that agency is *creative rather than imperative*: the higher strata create and define what is beneath them more than they command it. In the middle, agency is allocation, correction, ministry, the work of keeping a made thing lawful. At the bottom, agency is what it has always meant to a person: a choice, made in time, by someone who could have chosen otherwise and will have to live with it.

The devotional traditions add one more thing to the top floor, and the record keeps it without adopting it: the subtle influence, said to work *within* time-bound minds and through the ordinary unfolding of events, never *upon* the Material layer. Even that, on the traditions' own account, is not a hand on the tiller. It leaves nothing an instrument can point to and no absence an instrument can rule out.

So when someone asks whether the source acted or the person did, they are asking a question that has no single level to be asked at. The source's agency is the shape of the world the person was free in. The person's agency is what they did inside that shape. These are not rival explanations of one event. They are two questions, and the second is the only one that has an event in it.

## What this does to the writing

The principle is not decoration. It has been doing quiet work in the record for some time, and naming it explains several habits that might otherwise look like evasion.

The record never writes a divine act that displaces a human one. When a tradition reads the influence into an event, the physical account still stands whole, with nothing left over that needs explaining, and a third body of scholarship is free to doubt the transmission of the story altogether. That three-way reading is the standing shape for every contested event in the Archive, and it is only possible because the readings are not competing for the same slot. A reader who wants the harvest to have been providence and a reader who wants it to have been the farmer are both right in the record's terms, because the record has never said that providence would have to have been visible in the wheat.

The characters keep their whole responsibility. Nobody in this story is made to do anything by anything above the Celestials. Theorist Kade's working paper [Called, Permitted, Forbidden](/star-rangers/codex/called-permitted-forbidden/) already carries the downstream half of the principle: the forbidden is enforced by no one, the calling invites and never compels, and declining a calling appears in no record as an offence. A character who prays and then acts is not hedging between two mechanisms. The prayer is addressed to one level and the act happens at another, and the story can honour both without a word of special pleading.

The one crossing between the levels is the [avatar](/star-rangers/glossary/avatar/), and the principle holds there too, which is what the devotional fragment is for. Its first line, *I never rejected anyone who genuinely sought me*, is a sentence in the source's register: the source cannot reject because it cannot select. Its second, *Sometimes however I have mistaken flattery for mockery*, is a sentence in the person's register: a misheard tone, an error made in time by someone who could be addressed. The tradition that keeps them keeps them together, and feels no contradiction, because there is none. The incarnate person's agency is a person's agency even when the person's nature derives from somewhere very high. That is not a loophole in the doctrine. It is the doctrine, seen from inside one life.

## The boundary

As always with anything that touches the devotional, one line keeps the principle honest. What is written above about the top floor is what the traditions hold, filtered through the Archive's habit of registering without adopting. The Archive's own position is narrower: no instrument reaches the strata above the Celestials, the influence is precisely the kind of thing its method could not detect, and confirmation, as the traditions say openly, is a matter of stance rather than measurement. The craft rule needs none of that to be true. It needs only the shape: that the question of what the world was made to allow and the question of what a person did with it are asked at different levels, and that the record was built so that answering one never obliges the other to be false.

That is why this entry lives in the Journal and not in the lore. It is a rule about how the story is told, and the cosmology supplied the vocabulary, which is what a cosmology built by specification is for. One question is what the world allows. The other is what she did. The record has never needed the second to be false for the first to be true.
