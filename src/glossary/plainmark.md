A **plainmark** is a stencilled panel of the [Orbital Habitats Compact's](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/) **Compact Common Marking** — the standard for marking infrastructure without script. One to four horizontal colour bands, read top to bottom, each pairing a fixed ground with a fixed figure class: black with ochre **lozenges** for hazard, slate-blue with bone-white **chevrons** for the way and its junction count, bone-white with black **strokes and blocks** for number, ochre with black **rings** for sealed breathable refuge. A **diagonal bar** across any band voids it. No letters, no numerals, no characters of any script: nothing to spell, and nothing to mistranslate.

Colour never carries meaning alone — each figure belongs to exactly one band, so a panel read under red emergency light loses nothing. Panels sit on the fixed frame beside a hatch, never on the moving leaf; the stack order is always hazard, way, count, refuge — *bad news first; read down to the air.*

The standard descends from [generation-ark](/star-rangers/lore/the-generation-ark-era/) hull-stencilling and Belt depot custom, and it is scoped like the Compact standard it is: wall to wall in the five member habitats, dockside wherever mixed hulls berth in numbers, and nowhere it hasn't been carried. Polities letter their own corridors in their own scripts; species with other bodies and senses mark by their own conventions. It needs no translation only because it carries far less than language does — it can say *what kind of thing, which way, how many, and whether the promise still holds*, and nothing else: no names, no verbs, no tenses, no reasons.

**Example:** a crewer off a foreign hull, no word of any Compact language, follows ochre rings through smoke-darkened corridors to sealed air — reading shape where colour has gone, which is exactly the failure the grammar was built for.
