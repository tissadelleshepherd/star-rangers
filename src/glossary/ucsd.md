**Universal Concord Standard Date (UCSD)** is the civil dating standard of [the Solar System Concord](/star-rangers/lore/solar-system-concord/) — the shared spine that dates in the public record convert to, whatever notation they were first written in.

The count is continuous with old Earth's common-era civil calendar — no reset, one number line — renumbered by a fixed offset: **UCSD year = CE year + 200**. Converting is arithmetic, not a conversion table: a document that says *2140 CE* files as *2340 UCSD* and nothing more is required, and the count's zero point is given no in-world significance. The standard was adopted and named within the Concord's framework and applied retroactively to earlier dates, the way calendar reforms always are — the label is newer than most of the years it labels. How the count is actually kept — the unit, the ensemble of clocks, the loop-closing — is its own article: [Count and Calibration](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/).

Three things UCSD is not:

- **It is not the timeline.** There is one shared history; UCSD is one notation for it. Other civilisations keep their own reckonings for the same events — a source dating an event in its own calendar names the same moment a UCSD date names, and converting between notations is bookkeeping, not cosmology. An archive's conversion tables are craft, maintained the way any reference work is maintained.
- **It is not instantaneous.** There is [no faster-than-light communication](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/), so time, like every other kind of information, is *carried*: reference clocks are compared by physical transit, calibration certificates travel with courier runs, and a distant institution's clock is only ever as current as its last synchronisation. Distant records are transit-lagged, and their dates inherit the lag.
- **It is not perfectly uniform.** Relativistic time dilation between regions moving and sitting differently is real physics, and far-separated records accumulate small, predicted offsets. At the scales civil record-keeping cares about the effect is minimal: reconciliation applies the known corrections as a matter of routine, files the adjustment, and moves on. The whole point of making the corrections routine is that a discrepancy which *survives* them has earned an instrument's attention rather than a bookkeeper's.

- **The year turns at midwinter.** Early Winter and Deep Winter close a UCSD year and Late Winter opens the next, so *2831 UCSD, Deep Winter* is followed by *2832 UCSD, Late Winter* and then by *Early Spring*. A stamp that reads *Early Winter* belongs to the year whose autumn it follows.

**Example:** a field log written aboard a vessel dates itself by the ship's own clock; the archive that receives it files it in the author's notation with the UCSD conversion noted beside — the original is the document, the conversion is the courtesy.
