**Simian**, in record usage, means **any Earth-lineage primate that is not a human being** — the apes, the monkeys, and, by the record's deliberately generous drawing of the line, the lemurs, lorises and tarsiers a strict Earth taxonomist would have kept outside the word. The usage is a stipulation, and the record states it as one rather than inheriting it, because there was nothing coherent to inherit.

## Why the Word Had to Be Stipulated

Earth's own languages never settled a single word for this group, and the failure was structural rather than careless: **humanity sits inside the primate lineage, not beside it**, so "the primates except us" is a subtraction no natural grouping performs. Every candidate the old vocabularies offered either includes humans on inspection — *simian*, *anthropoid*, *hominid* all technically do — or cuts away too much, as *ape* and *monkey* both do. Earth science made its peace with the four-syllable phrase *non-human primates* and used it for centuries. The record, which files [Terran wildlife](/star-rangers/lore/crisis-centuries-terran-wildlife/) in earnest and cannot spend a phrase where a word will do, took the nearest word and fixed its scope by declaration: in these files, a simian is a non-human primate, the subtraction stated once and holding everywhere.

## A Lineage Word, Not a Shape Word

The stipulation earns its keep at exactly one boundary: **simian answers a question of descent, and *humanoid* answers a question of silhouette**, and the record [keeps the two questions apart on principle](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/). A chimpanzee is simian and shares humanity's actual ancestry; a [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) is humanoid — the [record's one other people on the human frame](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/) — and is no kind of simian, sharing with humanity no ancestor, no biosphere and no molecule of common descent. The two words cross cleanly and neither implies the other, which is the whole of their usefulness.

Worth noticing, because the record so rarely gets to say it: this is the one register in which a resemblance-word is legitimately a fact about descent. The standing caution — that *a name borrowed from an Earth animal describes the observers, never the ancestry* — exists for lineages outside Earth's biosphere. Inside a biosphere, the caution inverts. A simian genuinely is humanity's near kin, the family word is for once doing honest genealogical work, and within Earth's file "they resemble us" and "they are related to us" are the same sentence.

## Everyone Has This Problem

The dilemma the word solves is not a human peculiarity, and the record's species files show it recurring wherever there are species files to keep. **A sapient people does not arise beside its biosphere but inside it**, which means nearly every people on record shares its world with kin lineages that are capable, complex, and not people — forms close enough in build and behaviour that the resemblance is a fact of descent, and far enough in kind that filing them together would be the gravest category error the record knows. The [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) case is the cleanest on file: a vessik is built on the same physical principle as the people whose tunnels it lives in — same low frame, same substrate-hearing, same six-limbed answer to the same world — and is an animal, a burrow commensal, a companion and never a colleague. The Chthonari word that holds that line apart is as much a stipulation as *simian* is, made for the same reason: their language, like Earth's, grew up among the kin before the distinction needed legislating.

The operational edge of this is survey work. A first contact is, before anything else, an act of telling a world's people apart from that people's own near forms — and resemblance among kin lineages is one of the things that makes a sapient population *non-obvious*, which is precisely the case the [Frontier Transformation Protocols' sapience clause](/star-rangers/lore/frontier-transformation-protocols/) keeps its extended survey windows open for: a people is found rather than paved over, however much they resemble the animals beside them. The record's discipline here is the same one it applies [everywhere else](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/): kinship is a finding about descent, personhood is a finding about the mind, and no amount of the first ever establishes the second — a rule every sapient people on file turns out to have needed a word for, each inside its own family.

**Example:** A welfare filing describes a habitat's animal complement as "four felines, two canines, one simian." The one word settles, without a clause of qualification, that the third resident is an Earth primate, an animal rather than a person, and no relation whatever of any humanoid people aboard — three distinctions the old vocabulary needed a paragraph to hold apart.
