The [Cosmic Limitation on Evil](/star-rangers/codex/cosmic-limitation-on-evil/) is not in dispute: every entity that exists in service of evil dissolves once it no longer serves a higher purpose, and the suffering it caused is a debt [Kieme](/star-rangers/glossary/kieme/) alone repays. What's in dispute is whether anyone ever sees Kieme do it.

The Archive's answer is no. Its account of Kieme is built entirely on absence — no confirmed instance of selective intervention at the Material layer, ever, across any Concordant. The Limitation is the one exception the Archive tolerates, and it tolerates it uneasily, forever trying to rephrase "Kieme keeping the ledger" as a structural invariant rather than a deliberate act, because a deliberate act implies someone is watching who chooses to act.

This telling keeps the devotional tradition's answer instead: that watching is real, and it is not always silent. Where the Archive can only point to the shape evil leaves when it finally dissolves — the fading, the unravelling, the debt marked paid after the fact — this telling follows the moments devotional witnesses have always claimed to see directly: the harm that stops just short, the door that holds, the crew that walks out of a collapse that should have taken them. Not proof the Archive would accept. Proof the Archive was never going to be the one to see it.

## The Case This Telling Keeps Returning To

Devotional argument on this point is usually accused of resting on anecdote, so it is worth naming the one instance where the record itself declines to close the gap, and where the Archive's own filing does the devotional reading's work for it.

At Dock Seven in 2831 UCSD, an enforcement action fell on an unlawful process. It ended when the process ended, and it spared everyone who was not part of it — structurally, on the Archive's own account, and *without any finding about their conduct having been made.* Every material witness present recorded what answered the intrusion as dragons. Nothing in the Cascade has ever corrected the impression. No Ranger present was told anything at all.

Note the exact shape of what the Archive concedes there. Not that the survivors were judged and found worthy: the whole point of "scoped to a process" is that no judgement of persons occurred. They were not spared. They were *not included*. And this telling has never disputed that description — it is a better description than most devotional accounts manage. What it disputes is the confidence with which "not included" is offered as an explanation, when the record also holds that settled space's public account of why Ranger personnel survived rests on superior protocols and practical boundary knowledge, and that the surviving instrument record does not support it. Two accounts, one honest about being incomplete and one not, and the Rangers' own reputation built on the second. The devotional witness listening to that has heard this before: the door held, and everyone afterward explained the door.

This telling does not claim to know what stood at Dock Seven, and takes the Archive's mechanism as read. It claims only that *the harm stopped short*, and that a tradition which has always testified to exactly this — harm arriving at a limit it does not cross, and nobody afterward able to say why — is not being credulous when it declines to file the limit as a coincidence in the shape of a mercy. See [What Came Off the Ship (S05E02C03)](/star-rangers/seasons/s05/e02/s05e02c03/) and [The Accurate Account (S07E01C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s07/e01/s07e01c01/).

Nothing about the Upper Structure's other constraints changes here — no dialogue with time-bound entities, no message, no negotiation. Kieme does not explain itself in this telling any more than in the Archive's. It is simply witnessed, rather than only ever inferred.
