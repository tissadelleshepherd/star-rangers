Most first-contact records read like negotiations. The Federation of Sentient Beings' contact record with the Star Rangers reads like a supply manifest — because that is what it was.

The **Federation of Sentient Beings** is a voluntary confederation of member worlds, orbital habitats, and constituent peoples — organic, synthetic, and mixed — bound by a single working principle: any sentient being's claim to material survival and self-governance is valid on its own terms, without requiring that being to first prove itself human, humanoid, or familiar. It is not an empire, a market bloc, or a defence pact in the conventional sense, though elements of all three appear in its charter.

## Origins

The Federation predates the Star Rangers' contact with it by an uncertain span — its own member worlds date the founding compact differently depending on which calendar and which species' historical record is consulted, and the Federation has never insisted on reconciling them. What its member worlds agree on is the founding problem: a cluster of neighbouring systems, home to several sentient peoples with no shared biochemistry, no shared home star, and no realistic path to conquering one another, arrived independently at the same conclusion — that cooperation on trade, defence, and resource-sharing cost less than the alternative, and that no single member's internal governance was any other member's business.

First contact with the Star Rangers came through the Survey Corps' fold-route mapping operations, decades after the Rangers' 2723 UCSD charter. Federation vessels had already been operating stable trade routes between member systems for longer than humanity had known fold travel existed at all. The Rangers arrived expecting to make first contact. The Federation's boundary-monitoring outposts had already logged three Ranger survey passes before anyone crossed a comms channel to say so.

## Structure

The Federation has no capital world and no standing executive. Authority sits with a rotating assembly of member-world delegates, weighted so that no single species or system can out-vote the rest on matters of internal governance — only on the narrow set of compact-covered issues: mutual defence, trade-lane security, resource-sharing agreements, and joint disaster response. Everything else — law, culture, biology-specific accommodation — stays with the member world.

That structural modesty is deliberate and openly stated in Federation doctrine: a central authority strong enough to arbitrate between wildly different biochemistries and governance traditions would, sooner or later, be strong enough to overrule them. The Federation would rather stay weak at the centre than solve that problem by picking a winner.

The Federation keeps a military of its own, and it is held the only way a confederation without a central authority can hold one: as contingents contributed by member worlds, serving together under a mandate the members agree for the occasion, commanded by an officer the members appoint for it, and going home when the mandate ends. There is no standing Federation force and no Federation staff college; there are member forces that have learned to stand beside one another. Its posture follows from its composition. A force lent by many worlds to a purpose none of them owns is built for interposition — standing between parties, observing, holding a line agreed by others, supporting a member's own forces without replacing them — and not for conquest, which no contributing world would lend a contingent to. What it costs is what such forces always cost: a mandate written by committee, contingents that answer first to home, and a commander whose authority is exactly as wide as the agreement beneath it. The Federation regards that as the price of the other thing, and the [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/), who are not a military of any kind, regard it as the nearest thing in settled space to a military built on their own premise.

## Relationship with the Star Rangers

Where the Celtic Union of Planets maintains a standing information-sharing agreement with the Star Rangers' Survey Corps, the Federation's relationship runs deeper and older: a standing material-support compact, renewed by assembly vote each generation, under which member worlds contribute refined materials, fabrication capacity, and fold-route survey data toward Ranger operations — the same kind of voluntary outer-station contribution the Rangers' founding charter always assumed would come from many sources, not one.

The Federation also holds the longest-standing of the non-voting seats on the Rangers' [Oversight Council](/star-rangers/lore/the-oversight-council/), first filled in 2791 UCSD and filled since by a delegate the assembly appoints and replaces at each renewal of the compact — a seat that hears every inquiry and decides none, which is the only kind of seat on anyone else's council a confederation of this shape would want. The compact does not buy the Federation influence over Ranger conduct. The Rangers' unconditional neutrality applies to the Federation exactly as it applies to every government, faction, and unaffiliated world — a term the Federation's founding delegates are on record as finding entirely reasonable, since it is the same principle their own charter runs on internally.

## The Concord Is Not a Member, and Means Not to Be

The [Solar System Concord](/star-rangers/lore/solar-system-concord/) — and humanity generally, in every polity of it the Federation deals with — is **not a full member of the Federation, and characteristically so.** The relationship is warm and busy: standing trade agreements, friendly diplomatic relations, delegations exchanged and renewed without incident for as long as the two have known each other. What it has never been, and what no human government has ever seriously proposed it become, is membership. The record files the reason as a fact about human character rather than a grievance on either side: **humanity keeps its own counsel and its own helm** — it prefers treaties it can sign to an assembly it would sit in, and it would rather be the Federation's friend at full sovereignty than its member at any discount to it.

The Federation, of all institutions, has never pressed. A confederation founded on the sentence that *no member's internal governance is any other member's business* can hardly object to a neighbour extending the same principle one step further and governing itself outside the assembly altogether — and Federation delegates have been recorded observing, with evident approval, that the human posture is their own founding principle worn as a foreign policy. Nor is staying outside one posture rather than several: the [Cerebraun Hegemony](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/) also sits outside the Federation, on terms the record describes as correct rather than warm, which makes the human arrangement the demonstration that *outside* is a distance, not a temperature. And the seam the whole geometry turns on stays where it always was: the Federation's deep relationship — the material compact above — runs to the [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/formation-of-star-rangers/), not to the Concord, and can only exist because the Rangers are not the Concord. The Federation trades with humanity's governments, and trusts humanity's one institution that has none.

## Member Worlds

Federation membership spans systems with no shared star and, in several cases, no shared biochemistry. Materially, the Federation's most significant single contributor to the Ranger support compact is [Aspenar](/star-rangers/lore/planets/aspenar/), a rocky, resource-rich world whose refined ore and alloy exports underwrite a disproportionate share of the compact's fabrication capacity. [Verdance](/star-rangers/lore/planets/verdance/) anchors the compact's diplomatic standing through its Fourfold Accord, [Prismere](/star-rangers/lore/planets/prismere/) supplies the compact's fold-corridor expertise through its native [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) astrogation guilds, and [the Undersong Belt](/star-rangers/lore/undersong-belt/) contributes fold-scaffold engineering expertise through its native [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) — individual Chthonari also serve directly in the Star Rangers' own Engineering Corps in numbers well out of proportion to their population, a career choice rather than a compact obligation. Ilenne, member world of the indefinitely-lived [Mnemari](/star-rangers/lore/mnemari/), contributes disproportionately to the assembly's own internal function rather than to the material compact: Mnemari mediators and treaty-witnesses are routinely requested for assembly sessions revisiting old, contested compacts, on the strength of a memory that cannot drift the way institutional record-keeping can over enough centuries. [Cirrane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/cirrane/), the gas giant home of the [Ollune](/star-rangers/lore/ollune/), contributes neither material nor expertise in the ordinary sense but procedure: Ollune practice in drafting agreements that hold between parties who cannot confer, developed on a world where neighbouring cities are within speaking range for a few hours every several years, was adopted into Federation compact procedure generations ago. Cirrane is also the clearest test of the Federation's founding sentence — a member world whose people could not have presented themselves to anyone, having no fire, no metal, and no surface to launch from. Thavren, the deep-aired haze world of the [Ovruhn](/star-rangers/lore/ovruhn/), carries the compact's **joint disaster response** obligations out of proportion to its size: a people who hear the living through smoke, water and structure, and whose low under-song steadies a crowded shelter deck as plain acoustics, do abroad what they have always done at home, and an assembly that keeps no standing executive has found no cause to formalise what Ovruhn crews simply keep doing. Most other member worlds remain undocumented in Solar System record outside Federation registries, pending further survey contact.
