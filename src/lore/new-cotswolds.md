The Archive holds one settlement file it declines to write in the present tense. New Cotswolds is not lost, not abandoned, and not in any dispute. It is simply a long way past the last thing anybody can confirm about it.

## Geography Is Not Distance

The reason a human settlement exists in another galaxy at all is the reason most people find hardest to accept: under [fold transit](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/), proximity is not what makes a place reachable. A fold requires an admissible corridor and a [Concordant](/star-rangers/glossary/concordant/)-compatible destination, and where both hold, the separation being folded is not a meaningful figure. A neighbouring star with no admissible geometry is unreachable. A galaxy with one is not.

The Evenlode corridor is such a case — a single admissible route, surveyed once, holding since. It is not a shortcut anybody chose for its destination. It is the destination that happened to be on the end of it.

## Who Went, and Why It Looks Like This

The founding party came out of the [New London Space Habitat](/star-rangers/lore/new-london-space-habitat/), which had by then spent five centuries at Earth–Moon L5 doing the one job its charter gave it: holding a cultural inheritance intact through an emergency that had long since ended. A preservation institution offered a corridor to somewhere nobody could follow is a preservation institution presented with its own argument, and the record of the debate survives in full at New London because they kept it, as they keep everything.

What that produced at the far end is not a reconstruction and was never meant as one. It is a working agricultural country built by people who had an explicit brief about what they were carrying, which is the whole of the difference between this entry and [Winterbourne](/star-rangers/lore/planets/winterbourne/). On Winterbourne the record cannot establish whether the pattern was intended. Here the intent is a filed document with a date on it.

## The Stone

The settled band sits on an oolitic limestone shelf — a carbonate rock that splits along its bedding into flat slabs, weathers to a deep honey colour, and is soft enough to cut green from the quarry and hard enough to build with once it has dried.

Every consequence follows from that and not from taste. The walls are stone because stone is what comes out of the fields. The field boundaries are drystone walls rather than hedges for the same reason. The roofs are stone tile, and steeply pitched, because a stone tile is heavy and a shallow pitch would neither carry the load nor shed the water. The colour is not a choice at all.

Villages named in the last confirmed return: **Aldbury Magna** and **Aldbury Parva**, **Sheercombe**, **Netherwold**, **Stow Ambrey**, and **Coldharbour**, with the market at **Chipping Aldbury**. The naming is unremarkable and that is the point of it: *chipping* is a market, a *combe* is a valley, a *wold* is high open ground, and a **coldharbour** is a shelter with no one keeping it — a place to get out of the weather, and nothing more. People who name a settlement Coldharbour are not being picturesque. They are telling the next traveller what is there.

The galaxy's name has the same root as the settlement's. Somebody aboard worked out that *Cotswold* carries *wold* inside it, and the corridor's far end went into the log as Wolds End before anyone had seen the ground. It stuck, in the way a name written down early does.

## Why the File Stops

There is [no faster-than-light communication](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/) — FTL channels transit matter and do not carry standalone information, so a message crosses no faster than a hull carrying it. Every archive in settled space is working from a transit-lagged record, and no entry in this one is lagged like this.

Four crossings are confirmed. The last hull to return did so ninety-one years ago, carrying a census, a survey of the shelf, a schedule of the villages above, and the observation — recorded by the master, who was not asked for it — that the place smelled of rain on cut stone.

Everything after that is inference. The Archive's position is that New Cotswolds exists, on the grounds that a functioning agricultural settlement with a certified route and no known hazard is not the sort of thing that stops existing quietly; and that the Archive does not know what it is like now, has no means of finding out short of sending someone, and will not describe a present it cannot see. What is in the file is what came back on the fourth hull. It is dated, it is marked as dated, and it is not being extended.

A fifth crossing has been proposed four times and staged once. That entry is also in the file and it is also not being extended.
