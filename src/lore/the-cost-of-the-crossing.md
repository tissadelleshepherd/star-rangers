What makes an island is not the water. It is the crossing — and the survey measures insularity in crossing-cost, not kilometres, because the same strait can be a suburb's ferry ride on one world and a season's gamble on another. On the [Five Islands'](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/) storm ocean, distance is nearly the least of what stands between any two shores, and the Kingdom's whole shape follows from what the water charges to cross it.

## An Archipelago Is a Range That Lost the Argument

[Mountains are rates](/star-rangers/lore/stone-fire-and-ice/) — uplift against erosion — and an archipelago is what the argument looks like when the sea is allowed to join in. Drown a range and its summits become islands; the chain's curve on a chart is the ridge line it used to be; and sea level sits as the judge, promoting and demoting ground as the [water ledger](/star-rangers/lore/the-ledger-and-the-truce/) shifts. An island, read strictly, is a summit the water has not finished with.

The record's water worlds hold the full docket. The Five Islands are a **caldera rim's** surviving summits — volcanic architecture whose submerged remainder still breaks the storms, so the drowned part of the structure protects the standing part. [Thalassa's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/thalassa/) seamount shoals are islands that failed by metres, *too small and too submerged to build on*, which is why its cities float instead. And [Saltmere](/star-rangers/lore/planets/saltmere/) is the completed judgement: a scatter of seamounts *too deep to break the surface at any tide*, a world where every candidate island lost — which is the geological reading of why Saltmere's civilisation had to bring its own ground.

## Isolation Is a Laboratory

Cut a population off and it stops averaging with everyone else: what crosses rarely, diverges surely, and an island keeps what a continent overwrites. The record's great worked case is human. The Five Islands were settled by [a departure older than any Solar System record](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/), and the isolation that hid them also *preserved* them — a founder population left alone long enough to become its own civilisation, with its own crown, its own Old Houses, its own [order standing tideward](/star-rangers/codex/what-prayer-is-for/). Nothing on a connected world stays that distinct for that long. The Kingdom is not a survival despite isolation; it is what isolation is *for*, seen from the far side.

Crossing-cost also writes an archipelago's politics, because when the strait is expensive each island must commit to being something. The Five Islands govern as a set of specialisations — Vellamar the throne and its gardens, Casimor absorbing the archipelago's worst weather at the rim, Aduine terraced under the [Abbeylands](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/), Ostrel run through Kalenjar's anchorage and salt-houses — a division of labour enforced by water, the [grassland's truce](/star-rangers/lore/the-ledger-and-the-truce/) with a coastline.

## Islands Without Water

The record's driest islands never touch a sea. [Ynys Wydrin](/star-rangers/lore/planets/ynys-wydrin/) says so in its name — a dome-and-station world called the *Isle of Glass*, settled precisely because it was not a paradise: every dome an island whose sea is the sky, every airlock a harbour, insularity engineered instead of inherited. [Kernowek Reach's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kernowek-reach/) settled band is an island of breathable air moored on a volcano, its [Cloud Line](/star-rangers/lore/coasts-and-other-edges/) the shore. [Fliade's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) warm caverns are an archipelago in cold rock. And the settled void runs the pattern at its purest: a [habitat](/star-rangers/lore/new-london-space-habitat/) is an island whose crossing-cost is a berth and whose founder effects are cultural — New London holding a heritage intact at L5 for five centuries is the island preservation effect with a station-keeping budget, and the [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/) is archipelago politics without a drop of water involved.

Which leaves the record's most important island, and it is a planet. [Tír na nÓg](/star-rangers/lore/planets/tir-na-nog/) is the only world in the catalogue classified as naturally human-compatible — the one ground that asks nothing of a human who steps onto it, where even [Drithane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/drithane/), the gentlest case elsewhere, asks weeks of acclimatisation to thin, cold air — an island of compatibility whose sea is everything else in the sky. It is named, fittingly, for an island out of myth, and the survey's island doctrine applies to it entire: rare, bounded, irreplaceable, and reached only by paying what the crossing costs.

The instincts transfer, which is why this entry closes the set's arc rather than adding a case to it. Isolation preserves; bounds specialise; the crossing prices everything — and wherever the survey meets a bounded pocket of habitability, on water or off it, it reads the pocket with island eyes and files the crossing-cost first.
