There is one history, and there are many ways of writing its dates. The record's whole discipline of timekeeping follows from holding both of those at once.

## The Common Spine

[Universal Concord Standard Date](/star-rangers/glossary/ucsd/) is the civil standard the public record converts to: continuous with old Earth's common-era count, adopted within [the Solar System Concord](/star-rangers/lore/solar-system-concord/)'s framework, and applied retroactively to the dates that preceded it. A conversion target is all it claims to be. The spine exists so that two filings can be laid side by side, not so that every filing must be born speaking it. How the spine itself is kept — the unit, the ensemble of clocks, the loop-closing — is its own article: [Count and Calibration](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/).

## Many Notations, One History

Civilisations keep their own reckonings, and the record does not ask them to stop. A source that dates an event by its own calendar — a hearth-cycle, an administrative season, a reckoning from a founding the Concord has no stake in — names the same moment a UCSD date names. Conversion between notations is bookkeeping, not cosmology: the timeline is shared; only the numerals differ.

The archival practice follows: a document is filed in its author's own notation, with the UCSD conversion noted beside it. The original is the document; the conversion is the courtesy. An archive's conversion tables are working references, maintained and corrected like any other instrument, and a table's revision history is itself part of the record.

## Carrying Time

There is [no faster-than-light communication](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/) — no channel carries a time signal ahead of a hull. Exact time is therefore *distributed*, physically: reference standards compared in person, calibration certificates travelling with courier runs, a station's clock only as current as its last synchronisation. A distant institution is always working from a record that is transit-lagged, and its dates inherit the lag honestly rather than hiding it.

This is why timekeeping is courier work, and why the same neutral carriers trusted with everyone's messages are trusted with everyone's seconds.

## Relativity, Reconciled

Between regions in different states of motion, relativistic time dilation is real and measurable, and far-separated clocks accumulate offsets exactly as physics predicts. At the scales civil record-keeping operates, the effect is minimal — small, predicted, and corrected as a matter of routine. Reconciliation applies the known adjustments, files them, and moves on; the corrections are deliberately boring.

The discipline earns its keep at the margin: because the routine corrections are known and applied, a discrepancy that *survives* reconciliation is not a bookkeeping error to be absorbed. It is a reading — and readings are handed to instruments and the people who stand watches over them.

## No Galactic Calendar

There is no galactic year, and the record keeps none, for two reasons that are the same reason. A galactic year in the astronomer's sense is one orbit of a star about the galaxy's centre, some two hundred million of anyone's years, and no civilisation has lived through a hundredth of one. And a galactic calendar in the civil sense would need a galactic present, which nothing in physics supplies: with no signal ahead of a hull, there is no moment that is *now* everywhere, only each clock's own count and the courier work that reconciles them. A year is always somebody's year. UCSD is Earth's, with an offset; every other people's is its own, converted beside it and never replaced.

What a galaxy-wide reference can be is a thing to read, not a thing to count by. The Survey Corps keeps a pulsar ephemeris, the phases of a set of named millisecond pulsars whose beat is stable past any clock a hull can carry and visible from anywhere a hull can reach, and a courier's standard is read against it on arrival. The paper timescale that [Count and Calibration](/star-rangers/lore/universal-cosmic-stardate/) describes is reconciled after the fact; the ephemeris is what lets two standards that have never met agree on a phase before the reconciliation is done. It gives any two observers a shared coordinate. It does not give them a shared elapsed time, which their own motion still decides, and it does not give anyone a calendar.

## Naming the Present

The record's present is 2826 UCSD, and the Archive calls it that. It names no era for the years it is living in, because an era's name is given by the people who come after and who know what mattered; the names the record does carry, the Founding Era, the Imperium period, the charter era, the Concord period, were all given afterwards, and the last of them runs to the present without claiming to describe it. A people may name the present in its own mouth, and several do. The Archive files the name beside the date, as it files every notation, and keeps the date.
