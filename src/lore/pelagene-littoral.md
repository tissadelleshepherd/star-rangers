The Pelagene Littoral come from **Nerath**, which has **no stable landmass at all**. The surface is contested by weather; the photic layer is the edge of the known world; and below it the pressure transitions are steep and abrupt.

The first thing to correct is the name. *Littoral* implies a shore, and Nerath has none. **Their littoral is a depth band, not a coastline** — the lit, worked, inhabited layer between a dangerous surface and a dark, heavy, largely unvisited below. Everything about the Pelagene follows from being a people of a horizontal boundary, in a world where there is nowhere to stand.

## It Is Not an Ocean of Water

The second thing to correct is heavier, and the record's own vocabulary is the culprit. Solar System sources say *water*, *sea*, *brine*, *salinity*, *hydration* — and every one of those is a **translation**, made early, by humans, for human convenience, on the [Archive's standing principle](/star-rangers/lore/timekeeping-and-the-common-record/) that the original is the document and the conversion is the courtesy. The courtesy has been mistaken for the fact for most of a century.

Nerath's medium is not water as the Solar System means it. What can be said without over-claiming is functional rather than chemical: it is **dense, cold, strongly stratified, and not one fluid but several** — layers of differing composition and density lying stably on one another, mixing slowly or not at all, each with its own dissolved load, its own optical depth and its own chemistry. The Corps' *salinity* is a serviceable name for the dissolved-load gradients within a layer. It is not a claim about salt.

This is the fact that makes the species legible, and it is why [Mira of Brine](/star-rangers/characters/mira-of-brine/) is a specialist in **fluid-boundary anomalies** — a phrase that means very little on a single-ocean world and is the central feature of hers. A Pelagene grows up in a world whose principal structures are *interfaces between fluids*: where one layer meets another, what crosses, what refuses to, what accumulates at the seam. Her professional subject is her home's basic geography.

Two consequences worth keeping straight. **A Pelagene aboard a station is not in a familiar medium made thin** — air is a genuinely foreign fluid, and their competence with it is transferred skill rather than instinct. And **the record should not fix the chemistry.** Naming the compounds would date the entry twice, once when it chose and again when the choice looked quaint, and nothing in the story needs it: what matters is layered, dense, cold and not ours.

## A Body Oriented on the Vertical

There is no front. There is **up and down**, and they are not interchangeable: up is light and storm, down is pressure and cold. A Pelagene body is accordingly **radially symmetrical about a vertical axis** — organised like a thing that rises and settles rather than a thing that goes forward, with sensing distributed evenly around the circumference because a threat or a gradient can arrive from any bearing and only the two poles differ.

The **buoyancy organ** is the centre of the anatomy and the centre of the self-image, the way a heart or a hand is elsewhere. Trim is a Pelagene's continuous, half-conscious occupation, and holding a depth in a shifting column is the physical skill their whole culture treats as basic competence.

The integument is **water-permeable** — a large, folded, chemically active surface that manages gas exchange across wide swings in dissolved oxygen, and which is why Mira of Brine and every other Pelagene on a station runs [assistive hydration cycles](/star-rangers/characters/mira-of-brine/). The permeability is not a weakness at home. It is the organ of perception.

**Manipulation is by fine tentacular cirri** — many slender muscular hydrostats, no bones, no fixed number in use at once. This is dexterity of a kind hands cannot match at small scale and cannot match at all for *simultaneous* work, and it is poor at exactly one thing: applying a large force in one direction quickly.

## Reading the Medium

A Pelagene senses pressure, flow and chemistry over the whole body surface at once, which is a sensorium no instrument replaces and few species intuit. Mira's file records the working version:

> She reads fluid motion as primary signal. Condensation patterns on cold panels. Airflow vortices in corridor junctions. Coolant turbulence in transit tubes. **All of it is data before it is noise.**

That is not a talent. It is a whole people's native perceptual mode, applied to air because air is also a fluid. A Pelagene aboard a station is reading the station continuously and cannot easily stop.

## They Never Had Fire

This is the defining fact of Pelagene technology and it has no workaround. **There is no dry place on Nerath.** Not a beach, not an island, not a cave above the waterline — nowhere to strike a spark, and nowhere combustion could be sustained even in principle. Every technology a fire-using people takes as foundational was closed to them permanently: no smelting, no ceramics, no forging, no glass, no steam.

What they had instead was **an ocean of electrolyte, and the largest pressure and chemical gradients of any inhabited world in the record.** So they built the other technology.

**Metals are grown, not melted.** Pelagene metallurgy is electrochemical — metal won from solution and deposited layer on layer onto a form, which yields purity a smelter cannot easily reach and structures no mould could release. It is also *slow*, on the order of seasons for a large piece, and correspondingly deliberate: nothing is fabricated in a hurry because nothing can be.

**Force comes from pressure, not heat.** Hydrostatic forming, cold working, and the routine use of depth itself as a press. A people who can move a piece of work down a kilometre of water column have a machine tool no fire-using civilisation thought to build.

**And chemistry arrived early and stayed central.** Salinity, dissolved gas, pH and galvanic potential are the Pelagene's fundamental engineering variables the way temperature and stress are elsewhere — which is exactly why Mira is an environmental surveyor specialising in pressure systems, salinity gradients and fluid-boundary anomalies. Her specialism is her civilisation's physics.

The consequence a Ranger notices first: **a Pelagene is extraordinarily good with corrosion, coolant, sealing and cross-medium contamination**, and is finding those problems in a station's plumbing before human instruments flag them, because that entire failure class is the subject her people have studied longest.

## What They Are Structurally Bad At

**Nothing on Nerath ever stays where it was put.** There are no landmarks, no fixed places, no stable inventory — everything drifts, dilutes, sinks or is carried. A Pelagene is therefore superb at rates and directions and genuinely poor at fixed states, which Mira's own file records without naming the cause:

> Her field notes are organised around gradients rather than fixed states. She is less interested in where a system is than in **which direction it is moving and at what rate**.

That is a gift ninety per cent of the time and a real gap the rest. Asked what a system *is*, a Pelagene will tend to answer what it is *doing*, and a board wanting a flat number sometimes has to ask twice.

The technological version is sharper. **They cannot improvise fast.** A fire-using people repairs by heating, bending and joining in an afternoon; a Pelagene's materials tradition has no equivalent move, and a culture whose every object was grown over months finds the whole idea of a temporary fix faintly incoherent. Rangers who have served with them report that the awkwardness is not reluctance — it is closer to being asked to write a sentence in a tense one's language does not have.

## The Thing Outsiders Get Wrong

The hydration cycles get read as frailty: a delicate creature on life support, kept comfortable at the Corps' expense. It is the reverse. **The littoral is the most punishing zone on Nerath** — contested surface above, steep pressure below, and daily swings in temperature, salinity and dissolved oxygen that would kill anything specialised. The Pelagene are what evolves in the variable band, not the calm one. They are tolerant generalists from a hard place.

What the cycles actually measure is a clock nobody else on a station is running. **A Pelagene ashore is slowly drying**, every hour of every posting, and manages it the way a diver manages air: quietly, competently, and without expecting anyone to be impressed.
