The Fliade survey's opening judgement was wrong by most of the biosphere, and the record keeps that sentence where every survey team will read it. Cavern systems honeycombed the crust *on a scale the surface gave no hint of* — and the lesson generalises past Fliade to every world with a crust: the underground is the one country a survey cannot read from orbit, and every surface report is provisional about what is underneath it.

## The Slow Room

Go down far enough on any world and the weather stops. A few metres of rock erase the day; a few tens erase the seasons; and a deep cave settles at its surface's long-term mean temperature and holds it — which silences, one by one, every doctrine in this set. [No weather](/star-rangers/lore/weather-on-other-worlds/) reaches it, [no tide breaks in it](/star-rangers/lore/tides-without-shores/), no [erosion argument](/star-rangers/lore/stone-fire-and-ice/) runs at witnessable pace. The underground is the slow room of any world: the place where the rates that everywhere else wear the costume of places take the costume off and very nearly halt.

The stillness is itself an instrument. A deep cave's temperature *is* the surface climate's average, held in rock — which makes it the third sibling of the record's natural archives: the [glacier](/star-rangers/lore/stone-fire-and-ice/) keeps the years frozen in order, the [tree](/star-rangers/lore/the-forest-is-waiting/) keeps them alive in order, and the cave keeps no order at all, only the answer — the mean, remembered.

## Three Chemistries Make Caves

A world's cave inventory is readable from its geology map, because only three processes hollow rock at scale. **Dissolution** needs soluble stone and patient water: carbonate country is cave country, and [Winterbourne's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/winterbourne/) chalk is already doing it — the spring line is dissolution plumbing, water working conduits through the platform on its way to the clay floor, a karst in progress under every parish. **Fire** leaves tubes where lava drained out from under its own crust, so volcanic worlds — [Kernowek Reach](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kernowek-reach/), [Veyr-3](/star-rangers/lore/veyr-basaltborn/) — carry caves as a by-product of their spine. And **neither** is the granite verdict: [Aethelrock's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/aethelrock/) insoluble highlands are nearly caveless, an old hard world whose underground is simply more of itself. [Fliade](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) is the full expression — geothermal heat and subterranean water working together at planetary scale, a crust hollowed into a biosphere.

## The Survey's Blind Spot

Everything else in this set can be read, at least provisionally, from above: lakes counted, drainage traced, coastlines stamped with a ruler. The underground is walked. No instrument profile substitutes for the crossing of it, no orbit sees into it, and the Corps' doctrine on the matter is Fliade's correction made standing policy: a surface survey is a survey of the surface, and says so on its cover.

## The Quiet Has Tenants

Everyone who moves underground moves there for the same reason, and the record's underground census makes the point by its variety. Fliade's biosphere runs on the interior's heat and chemistry instead of sunlight — slower, stabler, older economics than any daylight ecology. The [Anstolik herds](/star-rangers/codex/proposal-to-collar-the-anstolik/) walk toward the deep networks on a schedule nobody set. The **Pandoids** — a people, and the record states it flatly — keep the deep networks and [keep their record by speaking it](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/): an archive held in voices, in the one environment constant enough to trust a memory to. The [Monasteries of Mars](/star-rangers/lore/monasteries-of-mars/) build into the terrain itself, pressure-hermitages, withdrawal made architecture. And the Concord's fastest machines are cave-dwellers by law of physics rather than choice: the [Cavern Constraint](/star-rangers/lore/quantum-computing-and-the-cavern-constraint/) puts every serious quantum computer deep under sealed rock, where both of its decoherence channels go quiet.

Fungus, herd, people, hermit, machine: the underground's one export is **constancy**, and every tenant on the list is a customer for it. What the surface offers is energy and change; what the deep offers is neither, reliably — and a surprising fraction of what a civilisation values most turns out to want exactly that.

The way in closes the set's loop: [a cave mouth is a coast](/star-rangers/lore/coasts-and-other-edges/), the crossing point between the fast country and the slow one, and it obeys coastal law — traffic concentrates there, both worlds trade there, and whoever holds the mouth holds the terms. The underground itself keeps the other rule this set has leaned on throughout, in its strongest form: it is the one country every world has, and almost no survey finishes.
