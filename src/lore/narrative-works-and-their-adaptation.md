A work of fiction here is not a thing you finish. It is a thing you finish *reading*, which is not the same, and the difference is visible in the shape.

## The Shape of a Work

The unit of composition is the **scene**, and scenes are gathered into larger runs — a sequence, a season's worth, whatever the work's own divisions turn out to be. What distinguishes the form from the long linear text of earlier centuries is what sits *inside* a scene. A scene carries **several viewpoints of the same events**, set down separately and kept separate: one character's account of an hour, then another's, each holding what the other does not supply and neither corrected against the other by the work itself.

Readers move through this in more than one order, and the form assumes it. Straight through is the common way. Following one viewpoint across a whole work and then returning for another is ordinary enough that most works are indexed for it. Nobody regards the second reading as a lesser one, and a work that collapses under it is generally held to have been thin.

Attached to the spine, and published with it, is the work's **apparatus**: its settled background, a register of its terms, its chronology, and — the part that most repays attention — its **sourced documents**. These last are not the author speaking. They are attributed to people inside the work, and they may be partial, self-serving, or flatly contradicted by the scenes they sit beside. A reader is expected to notice that and to weigh it, and a work whose documents all agree with each other is generally suspected of not having been thought about very hard.

## Why It Took That Shape

Three pressures, none of them artistic in origin, and the form is the place they met.

**The culture is documentary by disposition.** A civilisation that spent eleven centuries learning to ask *who says so, when, and how sure are they* was not going to keep making art that hides its sources. The habits that produce a [verification doctrine](/star-rangers/codex/the-verification-doctrine/) and a discipline of [marked absences](/star-rangers/codex/marked-absences/) produce a story with its own working shown, and the [audit apparatus](/star-rangers/glossary/creative-entropy-index-cei/) rewards it: a work that supplies its own apparatus is demonstrating structure, which is what novelty credit is actually looking for, rather than presenting a surface and asking to be admired.

**A single narrator stopped being credible.** The record's own hardest cases are the ones where several honest accounts of one event do not reconcile, and the practice that grew up around them — file all of them, adjudicate none, mark what cannot be settled — turned out to be a way of telling a story as well as a way of keeping one. Once the technique existed in the archive it was never going to stay there.

**And a work has to survive being carried across.** This is the pressure that shows most in the form. The audience is not one species, and the channels a work can use are not shared. Building it modular — separable viewpoints, separable channels, an apparatus that can be re-rendered without touching the spine — is what makes adaptation possible at all. The form is what it is partly because somebody, eventually, will have to take it apart.

## Picture and Sound, and What They Are Not

Works carry considerably more image and sound than the printed forms they descend from, and it is worth being exact about what that does and does not mean.

Imagery arrives on the same [matte, unlit surfaces](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/) as everything else — reflective, read by whatever light is in the room, changing without announcing the change. Nothing glows. Nothing is projected, and no figure stands in the air. Sound is present and is not theatre: a room does not darken and no one's face is lit by what they are attending to.

The result is closer to a scored and illustrated record than to a film, and it is unmistakably a thing you *read* rather than a thing that plays at you. A viewpoint may carry its own visual register — what that character actually looked at — and the discipline runs the same way as everywhere else in this culture: the image is evidence about the viewpoint, not a fact about the room.

## Adaptation, and What It Costs

**There is no machine that renders a work into another people's mode**, for the same reason there is no machine that renders a language at first hearing. Adaptation is done by people, it takes years, and it is credited.

The work is not translation of words alone. A people's communicative channel may not carry the distinctions a work is built on, and may carry distinctions the original had no way to make. The [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) case is the one adapters teach from: communication, art and — by their own account — thought all run on the same shared medium, so a human work entering it is not being translated into a Chthonari language. It is being re-composed into a channel where the separation between a character's thought and the work's telling of it does not exist to begin with. What comes out is defensible, arguable, and unmistakably somebody's reading.

The same holds in the other direction, and it is not symmetrical in difficulty. A Chthonari-authored work rendered for human attention loses the channel it was composed in; a human reader receives an account of it. The [Ollune](/star-rangers/lore/ollune/) precedent applies throughout and is worth stating plainly: **an adapter may decline.** Some things are not rendered precisely, on the adapter's judgement, and the refusal is recorded rather than quietly worked around.

So an adaptation is filed the way any account is filed. **The original is the work; the adaptation is the courtesy** — attributed, dated, revisable, and never mistaken for the thing itself. Serious works accumulate several, by different hands, disagreeing; and a reader who wants to know what an adapter thought a passage was doing can generally find out, because adapters are expected to say.

**One people is largely absent from all of this, and the absence is instructive.** The [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) are the nearest thing to human the record holds — the only other people built on the same frame — and human works land on them least of all. There is no significant body of adaptation into Krenyi use, and not for want of a channel: a Krenyi can read a human work perfectly well, and commonly does, taking the apparatus, which is measurement, and setting the scenes down. What never formed was the demand. The forms are calibrated for dread, fury and grief held open, and a Krenyi has none of the three to play on.

Adapters cite this more often than any other case when explaining the trade to newcomers, because it makes the point nothing else makes as cleanly. **The hard part was never the sensorium.** The species that shares almost the whole human body plan is the one human stories reach least, while peoples built nothing like us — who must be adapted for at enormous cost — turn out to want the work done. What a story is *for* does not travel with the shape of the body reading it.

**Leaving something unrendered is itself a statement**, and a well-understood one. Where a work keeps a people's own forms untranslated — a term, an address, a whole channel left as it stands — the adapter is declining the courtesy on purpose, and asking the reader to come the rest of the way. It is the same move a people makes by keeping its own calendar, and it is read the same way.
