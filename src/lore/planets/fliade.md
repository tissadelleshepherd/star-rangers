Most survey verdicts that matter arrive too late to be of any use. Fliade's arrived early, and the record has been quietly grateful about it ever since.

**Fliade** is a rocky terrestrial world orbiting far enough from its star to sit well below Earth-like temperatures across nearly all of its surface. Its equatorial band is the most hospitable ground on the planet and reaches cool temperate conditions at the peak of its season; even there, snowfall is ordinary and standing water freezes for long stretches of the year. The poles are permanently glaciated and support no surface-dwelling life worth the name. A visitor who judged the planet from orbit, or from a season on the equatorial plain, would file it as marginal.

The judgement would be wrong by most of the biosphere. **Fliade's life is underground.** Cavern systems honeycomb the crust on a scale the surface gives no hint of, warmed by geothermal activity and fed by subterranean rivers, and they hold self-contained ecosystems that evolved largely independent of the conditions above them. The surface is not where the planet failed to be habitable. It is the roof.

## Below the Roof

The caverns are not a refuge from the world; they are the world. Geothermal warmth seeps through porous rock and holds the deep networks at temperatures the surface never approaches. Bioluminescent fungi coat the walls in soft amber and blue-green, filling the underground with a dim, steady light that has no cycle to it — no dawn, no dusk, nothing that would teach an organism to keep a day. The air is humid and carries moss, mineral water, and the warm musk of what shelters there. It is breathable without apparatus, in the networks as on the equatorial plain above them, thinner on the plain and denser below it: the surface party works, talks and sleeps on Fliade in cold-weather clothing with nothing over the face, and smells the world it is on. The oxygen is the surface biosphere's, since any ground the Anstolik graze has producers of its own, carried into the networks through the passages that open onto the plain, which is how a party walks in. What the deep networks contribute to their own air, or draw from it, has not been measured; no survey instrument has been below the margin, and the record marks the question rather than answering it.

The surface, by contrast, is thin, dry and mineral-sharp. Wind cuts across open ground carrying the smell of ice and deep stone; the sun sits low and distant and rarely warms the sky past a pale grey-blue. Permafrost crunches underfoot. The most constant sound on the tundra is the low bellow of **Anstolik** herds marking territory — large migratory herbivores that range across the surface plains and the upper cavern levels alike, and defend feeding grounds and migration corridors with more conviction than their build suggests.

The survey named the Anstolik for a resemblance to Terran reindeer, and the name has stuck the way field names do. It should be read the way [every such name in this record is read](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/): the comparison belongs to the people who did the naming, and records what the encounter looked like to them. Fliade's biosphere began on Fliade. Nothing on it is related to anything on Earth, and [nothing on it is a metazoan](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/).

## The Pandoids

The deep networks belong to the **Pandoids**, and they are a people.

They are large, powerfully built and upright, with thick fur that reads as grey against the stone, and they move through broken cavern ground quietly enough that their presence is often announced by the shift of a boulder rather than by any sound they make. They descend from a bear-like Fliadan lineage — *bear-like* meaning, again, that the first survey to see one reached for the nearest familiar shape, and not that any bear is anywhere behind them. On the [cross-biosphere frames](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/) they fall in Endoskelia, which places them beside humans, the [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) and the [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) in a table that asks one question about skeletons and answers nothing else. It does not make them humanoid in the sense the record refuses; strength, gait, mass and face are all their own, and an observer who mistook one for a person at any distance would be reporting on the light rather than on the Pandoid.

They are calm and reclusive by every account the survey has filed, and they hold the deep networks by advantages the deep networks reward: strength, and an intimacy with vertical broken ground that no visiting instrument has matched.

**They do not travel in space, and they keep their record by speaking it.** Those are two specific facts about what the Pandoids do, and the record states them as such — not as a level, a stage, or a distance behind anyone, because [there is no such scale to be behind on](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/). A people whose world is a warm sealed interior beneath a lethal surface was never presented with the sky as a problem to be solved. A people who keep what they know in speech and in the memory of a community have a record with different properties from a written one — one that cannot be seized, cannot be edited by a later hand, and does not survive the loss of the people who carry it. That is a different shape of record. It is not a smaller one.

What the Archive holds about Fliade is, accordingly, **observational**. The survey watched, and what it learned it learned that way. Nothing here is an account the Pandoids gave of themselves, and the distinction is worth keeping in view every time this entry says *the Pandoids are* rather than *the Pandoids say*.

## Survey Status

Fliade is a **first-contact world**, and the [Frontier Transformation Protocols'](/star-rangers/lore/frontier-transformation-protocols/) sapience clause applies to it on the plain reading rather than the contested one. The protocols' extended survey windows exist precisely so that a slow-maturing or non-obvious sapient population is found rather than paved over; on Fliade that machinery did the thing it was built to do, before any proposal was on any table.

The Star Rangers' own position needed no assessment at all. The charter forbids the Rangers to transform, certify, or lend survey data to the transformation of **any world bearing existing life of any kind** — a threshold Fliade clears in the first cavern, without reference to the Pandoids. On this world the categorical clause and the sapience clause happen to point the same way, which is not the usual arrangement and is worth noticing when it occurs.

The planet also sits close enough to a boundary feature under study to bring the protocols' boundary-proximity requirements into force. **What that feature is has not been settled in this record.** It is the reason Fliade attracts scientific interest disproportionate to a cold world with no settlement case, and the reason the survey window is written to run longer than the geology alone would justify.

There is no settlement authorisation and none has been sought.

## What This World Asks That the Charter Cannot Answer

The Rangers enter a world by invitation and leave when the invitation is withdrawn. It is the constraint that makes the charter trustworthy, and it is the reason the [Cerebraun](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun/) withdrawal is cited as often as it is: the limit is real, it gets exercised, and it holds.

Fliade is the case where the mechanism has nothing to grip. There is no body on that planet that can issue an invitation in any form the charter recognises, or withdraw one — which means the protection is thinnest exactly where the asymmetry is greatest. The Pandoids did not come to anyone. They were arrived at, by a party that could leave whenever it chose and return with whatever it liked, and no term of that arrangement was ever put to them.

The record does not resolve this, and the standing doctrine has been to slow everything down: longest survey windows, lightest presence, nothing on the surface that the planet would keep if the station were abandoned. Whether restraint practised by one party toward another who cannot ask for it is a protection or merely a courtesy is an open question in Ranger doctrine, and Fliade is the case that keeps it open.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

**The Pandoids' own name for themselves.** *Pandoid* is a survey coinage, built the way survey coinages are built — quickly, from a resemblance, by people with nothing better to hand. It is the name in every filing, it will remain the name in every filing until somebody is in a position to be told otherwise, and it is not a name the Pandoids gave — to the survey. What the survey has not been told, the record's narrative now carries from the other side: the people call themselves, in the record's translation, [the Told](/star-rangers/glossary/the-told/), and are named individually by tellings on the same pattern. The two names belong to two sides of one encounter, and this entry keeps the survey's, because this entry is the survey's side.
