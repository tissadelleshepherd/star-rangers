The Ilveth come from **Sardain**: a slow, cold world far out around a small dim star, with air too thin and dry to hold the day's warmth for even an hour past sunset, and ground ice old enough to count as geology. Noon on Sardain is a bright dusk. Water is a mineral there, and what runs in an Ilveth is not water — which is [a statement about their chemistry, not a translation slipping](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/), and it is the reason they can be as cold as their world and alive in it.

Everything below follows from one fact: **the Ilveth are cold**. Their bodies run at the temperature of the ground they stand on, and that is not a hardship to be admired. It is the price of admission to the sense that defines them.

**They are not humanoid**, and [could not be mistaken for a person at any distance in any light](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/). An Ilveth is long and low — several paired limbs under a segmented body that keeps its mass near the ground and its surface small against the cold — with no fixed face to read: the forward third rises into a hooded **sensory crown**, and broad vascular **vanes** lie folded along the flank and crown like furled sails. What the crown carries, and what the vanes are for, the next two sections say.

## The Light the Living Give Off

Everything warmer than nothing glows. The glow of a living body is long-wavelength and dim, and most eyes in the record never notice it for a simple, unforgiving reason: **an eye warm enough to be alive glows in exactly the band it would need to read.** A warm creature looking for warmth is a lamp searching for lamps by lamplight — its own emission drowns the signal, and no refinement of the organ gets around the arithmetic. The sense is cheap for the cold and nearly impossible for the warm, which is why the record's warm-bodied peoples do not have it and the Ilveth do.

On Sardain the arithmetic runs the other way with room to spare. A body at ground temperature is quiet in that band; warm ground, open melt, a vent, another Ilveth's signal — each stands out of the dark like a struck match. The Ilveth see warmth as light, in no light at all, and the survey's first landing party learned what that means in practice: **they had been watched from horizon distance for three days before anyone knew the world was inhabited.** Nothing warm crosses Sardain unobserved.

## An Eye With No Window

The organ is not an eye in the usual construction, because it cannot be. Nothing an eye could be filled with is transparent at those wavelengths — a fluid-filled lens is a closed shutter to them — so the Ilveth sensory crown carries **dry mirror-pits**: polished concave hollows that focus by reflection onto a cold sensing floor, the way a dish focuses rather than the way a lens does. The pits are wide, and have to be; a long wavelength makes a coarse picture, and generous aperture is what buys the coarseness back.

Even bought back, the picture is not the one other species mean by seeing. An Ilveth reads **presence, temperature, and flow** — a body against the ground, warmth moving under a surface, the plume of breath before the breather rounds the corner — and cannot read an edge finely or a printed page at all. Fine work belongs to a ring of small photic eyes below the crown, modest and near-sighted, tuned to Sardain's dim day. The division of labour is total: the small eyes for what a thing is, the pits for where the warmth is, and an Ilveth asked which of the two is *seeing* will not understand the question.

The sense has walls, and they are worth knowing exactly. **Falling rain, mist and steam are opaque to it** — a wet sky is a shut door, which on dry Sardain almost never matters and off Sardain matters constantly. Smoke, by contrast, barely exists: the wavelengths walk through it, and an Ilveth in a burning compartment sees the living as plainly as through clean air.

## The Sky Is the Coldest Thing There Is

To a heat-sense under a clear night, the open sky is not a ceiling of stars. It is **the deepest cold in the world, directly overhead** — a blackness that visibly drinks warmth out of everything facing it, so that to an Ilveth a body in the open is a body being spent. Sardain's cultures agree on very little and agree on this: shelter is not about wind. It is about not showing the sky your heat.

The stars themselves were never hidden — the small photic eyes see them well enough on a clear night — but for most of Ilveth history they were beside the point: **a sky that contains no warmth contains nothing to want.** Astronomy arrived late, through instruments, as a discipline of the cold rather than of the light, and the Ilveth route to space was [being reached rather than reaching](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/) — the survey came to Sardain, and contact proceeded from there. A people whose sky was a sink rather than an invitation are not behind anyone for having read it correctly. There is no scale to be behind on.

## A Word Costs a Calorie

The Ilveth speak twice. A quiet click-speech, carried well enough by thin air, does the ordinary day's work. The register that matters is written in warmth: **signalling vanes** along the flank and crown, vascular surfaces an Ilveth floods and starves deliberately, so that meaning moves across the body as patterns of glow that every Ilveth in line of sight reads as plainly as a shout.

On a world where heat is the only wealth, that channel is never free. Radiating a statement into a Sardain night costs real metabolic coin, and the language is shaped by the price: no thermal small talk exists, emphasis is expenditure, and the warm-write is reserved for what is worth paying to say. Outsiders reliably read the resulting economy as reserve or coldness of manner. It is closer to the opposite. **A people for whom saying a thing costs something have never once said a thing carelessly**, and an Ilveth who warms a sentence for you has spent on you.

The vanes also leak. An exhausted, ill or frightened Ilveth flickers involuntarily, visible to every other Ilveth present, and Sardain's oldest courtesy exists for exactly this: **it is bad manners to answer what a body says instead of what a person says.** That rule was in place ages before the survey landed, and it turned out to be the single piece of cultural equipment the Ilveth most needed for contact — because warm-blooded visitors, by Ilveth lights, never stop leaking.

## They Dug for Fire

Every technological people meets the high-energy processing problem somewhere, and the record now holds four distinct answers: the [Veyr](/star-rangers/lore/veyr-basaltborn/) were handed fire by their own crust, the [Pelagene](/star-rangers/lore/pelagene-littoral/) never had it and never could, the [Serephine](/star-rangers/lore/serephine-dunekin/) took it out of the sky — and **the Ilveth dug for it.** Sardain's thin air makes open flame a laboratory curiosity, feeble and expensive; what the world holds instead is old warmth underground, in geothermal provinces where the biosphere itself has always crowded. Ilveth industry went down after it: deep heat-mines, buried working floors, metallurgy done slowly and hoarded jealously, insulation raised to the level other civilisations reserve for art.

One consequence is easy to miss and worth stating: **for a people who see heat, industry is illumination.** A working forge announces itself across a day's travel; a warm building at night is a building shouting; a leak in a wall is not waste but indiscretion. Heat-thrift is therefore an Ilveth virtue with the moral weight other cultures give honesty, and their engineering habit — seal it, bury it, bank it — reads as secrecy only to species that cannot see what an unsealed building is saying.

## What They Are Structurally Bad At

**Warmth.** The sense and the body fail in it together. A warm environment raises the mirror-pits' own floor until the picture greys toward nothing — a heated corridor at everyone else's comfort is, to an Ilveth, a featureless glare, the thermal equivalent of a snowfield at noon — and the same environment loads a cold-running chemistry with heat it has no good way to shed. An Ilveth in human-comfortable air is half-blind and slowly cooking, and neither half is a matter of acclimatising.

**And hurry.** Chemistry that runs cold runs deliberate. An Ilveth's fast is another species' unhurried, the recovery from real exertion is long, and no urgency moves either fact. What they offer instead is the thing cold chemistry is good at: they do not tire the way warm species tire, and an Ilveth watch that has lasted three days is being kept as well as it was in its first hour.

## In the Corps

Ilveth Rangers go where the dark is a problem and patience is the method: night survey, dark-side and deep-shadow operations, and above all **search and rescue** — through smoke, through blackout, through collapsed structure, an Ilveth finds the living the way anyone else finds a lit window, and has never once needed to be told where the survivors are. Aboard station they wear the modular field shell [fitted per body plan](/star-rangers/lore/rank-insignia-and-uniform/) as a cooling shell, keep chilled quarters, and pay a cost the roster should be read as carrying: in a warm hull the thermal register is unusable, so shipboard Ilveth are held to their click-speech — quiet, their fellows note, even for Ilveth.

## The Thing Outsiders Get Wrong

That they read minds — and its frightened corollary, that no one can lie to an Ilveth.

They see heat, not meaning. A fever and a fury look alike in the pits; a flush of anger and a flush of embarrassment are the same event at the same temperature, and early contact records are full of Ilveth solemnly answering human blushes as if they were statements, because in their own language a warmed surface *is* one. What an Ilveth actually knows about you is that you are warm, where you are, and that you are — by Sardain's standards — almost unimaginably extravagant: a creature spending a bonfire's worth of heat on simply existing, day and night, awake and asleep. The record has [an Ilveth surveyor's phrase](/star-rangers/characters/suvra-kel/) for humans on file, offered without malice and impossible to improve: *lanterns that cannot be put out.*

And having seen all of that, an Ilveth extends you the courtesy their own leaking vanes taught them long before anyone landed: they answer what you say. [Habitability is a relation, not a property](/star-rangers/lore/habitable-for-whom/), and so is being seen — what the dark hides from your eyes it never hid from theirs, and the Ilveth position on the resulting asymmetry has been settled since before contact: what a body says was not addressed to you, and a courteous person does not reply to it.
