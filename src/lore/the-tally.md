Every human people on the record can point at an older one behind it. The Celtic Union carried its languages out of the Solar System to keep them; the Knarr Line kept its Norse assembly's name unchanged; New London built three rings to keep a heritage within visiting distance of the world it came from. The Tally are the exception, and they are exact about it. Nothing behind them is theirs. They were not a people when they left. They became one aboard.

## The Departure

The [Generation-Ark Era](/star-rangers/lore/the-generation-ark-era/) records that an unknown number of unauthorised arks slipped the [Imperium's](/star-rangers/lore/the-imperium/) patrols during its outer-settlement consolidation, and that the Imperium closed the file on each as a *presumed transit loss*. One of those files has since been reopened. In the same decades that the Currach Fleet departed, a single hull left from the far side of the Belt carrying a company that had nothing in common but its reasons: crews and families off outer stations the consolidation was closing, drawn from a dozen languages and no shared one, fleeing more than they were sailing toward. The era's own rule is that such departures most often did not survive. This one survived by failing.

Two years out, well past the last of the Belt's traffic, something the company's earliest ledgers record only as *the fault* made the crossing impossible. The ledgers do not say what it was. They say what was done about it: the hull turned in toward the nearest body with water on it, a dark icy object of the scattered disc, and moored. The Tally date everything from that day. Their word for it is rendered in the record as **the Mooring**, and their calendar counts from it, not from any Earth epoch and not from the departure, because in their own telling nothing that happened before the Mooring happened to *them*.

## Becoming One People

What the era rewarded was the durability of a company across generations, and the company that moored had none of the things that usually supply it: no shared tongue, no shared custom, no founding cooperative that had chosen its members for cohesion. What it had was a sealed hull, a finite count of everything, and children.

The children did what children of mixed-language communities have done wherever the record has looked, on Earth and off it: they made a language. Within a generation of the Mooring the hull had one tongue, spoken by nobody who had come aboard and by everybody born there, built from the founders' several languages on a grammar none of them had used. The Tally's own name for themselves is a word of that language, and it is rendered here rather than reproduced, on the record's standing convention for a people's own name; the rendering *Tally* is the Archive's, chosen because the word names what the people think they are. It does not mean *the counted*. It means the count itself, kept.

The count is the culture. A closed company in a sealed hull cannot afford a single thing that is not accounted for, and the Tally turned that necessity into their whole ethics before their first century was out. Air, water, food, power, work and repair are tallied continuously and openly, as they have been since the first ledger; so is what each person has put back, which is the only standing the Tally recognise. There is no rank aboard that is not a role, no role that is not rotated, and no possession that is not on the books. Waste is the one obscenity in the language, and the word for it is also the word for a lie. The Tally are, by every account the record holds, exact, courteous and unhurried, and they find the surface peoples' habit of estimating slightly indecent.

## The Ledgers

The Mooring's first ledger has never been closed, and its successors run unbroken to the present. Three centuries of a closed system's complete account — every gram of volatiles mined from the body and returned to it, every failure of the hull's ecology and every correction, births and deaths and what each life put back — make up the longest continuous closed-ecology record in the Solar System, and the only one kept by the people living inside it. The [Habitat Threshold](/star-rangers/lore/habitat-threshold/) names, among the reasons a settlement builds up rather than out, *a set of books nobody else keeps*. The Tally are the case the sentence was waiting for. They did not build up to keep the ledgers. They kept the ledgers, and the settlement is what keeping them looks like after three hundred years.

Survey Corps has asked for the ledgers three times, courteously, and been shown them each time, in the hull, in the Tally's language, by a reader who turns the pages. Copies have not been offered and have not been asked for a fourth time.

## Re-contact

The Imperium's file stayed closed through the Imperium, the [Military Space Command](/star-rangers/lore/military-space-command/) and the [Solar System Concord](/star-rangers/lore/solar-system-concord/), and was reopened only two generations ago, when a [Knarr Line](/star-rangers/lore/knarr-line/) ice-prospecting convoy working the far edge of its allocation found a moored hull with lights in it where the charts had nothing. The Tally had known where the Sun was the whole time. They had not been interested. In their own account the Sun is *the light that was*, a fact about the founders and not about them, and the sky above the body is not something the Tally have a custom of looking at; there is nothing in it they count.

They are chartered to nobody. They are not a Knarr Line cooperative, since they extract nothing for a market; they are not a Compact habitat, since the [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/) governs Earth orbit alone; and whether they sit above or below the population line that would put them out of Star Rangers reach is a question the Archive cannot answer, because the Tally have declined to give it a count. The people who count everything have not been counted, and the record holds the refusal as it holds every refusal on a self-governing deck: as the settlement's own business. Rangers have been aboard by invitation, twice. Both visits are in the ledger.

## What the Record Does Not Carry

Their name in their own tongue, which is theirs. The fault, which the ledgers name and do not describe. The body's name, which they have and which is not for the sky's sake. Their number. And what the language sounds like, which no rendering can carry and the Archive does not pretend to.
