Every survey filing has a shape it wants to write, and the shape is ours: an individual is born, grows, reaches an adulthood where the record can deal with them, declines, and dies, having descended from parents by the mixing of an inheritance, at the end of a long chain the environment pruned. It is a good shape. It is the shape of the people who invented the filing. **Nothing in it is a law**, and this entry exists so that no reader — and no clerk — mistakes it for one.

## Nothing Here Is Written in Our Letters

The chemistry half is settled elsewhere and only pointed at here: no other biosphere shares Earth's starting chemistry, [no other people in this record is a metazoan](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/), and DNA is one solution, found once, on one world. When a filing says an alien *gene*, *parent* or *inheritance*, it is using an Earth word for a functionally similar thing, on the standing principle that the original is the document and the conversion is the courtesy. What this entry adds is that the courtesy extends past chemistry, to the whole architecture of a life.

## Gender Without Our Machinery

**Aliens may have gender.** A people may hold two genders, or several, or none, or a structure the word *gender* translates only approximately — and where it exists, it is that people's own institution, declared in their own terms, carried in the record the way any coined vocabulary is carried. What gender never is, anywhere in this record, is an inference: it does not follow from a body plan, from a reproductive role, or from the observer's expectations, and a survey that assigns it from any of those has recorded a fact about the survey. The familiar terrestrial bundle — two sexes, two genders, the one derived from the other — is one local arrangement, and the record has stopped being surprised at how rarely it recurs.

## Made Otherwise Than by Winnowing

**Sexual reproduction and natural selection are mechanisms, not laws.** Where a biosphere walked that road, its life carries the signature and its people's own sciences found it, as ours did. But nothing obliges every world to have walked it. The [Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/) settles that life and sapience are permitted shapes — grammar, standing prior to any world — and **no tier anywhere specifies the mechanism by which a world must arrive at them**. Heredity on one world [passes a partner rather than a gene](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/). The [noogenic route](/star-rangers/lore/noogenic-seeding-system/) exists, and is not selection. A people may descend from a long winnowing, or from a short one, or from a beginning the survey's vocabulary of *ancestor* and *descent* fits so poorly that the filing marks the absence and stops. The record's question is never *how did this people earn their existence* — existence is not earned — but only *what is true of them now*.

## The Arc Is Not the Rule

Birth, growth, adulthood, decline, death: **one shape of a life, already broken piecewise by peoples on the record's own roster.** The [Mnemari](/star-rangers/lore/mnemari/) have no natural bound on lifespan and no natural decay of memory — no decline, and death only by event. The [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) live centuries without degradation. The [Prismeri](/star-rangers/lore/prismeri/) have two structurally distinct adult forms and no hierarchy between them — the survey mistook them for two species, because the filing wanted one adulthood per life. Others hold stages the arc has no slot for at all: a sessile phase, a colonial phase, a continuation that is neither the same individual nor a new one. A medic, a magistrate or a quartermaster who assumes an *age*, a *maturity* or a *next of kin* has assumed a biography, and the [same discipline that governs habitability](/star-rangers/lore/habitable-for-whom/) governs here: the arc is a relation to one kind of body, not a property of being alive.

## Persons, Counted Otherwise

The deepest assumption in the filing is not the arc. It is the **unit** — that a civilisation resolves into individuals, each with one identity, one biography, one name the paperwork can hold. **Some sapient civilisations do not have individual personhood or identity in that sense**, and the record states this as a fact about them, not a deficiency in them.

The Concord's own standards have already yielded the premise once: [a body has never been required to hold exactly one person](/star-rangers/glossary/plural-minds/), and the coupling rule that permits several identities in one body is symmetrical — it has never required one identity to stop at one body either, nor required identity to be held at the scale of a body at all. A people may hold identity in a lineage, a chorus, a working, a place; may individuate briefly, for a task, the way other peoples convene a committee; may answer every census question the survey has, correctly, without any answer resolving into a headcount.

The consequences are not abstract, and the record keeps them in view rather than smoothing them flat. The charter enters a world [by invitation](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) — issued by whom, where no *whom* is the native unit? Consent, testimony, responsibility and standing are all built on the person the way the arc is built on the body, and each has to be renegotiated, case by case, in the other party's own shape. The one worked domestic example is small but exact: the law's answer to plurality was not to force the unit but to [bind the undertaking to all of it together](/star-rangers/glossary/plural-minds/). That habit — translate the obligation, never the people — is the whole of the doctrine, and the rest is casework.
