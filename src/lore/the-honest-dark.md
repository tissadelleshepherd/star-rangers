Every settled world in the record keeps its night sky, and none of them legislated for it. [Technology that does not announce itself](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/) does not wash out the dark: a civilisation of matte displays and grey-box power plants throws almost no light upward, so the sky over a settlement looks much as it did before the settlement came. Dark skies are not a heritage protection here. They are what the infrastructure fails to ruin by default — and the consequence, easy to miss for being everywhere, is that **every settlement in the record can still see where it is.**

## What Each Sky Honestly Holds

With no glow to flatten them, night skies stay as different as their worlds, and the record's are a catalogue of what a sky can lawfully do. [Drithane's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/drithane/) performs on a timetable: twice an orbit the world crosses the Bríd System's debris band and the drithle run — silent white sparks over snowbound valleys, under a single low bright moon, predictable to the hour before the founders had finished unloading. [Prismere's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/prismere/) rings made the *summary paragraph* of its first survey, and its permanently hazed upper air renders the corridor Lattice visible — a night sky that is also an instrument, read professionally. [Trígrian's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/trigrian/) three suns make true night a negotiated interval: dark is what happens when all three consent to be elsewhere, and the triple sunset that opens the burning season is the sky's warning shot. A world's nights are as derivable as its weather — same discipline, [lights-out edition](/star-rangers/lore/weather-on-other-worlds/).

## Moons: the Working Furniture

A moon is the most consequential thing a night sky can hold, because it works three jobs at once — lamp, calendar, and [tide-raiser](/star-rangers/lore/tides-without-shores/). The tides entry left each world's raisers to its survey file; two of those files are now open. [Tír na nÓg's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/tir-na-nog/) tidal flats have their raiser on record — a single large moon the founding cooperatives declined to rename, listed as **An Ghealach**, *the moon*, which is what Irish already called the one at home: the same instinct that kept the four-season calendar, applied to the sky. And Drithane's low bright moon does over snow what moons do everywhere they exist — it makes the night *legible*, which for a cold world with outdoor stock is not an ornament but a working condition.

A calendar is the other job, and the record's most deliberate calendar-keepers make the point by exception: the [Krenyi keep their own](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/), which is only a meaningful statement because everyone else's sky offers one for free.

## The Sky as the Oldest Stale File

One night sky in the record does something no other human sky has ever done. From [New Cotswolds](/star-rangers/lore/new-cotswolds/), in the Wolds End galaxy, the Milky Way is an *object* — a smudge of light a farmer can point at, containing every other human being who has ever lived. It is the only settlement whose night sky holds everyone else; and because [no signal outruns a hull](/star-rangers/lore/ftl-mechanics/), that smudge is also the oldest stale file anyone looks at — light that left home ages before the founders did, a record lagged beyond any archive's patience. The survey doctrine that *the local record outranks the file* has, at Wolds End, a nightly illustration hanging overhead.

Which is the honest dark's whole character, stated once for the set. A sky nobody dims is a public record that cannot be edited — position, season, and time of night readable by anyone who learns the hand it is written in — and on every world in this catalogue, from the drithle's timetable to the fig-garden's slow stars, the night still tells its people the truth about where and when they are. The record, which is in the same business, has always treated that as a professional courtesy between colleagues.
