Ask a Cerebraun a simple question and the answer sometimes arrives in three parts, seconds apart, none of them contradicting the others. Xenolinguists spent a decade assuming this was evasion before admitting it was just how the species thinks.

The **Cerebraun** are a sapient people that a human observer tends to describe as cephalopod-like. That is a likeness, not a lineage: [no other people in this record is a metazoan](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/), and the Cerebraun share no descent with Earth's octopus or squid. What distinguishes them from every singly-brained species on record is a single structural fact: cognition, for a Cerebraun, is not one thing happening in one place.

## Physical and Cognitive Characteristics

- A dominant central brain handles executive judgment, language, and long-term planning — the "voice" a Cerebraun presents to the outside world.
- Each of the species' eight primary limbs carries its own semi-autonomous ganglion, capable of independent sensory processing, reflexive decision-making, and — controversially, among xenobiologists who first documented it — a measurable degree of localized preference and mood.
- The central brain does not fully override the limb-ganglia. It negotiates with them. A Cerebraun under stress can visibly experience one limb's ganglion acting against the central brain's stated intention, a phenomenon their own physicians treat as ordinary rather than pathological.
- Skin bears chromatophore and texture-changing tissue capable of rapid, high-bandwidth display — used for identity signaling, emotional broadcast, and a formal display-language reserved for precise technical and diplomatic communication.
- The body has no head and no front. It has an upper side and an underside and is otherwise the same from every direction. A Cerebraun has no pair of eyes. They see through many small eyes set along their limbs, so each ganglion watches its own stretch of the room.
- The eight limbs grow in two tiers of four and do different work. The lower four are short and bear the body's weight; the upper four are longer and do the handling. None carries suckers.
- At rest the skin is dry, matte and finely grained, and holds one quiet tone; the display described above is something a Cerebraun does, not how one looks when still.
- A Cerebraun can breathe the air humans keep on a station or a ship, and is not harmed by it: it is not toxic to them. It is not the air they would choose, though. Their tolerance for it is limited, and over prolonged exposure they come to dislike it, so a long posting among humans is a cost a Cerebraun carries rather than a danger they are protected from. What air they would choose, the record has not established.
- A note on rendering: a Cerebraun is **they** in the record's translation register. The nine centres are one person, and the pronoun counts that person, not the centres. It claims nothing about how the Cerebraun themselves mark a person, which the record has not been told. Where this page says *it*, it is speaking of the body or of one of its parts.

## Complex Inner Worlds

Outside observers who describe Cerebraun psychology as "layered" are, if anything, understating it. A single Cerebraun can hold what a human psychologist would classify as several simultaneous, only partially reconciled trains of affect and intention — not as internal conflict in the human sense, but as a normal condition of having cognition distributed across nine semi-independent processing centers instead of one.

This makes Cerebraun notoriously difficult for other species to model predictively. A human diplomat can learn to read a [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/)'s directness or a fellow human's tells. Predicting a Cerebraun requires tracking which of nine centers is currently weighted heaviest in a given exchange — information no outside observer has reliable access to, and which the Cerebraun themselves do not always resolve before they act.

## Communication

Cerebraun conversation operates on at least two simultaneous channels: spoken or written language carrying the central brain's stated position, and skin-display carrying real-time affective and confirmatory signal from the limb-ganglia beneath it. A trained Cerebraun listener reads both. Untrained outside observers — nearly everyone who isn't Cerebraun — reliably miss the second channel entirely, which is the single most-cited cause of cross-species misunderstanding in Hegemony diplomatic records.

Neither channel reaches a human directly. A Cerebraun has no voice a human can hear unaided; whether their spoken language is sound outside human hearing or not sound at all, the record has not established. So every exchange between a Cerebraun and a human passes through machine translation, and still does, however long the two peoples have worked side by side. The instruments render the stated position, the first channel, into words a human can parse. The skin they leave as it was, there to be read by anyone trained to read it, which is almost no one.

The failure is rarely dramatic, which is why it persists. What an untrained observer typically takes away is a correct transcript and a wrong meeting: the words were courteous and non-committal, and the second channel — where the cost of saying them was being paid in real time — was not read at all. [Filed Under Noise (S03E01C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s03/e01/s03e01c01/) and [Independent Verification (S03E01C02)](/star-rangers/seasons/s03/e01/s03e01c02/) run a Survey Corps reclassification through a Hegemony administrator across two chapters, and the material an outside reader has to work to catch is almost entirely on the channel the transcript does not carry.

## Social and Political Reflection

Cerebraun governance philosophy is, by the species' own account, a direct extension of their own neurology: legitimate authority is centralized in one dominant voice, and every subordinate part — whether a limb-ganglion or a subject administration — is expected to execute that voice's decisions rather than bargain over them as an equal. The [Cerebraun Hegemony](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/) is that governing philosophy applied at civilizational scale.

## What the Cerebraun Make of Humans

The record keeps each people's account of the others beside its own, attributed and never adopted, the way it keeps [a rendering of the rank ladder](/star-rangers/lore/the-ladder-in-other-tongues/) or [a likeness drawn by whoever is looking](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/#a-likeness-belongs-to-whoever-sees-it). The Cerebraun account of humans is on file on those terms. It is the Hegemony's, stated in its diplomatic register, and the record has met no Cerebraun who put it differently on the first channel; what the second channel carried while they said it is in no transcript. It is not a finding of the Archive's, and nothing below is.

In the Cerebraun account, humans are the descendants of predators: violent, warlike, vicious and savage, four words the Hegemony's register does not soften. The moderation came late and came slowly, once the species achieved sentience and became aware, imperfectly, of higher powers — and the imperfection is the part of the account the Cerebraun dwell on. For a long age after the awareness arrived, humans went on attributing their own violent behaviours to their gods and deities, as the account puts it. The appetite was old. The voice that might have governed it was new. And the first thing the new voice did with the old appetite was to file it upward.

Why the account has this shape is not hard to see, and the Cerebraun do not hide it. A Cerebraun body is [nine centres and one voice](#physical-and-cognitive-characteristics), and the Hegemony reads a polity as a body; [its own doctrine says so](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/#governing-philosophy). It reads a history the same way. A people's past, to a Cerebraun, is the record of a nervous system settling: the limbs act first, because limbs do, and the central voice arrives afterward to negotiate with what the limbs already were. On that reading a human prehistory of hunting and a human history of war are the limb acting before the voice, and the gods of the early traditions are a limb's behaviour taken for a voice's instruction — the confusion a Cerebraun knows best from the inside, since a limb acting against the central brain's stated intention is a thing their own physicians treat as ordinary. A Cerebraun hearing that a people once held its gods answerable for its wars does not hear an impiety. They hear a ganglion that had not yet learned which of its signals was its own.

What the account leaves out is on the same record, and the Archive files it beside the account rather than against it. The Hegemony [keeps a force of its own](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/) and administers [Meridian](/star-rangers/lore/planets/meridian/) over objections it records and does not act on; the moderation the account asks of humans is not a question the account puts to the Cerebraun, and the record does not put it for them. The account's shape is a ladder, savage, then sentient, then aware, and the Archive [ranks no people on one](/star-rangers/lore/what-the-record-refuses/#and-the-ladder-itself-is-refused), holding that a people's past is a shape and never a height. And the shape of the account is a Cerebraun shape: one voice, limbs that act before it, a history read as the voice's long arrival. The [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) have a name for an observer's own form mistaken for a law. The record does not apply it here. It notes only that the account would read the same whether it were true of humans or true of the Cerebraun.

It is worth setting beside the human account of the Cerebraun, which the Academy now teaches against. For a decade, xenolinguists filed the three-part answer as evasion, and the Academy keeps the case under [reading an economy as a temperament](/star-rangers/codex/xenolinguistics-handbook-failure-modes/#4-reading-an-economy-as-a-temperament): a pattern of cost mistaken for a fact about character. A human listener heard nine centres and filed them as one evasion; a Cerebraun reader saw one voice arriving late and filed a whole species by when it came. The record keeps both readings on the same shelf, and has adopted neither. Humans have their own accounts of themselves, more than one, and the [devotional traditions](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/) the Cerebraun account names have their own accounts of what they attributed and why; the record ranks none of them, and keeps the Cerebraun account as one more, from further off.
