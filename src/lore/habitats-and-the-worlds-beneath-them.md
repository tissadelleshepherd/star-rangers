A habitat makes its own air. Everything follows from that, and most of what people assume about orbital settlement follows from forgetting it.

## The Principle

The environment inside a ring is engineered end to end: pressure, mixture, temperature, spin gravity, light and its period. None of it is drawn from the planet below, none of it is constrained by the planet below, and the planet below cannot supply any of it. A habitat in orbit around a world is in the same position as a habitat in orbit around nothing at all, plus a view.

So **the species a habitat supports is a fact about the habitat and not about the world it orbits.** The two are frequently different, and there is no sense in which that is an anomaly requiring explanation. The [Guest Ring](/star-rangers/lore/the-guest-ring/) holds an atmosphere that would kill anyone on [Drithane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/drithane/) and Drithane holds conditions that would kill everyone on the Ring, and neither fact has any bearing on the other.

## Why a Ring Is Where It Is

Since compatibility is not a consideration, orbit gets chosen for the things that actually vary: access to a [fold-staging site](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/) or a harmonic corridor terminus, position in a traffic pattern, proximity to something worth being near, or simply an unobstructed and continuous view of the surface — which is a real reason and, at [Greenward](/star-rangers/lore/greenward-habitat/), the main one.

The corollary is worth stating because it is legally load-bearing: **a habitat above a world is not a claim on it.** Orbiting something confers nothing. The [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/formation-of-star-rangers/) hold no planetary or large-habitat jurisdiction at all and are present anywhere by invitation, which makes the point sharply, but the point is general and is not about the Rangers. A ring is a place people live. It is not a flag.

## Where the Assumption Comes From

The contrary assumption — that a habitat and the world beneath it are naturally the same people — is nearly universal in Solar System usage, and it has a traceable and slightly embarrassing cause.

Everybody learns what a habitat is at Sol. The five habitats of the [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/) are human, above a human world, administered under a human civil authority, and so is [New London](/star-rangers/lore/new-london-space-habitat/) at Earth–Moon L5. Six examples, all agreeing, none of them agreeing for any reason connected to orbital mechanics: they are human above a human world because humans built them at the only world humans then had. The rule people extracted from that sample is a description of one system's history, and it does not survive first contact with a second.

The Archive's own registry made the same mistake structurally and has not fully corrected it. Habitat entries inherit a `system` and are filed under the world they orbit, which is correct as geography and misleading as everything else — the Guest Ring appears in the atlas beneath Drithane, and a reader who has not been told otherwise will reasonably conclude the two have something to do with each other. They have a treaty. They do not have a relationship of the kind the filing implies.
