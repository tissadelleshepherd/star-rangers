The charter does not define an orbital habitat by its size, its shape, or how many rings it turns. It defines one by how many people have stopped intending to leave.

The **habitat threshold** is the population figure written into the [Star Rangers charter](/star-rangers/lore/formation-of-star-rangers/), above which a self-governing orbital settlement ceases to be a place the Rangers may enter at will and becomes a place they may enter only by invitation — and must leave when that invitation is withdrawn. Below the line, a structure in orbit is an installation, and the space around and inside it is the Rangers' proper domain. Above it, the same structure is a polity.

## Station and Habitat

The distinction is legal rather than architectural, which is why it confuses nearly everyone who meets it for the first time. Two structures of identical mass, power draw and ring diameter can fall on opposite sides of the line, and a station does not become a habitat by being enlarged. It becomes one when enough people live aboard permanently that the settlement acquires a government of its own — and the charter recognises that government by declining to reach past it.

This was not a drafting convenience. The threshold is one of the [hard-won concessions](/star-rangers/lore/planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters/) that outer-station delegates extracted before they would ratify the Consolidation Accords at all, on the reasoning that a boundary-operations body with unrestricted right of entry would simply have been the [Military Space Command](/star-rangers/lore/military-space-command/) under a better crest. The Rangers accepted a jurisdiction with a hole in it, deliberately, because the alternative was no charter.

What the drafters appear not to have anticipated is that a line a settlement can *cross* is a line a settlement can aim at.

## Why a Settlement Grows Toward the Line

Earth's own orbital population has an obvious founding cause, and it generalises badly: the [Crisis Centuries](/star-rangers/lore/crisis-centuries-terran-wildlife/) drove people off a damaged surface, and the habitats above it were built to hold them. Most worlds have no such history. The reasons an orbital settlement elsewhere might grow past the threshold, rather than staying an efficient working station with a rotating crew, are more various — and, in several cases, more deliberate.

**Because the point in space is the asset.** Fold geometry does not move. [Lagrange fold points](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/) sit where they sit, and a route worked continuously needs people living at its mouth rather than posted to it in shifts. [Eden](/star-rangers/lore/eden-space-habitat/) sits beside its Passage for this reason. A crew can be rotated; a community that has learned a particular corridor's behaviour over three generations cannot.

**Because the surface is not available yet.** [Frontier Transformation Protocols](/star-rangers/lore/frontier-transformation-protocols/) make terraforming slow by design, and a charter world may spend centuries with a surface that is being worked toward habitability rather than lived on. Orbit is where the population waits — and a population that waits long enough stops being a waiting population and becomes a resident one, with schools, courts and an electorate.

**Because the industry never came down.** Where shipyards, fabrication and ore processing already sit in orbit, lifting a workforce down a gravity well at the end of each shift is the expensive part of the arrangement. [Mars](/star-rangers/lore/planets/mars/) orbit demonstrates the pattern without ever completing it: substantial [orbital manufacturing](/star-rangers/lore/the-imperium/) and a command presence that outweighed Earth's delegations in the old [United Space Consortium](/star-rangers/lore/united-space-consortium/) assembly, but no civil population governing itself, and therefore nothing above the threshold at all.

**Because the species never needed a gravity well.** The [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) evolved for negligible gravity and settled the [Undersong Belt](/star-rangers/lore/undersong-belt/) the way another people might settle a river system. For them the threshold question is inverted: there was never a surface to prefer, and a planet would have been the eccentric choice.

**Because of what the line itself buys.** A settlement above the threshold cannot be boarded by a Ranger cutter on the Rangers' own initiative. For a community with something to protect — a language, a doctrine, a trade practice, a set of books nobody else keeps — that is not a technicality. It is the whole reason to build up rather than out.

What the line does not supply is anything to do next. Crossing it removes other people's authority from a settlement's decks and puts nothing in its place: a polity above the threshold still has neighbours, still needs traffic agreements and clearances and somewhere to send a complaint, and has forfeited the shared administrative ceiling that would otherwise have handled all three. This is why the settlements above the line tend to acquire something else instead — [the Commonwealth](/star-rangers/lore/the-commonwealth/) in New London's case, the Thing in the Belt's. Sovereignty is a wall, not a telephone, and every polity that builds the wall discovers it needs the telephone.

## Where the Concentrations Are

Three patterns are documented, and they are governed very differently.

**Earth orbit** is the densest and the most administered: five self-governing habitats under the [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/), sharing a Governor, a coordination office and a common civil chain. It is also incomplete, because [New London](/star-rangers/lore/new-london-space-habitat/) sits in the same orbital space, above the same threshold, and never entered the Compact at all — the largest inhabited body in Earth orbit that no Compact officer has jurisdiction over.

**The Belt** is the opposite arrangement. Every habitat and station chartered to the [Knarr Line](/star-rangers/lore/knarr-line/) sends delegates to the Thing in proportion to crewed tonnage, and what the Thing binds is deliberately narrow — claim boundaries, convoy safety, and the ice-and-ore allocation that stops one habitat starving another. Everything else stays with the habitat. Comparable population, a fraction of the shared government.

**The Undersong Belt** is neither, being a concentration by excavation rather than construction: dozens of independent burrow-clusters through an asteroid population, tuned to each other acoustically long before anyone thought to coordinate them administratively.

Beyond Sol, the [Federation of Sentient Beings](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) charters "member worlds, orbital habitats, and constituent peoples" in the same breath, which suggests the orbital polity is unremarkable out there. The Survey Archive has never enumerated them.

## The Standing Problem

Put the threshold and the Compact together and a tension falls out that the Compact's own administrators are careful not to state plainly. Each of the five member habitats individually benefits from being unboardable. Their shared coordination office cannot direct any of them. And the neighbour that declined to join is proof that membership was always optional.

The Compact holds because coordination is worth more to five habitats than independence is — so far, and on the evidence of a Governor who built the dual accountability line precisely so that no habitat's administration would ever have to choose between local legitimacy and Compact-wide efficiency. It is not a chain of command. It is a standing agreement among five governments that could each, at any point, simply stop agreeing.

The bill for the arrangement is not usually presented at the constitutional level, where it would at least be argued about. It arrives as a case file. Five unboardable habitats means five detective bureaus each with jurisdiction over one habitat and no power to follow anything across a line — so an offence organised to cross those lines is an offence that five competent bureaus can each investigate a fifth of. [The Desk That Called First (S02E01C01)](/star-rangers/seasons/s02/e01/s02e01c01/) is that gap arriving as an Eden compliance referral dull enough that nobody expected it to test anything. The Compact's answer, [Orbital Five-O](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/), is instructive about the shape of the problem: a unit taking its taskings from the Governor personally rather than through any habitat's chain, because the sovereignty the threshold buys is precisely the thing that cannot be asked to hurry.
