A Ranger survey vessel came out of a fold it had not known it was in, and found someone already living at the exit.

**Tsittak** is a [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) settlement cut into a lone body of nickel-iron, a few kilometres across, drifting in deep interstellar space with no star within several light-years. It sits beside the mouth of a [quiet-zone tunnel](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/#quiet-zone-tunnels-undetected-natural-fold-conduits): a stable, natural fold conduit threading a region of space flat enough by sheer distance from every mass. It is small, a few hundred people by the settlement's own reckoning, and it answers in practice to no one but itself.

## The Name

A Chthonari name is struck, not spoken, and a place's name is struck the same way: a short signature of taps on whatever structure is underfoot. *Tsittak* is how that signature sounds to a human ear through a deck plate, which is how the record comes to spell it. The Survey Corps filed the body under a catalogue number before it knew the place had a name, and the number stands on the register beside the name. The Corps uses the name.

## Found at the Exit

The Survey's own guidance on quiet-zone tunnels says there is no standing way to detect one in advance, and that a vessel not watching for it may get its first confirmation at the exit. That is how the record met Tsittak. In 2794 UCSD a Ranger survey vessel on an ordinary deep-space transit registered a corridor condition forming around it, and the next reading was the far mouth of a tunnel nobody had charted, with a worked metal body a few hundred kilometres off and a Chthonari hail, relayed through a translating set, asking whether the crew needed anything.

The settlement was not new. How long it had been there, and how its founders first came to the tunnel, are not things the record holds: Tsittak's own account is kept in the Undersong, cut into the structure of the body itself, and the record has a description of that account rather than a reading of it. What the record does hold is that the settlement had a name, a working dock and a habit of greeting traffic before the Corps knew there was any traffic to greet, which is the same shape as the [Federation](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/)'s boundary outposts logging three Ranger survey passes before anyone spoke.

## Living on the Rock

There is no daylight at Tsittak, and its people do not miss it. A Chthonari reads the world through structure rather than light, and a body of native nickel-iron is, to a Chthonari, already most of a town: the members are left, not built, by cutting away everything the rock will not need to be doing. The settlement runs on fusion, like everything else in settled space, and keeps its warmth inside the metal; seen from outside, it is a dark body with a lit dock and almost nothing else.

What the rock gives Tsittak that no instrument has given the Survey is the tunnel's voice. When a corridor condition forms in the tunnel, the gradient around the mouth shifts, and the shift reaches the body as a faint, slow stress in the metal. To a people who feel a fold scaffold's gradient error through a hull, that is audible, in their sense of the word. A Chthonari standing in Tsittak knows when something is inside the tunnel, and roughly how long it has been there, before any sensor aboard an arriving vessel registers it.

That knowledge is the settlement's, and it does not travel. It comes from generations of listening to one tunnel through one body of rock, and it gives no way to find another tunnel or to hear one from anywhere else. The Survey Corps still has no protocol for detecting a quiet-zone tunnel in advance. It has, at this one, neighbours who already know.

## Standing

Tsittak is well below the [habitat threshold](/star-rangers/lore/habitat-threshold/), so by the letter of the charter it is ground the Rangers may enter at will. By custom they do not: a Corps vessel bound for the tunnel calls Tsittak first and asks, which is how the Rangers came to know the place and how the place prefers to be known. Many of the Corps' Chthonari scaffold engineers have rotated through it, and some have come back to stay.

It belongs to no polity's administration in any working sense. The Undersong Belt, the Chthonari's home, is a member territory of the Federation, but the Federation has no office that could govern a town this far from anything, and Tsittak has never asked it to.
