The Corps has never commissioned a song, and it keeps a corps for music. Both facts are true, and the second explains the first.

**The Music Corps** is the Star Rangers' specialist corps for music and for whatever stands in music's place among the peoples the Rangers meet. Like the others it is a college of the [Academy](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-academy/), owns its own standard, confirms its own cadets, and answers to no rank on the command ladder for what it teaches. It is no more military than the [Survey Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-survey-corps/); its Rangers arrive, as the record has it of everyone the Corps sends, with an instrument, a page and a question.

## Two things it does

The first is the plainer one. The Corps' **musician specialization**, the designation carried by Rangers such as Perrin Ashgrove and Marlow Calloway of [Slipwave](/star-rangers/glossary/slipwave/), is the Music Corps' own. It exists for the reason [Rank and the Chain of Command](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/) gives for every sideways designation: so that someone extremely good at one thing is not required to stop doing it in order to keep being promoted. A musician-specialized Officer plays muster halls and dock common rooms wherever posted, and the Corps commissions none of it. What circulates, circulates on its own.

The second is the study, and it is the reason the corps is a corps and not a hobby with a budget. It has a branch of its own, [Xenomusicology](/star-rangers/glossary/xenomusicology/), a school of the college constituted when the body of work outgrew the musicians' standard. Its question is how the peoples the Rangers meet understand music, or whatever stands in its place, in their cultures and in their bodies. The record already holds the cases that make the question serious. Among the [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/), music and mathematics are nearly the same thing. On Thavren, [Ovruhn](/star-rangers/lore/ovruhn/) law is precedent chanted, and the Under-Song is a legal fact before it is an aesthetic one. The Quiet-Built among the [Krenyi](/star-rangers/lore/krenyi/) take little interest in music at all, which is as much a datum as the others. The [Undersong Belt](/star-rangers/lore/undersong-belt/) is worked from something closer to a score than a survey. And the [Sentinel](/star-rangers/lore/planets/sentinel/) signal sounded, unmistakably, like music, to an officer who had been trained to ask what that word means to someone else.

The study is comparative and it is patient. A Music Corps Ranger on a Survey rotation is not there to perform; she is there to notice when a people's arithmetic has a tempo, or a people's law a key, and to write down what she can show rather than what she heard.

## Intake

The college takes its novices on the way every college does, provisionally, under evaluation, on its own schedule. What is particular to it is the placement: a Novice is put with a working ensemble for the time the study needs, and a civilian one serves as well as any, because the point is to learn how music holds a room before learning how it holds a people. In the Xenomusicology branch's files each placement is a project, named for the ensemble. The college confirms Cadet standing when it is satisfied, and the cadet's service track from there is whatever the ladder makes it; tracks are keyed to service, never to corps.

The record's own example is [Tissadelle Shepherd](/star-rangers/characters/tissadelle-shepherd/), who came into the Corps through this college as a Novice of the Xenomusicology branch, with the study to carry and the branch's Slipwave project to hold, and whose Cadet standing, once confirmed, put her on the general operations track that her Season 1 opens on. The band's story is told under its own name. What the college gave her is the habit the Sentinel section of her page records without naming: reading the space between the notes.

## What it is not

It is not a band, a choir or a ceremonial unit, and it has no repertoire. The [Star Rangers Anthem](/star-rangers/codex/star-rangers-anthem/) reached the musters without a decision by anyone, and the Music Corps was as surprised as the rest of the Corps to find cadet cohorts singing a dock-circuit recording. It has never adopted the song, and it never will, for the same reason it exists at all: it studies what music is to people, and a commissioned anthem would tell it nothing.
