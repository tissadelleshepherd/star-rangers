The survey has no checkbox marked *habitable*, because the word is an unfinished sentence. A world is habitable **for** someone, and the file always says whom. Unqualified, the record allows the word one meaning only — *bears its own biosphere* — and that category is generous. The category people usually mean — *the place itself asks nothing of them* — is so narrow it has two confirmed members, and one of them is home.

## The Envelope, Not the Frame

Every species carries an **envelope**: the band of gravity, atmospheric pressure and chemistry, temperature, and light inside which it lives unassisted. *Habitable for X* means, precisely, *inside X's envelope* — and the envelope is a different fact from the body plan. [Frames describe structure and claim no ancestry](/star-rangers/lore/cross-biosphere-taxonomy/); an envelope describes tolerance and claims no shape. The two do not travel together. The [Pandoids](/star-rangers/lore/planets/fliade/) are upright, furred, and near-humanoid by frame, and their home is a geothermal cavern network under a surface that would kill an unprotected human by cold; a shared silhouette predicts nothing about shared air.

So the loose phrase *humanoid-habitable* is the one the survey declines, gently: the accurate index is the species, never the shape. What the phrase is reaching for — worlds inside the *human* envelope — the record files under its own name.

## The Wider Category Is Full; the Human One Is Nearly Empty

The general category, worlds habitable for someone, is well populated, and each entry is precise about its someone. [Meridian's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/meridian/) dense, chemically hostile seas are a temperate homeland — for Meridian's own armored people. [Nerath](/star-rangers/lore/pelagene-littoral/) is habitable in a boundary layer of its water column; [Kharis Belt IV](/star-rangers/lore/serephine-dunekin/) in thin air under hard ultraviolet; [Veyr-3](/star-rangers/lore/veyr-basaltborn/) under crushing gravity beside working volcanoes. Every one of these is somebody's comfortable world and every one would kill a human — which cuts the other way too, and canon states the symmetry flatly: **no alien people surveyed is natively compatible with Earthlike gravity or atmosphere, on their own world or anyone else's.** The peoples of Meridian and Nerath can no more walk Tír na nÓg than a human can swim their seas.

The doctrine underneath is the entry's whole cargo: **nobody's envelope is the universe's default.** *Hostile* is as unfinished a sentence as *habitable* — Meridian is not a hostile world but somebody's mild one — and a survey that files another people's homeland as hostile has only recorded whose file it is.

Meanwhile the class people actually mean — the full human envelope, occurring naturally — holds Earth, and [Tír na nÓg](/star-rangers/lore/planets/tir-na-nog/), and nothing else in the catalogue. Its edge is instructive, because the edge is a spectrum and not a wall: [Drithane](/star-rangers/lore/planets/drithane/) comes closest, its thin cold air breathable at the price of weeks of acclimatisation and its agriculture under glass — walkable, but not free — and [Kernowek Reach's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kernowek-reach/) native air is merely survivable, which the survey notes is a different word from habitable. Below that line, settlement is engineering: [Ynys Wydrin's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/ynys-wydrin/) domes, Kernowek's tended band, the platform worlds' [engineered perimeters](/star-rangers/lore/the-cost-of-the-crossing/), and the [habitats](/star-rangers/lore/habitats-and-the-worlds-beneath-them/), which manufacture an envelope outright — the species a habitat supports being a fact about the habitat. Human settlement, read honestly, is mostly envelope manufacture; the two worlds where the envelope occurs naturally are the exception the whole industry exists to substitute for.

## What the Relation Costs, and Buys

Three consequences the record already lives with follow directly.

**Meetings happen in rooms somebody built.** With no shared ground anywhere in the catalogue, a human and a Meridian can only ever meet inside an envelope one of them manufactured — which is why first contact is a matter of ships, orbits, and [reciprocal rings](/star-rangers/lore/greenward-habitat/), and why the Guest Ring's non-descent clause is [one sentence covering two facts](/star-rangers/lore/the-guest-ring/): protocol where the crew could land, chemistry where no permission would help.

**Filtering by envelope is not ranking by worth.** The survey's habitability index says who can walk where and nothing else; Meridian's people are not less at home in the universe for the narrowness of their band, nor humans for theirs. Everyone's band is narrow. That is the finding.

**And Tír na nÓg's status gets its doctrinal name.** The [islands entry](/star-rangers/lore/the-cost-of-the-crossing/) called it the record's most important island; this one says why in the survey's own terms: it is the only ground in the catalogue where the human envelope occurs *naturally*, off Earth — one world, in a record of hundreds, where the sentence *habitable for humans* finishes without an asterisk. Everything about how the record treats it follows from that sentence having almost nowhere else to be true.
