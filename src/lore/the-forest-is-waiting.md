Twice in Winterbourne's parish records, a stretch of downland lost its flock — and went to scrub within a decade and toward woodland inside a century, both times, until the sheep came back. Nothing planted it. Nothing needed to. On every watered world in the record, the forest is not one possible outcome among several; it is what the land does when everyone else stops.

## The Default and the Bet

Those two facts sit in tension, and the tension is the whole ecology of trees. A forest is the **default** cover of any ground wet enough to afford it — the state the land returns to whenever [the grassland's enforcers](/star-rangers/lore/the-ledger-and-the-truce/) lapse, which is why that entry could call the truce a truce. But a tree is also a **bet**: a structure that takes decades to pay for itself, wagered on the claim that the next century will resemble this one closely enough for the investment to mature.

[Trígrian](/star-rangers/lore/planets/trigrian/) is the world that refuses the wager, and its page says so in the record's own economy: *no forests, no year that resembles the one before*. Grass bets a season and loses gracefully; a tree bets a century, and on a world whose climate can follow a decade of generosity with a continental burn, the bet never survives to maturity. The absence of forests on Trígrian is not a poverty. It is a correct price.

## A Forest Makes Its Own Weather

A canopy is the one biome that builds its own building, and inside it the [weather doctrine](/star-rangers/lore/weather-on-other-worlds/) runs at local scale: shade against the insolation, stillness against the wind, humidity held and re-spent as transpiration until a large enough forest is partly watering itself. [Verdance's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/verdance/) canopy belt is the record's full expression — a multi-layered rainforest whose upper storeys form a nearly continuous living roof, dense enough in endemic species to lead the catalogue for every surveyed world — and its politics have the same architecture as its ecology: the Canopy Concord governs from cities built *into* the forest structure rather than cleared from it, a polity that treats the roof as load-bearing because it is.

The working end of the same fact is small and canonical. [Winnow Hanger's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/winterbourne/) hillside wood combs water out of passing mist — condensation on the canopy, dripping clear of the leaves into the dew pond below — which is why the wood is not cut, and why a settlement four kilometres from any spring exists at all. A wood can be a water instrument, and the oldest forestry decision on any world is the one Winterbourne made without writing it down: some trees earn their ground by standing still.

## A Wood Is a Clock

Trees keep the living half of the archive the [glaciers keep frozen](/star-rangers/lore/stone-fire-and-ice/). The bookkeeping is the same — one layer per year, sealed in order, readable by core — but a tree's version stays alive while it records, which makes old trees the survey's favourite kind of witness: present for centuries, silent, and datable to the ring. On Vellamar the [Kingdom's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/kingdom-of-the-five-islands/) garden quarter holds specimen trees that predate the current dynasty — a court that can, in principle, check its own history against a fig — and the record's regard for such trees is the same regard it holds for any long, continuous, locally kept record: [they cannot be imported, only have been kept](/star-rangers/lore/weather-on-other-worlds/).

## The Strange Woods

Two of the record's named woods are not woodland in the ordinary sense, and each is worth holding apart from it.

[Prismere's](/star-rangers/lore/planets/prismere/) **glasswood** is a forest by structure and a lens by function — slow-growing, crystalline-barked, its translucent foliage catching Lattice light and starlight and re-emitting them softened — a canopy that participates in the sky-reading its world is known for. It is genuinely flora, genuinely a wood, and doing a second job.

[Dryadic groves](/star-rangers/lore/dryadic-trees/) are the sharper case, and the [drainage doctrine's](/star-rangers/lore/rivers-old-lakes-young/) diagnostic bears repeating from this side: an ordinary treeline follows the water, and a dryadic grove follows the [Etheric fault lines](/star-rangers/lore/dryadic-trees/) instead. This entry is about the woods that follow the water. A dryadic grove is boundary apparatus wearing woodland's shape, and a surveyor who files one as forestry has mis-filed a layer — the botanical equivalent of the mistake the record refuses over [fault lines](/star-rangers/lore/stone-fire-and-ice/) themselves.

What every true wood in the record shares, strange or plain, is the thing this entry opened on: patience as a strategy. The forest does not compete for ground the way grass and fire do, loudly and annually. It waits for the enforcement to lapse — and on every watered world the survey has ever closed the file on, it was still waiting.
