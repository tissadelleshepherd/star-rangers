A corridor that was safe last year is not a corridor that is safe. The Navigation Corps exists because that sentence costs money and nobody has found a way to stop it being true.

The **Star Rangers Navigation Corps** certifies routes. The [Safety Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-safety-corps/) certifies hulls. A lawful fold transit requires both, issued by two bodies against two different objects, and neither corps can issue the other's certificate or waive it. An operator holding one and not the other is not partly cleared. They are not cleared.

## Drift routes

Fold adjacency is a relation that holds for a while and then stops holding. A route is therefore certified *as of a date*, and the Corps' standing work is not discovery but **re-survey**: establishing how fast a given corridor is moving away from the survey that cleared it, and how much of the interval that leaves.

The consequence is the one operators find hardest to plan around. **A route certificate expires. A hull certificate does not.** A vessel that has changed in no respect, on a passage it has flown for years, can become uncleared overnight because the corridor moved and the interval ran out — and the Corps has never found a defensible way to soften this, having tried at intervals for most of its existence.

Drift is lawful, gradual, and in most corridors well characterised. That is precisely what makes the failure mode dangerous rather than dramatic: nothing announces itself, the passage looks exactly as it did, and the margin is gone before anything is visibly wrong.

## Clearance tiers

The Corps carries the [Lagrange classification](/star-rangers/lore/lagrange-fold-points/) for fold-staging positions, and the tier system is a gravity argument before it is a navigational one — consistent with the general rule that [harmonic operations are safer the shallower the well](/star-rangers/lore/teleportation-limitations/).

**Tier 1** covers L4 and L5 of any stable planetary system, where a vessel holds position passively while the scaffold forms and small disturbances damp rather than amplify. **Tier 2** covers L1 and L2 where the mass ratio produces a shallow instability gradient or station-keeping infrastructure already exists, and requires explicit clearance rather than standing permission, because continuous station-keeping load competes with scaffold preparation for exactly the systems that must not be competed with. L3 is not cleared, its position on the far side of the primary making real-time monitoring impractical.

## Why it is not the Survey Corps

The authority was the Survey Corps' own for most of the Star Rangers' history, and older protocol documents still name it that way. Two things ended the arrangement.

The first was practical. The Survey Corps' remit is enormous — boundary monitoring, the mapping of frontier worlds, and on occasion the first contact that follows from mapping them — and route clearance sat inside it as an administrative appendage of a body whose attention was, reasonably and permanently, somewhere else. A function that issues permissions cannot be the thing a corps does in the margins of its real work.

The second was the same founding principle that had already produced two other corps — that **a body whose conclusions matter cannot answer to the command chain those conclusions might embarrass** — applied one turn further, to a body whose conclusions could embarrass *itself*.

The distinction is worth stating precisely, because it is the whole argument:

**The Survey Corps records what a corridor is doing. The Navigation Corps decides who may fly it.**

Held in one body, those two jobs put a measurement within reach of its own consequence. A drift reading that closes a heavily used passage costs somebody a great deal of money, and the officer taking the reading should never be the officer who will be asked to justify the closure. Separation does not make the reading correct. It removes the single pressure that would make an incorrect reading comfortable — which is a smaller claim than the Corps' founding documents make for it, and the only one its own archivists will defend.

The [Survey Corps](/star-rangers/codex/survey-corps-protocol/) still takes the measurements. It has never issued a clearance since.

## Where it sits among the specialist corps

The Survey Corps collects. The [Science Corps](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-science-corps/) explains what was collected. The Navigation Corps clears — and of the three it is the only one whose output is a **permission** rather than a finding.

That difference is not cosmetic. A finding can be disputed, republished, or superseded, and the Science Corps' independence exists so that it can be. A permission is acted on immediately by people who will be somewhere else by the time anyone reviews it, which is why the Corps is the most procedurally encumbered body in the Star Rangers and the least apologetic about it. Like every specialist corps it answers to nobody on the command ladder for what its determinations say; see [Rank and the Chain of Command](/star-rangers/lore/star-rangers-command-hierarchy/).

## Whose certificate counts

Not every polity that folds maintains anything resembling a Navigation Corps, and the Corps' authority stops precisely where the Star Rangers' charter stops.

Some do. Several powers within the [Federation of Sentient Beings](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) certify passages through bodies arrived at independently, by reasoning the Corps did not supply and against standards it has no standing to audit. That two civilisations separately concluded a corridor should be re-surveyed on a schedule is, to the Corps' archivists, the least surprising convergence in the record and the one they are fondest of citing.

Others maintain nothing of the kind — some because they do not fold, some because the function is discharged inside an institution that looks nothing like a corps, and some for reasons nobody has been able to establish because nobody has been invited to ask.

What the Corps does about this is constrained by the charter rather than by policy. It holds **no jurisdiction beyond invitation**, enters only where invited, and leaves when the invitation is withdrawn — the [Cerebraun Hegemony](/star-rangers/lore/cerebraun-hegemony/) having withdrawn one without explanation and without objection from the crew involved, which is the case the Corps points at when asked whether the limit is real. So in space that is not the Concord's, the Corps cannot clear a route and does not pretend to. It publishes what it has surveyed, publishes plainly where it has surveyed nothing, and leaves the decision with whoever holds the authority there.

The distinction operators care about is narrower and more awkward. A certificate the Corps has not audited is not thereby a bad certificate — a great many are demonstrably better than the Concord minimum — but the Corps will not underwrite what it has not checked, and its refusal reads to some polities as exactly the condescension the charter was written to prevent.

## Mutual recognition, and what it costs in time

Where a sister organisation exists, both bodies will ordinarily attempt to certify each other. Each audits the other's standard, its testing regime, its re-survey intervals, and — the part that consumes the time — the assumptions neither wrote down because neither had ever needed to.

**The process is measured in centuries.** This is not obstruction on either side. A mature standard encodes a great deal its own authors cannot articulate on request: what a tolerance was set against, which failure the interval was chosen to catch, what the silence in a clause is understood locally to cover. Two bodies discovering each other's silences is slow work, and neither will accept an audit it did not perform, which means the work is done twice by construction.

Three outcomes appear in the record, and the Corps' archivists are careful that none is described as success or failure:

- **Merger.** The audit concludes that the two standards were the same standard reached by different routes, and the bodies stop maintaining two of them. Rare, and generally taken as the strongest available evidence that the underlying physics was doing the work rather than either institution's culture.
- **Alliance.** Mutual recognition without merger. Each body certifies within its own space and honours the other's certificates in it, both standards persisting, both continuing to diverge slowly and deliberately.
- **Neutral standoff.** No recognition in either direction, neither body disputing the other's competence, and neither willing to underwrite what it could not fully audit. The commonest outcome by a wide margin.

The Corps' published position is that a standoff is not a breakdown. It is an accurate statement that the audit did not complete, left standing rather than papered over — which is the same discipline it applies to a corridor it has not re-surveyed, and for the same reason.

Two recognitions are settled, and between them they are the reason a Corps certificate is worth carrying anywhere.

The [Solar System Concord](/star-rangers/lore/solar-system-concord/) recognises the Star Rangers at home. That is the domestic case and the older one: a Corps certificate is lawful currency across every polity that has adopted Concord standards, which is most of them and never all.

The [Celtic Union of Planets](/star-rangers/lore/celtic-union-of-planets/) recognises them as well, which matters more than the arithmetic of one confederation suggests: Union space carries a great deal of the traffic the Corps clears, and a standard honoured at [Tír na nÓg](/star-rangers/lore/planets/tir-na-nog/) is a standard honoured along most of the routes that reach it.

The [Federation of Sentient Beings](/star-rangers/lore/federation-of-sentient-beings/) recognises them beyond it. That recognition took the long form of the process above, and it is the sole reason a Ranger certificate means anything whatever outside human space. The Corps' own archivists note, without pressing the point, that the charter constraints which made the Rangers legible to a non-interference confederation were written by outer-station delegates who had never met one and were arguing about something else entirely.

## What it files as its own

The Corps' loss-adjustment practice is unusual and has never been adopted by its counterparts elsewhere. Where a vessel is lost on a **lapsed route** and the re-survey interval was the Corps' to schedule, the Corps files the loss against itself rather than the operator — both certificates having been genuine, both bodies having done their work, and the corridor having simply moved while nobody was looking.

Its underwriters have never enjoyed this. The Corps' position, unchanged through several attempts to reform it, is that an authority which issues permissions and accepts none of the consequences of issuing them will eventually issue them faster. See [Catastrophic Failure in Fold Transit](/star-rangers/lore/fold-transit-catastrophic-failure/) for the loss classes and the three readings settled space gives them.
