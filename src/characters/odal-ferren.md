Odal Ferren keeps a line on [Quern](/star-rangers/lore/planets/quern/): a run of turning-stones along the night-ward edge of the Howl, where the settlement logs how far the standing rain holds and so keeps the only calendar the world has. The record holds one document of Ferren's, and it was not written. It is the address a line-keeper gives a watcher standing a first rain-watch, taken down at the speaker's dictation under the standing transcription arrangement, checked against the settlement's own copy, and returned with a message that the transcript was accurate, that most of it was none of the record's business, and that it could be filed anyway.

It is the first Quernish cultural document the record holds. The Survey Corps, which could not build a hut on the line that the Howl would not take, copies the settlement's log by hand instead, and Ferren tells the new watcher plainly what that makes the watch: the oldest unbroken measurement anybody owns, on the question the Corps' instruments cannot sit still long enough to ask.

## Known History

- Line-keeper on Quern, undated. The settlement was founded in the first decades of fold transit, and the record does not say where in its century Ferren's keeping falls. [What the Stones Say](/star-rangers/codex/what-the-stones-say/).
- Stood the two witnessings, as every Quernish adult has, and speaks of having seen the sun once, through the last of the rain.
- Has people past the far stones — "mine are; yours will be" — and has walked night-ward with a carrying and had what the settlement holds about the Hush said there, once: "That was enough for me. I have known nobody it was not enough for."
- Was kept awake one year, when new, by the question every survey pass asks, whether the air is leaving, and gives the watcher the settlement's standing answer: the stones say where the rain is, not where it is going. The Quern lore entry cites the line-keepers' teaching as the settlers' reply.

## Character Notes

The address shows a teacher who gives reasons, withholds nothing the watcher needs, and says nothing the watcher does not. Ferren speaks of the Burn as a debt, seldom and without jokes; of the Howl as the only thing on the world that has never lied to anyone; of the log as the watcher's own word, because "a machine cannot be believed, and the year runs on being believed". The one personal admission, the sleepless year, is offered so that the watcher's own will not shame them. The last instruction is the shortest: stand where the wind was already going, log what holds, be worth believing.

The record holds the name, the office and the address. Ferren's age, which line, the household, how long the keeping has run and whether it still runs are not on file. The settlement reviewed the transcript and said most of it was none of the record's business, and the record, to its credit, has asked once and not twice.
