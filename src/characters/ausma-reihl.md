Ausma Reihl works in the fold-dynamics unit of [the Institute](/star-rangers/lore/the-institute/) and is the author of Institute paper no. 8811, [A Material Account of the Dock Seven Event](/star-rangers/codex/dock-seven-material-account/): the account of the loss at Dock Seven in 2831 UCSD that reached the newsnets, that the fold-transit safety courses teach, and that a majority of people in settled space would give if asked. The Survey Archive filed it with the note that it is not the Archive's account, and that where the two differ the Archive has largely stopped trying to persuade the Institute.

The paper says the strong version of its finding, and says that it is doing so. An aperture generated by [Hyperfold Yield Combine](/star-rangers/lore/hyperfold-yield-combine/) fold equipment, overused and out of calibration, was held open for about four months at a site already carrying a chronometric anomaly, and the result was a shear event of a class the models had predicted since they were written. Nothing about the failure, Reihl writes, requires explanation beyond the equipment and the duration. The shapes fifty-one witnesses reported are read as structured discharge, named with the first word available; the survival of every Star Rangers crewman is read as preparation.

## Known History

- Author of [A Material Account of the Dock Seven Event](/star-rangers/codex/dock-seven-material-account/), Institute Papers no. 8811, circulated open, written after the loss at Dock Seven in 2831 UCSD. The record holds the paper correct about the mechanism: industrial-scale unauthorised folding is what tore that boundary.
- Printed in its fourth section, at the finding's length, the five observations the paper cannot account for: a closed figure established in under one second, which Reihl calls the paper's most serious unresolved item; four sources holding position outside the figure for eleven minutes; the loss of every hull on the Combine's registry to some nine hundred thousand kilometres and of no other; a warm residual signature at the failure point that has not decayed; and a Star Rangers field analyst's log of the selectivity, made within the hour and never asked for again.
- The conclusion expects the five to be closed by better instruments. The archivist's note calls the third of them the only place in settled space where the Institute's house rule has printed the evidence against the Institute's own finding, at length, unhedged, in the same paper.

## Character Notes

Reihl writes with the Institute's two refusals held together: the refusal to doubt a witness and the refusal to use a witness's word. The gallery crews observed a genuine phenomenon and reported it with the vocabulary settled space had supplied them. The paper knows its account is the less satisfying one and says so, and says it does the Corps more credit than the alternative. What the writing shows most is the house rule kept past the point where it costs the finding: the paper says, in its own voice, that preparation does not explain why a Combine tender nine hundred thousand kilometres out, crewed by people doing nothing at all, stopped returning a signature, and leaves the sentence standing beside the conclusion it undercuts.

The record gives Reihl no species, no age, no world and no other paper. It places Reihl at the Institute after 2831 UCSD and nowhere else, and carries nothing of what became of the five open items.
