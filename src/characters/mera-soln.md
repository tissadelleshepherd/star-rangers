Mera Soln stood the night watch at [Threshold Station](/star-rangers/lore/threshold-station/), 00:00 to 04:00 station time, from the Operations Desk. The desk is one of the station's several chains, beside the Survey Corps detachment and the Maintenance Division, and it does not answer to them. Its concern is docking: whether a vessel can be handed off on a clock everyone agrees on.

The one document in Soln's name is an addendum, not an incident report. It opened no new incident number and did not try to. Its stated purpose is watch continuity: that the next officer on the night watch know the Dock Seven timing asymmetry was recurring, whatever the file said about it.

## Known History

- 2815 UCSD, Late Summer, Day 52: filed the *[Dock Seven Night Watch Addendum — Day 52](/star-rangers/codex/dock-seven-night-watch-addendum-day-52/)*, dated the day of Station Manager Cors Delveyne's closure note on [Incident Report 001](/star-rangers/codex/incident-report-001/) and filed after it. At 01:22, after a clean reset at shift start, chronometers C-7A and C-7B had diverged by forty seconds and held there, with no progressive drift, nominal relay diagnostics and no thermal or electromagnetic spike in the dock. Three nights earlier, on Day 49, [Technician N. Isern's maintenance log](/star-rangers/codex/maintenance-log-day-49-dock-seven/) had recorded the same return at 03:10.
- The same addendum records Traffic Control's view that the delay from dual-clock confirmation was manageable then and unsafe under surge conditions, and the watch's own assessment that the classification *calibration issue* was no longer operationally useful, since the recurrence profile matched structured anomaly behaviour rather than instrument wear.
- Its three recommendations: keep dual-clock verbal callouts on every night-watch docking transaction; keep the addendum attached to the [first drift report](/star-rangers/timeline/year-minus-11-first-drift-report/) in local briefing packets; escalate to external survey review if the gap ever widened beyond forty seconds.
- 2826 UCSD: the Survey Archive's pass over the file marked the addendum as one of the few surviving records that preserve operational dissent after the original closure decision.

## Character Notes

Soln's addendum does not argue with the manager. It records what the clocks did, what the dock crews had to do about it, and what the watch thought the word on the file was worth, and it asks for nothing beyond the callouts the watch was already making. The dissent is in the sentence *classification is no longer operationally useful*, and in filing it at all on the day the report was closed. The eleven-year record of the forty seconds runs through documents like this one, kept by people whose job was the next vessel in rather than the boundary.

The record holds nothing of Soln beyond the one watch: no origin, no species stated, no rank before or after, and no later filing. The record places Soln on the Dock Seven night watch in Late Summer 2815 UCSD and nowhere after.
