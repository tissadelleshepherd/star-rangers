Some officers retire from the Star Rangers into memoirs, some into consultancy, and a few into silence. Asteria retired into a chapter house.

She was about a hundred years old when she did it, which is late by any standard except the Corps' own: the maximum retirement age is around a hundred and ten, and a full pension had been hers to take for a quarter of a century before she took anything. She used most of that optional stretch. Whatever else the chapter house was, it was not somewhere she went early.

**Asteria Wessex** is a retired Star Captain of the Star Rangers. She withdrew to a quiet planet after her service and now leads its local [Fellowship of Light](/star-rangers/lore/fellowship-of-light/) chapter, where she is generally called **Asteria the Sage** — the honorific is the chapter's, not the Rangers', and she is said to tolerate rather than encourage it. The surname survives chiefly on a service record nobody on the hill has read.

The ordinary route she did not take is worth naming, because it was closed rather than declined. Most Rangers who want to stay useful to a world after active service transfer to the [Liaison Corps](/star-rangers/lore/planetary-liaisons-and-recruiters/) and hold a planetary representative's posting. Its senior post is **Chief Liaison Officer**, and an officer leaving at Star Captain — or at High Captain, the rung above — is over-ranked for a Chief's berth. The Corps does not invent a larger title to accommodate a large career. So the third path, which absorbs a great many Rangers, is simply not available at the top of the ladder, and what a departing flag-tier officer does next is her own affair.

## The Two She Taught

Asteria's mentorships bracket her retirement, one on either side, and neither was ever an assignment.

[Karla Wender](/star-rangers/characters/karla-wender/) came first, while Asteria was still serving. Wender was climbing the certification ladder — a Novice around the turn of the century, a Chief by the early 2820s — and Asteria was a Star Captain in her nineties with no obligation whatever toward a junior pilot on the technical track. The Corps' formal mentoring policy does not begin until Starwarden, and Wender did not reach it until after Asteria had gone. So whatever passed between them was outside every structure the Corps maintains for the purpose, which is the only reason it tells you anything about Asteria.

[Galahad Thorne](/star-rangers/characters/galahad-thorne/) came after, on a frontier posting during his twelve unbroken Survey Corps years, by which time she had withdrawn and had even less reason to bother.

The pattern is the same in both directions and it is not sentimental: she taught the ones who arrived, held no post that required it, and kept no record of having done it. Wender now holds the assigned version of the same duty and had been discharging it unassigned for years before anyone wrote it down.

Asteria's answer was an institution the Rangers do not run. That is not incidental. The [Fellowship](/star-rangers/lore/fellowship-of-light/)'s whole standing rests on having renounced the interests everyone else keeps, which is the one qualification a Star Captain's service record cannot supply and cannot compromise either. A chapter house asks nothing about what she commanded.

A career spent commanding a Ranger vessel through boundary zones leaves a person with firsthand working knowledge of things most people only meet as doctrine, which is why visitors still find their way to her with questions about the [Cosmic Cascade](/star-rangers/lore/cosmic-cascade/). Her most-quoted contribution to Cascade pedagogy is the sigh she gave one such questioner before answering: *"Where do I even begin?"* — a line the Survey Archive has since adopted, with her implied permission, as the honest opening of its own primer on the subject.
