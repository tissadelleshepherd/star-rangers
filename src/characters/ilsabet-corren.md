The Institute employs a great many people who point instruments at boundary phenomena and decline to say what the readings mean. Ilsabet Corren points the same discipline at something nobody else at the Institute thought needed instrumentation: the records institutions keep about themselves.

Her fellowship — Comparative Records — is small enough that she is most of it, and its founding observation fits in a sentence: an institution's register is the output of a slow instrument, and settled space has been running some of those instruments, uncalibrated and unread, for centuries. A certification body's revision history, a returning officer's published counts, a compact's [enrolled amendments](/star-rangers/glossary/enrolled-text/) — Corren reads them the way a survey analyst reads a boundary log, asking what was measured, what drifted, and what the instrument cannot see about itself. It is Institute method to the letter, applied one shelf over from where the Institute usually stands, and it has made her mildly famous in two professions that rarely cite each other.

## The Verifier's Apprenticeship

Corren's method has an origin she tells against herself, which is how you know it is load-bearing. Before the Institute, she exercised a right almost nobody uses: [Schedule C's verification clause](/star-rangers/codex/the-seat-allocation-protocol/), under which any elector may re-perform a habitat's seat count from the published record and must arrive at the same seats. She re-performed one. She arrived somewhere else. For one long evening she held, checked, and privately rehearsed announcing the second possibility — the one the electoral regulations treat as the emergency it is — before finding the error, on the third pass, in her own arithmetic.

She describes that evening as the best training the Institute never gave her. The count had been right all along; what the exercise measured was her. Everything characteristic about her later work is downstream of it: the refusal to assert a finding before the derivation survives her own attempts on it, the insistence that a chart carry its limits as a caption rather than an appendix, and the habit — Institute house rule, but she holds it like a private vow — of publishing what she failed to find at the same length as what she found.

## The Atlas

Her visiting paper at the Survey Archive, [The Amendment Atlas](/star-rangers/codex/the-amendment-atlas/), proposes treating the Concord compact's three centuries of enrolled amendments as survey data — four charts of how the law moved, beginning with the boundary of the person. The idea itself, she is the first to note, is raised at the Archive roughly once a generation and dies each time of scope; her contribution was to file it with one. The paper's other signature is its worked figure's caption, which has since travelled further than the paper: **the record's line, not the people's**. An atlas of amendments charts the law's opinion moving, and any sheet that lets a reader mistake the motion of the opinion for the motion of its subjects has failed as an instrument, whatever it achieves as a picture. The Archive's Senior Archivist made that sentence a standing condition of assistance; Corren had already made it a rule, and the two of them have politely declined to settle who is enforcing it on whom.

The Atlas remains what the record says it is: proposed, not built. Corren regards this as the correct current state of a proposal under comment, and answers questions about timescale by pointing out that her dataset has waited three centuries and is not visibly impatient.

## Character Notes

Corren is precise company rather than cold company, and the distinction matters to her. She will not tell you what a finding means — she is the Institute's, and takes no position — but she will tell you exactly what it cost to establish, who could re-perform it, and which part she is least sure of, unprompted, because a stated limit is in her view a form of courtesy. Colleagues at Threshold Station note that she treats the Archive's reading desks with the specific respect of someone who has been wrong in public arithmetic once and intends the count to stay at one.

## In the Record

Corren's signed work is the [Amendment Atlas proposal](/star-rangers/codex/the-amendment-atlas/), filed to the Survey Archive's visiting papers with [Sen's](/star-rangers/characters/sen/) shelf comment beside it — favourable, conditions standing. Her fellowship's working notes cite the Archive's [Verification Doctrine](/star-rangers/codex/the-verification-doctrine/) and Sen's [Three Disciplines of the Record](/star-rangers/codex/three-disciplines-of-the-record/) as the nearest prior statements of her own method — a debt she records plainly, in keeping with the discipline all three documents describe.
