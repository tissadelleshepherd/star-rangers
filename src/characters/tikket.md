Tikket is the youngest of four [Chthonari](/star-rangers/lore/chthonari/) on a Federation contractor crew cutting new members under [Eden Space Habitat](/star-rangers/lore/eden-space-habitat/)'s dock ring in the spring of 2828 UCSD — the understructure the fold-approach monitoring's mounts bolt through — and the first of the crew to hear one of those mounts ring under load where the other five did not. The name is a struck signature, two quick strikes and a close, spelled the way a human ear renders it.

## The Crew

Four on one deck, on their first posting off the [Undersong Belt](/star-rangers/lore/undersong-belt/): [Kattik](/star-rangers/characters/kattik/), who keeps the log; [Tekka](/star-rangers/characters/tekka/), who agrees with Kattik about most things and disagrees about all of them; [Tsikk](/star-rangers/characters/tsikk/), who asks the habitat; and Tikket. The cutting was good — members cut to what the ring would still be doing when the cutting stopped — and the habitat's monitoring read the run and called it normal, and the certification went up, and the crew stayed on for the snagging list, which is the part of a job where one stands on what one has left and listens to it. A sealed module clamped to the understructure is the burrow; the deck plating between the four of them is the Undersong, as much of it as Eden carries, and it does not travel through air.

## What Tikket Cannot Do

Hear a voice, or read a face. Nothing in the airborne layer reaches a Chthonari, so Tikket cannot be charmed, and cannot tell a human who is joking from one who is frightened. Hear anything at all when not in contact with structure. See a thing as raw — a stock plate is already good for something, and so is a person. Explain the Undersong to anyone who does not stand on it; the crew can be taught that it exists, and that is the ceiling.

## Character Notes

Tikket has learned the course and has begun, somewhere in the last year, to listen to the tunnel — the distinction Chthonari spectators watch for in the Long Skitter, and the one that separates an apprentice from an engineer. On Eden it showed as a step that was a finger's width lower than the last: the third mount rang a little, and Tikket put it with the course, because Eden is a tunnel nobody has tuned and everything in it rings a little. By the second day the other five mounts had settled into what they were and the third had not. Kattik said it was the course. Tikket did not argue, which in a crew of four on one deck is not nothing.

What Tikket found, standing on the mount with the ring quiet at the end of a shift, was not a fault. The mount was ringing *right*, for a mount carrying more than the certification said it carried — a structure doing its job under a load nobody had written down, telling the truth to the one person in the room who could not repeat it. That is the crew's whole posting in one member: the humans are kind, the module is sealed, and everybody who can decide anything lives in the air.

Kattik told Tikket once, plainly, and will not again: *You were not wrong about the mount. You were wrong about what it meant. Those are different, and the second is the one that gets better.*
