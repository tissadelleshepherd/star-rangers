Peran Slade arrived at the chapter house on the green hill at twenty, on foot, with a letter of introduction nobody asked to see. They have been a novice there for two years, which the house does not consider long, and which Peran considers an eternity, and both of these judgements are recorded here because both are correct.

What brought them is not in question. Peran notices. A room's temperature before anyone names it, the hour a place goes quiet, the small wrongness in an evening that resolves, three days later, into a neighbour's bad news — the unmeasured noticing the [Fellowship of Light](/star-rangers/lore/fellowship-of-light/)'s contemplative houses have trained for centuries, arriving in this case untrained and unasked. The house tested it the way such houses do, which is to say it gave Peran the lamps to trim and waited a season, and by spring the question was settled: the vocation is real. What is not settled — what Peran came hoping to have settled *for* them — is its shape.

## The Seeking

The Fellowship recognizes [two expressions of the same vocation](/star-rangers/lore/fellowship-of-light/): the contemplative chapters, which stay and watch, and the operational chapter, which goes — and which the rest of the record calls the [Star Rangers](/star-rangers/lore/formation-of-star-rangers/). A novice does not choose between two callings. A novice learns which shape the one calling already has, and the house's entire method for teaching this is to decline to hurry it.

Peran has asked directly. The house does not do direct answers to that question — not from policy but from honesty, because [the record itself shows](/star-rangers/codex/called-permitted-forbidden/) that a calling invites, costs, and never compels, and no one at the house is willing to compel what the Cascade itself will not. Peran finds this comfortless. The house is aware Peran finds it comfortless. The lamps still want trimming.

What tips no scale either way, and is therefore the detail Peran turns over most: the Rangers' own certification ladder begins at a rank called **Novice**. The word is the same on both sides of the door. Peran wrote this down in the house's margin-book the second winter — *the door has the same word on both sides* — and was mildly outraged to find, some weeks later, that someone had marked it with the small sign the house uses for entries worth keeping.

## Character Notes

Peran is not torn between faith and doubt; nothing about the Fellowship is in doubt for them. They are torn between staying and going, which the house regards as a much older and much more respectable problem.

They keep the house's smallest custom without being asked — the last lamp is trimmed after the others are lit, not before — and they keep it on the nights they are angriest with the house, which the chapter leader has noticed and not remarked on.

The chapter leader is [Asteria Wessex](/star-rangers/characters/asteria-the-sage/), who spent a hundred years in the expression Peran cannot decide about and retired into the one Peran cannot decide about either. Peran asked her once, plainly, which one they were. She looked at them for what witnesses agree was a long time, and said that the ones who stay and the ones who go are watching the same thing, and the only mistake on offer was to watch the door instead.
