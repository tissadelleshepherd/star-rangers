Wendell Albercombe reports to Superintendent [Rasa Oyelaran](/star-rangers/characters/rasa-oyelaran/) at [Eden Space Habitat](/star-rangers/lore/eden-space-habitat/) and carries the bureau's ordinary caseload: theft, contract disputes, and the occasional [Orbital Habitats Compact](/star-rangers/lore/orbital-habitats-compact/) compliance-framework referral that turns out to be exactly as boring as it reads. On paper, this makes him the least remarkable name on Oyelaran's roster. On paper is doing a great deal of work in that sentence.

His closure rate on the "boring" referrals is, by any honest audit, better than the bureau's own investigative intelligences manage on comparable cases — [Reeves](/star-rangers/characters/reeves/) included, a fact Reeves itself has noted once, in passing, and not raised again. Albercombe attributes this to "knowing the habitat," in the tone of a man who would rather refile a compliance-framework referral by hand than credit [Jeeves](/star-rangers/characters/jeeves/), the welfare-program household intelligence Eden assigned him three years ago and has since stopped trying to have removed.

## Character Notes

Albercombe complains about his caseload constantly, at volume, usually over dinner — which is, not coincidentally, where most of it gets solved. He has never once connected the two facts aloud.

He is fond of Jeeves in the specific, undeclared way of a man who signs every closed case under his own name and has never once been asked why the closure rate improved the year the household intelligence moved in.

## Kate

He and [Kate Ukrainka](/star-rangers/characters/kate-ukrainka/) were together for a few months, some years ago now, and it did not end so much as thin out. Their interests diverged — hers ran to what it is like to be someone, his to what can be shown and closed — and neither of them was wrong about the other, which is the part he has never found a way to be aggrieved about.

What actually happened is simpler and less flattering than "diverged," and he knows it. She kept writing to him. He answered later, and then later than that, and then to about one message in three, and at no point did he decide anything. There was always a caseload. There was always a reason the reply could wait until after, and eventually the afters ran out and she stopped sending them, which he noticed, and about which he did nothing.

He feels slightly guilty. Not much, and not often — a small snag rather than a wound, the kind that catches when her name comes up in a habitat bulletin or one of her books turns up in an evidence box belonging to somebody's child. He has no wish whatever to pick it up again; that is not what the guilt is about. It is about being a man who is genuinely good at noticing what a silence means in a witness statement, and who let one run for the better part of a year in his own messages without once treating it as evidence of anything.

He remembers the name of one of her cats and could not, asked cold, produce the other's — which is exactly the sort of asymmetry he would flag in somebody else's statement inside four seconds, and has never once turned on the statement he would give about himself.

Her regard for him, at the range the habitat enforces — a deck bulletin, an inquest list, the accounts of people who know them both, Eden being Eden — is by everything he can observe entirely warm: unhurried, faintly amused, without a grievance in it anywhere that anyone has managed to find. He does not fully trust it, and because he is who he is, he has worked the distrust the way he would work a statement, and filed the result with everything else he cannot close.

Part of it is arithmetic. He knows what that warmth would have to be built over, because he was there when the foundation went in — the replies that came later and later, the silence he let run, the stop he never asked about. Regard he had earned would not puzzle him. Regard in excess of the record is exactly the class of entry his trade exists to query, and kindness aimed at the man who did what he did has, whether she intends it or not, the shape of gentle irony: praise that outruns the evidence always reads as commentary on the evidence. He knows the feeling from interview rooms — it is what being handled feels like from the inside. It is also, and this is the part he cannot resolve, what being forgiven feels like, and at a distance the two are indistinguishable.

Part of it is her trade. Warmth is what she makes. Every animal in her books is adored by strangers who will never meet it; she can build affection that works, and a thing she can build is a thing he cannot verify by inspecting the finish. He would be the first to say the craft is not evidence of anything — that is precisely the trouble. It removes the surface as a place where evidence could live.

And the part he likes least he has assembled entirely secondhand, having never opened one of her books: that what the books keep being about, on the testimony of everyone who quotes them at him, is the exact moment a person begins asking for less than they need in order to keep something easy for somebody else. He can date that moment in her messages to him without consulting anything. He supplied the somebody else. Warmth from the author of that shelf may be kindness, and it may be a specimen jar, and being read is not the same as being cared for — and asking her which it is would tell her more than any answer could tell him.

None of this is a finding against her, and he knows that too. Affection is proven by what it spends, and nothing in their present acquaintance costs her anything — so the question cannot close, and it sits where every unclosable thing in his files sits: open, dated, and never quite worked on.

She writes her mornings in the Deck 7 galley. His caseload takes him through Deck 7 perhaps twice a month. In several years this has not produced a conversation, and neither of them has arranged for it to.

## Orbital Five-O

Albercombe has no standing with [Commander Kai Larsen](/star-rangers/characters/kai-larsen/)'s task force and no particular interest in acquiring any — Five-O's jurisdiction runs to matters the Compact's own habitats can't close alone, and Albercombe's caseload is, almost by definition, everything Five-O doesn't need. On the rare referral that turns out to be bigger than it read on the intake form, he's the desk Five-O's liaison calls first, purely because he's the one who already knows which deck it happened on.
