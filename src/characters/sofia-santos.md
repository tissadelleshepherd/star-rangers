Sofia Santos holds more jurisdiction than anyone else on Thalassa and cannot schedule a dock.

Everyone calls her President of Thalassa. The instrument that created the office is explicit that Thalassa has no planetary government and that this is not one, and it says so in the first clause rather than the last, because saying so was the only condition under which either city would sign. Both cities have used the short form from the week she was elected. Both cities also know exactly what the first clause says, which is most of what anyone needs to understand about the arrangement.

## An Office With Almost Nothing In It

[New Venice and New Atlantis](/star-rangers/lore/planets/thalassa/) run independent municipal councils and have never agreed about how to be a floating settlement, which was survivable for as long as everything under dispute was above the waterline. Below it there is one ocean. Seabed tenure, the tether fields the [mooring towers](/star-rangers/lore/planets/saltmere/) stand in, current loading across shoals both cities moor to, and the thermal and biological state of a single global water column are not two cities' business conducted twice. They are one object, and no municipal council has ever been able to hold half of it.

So in 2822 UCSD the two councils ceded, jointly, exactly the powers neither could exercise alone, and nothing else whatever. The office has no legislature, no revenue of its own, no enforcement, and no authority over any person. Dock-priority scheduling — the standing rivalry that [Solar System Defence Command](/star-rangers/lore/military-space-command/) liaison staff have long described as the most reliable source of paperwork on their desk — remains precisely where it was.

The design was not generous. Each council expected the other to try to capture the new office and built it too weak to be worth capturing. What neither had planned for was a first holder who would not test the limit once.

## Known History

- Born 2781 UCSD in New Atlantis, into the second-wave engineering families who built low, wide and structurally honest and made a civic motto of it — *built to bend, not to boast*.
- Qualified in mooring-tether load analysis and spent fourteen years on tether-field survey across both cities' anchorages, which is why the seabed instrument that eventually created her office is written in vocabulary she had been using professionally for a decade before anyone proposed it.
- Elected to the New Atlantis municipal council in 2812 UCSD; served on the joint arbitration that followed the two cities' competing claims over the northern shoal anchorages, and wrote the minority annex arguing that the claims were not competing but unanswerable, because the thing being claimed was indivisible and no council seated on either platform had standing over it.
- Elected President in 2822 UCSD in the first planet-wide vote Thalassa has ever held, carrying New Atlantis heavily and New Venice narrowly — an outcome New Venice's heritage families have described, without much warmth and without disputing it, as the correct result.

## Character Notes

What people who have negotiated against her mention is the smile, which arrives on a schedule they cannot predict and she can. It lands where an opponent has just committed to a position, and it means she has finished working out what the position will cost them. New Venice reads it as courtesy and New Atlantis reads it as arithmetic. Both readings are correct and she has never been troubled by the disagreement.

She is photographed constantly on both cities' civic feeds and the images are no use to anyone trying to read her: the green eyes and the same unhurried attention are in the frame where she has won and the frame where she has lost. What can be read is the record, and the record shows an officer of an office with nothing to trade. She has no appointments to make, no schedule to adjust, and no funds to direct. What she has is a room's attention and the load figures, and she has never yet been the person in the room who understood the figures second best.

Off-world coverage reaches, reliably, for *first woman*. She declines it every time, and not out of modesty — she is entirely willing to be described grandly where the description is true, and does not pretend otherwise. Her stated objection is that the phrase describes a scarcity Thalassa does not have. Both councils have seated women continuously since founding, New Atlantis's first council chair was one, and accepting the framing would mean accepting an account of her planet she knows to be false in order to be flattered by it. What she is first at is the office, which nobody held before because nobody had made one, and she considers that the more interesting fact about her in any case. It means she is the first person on Thalassa who can be wrong for everybody at once.

Her resolve is visible in the record rather than in her manner. Three times she has let a vote go against her rather than trade a seabed clause for a dock clause she had no standing to trade, and on each occasion she has been offered the trade by someone who expected her to want it. She has never withdrawn a finding. She has also never once asked either council for a power the instrument does not give her, through four years in which she could have had several of them for the asking, and this is the part that is not restraint.

Two councils built a weak office to keep each other out of it. The first person to hold it has spent four years demonstrating, in public and in the minutes, that the office does not reach for things — which is the only argument that was ever going to make either city willing to extend it. She is aware of this. It is a strategy rather than a temperament, she has not claimed otherwise when asked, and what she wants at the end of it is on the record too: a petition for full self-governance that Thalassa has never made, and that the arithmetic does not yet support. Nobody on the planet knows the arithmetic better than she does, which is why nobody who has counted against her expects her to stop.
