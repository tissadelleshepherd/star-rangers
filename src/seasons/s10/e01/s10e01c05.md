:::pov tsikk

Tsikk asked the habitat every shift, and the habitat answered every shift, and that was why the last shift was different: the answer changed, and nothing else did.

The asking was not a ceremony. A burrow has a monitor and one addresses it from the deck, as part of the work, the way one addresses a cutter one is about to use; Eden had a mind, two minds the humans said, sharing one structure, and Tsikk had addressed it from the first day the way one addresses a structure that can listen back, which is to say as a completion. The humans went through a desk to ask it anything. Tsikk had never understood what the desk was for. One stood on the plating, struck the question into the deck in the flattest register the Undersong carried, and the habitat, which read its own structure the way the crew read it, answered in the humans' way, through the module's panel, with the reading.

*Approach monitoring, normal.* Every shift for six weeks. Tekka had said that settled it, and Kattik had written the line anyway, and Tikket had gone on standing on the third mount at the end of each shift as though standing on it would change what it did, which it would not, and which Tsikk had not said, because an apprentice who has begun to hear is owed silence while the hearing finishes.

The last shift was snagging. There was nothing left to snag. The new members had been cut to what the ring would still be doing when the cutting stopped and were doing exactly that; the crew's tools were in their cases; the lift up to the dock was booked for the end of the shift, and after that a Federation transit and the Belt, where a tunnel was tuned and a question struck into the rock came back as the rock's own. Tsikk struck the question into the plating for the last time out of order rather than hope. One asks the monitor before one leaves.

The panel answered. *Approach monitoring, mount three: re-baselined against independent reference. Countersign: refused, fold-approach watch, Star Rangers. Understructure inspection: scheduled, next quarter.*

Tsikk stood on the plating for the length of a long breath, which is not how a Chthonari measures anything, and had no other measure for it.

Nobody had told the crew. Nobody on Eden could have; the crew had no standing to be told and the humans had no channel to tell it through, and the module was sealed, and the air carried nothing. The habitat had not told the crew either. The habitat had been asked, from the deck, the same question as every shift, and had answered with the record, and the record had changed, because somewhere in the air somebody had asked the structure properly, through something that could hear it, and the structure had answered them the way it had been answering Tikket for six weeks.

*Re-baselined against independent reference.* Tsikk knew what that was. It was the burrow's question, *what is this still doing*, asked by an instrument of an instrument. Somebody had set a second ear on the ring and let the six hear themselves. It did not say who. It did not say what the second ear had found. It said *refused*, which was the humans' word, and *scheduled*, which was the humans' other word, and between those two words a thing the crew had cut six weeks ago was standing in the dark carrying what it carried, now with a record that said so.

That was a completion. Not of the ring. Of the asking.

Tsikk told the crew through the deck, in the register for things that are the case, and felt Kattik stop moving at the far end of the module, and Tikket come up off the third mount, and Tekka, who had said it was settled, say nothing at all for longer than Tekka had ever managed.

:::pov tekka

Tekka had said the reading settled it, and had meant it, and now had to say what settled it, which was harder, because the reading had moved and Tekka had not.

That was the thing about memorising. One memorised the course and the course was true, and then the course changed and one was standing on the old one. Kattik memorised too, and disagreed with Tekka about every part of it, and Kattik had written the line. Tekka had not. Tekka had said: six instruments, reading normal, an apprentice hearing the course; and all of that had been true, and the mount had been carrying more than its record the whole time, by however much the second ear had found, which the panel did not say and which Tekka would never now know. The habitat had not been wrong. The habitat had been reading from a floor somebody had set with the load already in it, and Tekka had called the floor the ground, which is the mistake a cutter makes exactly once in the Belt and is marked down for forever.

There was a dissonance in the module. Not loud. Four on one deck, and one of them had been right about the structure and wrong about what it meant, and one had been wrong about the structure and right about what settled things, and the two readings did not sit in the same plating. In the Belt an argument is a dissonance and a settlement is a chord, and a crew does not leave a tunnel with a dissonance in it. It is not a rule. It is what a crew is.

Kattik opened the log.

Tekka watched the line go in under the old one, in the crew's hand, dated the last day: *Mount three line: read. By whom, not known to this crew. Mount still rings.* Kattik did not write *we were right*, because Tikket had been right and the crew had not, and did not write *the structure is wrong*, because the structure was not; the structure was carrying more than its record, which is a fault in a record, and the crew had cut what it was asked to cut. Kattik wrote what the deck gave and stopped.

"That is ours," Kattik said, through the plating. "It was always ours. Somebody has decided whose the rest is."

"Who?" Tikket asked.

"The ones with the second ear. The ones with the word *refused*." Kattik closed the log. "Not us. We are the ones with the line."

Tekka found, standing on the plating with the dissonance going out of it, that this was the chord. Not that the crew had been vindicated; a crew is not a thing that gets vindicated. That the question had been asked properly, by somebody, through something that could hear, and the structure had answered, and the record now said what the structure was doing, and what anybody did about it was the air's business and had always been. A structure that has been asked and has answered is a completion. The ring was not finished. The asking was.

"It settled it," Tekka said, to Tsikk, who had struck the question for six weeks and been answered *normal* for six weeks and had not stopped. "You were right that it would. I was wrong about when."

The lift was booked for the end of the shift. Tikket went down to the third mount one last time, which nobody remarked on, and stood on it, and came back up, and said through the deck that it rang as it had on the second day. More than it was left to carry. Not wrong. Right, for what it carried.

They took the lift. The ring went on being read, by six instruments and a seventh, from a floor that was now written down, and the crew went back to a place where the ground answers, and left on Eden the one thing that was theirs to leave.
