:::pov galahad

By the time they returned to Threshold Station, the sky over Dock Seven had gone from black to the colour of old steel.
Nobody suggested sleep.

Thorne stood at the operations table while Rook-7 projected three overlaid traces: station chronometer drift, the residue pattern Aldera had captured at the causeway, and the grove signal from the oldest dryadic tree.

They lined up.

Not perfectly. Not point-for-point. But in the way that mattered — phase families and recurrence intervals — they lined up enough to stop pretending these were separate problems.

"Say it plainly," he said, looking at Syra. "What are we looking at?"

She did not hesitate. One structure, she said, seen through two sets of constraints, and she gave him the ratio and the layers and the lock arithmetic in the order a person could carry it, and finished where she had evidently decided in advance to finish.

"Your safety margins become fiction."

That landed harder than anything else in the briefing.
There were ships due through this sector in two days.

Rook-7 put the recommendation set on the table under the traces, three lines, the first of them an advisory: conservative lock margins for all inbound harmonic transits. The sentence was not difficult. Where it went was.

A boundary advisory to Traffic Control was the survey's to draft and the station's to carry. The station administration had put in writing, twice, that the thing the advisory rested on was not reportable, and a third paper from the survey office would get the response the first three had got, in the time the first three had taken, which was not two days. The other route was shorter. A certified boundary analyst's finding went to Traffic Control under the survey's own hand, and the administration could review it afterward, and would. Thorne had been in the Corps twelve years and had watched people take that route and had watched what it cost them, and the cost was always paid later and always by the person who had signed.

He thought about the forty-eight hours. Every recalibration the station had ever run had held for about that long before the forty seconds came back.

"The advisory goes to Traffic Control under the survey," he said. "Now. The administration gets its copy in the same transmission, not after. Over, not round. If they want to review it they can review it with the ships still in the corridor." He looked at the traces again, because saying it had been the easy part. "Rook. How much warning can you actually give them?"

:::pov syra

Humans often asked for explanations as if explanations were comfort.
Sometimes they were. Usually they were responsibility.

Syra drew three circles on the table display and linked them with thin lines.

"A legal harmonic transit needs three things at once," she said. "A corridor topology that exists. A vessel phase signature that matches it. An exit state the local [Concordant](/star-rangers/glossary/concordant/) can accept. Lose any one and you do not have transit; you have failure."

Thorne nodded once. He was tracking.

She highlighted the ratio Aldera had flagged.

"Three to seven is not numerology. It is band coupling. The causeway residue sits in the same harmonic family as your station drift, offset by layer. You have been carrying the one as instrumentation error and the other as folklore. They are the same structure seen through different constraints. The station drift and the grove anchor are in different layers, and they are exchanging coherence pressure."

Elvira, standing near the wall, was listening with the same still concentration she had used at the grove.

"So my failed closures fed the same structure that was distorting the station clocks," Elvira said.

"Yes," Syra said. She did not soften it. "You were working one face of the problem. Blindly, but not incorrectly."

She expanded the model to include projected corridor windows.

"Here is the operational part," she said. "In a stable zone, lock tolerance gives you minutes. Here, with overlap active, you get seconds before ambiguity climbs. That is why drop-out risk spikes at boundary stations."

She looked at Thorne.

"If you file this as a local anomaly, people die later and far away."

She had given him the model and the margin, and she had given him nothing she could not support. The rest was his. A boundary analyst's certification said what the instruments carried. It did not say who had to be told, or past whom, and she had watched him understand that the moment the recommendation set went on the table, and had watched him not look at her while he decided it, which was correct.
