:::pov elvira

Elvira dreamed that she did it, and knew it for the first thing wrong with the dream before she was awake enough to know she had been asleep.

In the dream the chalk took. That was how the village believed it went: the Enchantress draws the line, and the line does what she wants. In the dream it went that way. The circle held on the first setting, which circles did not, the thread ran down into the chalk and the chalk into the ground and the ground answered, and Aldera, who had been sitting outside the ring matching nothing, stood up inside it. Stood. On two legs, at the height of a person, the tabby markings gone to a coat and the paws gone to hands, and the face still a cat's face, level with her own, wearing the expression Aldera kept for people who had made a decision about her without asking.

Elvira had, in the dream, been pleased. That was the second thing wrong with it.

She woke in the cold hour before the marsh light, and did what she did with a failed circle, which was to take it apart in order rather than all at once.

The mechanism first. A working did not take on the first setting. It did not take on the fourth. It produced a measurable result or it did not, and the result was never a shape, because Etheric structure expressed through the substrate and a cat was not substrate. The working in the dream had been the village's working, the one she had spent three years declining to be credited with, and the dream had handed it to her whole and let it run. She noted that she had recognised it as the wrong mechanism even while it was working, and had not stopped.

The shape second. A cat was not a person standing up. Whatever the marsh was loose about, the world was strict about that: she had been told as a girl which shapes it kept and which it did not, by someone who was careful about such lists, and a person's frame on an animal's face was on the second list, near the top. The dream had built it anyway, and built it well. That was the part that would have frightened someone else. It did not frighten her; it interested her, in the exact way a well-made forgery interested her, and she set the interest aside because it was not the point.

The pleasure third, and last, because it was the one that was hers. She had wanted something that could speak to her. She had wanted it enough that a dream had made it out of the only other person on the causeway, and she had stood in a chalk ring and been glad of it.

The outpost was Class II oscillatory and the Interval was narrow here. Dreams at a thin place were on the record; her predecessor's books held eleven, and hers now held one. The doctrine on them was short and she agreed with it: weather, not a message, and never evidence about who sent it, because nothing did. The marsh had supplied the strangeness. She had supplied the wanting.

She wrote it in the record book, under the date, in the column for observations she had not intended to make. *Dreamed a working that took on the first setting and changed the shape of a person to suit me. Mechanism was the village's. Wish was mine. No action.* Then she went out to the stones and reset the circle, which failed, correctly, as it had the night before.

Only one of the two things she had supplied and been supplied with was on the list of what she was here to measure. She measured that one.

**DETECTIVE AGENCY LOG — Aldera, Field Unit**

Subject A rose before light and went to the stones before the fire. She checked the circle she had reset at dusk and found it as she left it, failed, and appeared satisfied that it had failed in the ordinary way. She then looked at me for longer than her habit, filed something in the second book, and did not say what.

Assessment: a dream. The causeway produces them; the outpost file holds eleven and the previous keeper's hand grows worse across them. What is unusual is not the dream. It is that she looked at me as if she owed me something, which she does not, and as if she had decided not to say so, which is correct.

I did not ask. Information asked for tends to be used. Information volunteered tends to be dismissed. Information about a dream, offered by the dreamer to the person dreamed of, tends to be an apology, and I have no use for one.

She will say when she is ready, or she will not. Either is acceptable. The circle failed correctly. No action.
