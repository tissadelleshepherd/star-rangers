:::pov galahad

The dock smelled of ozone and old metal. Galahad had learned this combination years ago: it meant *boundary proximity*, though no one in the Survey Corps used that term. They called it Threshold Stink. He had stopped correcting them.

Dock Seven was the furthest point out. The instruments here never quite agreed.

Two chronometers on the wall showed different readings — barely forty seconds apart, but you noticed forty seconds when you lived here long enough. The survey office had filed three incident reports about it. The station administrators had filed two responses explaining there was nothing to file reports about.

He adjusted his coat and watched the new arrivals come through the pressure seal.

The tall woman came first — pale, unhurried, moving with a quality of attention he had learned to recognise at boundary postings. She was not scanning for threats. She was *reading* the space. Her eyes moved across the dock like a hand moving over a page in the dark, feeling for the letters.

*Krenyi.* Certain. He had worked with three before.

Behind her: a bipedal robot — civilian chassis, older by the joint-casings, but with sensor arrays that were emphatically not standard.

He checked the roster on his handheld. Three new transfers. All assigned to the boundary survey. He was apparently now their team lead.

This was going to be interesting, and not in the way his supervisor had promised.

:::

:::pov syra

The station was exactly as the approach data had described and entirely unlike what the data had implied.

In a century and a half of navigating human-built environments, Syra had learned the difference. Data described surfaces. Implications described pressures. The data said: *mid-grade shore station, boundary-proximate, crew complement forty-seven, mixed species.* The implication said: *everyone here is waiting for something to go wrong, and they have all agreed not to say so.*

She could feel the [boundary](/star-rangers/lore/boundary-zones/) through the station's hull. Not as sensation exactly — more as a faint discontinuity in her own coherence. A slight lag between intention and action. A breath held somewhere behind the physical.

She filed it. Not a threat. Information.

The human in the survey coat was watching her. She had been watched before — it was a standard feature of arriving anywhere — but he was watching in the specific way that meant *I have worked with your kind before and I am deciding whether to update my model.* She found this preferable to the other kinds of watching.

She noted the robot behind her in the reflection of the dock window. Rook-7's sensor arrays had been active since they cleared the pressure seal. Good. That was the correct response.

"You're the team lead," she said to the human. It was not a question.

:::
