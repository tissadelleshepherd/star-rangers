::: pov bubochka

She had kept counting the night pass after the detective's test ended, because a route is a fact about the floor and the floor is her job, and on the first night of the new month the route was wrong.

Not wrong. Longer. The housekeeping unit came through at its third hour the way it always did, slow and square, and then, where it should have turned at the end of the walkway, it went on — along the evidence shelf, which it had never done — and its tray came back with three small bagged things in it that had been on the shelf with the bureau's own tags on them. It stopped at the records-room door and did the thing with its front panel. Bubochka filed the route, and the count, and the time, and sent it up, and by morning Barsik had answered with one line, which was that he had read the reason two days earlier and she should keep counting.

She kept counting. Every night the unit took what was on the shelf. Every day somebody put more on the shelf, because that is what an evidence shelf is for. Nobody on the floor looked at the shelf, because nobody looks at a shelf until they need what is on it. She put that away whole: a thing can be going wrong in plain sight for exactly as long as it takes somebody to want it.

On the twelfth night Barsik came down off the cabinet top, which he does not do at night, and told her that tonight's exercise was to watch.

She watched. The unit came at its hour. Barsik stepped onto it as it passed the cabinet — onto the flat top beside the tray, the way a person steps onto a moving walkway, without hurrying — and rode it down the length of the floor, sitting upright, entirely composed, like an inspector being driven. At the records-room door the unit's service hatch opened at floor height, the door itself locked above it, and the unit went through with the cat on it, and the hatch closed.

She counted. It was longer than a route.

The hatch opened again and the unit came out with the cat still on it and something in the cat's mouth: a small clear bag with a slip clipped to it, a bag of the kind she had seen six of in the spring. He rode as far as the Inspector's desk, stepped off onto the chair and from the chair onto the desk, set the bag down in the exact centre of the blotter, and sat beside it for a moment, adjusting nothing. Then he came back to the cabinet top and did not look at her.

She asked, in the morning, why he had waited twelve nights when he had known on the first.

He said: *A report delivered before the question exists is volunteered. A report delivered after the answer is gone is nothing. There is one day in between. That was it.*

She wrote that down exactly, and then, because it was the first time she had ever seen him carry anything, she wrote down that too.

::::: scene 2

::: pov albercombe

There was a bag of bolts on his desk.

Wendell Albercombe looked at it for a while before touching it, which is the habit of a man who has been handed enough things by this habitat to have learned not to. It was his bag of bolts. The tag was the bureau's: the Kessler fastener dispute, two dock contractors each swearing the other's lot was the substandard one, a case so boring it had been on his desk for six weeks and was due at a hearing in four days. He had last seen the bag on the evidence shelf, where evidence goes. It was now on his blotter, and clipped to it was a form he had never seen before in his life.

*Unattended Article Report.* Location: bureau floor, shelf E3. Time: 0304. Collected by: housekeeping unit four. Disposition: lost property, records room, fourteen-day hold.

He read it twice. Then he went to the evidence shelf and stood in front of it.

The shelf was clean. Not tidied; clean, in the way of a thing that has been emptied on a schedule. Six weeks of a boring man's boring cases — samples, seals, a contested spoon, the bolts' companion bag with the other contractor's lot in it — gone, and in their places nothing at all, which is the one thing an evidence shelf must never hold.

"Who," he asked the floor, "has been tidying my shelf."

The floor did not know. The junior detectives had not touched it. The Senior Sergeant had assumed the Inspector was reorganising. The Superintendent, passing, looked at the empty shelf and then at him and said nothing in a way he had known her long enough to translate, which was *that is your shelf, Inspector, and this is your morning*. Reeves, asked directly whether it had moved anything, said directly that it had not, and that it held an inference on the question and would state it if asked. He did not ask. He was not, at that moment, in a condition to be told anything by anyone politely.

He went to the records room instead, with the form, and found the lost-property bin, and found in it six weeks of his caseload in clear bags with a slip on every one, filed as litter. Every slip said the same thing in different hours: shelf E3, housekeeping unit four, fourteen-day hold. The oldest was dated twelve days ago.

He took the bolts' companion bag and the spoon and left the rest, because an Inspector who empties a lost-property bin into his coat is a different sort of referral, and he went back to his desk and sat in front of the question that the form had not answered. He knew who had tidied his shelf. It was unit four. What he did not know was why a machine that had walked the same square for three years had, twelve nights ago, decided that his evidence was rubbish.

He did not ask Reeves. He did not want the answer in the bureau's voice, with an inference attached and the Superintendent's translation already waiting. He wanted to complain about it first, properly, at volume, to somebody who would not write it down.

::: pov albercombe

"It's not a case," he said, over the soup. "I want that understood. It's a machine and a shelf."

"Yes, sir."

"Three years it's gone round that floor. Three years. Same square, same hour, never once so much as brushed the shelf. And twelve nights ago it decides my evidence is litter and starts filing it. *Filing* it. There's a form. They've a form for tidying my bolts away and not one for telling me they've done it." He put the spoon down. It was the contested spoon; he had brought it home by mistake and was now eating with it, which Jeeves had noticed and declined to regard as a subject. "And in two days the bin releases it to reclamation. Six weeks of cases. To be melted into somebody's bulkhead."

"Two days, sir. The hold is fourteen; the oldest slip is dated the third."

Albercombe looked at him. "You've read the slip."

"I read the form when it circulated, sir. The Compact's compliance office issues a circular with each new requirement. One likes to know what the household's furniture is now obliged to do."

"What new requirement."

"The thirteenth, sir. Effective the third of the month. It extends the loose-article control to *every deck area not within a registered berth or sealed store*, citing the fold-proximate monitoring baseline." A pause of exactly the right length. "The bureau's evidence shelf is an open shelf on a common floor, sir. It has been, I gather, since the bureau was built. Under the twelve it was nobody's business. Under the thirteenth it is unit four's."

Albercombe sat with that.

"A spoon," he said eventually. "What does a spoon do to a fold."

"The circular does not say, sir. I have found that the circulars seldom do."

He picked the spoon back up, looked at it, and put it in his pocket instead of the soup. "Fine. Fine. The shelf gets a door. It gets registered as a sealed store, tonight, on a route reconciliation, which is a maintenance discrepancy and goes in the queue that comes to me, and I approve it, and unit four goes back to its square." He was already standing. "And I want to know who put that bag on my desk."

"I couldn't say, sir."

"You've a theory."

"I have an observation, sir, which is that in three years the bureau's cat has never once been seen to carry anything, and that this remains the case." Jeeves began clearing. "Your coat is by the door. The records room is unlocked until midnight."

::::: scene 4

::: pov barsik

The route reconciliation entered the compliance queue at 2251, category maintenance discrepancy, priority none, and Barsik read it from the cabinet top before the Inspector had finished walking away from the terminal. Shelf E3 to be enclosed and registered as a sealed store. Housekeeping route four to be reconciled to the register as amended. Approved, Albercombe, same hour, because it was his own queue and he was the man at the bottom of it. Unit four's square was a square again by morning. Nothing was released to reclamation. The hearing had its bolts.

He filed the outcome and then sat with the part of it that was his.

The doctrine is two words, and for twelve nights they had held. He had read the thirteenth requirement the day it circulated — he reads the circulars; someone should — and had known by the second paragraph what unit four would do with an open shelf. He had said nothing, because there was nobody to say it to who had asked, and a report delivered to a bureau that has not yet noticed its own shelf is a cat volunteering an opinion about compliance policy, which is the end of a posting. So he had watched the shelf empty, and watched nobody look at it, and counted with the trainee, and waited for the day the bureau would want what it had lost.

The bureau did not want it in time. That is the ordinary case. Most things go to reclamation with the question still unasked, and the doctrine would have let this one go too, and the doctrine would have been wrong. He wrote that down plainly, because it was the finding: *filed, not volunteered* is a rule about the order of things, a question before an answer, and it says nothing about a clock. When the answer has a day left and the question has not arrived, the order can be kept and the clock still lost. On that one day the doctrine yields, not to volunteering — he had volunteered nothing; he had put the bureau's own form where the bureau's own Inspector would find it, which is filing by other means — but to time.

The cost was a cat seen carrying. He had accepted it, at night, on an empty floor, with one witness whose watching was the exercise. In the morning the Inspector had asked the floor who put a bag on his desk, and the floor had not known, and he would, Barsik judged, let that stand, because a man who wants his evidence back does not much care who returned it. A household intelligence with no bureau standing and an excellent memory had, he did not doubt, declined to say. So: two on the habitat who had not finished a question about him, one who had asked and would let it go, and one who had chosen not to start. He recorded the count and did not adjust the doctrine for it. A cover that nobody wonders about is a cover nobody is looking at; a cover that competent people decline to examine is the working kind.

The trainee's log, last.

*Exercise five: to watch. She counted the route for a month after anyone asked her to, which is how the agency knew on the first night what the bureau learned on the twelfth. She has asked the right question about the waiting and written the answer down exactly. Note for doctrine, from her file and not mine: the day in between is found by counting, not by waiting for it.*

He considered the last line for some time, decided it was hers, and let it stand under her name.
