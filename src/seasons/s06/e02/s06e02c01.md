::: pov wender

The submission arrived on a Tuesday, which Wender noted and then declined to find meaningful.

It came through the Council's liaison officer, who had been on the station eleven days and had the look of a man who had stopped expecting to sleep in his own bed before the inquiry closed. He put it on her desk the way people put down a thing they have been asked to carry and would rather not have been. He was not unkind about it. He was tired in the specific way of somebody administratively capable, still making correct decisions, and running out of whatever correct decisions cost, and Wender had commanded long enough to know that this was not a man to be argued with but a man to be given, if it was at all possible, a Tuesday.

It was not going to be possible.

The submission was lodged with the inquiry by counsel for a body of inner-system licensed fold operators — the same body that had spent the seasons before Dock Seven saying in public, and in some detail, that Ranger neutrality was an expensive habit and the Corps' boundary expertise was a story it told about itself. Now it said two things, and said them well.

The first was the survey. A Line Captain on fleet-inspection review had flagged a Union land-survey entry for fleet-priority survey, citing her grounds in four clean lines. The coordinate flagged lay, by the Union's own public register, on land held under the name Shepherd, in the Boirinn Uplands of Tír na nÓg, for six generations. The Line Captain had disclosed no interest. The sweep her flag produced had found, on its third day, a licensed operation belonging to a concern that had declined Safety Corps certification and could not be compelled to hold it, and by the end of that day the concern no longer existed. Counsel did not use the word *pretext*. Counsel wrote that the word was for the inquiry to choose, and then built the paragraphs so that only one word fitted.

The second was the hull. The same officer had convened a Field Triumvirate on her own motion, sat on it as ranking officer, carried it without dissent, and taken a Ranger vessel inside twenty kilometres of a held aperture, after which the aperture had failed catastrophically and every hull on the Combine's registry within nine hundred thousand kilometres had been lost. Counsel quoted a recruiting page. *Practical boundary knowledge.* Counsel let the phrase sit next to the figure and did not comment on it.

Wender read it twice, once for content and once for what the content was for.

Everything in it was from the record. That was the craft of it. The Triumvirate log had been built so that anyone who wanted to pull it down would have to say which specific piece they were pulling, and counsel had said which piece — not the conditions, each of which had been made to stand on its own, but the grounds. The submission cited the log for every fact it needed and disputed none of them. It disputed what the facts were for. It quoted every line of the flag except the fourth.

The liaison wanted a response by the end of the week. The Council would answer the second claim itself, he said, and would prefer that the Corps answer the first.

The second she could have answered in her sleep. She had read that log twice fifty-three days ago looking for the place where it stopped being right and had not found one, and she could put that on a page in an hour.

The first she could not answer at all. Not because it was true. Because she did not know whether it was.

She had watched Shepherd read item sixteen twice, and she had decided not to ask. It had been doctrine, that decision, and she had built the doctrine on purpose over four seasons so that good officers would keep bringing her the truths that were hard to explain, and it had never once cost her anything she had to pay in public. The only honest sentence she owned was *I did not ask her, and I will not now say what she would have said* — and in the grammar of a submission that sentence was a confirmation. The other sentence, *there was no interest*, was a claim past her evidence, which was the one thing the officer it would have defended had never in her life put on a record.

"The Corps does not answer the first claim," she said.

"That will read as —"

"I know how it will read."

She was aware that a High Captain who left a claim of that shape standing on an inquiry file, unanswered, beside the name of an officer who was not present to mind, was writing a line in her own record as well, and that the Council had ways of remembering. She weighed it honestly, because pretending a cost was zero was how you paid it by surprise. Fifty-three days ago she had declined to sign a citation because every sentence in it was true and the sum was a lie. She was declining now for the same reason from the other side: she could make every sentence of an answer true, and its sum would be a claim she could not support.

She set the stylus down.

*State your grounds and say nothing further.* It had been Shepherd's policy, drawn in the same place twice in ten days until it stopped looking like an exception, and Wender had let her keep it. She was finding out what the policy cost when the woman who held it was not there to hold it.

::: pov sen

Inquiry documents were copied to the Survey Archive for filing, which was how the submission reached the reconciling desk, and the desk did what it did with everything: it checked.

Cael went first, because Cael always went first. *Union land register, Boirinn Uplands, entry current. Holding recorded under the name Shepherd for six generations. Item sixteen, general flora anomaly, older than her enlistment, never flagged for follow-up until the Line Captain flagged it. The flag: four lines, grounds cited as phase-lag consistency with item fourteen. Every citation in the submission checks.* A pause, of the kind Sen had learned to wait through. *It is tidy.*

Tidy was the word Cael used for the things it trusted least. A record that closed cleanly was what a closed file looked like from outside, and Cael had reopened enough of them to know the shape. But there was nothing here to reopen. Nothing in the submission was false. It was, Cael conceded, with the reluctance of somebody handing over a finding they had hoped to keep, entirely accurate.

*Accurate,* Wyn said, *and who is it for?*

That was Wyn's question and Wyn asked it of everything the desk touched — who was protected by this closure, and who paid. Sen let the question run, because it was the desk's other half and had been for eleven years.

*A family kept a rule for six generations about not being looked at,* Wyn said. *The Union keeps a version of the same rule itself — a Highland Guard detail on any Ranger on leave, from the moment she is met, whose planet it still is. The family kept theirs through the landings and the charter and a daughter going to Eden in a uniform. It is now in an inquiry file, under a heading, with a register reference. That is who paid. And the person whose name is on the heading is not here to be asked whether she minds.*

*She would not have answered it either,* Sen said.

*That is not the same as it not costing her.*

*No,* Sen said. *It isn't.*

Sen fronted, and held the desk, and decided what the desk would do, which was the part the other two left to Sen because somebody had to sign the timesheet.

The desk's work was reconciliation. It read what a station had filed against what the station's own instruments had logged, and where the two disagreed it added a note, unsigned, at the bottom, naming the gap and — Wyn's contribution — the cost of the silence that had let the gap stand. That was the whole of the job and Sen had never once been asked to do more of it. Eleven years of notes, and not one of them had ever argued. They put two things next to each other and let the adjacency do it.

Here nothing disagreed. The instruments said what the log said. The log said what the flag said. The submission did not sit in a gap between the record and the truth; it sat on top of the record and read it in a particular direction. A reading was not a gap. The desk did not annotate readings. If it began to, it would be an editor, and an editor was the one thing an archive could not afford to have at the reconciling desk, because the next reading it declined to annotate would be an opinion too.

So Sen filed the submission in the Dock Seven reconciliation binders, in the place where it belonged, which was adjacent to the Triumvirate log — the whole log, the fourth line of the flag included — and closed the binder, and added nothing.

*You could,* Cael said. *There is a gap. The submission quotes three lines of a four-line flag. That is a discrepancy between a document and its source, and that is exactly what a note is for.*

*It is,* Sen said. *And the fourth line is on the next page, in her own hand, and anyone who turns the page will find it. A note that says* see the next page *is not a note. It is an argument wearing one.*

It was the first time in eleven years the desk had declined to add a note where it could have, and all three of them knew it, and none of them said so. Sen wrote the date in the binder's index and nothing beside it.

::: pov rook

**Field Analyst Rook-7. Log open. Two documents received from the inquiry file, both public, both concerning the Line Captain, neither addressed to this desk. Logging them anyway. Nobody else on the station is going to.**

The first was the Oversight Council's statement in answer to the competence claim, and Rook-7 read it once and filed its assessment at the top, because the assessment was short.

*Ranger personnel survived Dock Seven because of superior protocols and practical boundary knowledge.* The Council's press office had issued it; the Corps had not; the Corps had not corrected it. Rook-7 noted that this was the second impression concerning Dock Seven that the station had now declined to correct, the first being the one nine witnesses on the outer gallery had given it at 0549, and that the two impressions were incompatible with each other and each was compatible with the outcome, which was the ordinary condition of an outcome nobody had instrumented properly.

**Assessment: the statement is consistent with the outcome and unsupported by the instrument record. The record shows a perimeter drawn around a process. It shows Ranger hulls not included in the process. It does not show Ranger hulls protected by a protocol, because no protocol was running; the Line Captain's protocol was to hold the aperture's edge, and the aperture did not survive to be held. Rook-7 filed this on the day, at length, flagged as interpretation. It has been read zero times. Refiling.**

The second document took longer, and Rook-7 did not begrudge it the time, because the second document had a shape and shapes were what its arrays were for.

Counsel's submission cited the Triumvirate log thirty-one times. It cited the survey flag once. It quoted the flag's first three lines in full and the fourth not at all, and the fourth was the grounds, and the grounds were the only line in the flag that answered the claim the submission made. Rook-7 examined that asymmetry for some time. A claim that drew every fact it relied on from the record it disputed, and left out the one line of that record which disposed of it, was not a claim in the sense Rook-7's logs used the word. It was a pattern being sustained from outside — every reading supplied by somebody else, and one reading withheld, so that the pattern could hold a shape it could not hold on its own.

Rook-7 did not call it a lie. A lie was a claim about intent, and it had no instrument for intent. It logged the shape.

It also logged a second observation and, this time, sent it, because it had resolved the status of the thing its logs sometimes did and the resolution was that it did not matter what the thing was called.

**Observation. The Line Captain is not present to answer the submission. The Line Captain, if present, would not have answered it: she stated her grounds and said nothing further, on this station, twice in ten days, as a policy. From outside the two facts look alike. They are not alike. An officer who declines to answer has chosen the silence and can be asked why. An officer who is absent has had the silence chosen for her, and it will stand beside her name in a public file for as long as it takes, and she is the one person on this station who will not be consulted about the cost. The difference is going to be lost on every person who reads this log.**

**Logging that too.**

The High Captain's refusal to answer went into the file at 1612 station. Rook-7 read it. It was one sentence. It was the sentence the Line Captain would have written, in the Line Captain's hand, and Rook-7 filed the resemblance and did not know what to do with it, and left it there.

**Note. The Line Captain taught this station that a thing can be filed with its grounds stated and nothing further, and that the filing will stand. The submission has now shown the other half: that a thing filed that way can be accused of anything, and answered by nothing, and that both halves are the same discipline seen from two sides. Rook-7 assesses, at low confidence, that she knew. It has no instrument for that either.**

**Recommend nothing. The claim is on the record. So is the log. So is the fourth line. A pattern sustained from outside stops when the sustaining stops, and nothing in this file is sustaining it but the people who wrote it. Rook-7 has logged eleven of those. It knows the shape.**

**Log held open.**
