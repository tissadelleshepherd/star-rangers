:::pov tissadelle

The order returning her to general operations had come through nine days before the rotation closed, and the backlog queue had not noticed that either.

File five hundred and ninety. A survey pass over a gas giant's outer moon, two decades old, logged, filed, nothing in it that needed an ear. She cleared it and the next one and the one after that, because the Principal taking the queue from her would arrive to find it exactly as long as the day she had found it herself, and the only thing Shepherd could do about that in nine days was make the number smaller. Threshold was in the order by name. General operations, the station's boundary watch, the work she had been certified for before the Survey track had taken her sideways to a relay station at the edge of someone else's jurisdiction. She had not asked for either posting.

The request came on the liaison terminal, under the seal she had learned to read in the winter, and it was not from Sohrel's office. It said so, in the Hegemony's way of saying things, by naming the office it was from. *The Prime Ganglion's office requests the Ranger officer's assessment, for the Sentinel file, whether the Sentinel's pulse and Meridian claw-percussion are the work of one author. Assessment to be entered on the Hegemony record under the officer's name.*

She read it twice.

In the winter a review chamber had told her what she was entitled to file, and she had filed it: structure, not origin. In the spring the Long Shelf had asked for her method and not her conclusions, and she had sent the method, four paragraphs, and nothing else, and been thanked for the nothing else in a sentence somebody on the far side had asked to have included. This was the third time the Sentinel had asked her for something, and it was the first time anyone had asked her for the thing she was not entitled to. Not whether there was structure. Whose.

She had an opinion. She noticed that, the way she would have noticed a reading she had not asked an instrument for: she had listened to the pulse for more hours than anyone else on the station, and a person who has listened to a thing that long has an opinion whether she files one or not. The Hegemony was asking her to file it under her name, on a record she would never see again, in a system where the Rangers were guests. An opinion entered on a record stops being an opinion. It becomes a thing other people can act on, and the people who would act on this one were not the people it was about.

She wrote: *The officer has no finding on authorship and enters none. The comparison on file is the Long Shelf's, made by its own analysts from the officer's documented method, and establishes resemblance in phrasing and rhythm. It establishes nothing further. The officer has nothing to add to it.*

There was a sentence after that. She had it drafted before she had decided to draft it, which was how she knew it was an opinion and not a finding: that there was one party in the Halyx System able to say whose hand the pulse was, if anyone could, and that nothing on the file in front of her showed that party had been asked.

She looked at it for some time. It was true. It was also not hers: it was a sentence about what another people's administration should do with its own subject world, written by a guest, and it claimed, underneath, that the reef-cities could answer the question, which she did not know. The review chief had said *keep filing the distinction you're actually entitled to*. He had not said it would ever feel like the smaller thing to do. It felt like the smaller thing to do.

She deleted the sentence and sent the four that were hers. Then she cleared file five hundred and ninety-three, which was a fuel manifest, and did not listen to anything for the rest of the shift.

:::pov sohrel

The office had not asked Sohrel. It had asked the Ranger, through Sohrel, which the central brain had understood on the first reading and filed on the second as the most precisely drafted instruction the Halyx portfolio had received in a decade.

The shape of it was plain enough to anyone who had held the portfolio three centuries. The Star Rangers were the one party in the system that the Hegemony and the reef-cities would both, on their own records, have to accept as holding no stake. A Ranger officer who entered *one author* under her name would have given the Prime Ganglion a finding it could file against four standing objections without ever having put a question to the people who made them. A Ranger officer who entered *not one author* would have closed the question on a neutral's word, which was the only kind of word that closed anything on Meridian. Either way, the asking would have been done, and the Hegemony would not have done it. The central brain recognised the method. It had drafted the method itself, in other files, in other decades.

The reply arrived in four sentences, and the central brain read it for the one that was not there.

It was not there. *The officer has nothing to add to it.* Sohrel had learned, on the far side of this channel over one winter and one spring, that the Ranger's four-sentence answers came with a sentence that had been taken out, and that the sentence taken out was the only one worth the reading. This one had been: that the Long Shelf had not been asked. A human with the standing to write it, on a record the Hegemony would have had to keep, had looked at it and taken it out. The arm beneath the desk shifted four centimetres of colour and settled, which translated, approximately, to *she had it, and she knew whose it was to say, and it was not hers*.

The office's second message was drafted for an Administrator-Voice who had declined four internal requests without a word and authorised a reef-city's own petition without a second's pause, and it left exactly the room such an administrator could be expected to need, which was none. *The Administrator-Voice will put the question of the Sentinel's authorship to the reef-cities of Meridian through the customary channel, or will state for the record the reason it is not put.*

Three centuries of correct answers, and the correct answer did not draft itself. There was no procedurally sound form of *no* that was not also a reason, and the office had drafted the message to make sure of that.

So Sohrel wrote the reason. It had never been written, and the writing of it took longer than the decision had, in the spring, to authorise the thing the reason was about.

*The question is not put because the Hegemony is not prepared to act on the answer.* That was the whole of it; the rest was the arithmetic the office would want shown. If the Long Shelf answered that the Sentinel was its own, the Hegemony would have to recognise a Meridian holding in a system where it held the extraction portfolio, or refuse to recognise it, on the record, and it was prepared for neither. If the Long Shelf answered that it was not, the Hegemony would have taken a finding from a subject world and given nothing for it. If the Long Shelf answered that it did not know, the Hegemony would have made a people say so of its own neighbour, for a file. In every case the party asked paid and the party asking did not. The Long Shelf had consulted on the Sentinel in the spring on its own account and no other's, and had kept the Sentinel apart from the extraction dispute by its own insistence, in a clause it had drafted itself; the question of authorship was the one question that would join them. *The Halyx administration will put it when the Hegemony is prepared to act on any answer the reef-cities can give, and not before.*

The arm beneath the desk did not move while the central brain wrote it. It did not need to. It had been making the case for three centuries, in colour, to nobody, and had just watched it entered in words.

Sohrel filed the reason, and noticed, filing it, that it was the first thing in three centuries of the Sentinel file that had Sohrel's own reasoning in it rather than Sohrel's signature under someone else's. The central brain declined to call that satisfaction. The arm, for once, did not argue the word.

:::pov tissadelle

The request to attend the Administrator-Voice's office before she left gave no reason, which she had begun to understand was how reasons travelled on this station.

She had never been in the room. In one winter and one spring everything between the two offices had gone by the channel, and she had built the Administrator-Voice out of two-line messages the way she had built the Sentinel out of a waveform: a shape, a return, a thing that came back almost the same and not quite. The room was dim, by human lights, and the desk was bare, and the person at it had no front she could address, so she addressed the desk and let the instrument on it carry what she said.

What she said was little. The instrument said, in the flat voice it gave everything, that the officer's original telemetry log remained preserved unmodified, that the file remained open, and that the Long Shelf's thanks for the method stood on the record as entered. Then it asked whether the officer wished to add anything to her assessment before the rotation closed.

"No," Shepherd said.

That was the exchange, on the record. She would have said it was the whole of it, and had she written it up it would have been two lines, and both true.

What was not on the record was that she had looked at the limb beneath the desk. She had not meant to. The upper limbs lay on the desktop, slate-grey, dry, one quiet tone, and the small dark eyes along them were on her and on the door and on the wall, and she had taken that in and filed it as a body she had read about and never seen. One limb hung beneath the desk, and its underside carried a patch that was warmer than the rest, and the patch was moving. Not fast. The way a note moves when a system is compensating for a drift it has not been told about, slowly, in one direction, and she had spent a year on a dock stage learning to hear exactly that and had never once seen it.

Then it stopped.

She knew the stopping better than the moving. She could not read the patch. Nobody on this station could; the file said so, in the plain way files say the things they are sure of. She was not going to try. A Principal who had been believed, narrowly, about a pulse because she had claimed only what she could show was not going to walk out of a Hegemony office having read an Administrator-Voice's skin on the strength of a dock-circuit ear and a different instrument.

But something had been there. That part she could stand on. And then it was not, and that part too.

She thanked the office, through the instrument, and the instrument thanked her back in the Hegemony's correct register, and she went to clear what she could of the queue before the transport. She filed the patch where she filed the things she could not support, beside a line of thanks somebody on the far side had asked to have included, beside a sentence about the Long Shelf that she had deleted, and the file was getting longer, and she had no intention of reading it.

:::pov sohrel

The human had looked at the arm.

Three centuries on a relay station, and the central brain could not recall a human doing it. They looked at the upper limbs because those were on the desk, and at the instrument because it spoke, and at the eyes once they found them, and then they looked at the instrument again. This one had looked at the desk and the door and the eyes and then down, once, and had held the look, and had said *no* to the one question and nothing else, and had not asked what she had seen. The central brain considered, with no urgency, what the officer's own classifier would have entered against the arm, had it been given the feed: an unmodulated source, no protocol match, consistent periodicity. Low priority. No further action recommended.

The office's answer had come that morning, before the officer, and Sohrel had read it before receiving her, which was why the arm had been moving and why it had stopped.

*Reason recorded against the Sentinel file. The question of authorship is reserved to the Prime Ganglion's office and is not to be put to Meridian by the Halyx administration without its direction.*

Correct. Procedurally sound. The office had taken Sohrel's own sentence, *and not before*, and had kept the *not* and moved the *before* out of the Halyx administration's reach. For three centuries the question had been Sohrel's to put or not, and *not yet* had been Sohrel's word, and a word that was Sohrel's could be changed on a morning Sohrel judged the Hegemony ready, or on a morning the Long Shelf asked, through a channel that argued with itself over connotation for half an hour at a time, to be told what was being said about it. A silence had kept that morning possible. A reason was a document. A document could be filed where the office filed things, and an office that had never once heard the Long Shelf's channel argue with itself could act on it, and had.

The reclassification had cost *noted*. The consultation had cost a notice with the extraction portfolio named in it. This had cost the question. Sohrel's central brain set the three in a column and did not draft anything under them.

The arm beneath the desk had gone still while the human was in the room, and it was still now, and it was not the stillness the central brain knew, of an argument declining to spend itself on a fight already lost. It was quieter than that. It was the stillness of a limb that had made its case for three centuries and had finally been given the words, and had watched the words go where limbs cannot reach.

The Ranger's transport was listed for the morning. Her log would stay on the file unmodified, as authorised in the winter, under the only signature on the Sentinel's record that had never claimed past what it could show. The question of whose hand had made the pulse would stay where the office had put it. *Not yet* was still on the file.

It was no longer Sohrel's.
