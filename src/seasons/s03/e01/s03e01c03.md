Three weeks after the review chamber, the backlog queue had not noticed anything had changed, which Shepherd found she preferred to any of the alternatives. File four hundred and thirteen was a fuel-manifest discrepancy from a supply run nobody remembered. She cleared it in under a minute and moved on, glad of the ordinariness, the way a hand is glad of a plain task after it has been asked to do something it wasn't sure it was allowed to do.

The liaison bulletin arrived on the same terminal the reclassification had, which felt, obscurely, like the terminal's business rather than hers. Two lines, routed through the standing data-exchange arrangement and stamped with a Hegemony seal she had learned to read in the last month for the first time in her career:

*Consultation authorised: Sentinel telemetry, comparative analysis, Long Shelf reef-city council (Meridian). Requesting Ranger officer's original audio-routing method, documented, for use by Meridian analysts. Conclusions not requested.*

She read it twice, the second time for the two words doing the actual work: *not requested*. Somebody on the Hegemony side had been careful to write that in, and careful, she suspected, for her benefit rather than the Hegemony's own.

She sat with it longer than a two-line request should have taken to answer. Writing up how she'd listened to the pulse was not the same as writing up what she thought it meant, and she knew the difference the way she'd known it in the review chamber — but the review chamber had been her own institution testing her own claim, and this was a subject world's council, on the far side of a jurisdiction the Rangers were only ever guests in, asking to borrow the way she'd found the thing rather than what she'd found. She thought about the review chief's line, which she had been carrying around for three weeks the way she carried the hillside rule, because it had turned out to be the same rule wearing a different uniform: *keep filing the distinction you're actually entitled to.* Method was not meaning. She was entitled to hand over the first and had no standing to offer the second even if anyone had asked her for it, which nobody had.

She wrote up the routing procedure in four short paragraphs, plainly, the way she'd have written it for a cadet, and appended nothing about what she'd heard in it or what she thought it might turn out to be. Filed it back through the same channel before she could decide there was a version of caution that was really just reluctance wearing caution's uniform.

The acknowledgment came back nine hours later, formally worded, Hegemony-correct on every line — and one line longer than it needed to be, a small addition that read less like protocol and more like something a person had asked to have included: *The council notes the method costs nothing to receive and states nothing it did not already suspect. It is grateful regardless.*

She had no way to read whatever channel that sentence had actually travelled on before it became words a human could parse, and she found, turning it over at her terminal with the queue still running behind it, that this did not bother her nearly as much as she'd have guessed it should. Some things arrived translated. That they arrived at all was the part worth keeping.

:::pov sohrel

The petition did not arrive through any of the channels a fifth request was supposed to use, which was the first thing Sohrel's central brain noticed and the last thing it managed to file as merely procedural.

Four times, the request had come from inside the Hegemony's own administration — an analyst two offices over, forwarding a colleague's question without quite attaching a name to it, careful in the specific way of someone who wanted a thing to happen and did not want to be the reason it did. This was not that. This arrived through the relay station's least reliable instrumentation, the chemical-and-percussion translation channel Sohrel's office maintained mostly out of obligation and rarely trusted past a paragraph, and it produced, after the usual half-hour of the instrumentation arguing with itself over connotation, the clearest sentence Sohrel had read in three centuries of holding the Halyx portfolio: *The Long Shelf asks to be told what is being said about it, and to say what it knows in return, on its own account and no other's.*

*On its own account and no other's.* The council had drafted that clause itself, had insisted the translation carry its exact shape rather than a smoother Hegemony paraphrase, and Sohrel's central brain understood immediately what it was being asked to leave out: the extraction dispute, the deep-shelf beds, four separate formal objections filed and unslowed by any of them. The Long Shelf was not offering the Sentinel as a bargaining position. It was refusing to let the Hegemony read it as one either, which was, Sohrel's central brain noted with something that was not quite admiration and was not quite its opposite, a more disciplined distinction than most of the internal requests had managed to hold.

The correct answer drafted itself in under a second: *pending review, resource allocation matters outside the present filing's scope, further internal consultation required.* True. Defensible. The kind of answer three centuries of holding this portfolio had trained the central brain to produce before anything else had finished thinking.

The third dorsal arm's ganglion did not wait for the second line this time. It had waited four times already, for four requests that were never going to be the one worth spending on, and something in its slow color shift across the desk read less like dissent now and more like recognition — the particular stillness of an argument that had finally been handed the case it was built to make.

Sohrel authorised the consultation on the council's own terms before the correct answer finished drafting itself underneath it.

What came back from the Prime Ganglion's office, four days later, was not two lines. It named the extraction portfolio directly, which the first notice on the Sentinel file had gone to some trouble not to do, and it did not use the word *noted* in isolation this time. Sohrel read it twice, the second time the way three centuries had taught the reading to go — for the shape of what a Hegemony office chose to make explicit rather than leave to be inferred — and understood, without needing the arm to say so, that this was the larger of the two costs, spent on purpose, against a portfolio that had thirty years left in it at the outside and would not have thirty years' worth of *noted* to spend them against.

The comparison itself, when the Long Shelf's own analysts finished it, said less than either side had let itself hope and more than the Hegemony's classifiers had managed in three centuries: resemblance, not authorship. Rhythm, not proof. The Sentinel remained exactly as unresolved as it had been the morning Shepherd first put it through a headset instead of a spectrum display — which the council, when the finding reached them, filed without complaint and without surprise, in the manner, Sohrel's central brain reflected, of a people who had never expected the sea to answer a question quickly.

Some costs bought an answer. This one had bought only the right to keep asking, on the Long Shelf's own account, and no other's. Sohrel's central brain filed the distinction as sufficient. The limb beneath the desk, for once, agreed with it outright.
