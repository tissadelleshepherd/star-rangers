The finding came up to the Chief's desk with the morning's other papers, and Arashobal read it for its length, as she read everything.

It was short. *Section Lead Shepherd, T., examined at Chief, general operations track. Certified.* Three initials under it, and a date, and nothing a Krenyi needed to read twice. The assessment that had put the officer in front of the board had been Arashobal's own, written after the window in the summer, four paragraphs, and the four paragraphs had gone to the board and come back as one line, which was the correct ratio. A board that needed more than the material part from a Chief had been examining the Chief.

She had not written what she thought the officer would become. That was not an assessment's business. She had written what the officer had done with a section in one summer: the watches set; thirteen years of readings read before a window was stated; the window stated as a band and left standing when the room pushed; and one Principal's cost noticed, and the watch moved, nine days before the Principal reported it. The board had examined the rest. What a board examined at Chief was not whether an officer could run a section, because the section had already shown that. It was whether she could answer for the people who ran one.

The second paper was the order, and the order was not Arashobal's to give. Threshold had one general operations department and one Chief Ranger, and the Corps did not promote a Section Lead into a chair with a Chief still in it, and had never been asked to. The department of a Survey Corps mission, however, was a chair that existed only while the mission did. *Chief Ranger, general operations department, Survey Corps revisit to the surveyed membrane of Counterpane, the seventh. Department forming. Transit, early spring. Effective on the date of this order.* The Survey had asked for a general operations Chief who would read the readings before stating a window, which was a sentence somebody on the Survey had plainly taken from the summer traffic plan, and the Corps had looked at its lists and found that it had one.

Arashobal filed the two papers together and sent for the officer, and went on with the department's work until she came, which was quickly.

She came in as she had in the summer, and stood, and did not fill the room with herself. Seven bars on the collar. The finding had been on the desk an hour and nobody had told her, because that was the Chief's to do, and Arashobal did it at the speed a human could take it, which for this one was the speed of the sentence.

"The board has certified you at Chief," Arashobal said. "The order posts you to the general operations department of the seventh Counterpane revisit, forming now, transit in the early spring. It is a mission department and it is yours from today. The section passes to its senior Principal pending a Lead. You stand no watch on this station from this hour."

"Yes, Chief."

"Not Chief. Not to me, from this hour. The rank is level." She let the human hear that, since humans found the levelling the strange part and the Krenyi found it the only part. "The department is the material part, so I will say it. A section is a watch and the people on it, and you answered for who you put there. A department is sections, and you answer for who you put over each one, and for what they put on their watches that you will not see, and for noticing their cost through a Lead's noticing, which arrives later than your own did. On a mission department there may be no Lead between you and the watch. Then you are the Lead as well, and you state the window yourself, and you are still answering as a Chief for a Lead who happens to be you."

The officer took that without the nod. Arashobal registered the absence and filed it under nothing: a thing offered and not yet taken, because the officer had not yet stood where it was.

"The roster," Shepherd said. "Who fills it."

"You do. The Survey states what the department must carry. The Corps gives you the lists. You name who carries it, and the names are yours, and so is what it costs them." Arashobal gave her the lists, which had come up with the order, and the officer did not read them standing. She looked at the thickness of them, and at Arashobal, and said that she would read them in the morning, and Arashobal, who could not un-notice anything, noticed that this was the first thing she had seen the officer put down before reading it.

She went. The door closed. Arashobal returned to the summer traffic plan, rewritten now, and to the pilot department, which had a new Chief Pilot, the old one's ring having gone up to the command ladder since the summer, and which flew a band as a band because it had been taught to before the new Chief came; and to the outer docks, which were thin, and were a Principal's until the spring.

:::pov tissadelle

The tab came off with a thumbnail, in the corridor, because there was no dock between this rank and the last. The length of a corridor was the interval the Corps gave her this time, and she had the new one in the order's sleeve before she had decided to take it out.

Seven bars, slate blue, one summer old. She had worn two on this collar as a cadet and seven as a Section Lead, and the summer she had worn seven had been one of the shortest postings of her service, and the one she would have kept. The new tab was a bar and a ring. The slate insert sat at the bar, the last place on the ladder a tab said what track a person had climbed, and above it there would be rings and nothing else. She seated it. The magnet took, and the tab sat proud of the cloth, visibly removable, so that nobody could mistake it for something sewn.

*One bar,* Tobble said, inward. *One ring. Counted twice. The bars have stopped.*

She stood no watch on the station from this hour. The Chief had said so, and the Chief did not say things that were not the case, and so she let him.

*Stopped,* she said.

*Seven was the last count that went up by one. The record shows a change of unit.* Then the pause that was his for a thing still being checked. *Rings next. I will count those.*

She did not ask him what he made of the ring. He would say when he had checked it, and he had not, because there was nothing yet to check it against.

What she had was a department that did not exist. A roster, in two lists, that she had put down unread for the first time in her service, because she had understood, looking at the thickness of it, that it was not a thing to be read standing in a Chief's office with the Chief going on with her own work. It was names. Each of them was a person who would be on a shelter floor on a membrane where the dice were not ours, because she had written them there, and would be noticed late, through somebody else's noticing, if they were noticed at all. In the summer she had been given a watch and the people on it and had gone down to the dock that night to look at the dock before she set anything. There was no dock to look at now. There would not be one until the spring, and then it would be a cold shelter she had never seen, and the people in it would be people she had chosen from a list in a warm room.

So she went down to the one she knew.

:::pov tissadelle

Dock Seven kept two times, and the watch log had one line for it, and she had no business with either.

She had come down at the hour she had always come down, which was no hour a cadet could learn, and the cadet had learned it anyway, because the cadet was on her feet before Shepherd was through the hatch. The cadet looked at the collar. The cadet said, "Chief," and on this dock that word had meant one person for as long as the cadet had been standing on it, and Shepherd heard the word go somewhere it had not been before and did not correct it, because it was correct.

She told the cadet to sit, and the cadet sat.

The two chronometers were where they had been, one above the pressure seal and one above the inner hatch, the station's and the other one, each of them certain. She did not look at them yet. She read the log standing, from tonight's line back, page by page, at the speed she read anything she was going to initial, and the time line carried one reading, every watch, marked *station time*, left-hand instrument, in a cadet's hand. Under each line the cadet's initial. Under the summer's lines a second, hers, at hours no roster had set. Under tonight's, nothing yet.

She had the stylus in her hand. She noticed that the way she noticed most things, afterwards, by the shape of it.

The section was a Principal's tonight, pending a Lead. The log would go up at the turn of the station's day to the Chief's desk, and the desk was not hers; it had never been hers, and from this hour it was not even the desk she answered to. A Chief who initialled a section's watch log on a dock she did not answer for had not checked anything. She had made a claim, under a cadet's initial, about whose dock this was, and it was not the case, and the Chief upstairs would read the claim at the turn of the day the way she read everything, for its edges.

That was the summer's sentence, in a different uniform. A Section Lead who wrote the right-hand reading into the time line had made a claim about what the dock was for. A Chief who wrote her initial under a watch that was not hers had made a claim about whose it was. Both true. Neither the log's. She had learned the first on this sill in the summer and she was learning the second on it now, and she found that the learning had the same shape both times: a sentence already drafting itself in her hand, and the hand stopped.

She put the stylus down.

What she had been handed in the summer, she had taken for the dock. The watches hers to set, the people hers to answer for, the window hers to state. *Not present, and entirely responsible*, which was the Principal's manner, copied and then understood. She had thought a Chief was that manner one rung further out, and it was; but she had thought the rung went outward from the dock, and it did not. It went outward from being there at all. A Chief answered for a Lead who answered for a watch, and the Lead's noticing arrived late, and the Chief's arrived later, and the one thing in the whole arrangement a Chief could not do was come down at an hour nobody could learn and read the log standing and put her initial under a cadet's. That was the first thing the rank took, and it took it the hour it was given, before the department existed to be answered for.

Then she looked at the right-hand instrument.

Forty seconds. She had counted it on forty-one nights as a cadet and had not needed to count it since, and it had not moved. Thirteen years the station had carried it, outside the log by standing practice, and in the summer she had gone up the stair telling herself the practice was hers now to let stand. It had been, for a summer. It was a Principal's tonight and would be a Lead's in the spring, and it had been nobody's for longer than a posting since the day a reporting officer wrote the first forty seconds down, which was what *standing* meant, and she had not known that until the stair. Nothing she had been handed in the summer had been the dock. It had been the length of time the dock was hers.

She told the cadet the watch was in good order, which it was. She closed the log with the time line carrying one reading and one initial, and went up, and the right-hand instrument read forty seconds later than the log as she went, as it had on every night it had been hers, and on every night it had not.

The general operations log came up to the Chief's desk at the turn of the station's day, every section's, with the initials on it.

Dock Seven's carried one. The cadet's, under each line, as a cadet's did. Nothing struck. Nothing added. The time line carried the station's time from the left-hand instrument, marked, and under the time line there was no second initial at an hour no roster had set, and the absence had edges. Arashobal read the edges the way she read a thing left out of a file: a Chief had gone down to the dock on the evening of her order, with no watch to take and no desk to answer to, and had stood where the initial went, and had not written it.

The Quiet-Built kept no fact whose use was not yet shown. This one had shown its use twice on the same sill, in one summer and one evening, so she kept it, and returned to the department, whose outer docks were a Principal's until the spring, and whose log would carry one initial on that line until a Lead was found, which was the correct number.
