:::pov tissadelle

Everything passed.

That was the first morning's work and the whole of it: a bench of instruments brought through a fold, each one asked the question it had been built to answer, each one answering as it had answered at the staging site on the far side. The atomic standard kept the second. The spectrometer found the handbook's lines where the handbook put them. The chemistry ran, the air was air, and the shelter's own clock, which had been told nothing about where it was, agreed with the one on her wrist to the limit of either. Shepherd signed the single-system audit at the foot of each sheet, because the Survey's rule for this membrane was that general operations certified the bench was the bench and the Survey certified what the bench said, and a Chief Ranger who did not know where that line fell had no business commanding the section that stood on one side of it.

She had read the file on the transit out. Six revisits before this one. One membrane of a neighbouring archecluster, reached by fold because the manifold was common and a fold did not care whose vocabulary the far end used; a first party that had logged nothing for months, because nothing here was wrong that an instrument asked one question at a time could find; and then a calibration run, routine, that had produced a discrepancy, and the discrepancy had survived every correction anyone predicted for it, and the Survey had done the thing the Survey does with a discrepancy that survives, which was to stop calling it one. The file did not say *anomaly*. It said *reading*, at the width of one membrane, and it said that every revisit since had been to confirm the reading with instruments that would afterwards go home and be audited by people who had never left.

The revisits had a discipline, and the discipline was hers to hold. Nothing taken here was a finding. A number read on this bench was a number read on this bench until the hull had folded home, the bench had been taken apart on a floor that had never been anywhere, and every instrument had been asked whether the transit had changed it. Only then did the number become a reading. Until then it was carried, like cargo, and the log said so. She had asked the Survey Chief why, on the transit out, in the form she asked questions in, which was the form that could be answered in one sentence. Halvorsen had said: because the one thing we cannot calibrate against here is here.

So Shepherd held it. She wrote *carried* against every value the bench produced, and she wrote the departure geometry as the file had it, Tier 2, the shelter sitting at the quietest gradient the first party had been able to locate, which was not a Lagrange point of anything; and she stated the fold window home. The pilot wanted a point. She gave him a band, nine hours wide, with its edges and the readings that put the edges there, and the pilot took the band, because the pilot had flown with her before and knew what he would get. The re-sync debt on the far side would be whatever a Tier 2 departure made it, and that was the pilot's, and she said so.

Then the habit.

Halvorsen had three sources on the bench, independent by construction, each a small sealed thing that did its one thing and had been doing it, unminded, since the staging site. The Survey's whole purpose here was to braid them and read what three of them did together. Before the braid there was the stream from each, one at a time, and a stream could be run as audio, and Shepherd had not been able to leave a stream unlistened-to since the winter she had run a Hegemony backlog as sound and heard a reef-city in it. She asked. A Chief Ranger does not touch a Survey bench, and she did not; she asked for the stream, and Halvorsen gave it to her without a word, and she sat on a crate at the shelter's cold end with the first source in her ears.

She had thought, on the transit out, that if there was a difference here she might hear it before the bench read it. That was vanity. She had known it for vanity and brought the habit anyway, because the habit had never once been wrong about whether something was there, and because being wrong about that was the one thing she had never had to be believed about.

She listened for most of an hour. She heard what a source sounds like doing its one thing: the grain that randomness has when it is honest, with the small structure under it that every such source she had ever heard carried, and nothing else. She changed to the second source and heard the second source. She did not need the third. Nothing was there. Not nothing wrong, which was a thing she had heard before and knew the sound of. Nothing. A source, at home.

She took the stream out of her ears and said so. Nothing single is, Halvorsen said, and began to braid.

Shepherd watched her do it, because there was nothing else for general operations to do until the window opened, and because she wanted to see the face of someone reading a thing that could not be heard. The joint distribution came up as a shape on a surface. Shepherd could not read it. It was the Survey's, and she had rotated off the Survey track the summer before and had never been on this branch of it. What she could read was Halvorsen, and Halvorsen's face did not change, and Shepherd understood after a moment that this was not because the shape was ordinary but because changing one's face at a shape was a claim, and the shape was cargo until it was home.

She said one thing more, when the braid was down and the value written as carried. She asked what a source sounded like, braided. Not the stream. The three together.

"Like three sources," Halvorsen said.

Shepherd nodded and went to check the window. On the transit out she had thought about the review chamber at Halyx, a year ago now, where she had sat across a table from three careful people and been believed, narrowly, on the strength of an ear nobody had trained. She did not think about it now. She wrote the log, which said that the audit had passed, that the window was stated as a band, that the departure geometry was Tier 2, and that nothing had been filed. All of which was so.

:::pov ingrid-halvorsen

Nobody listened to the bench.

Six revisits, six commanding officers, and not one of them had asked for a stream. They had signed the audit, which was theirs, and stated the window, which was theirs, and stood at the warm end of the shelter while she braided, and some had asked what the shape meant and been told that it meant a value would be carried home, and had left it there. It was the right place to leave it. A Survey Chief who told a commanding officer what the shape meant, on the membrane, before the audit, had made a finding where no finding could be made yet, and would deserve what the audit did to her.

This one had sat on a crate for most of an hour with a source in her ears.

Halvorsen had let her. There was no rule against it, because nobody had thought to need one, and because a stream was a stream: a single source, doing its single thing, and the one fact about this membrane that six revisits had confirmed past any arguing was that a single thing here was the single thing. The second was the second. An atom was an atom. A source, run as sound, would sound like a source. She had known what the Chief Ranger would hear before the Chief Ranger had heard it, and had said nothing, because a Chief Ranger who is told in advance what she will hear has been given a reading, and the Survey does not give readings. It carries them.

She had watched, though. She had watched the way she watched a drift figure settle: not for the number, for whether it would stop. The Chief Ranger had listened the way a person listens who has been right before and is checking whether she is still the person who was right. The first source, most of an hour. The second, a few minutes. Not the third. Then the stream out, and *nothing is there*, said flat, in the form a thing is said in when it has been checked and the person saying it is not asking to be agreed with.

Halvorsen had agreed with her anyway, in two words, because it was true and because the Survey did not withhold what was true merely because it was not yet a finding. Nothing single is. Then the braid.

The braid was the Survey's. Three sources, independent by construction, each sealed and each ignorant of the other two, run together, and the joint distribution read. She had read it six times on this membrane and more times than she had counted at home, and what she read now was what she had read six times: a shape that was not ours. Not wrong. Not loud. The shape three honest sources make together, drawn to a different rule, and drawn to it exactly, every revisit, with the patience of something that had never once been asked to be anything else. The first party had called it a discrepancy for a week and then stopped, because the Section Lead who ran the calibration had written *survives correction* under it and refused to write anything softer, and had been believed, eventually, by people who had never left. Halvorsen had been on the second revisit and every one since, and the shape had not moved, and her face did not move either, because the audit was at home and her face was not an instrument.

The Chief Ranger had watched her face. Halvorsen had felt it, the way one feels a gauge being read.

Then the one question, after the value was written as carried: what three sources sounded like, braided.

Halvorsen had answered it exactly. Like three sources. Which was so, and which was the whole of what the Survey could say on the membrane, and which the Chief Ranger had heard for what it was, because she had nodded and gone to the window and not asked the next question. There was a next question. Halvorsen had been carrying it for six revisits and had not asked it of anyone, because it was not a Survey question and she did not know whose it was. It was something like: what else is a bench. What else has been doing its one thing, honestly, at home, in the grain every such thing carries, and has never once been braided against a rule it was not drawn to.

She did not ask it now either. The Chief Ranger was at the window with the pilot, and the window was theirs.

The bench came apart in the order it had gone together. She packed it the way she packed it every time, each instrument sealed and labelled and written as cargo, which was what it was until it was home.
