::::: scene 1

::: pov stone-first

This is told the way things are told below the roof, and this time it is told by the one who was there, because for the whole of it there was only one.

Stone-First was on the line. The count had a number, and the number was not small any more, and Stone-First had stopped thinking about it as a number and begun thinking about it as a place, the way a long passage stops being a length and becomes somewhere you are. On the line, in the open, with the two shapes of feet in the silt and the small one's third shape beside them, on all the times when nothing happens.

The up-people came down, up above, the way they did. Stone-First could not see them and did not need to. You could tell. The loud came down the long passage the way water comes down a passage, all at once and then steadily, and settled at the junction, and became the working.

Then it did not.

Stone-First noticed the not before noticing anything else, because keeping a place is mostly noticing the shape of what is usual. The loud had come down the passage and had not settled. It had come on. Down the third passage, toward the crack, which it had never done at the start of a working time in all the tellings since the humming stone was lifted, and only once ever before that.

The light came into the squeeze.

Stone-First had seen the light on the deep side once, from far down the dark, and it had come through the way the youngest said they came through everything, loud, all three. This was not that. This was one light, low, close to the ground, moving the way a person moves who is trying not to throw anything, and the sound in the crack was one person's, scraping, the way the youngest scraped, the way the small one had.

One of them was coming through.

Stone-First did the thing Stone-First does, which is nothing, with the whole body still, so that nothing done while a thing is happening becomes part of the telling by mistake. And the light came out of the crack onto the silt, held low, and behind it the smallest of the three, the one the youngest had told of, who knelt to the humming stone, and the light lifted a little, and found Stone-First's feet, and went up, slowly, the way a person looks.

It stopped at Stone-First's face, and stayed there.

Stone-First did not move. The up-person did not go back.

::::: scene 2

::: pov stone-first

Here is what the up-person did, in order, because it is the thing the whole season has been about and it is worth getting right.

They were carrying a stone. Stone-First had not seen it in the crack; it had been held to the front, the way you carry a thing you do not want to scrape. A stone of the upper levels, the runners' ground, the size of two hands, held in two. The up-person stood on the silt with the light low and the stone held, and looked at Stone-First for as long as a short telling takes, and Stone-First could see them wanting to go back through the crack with every part of the body that was not the eyes. They did not go.

Then they knelt.

They knelt the way the youngest had said they knelt to the humming stone, and set the stone down on the line. On the silt, a hand from Stone-First's feet, exactly on the line, as if somebody had measured it. It made the sound a stone makes when it is put down on ground, small and plain and complete, and the ground took it, and told Stone-First what the ground tells you, which was that someone was here.

Late. That was the first thing Stone-First thought. The stone was late. The manners say you move it before you are seen, so that nobody is surprised by anybody, and the up-person had come all the way through the crack and stood in the open and been seen, and then moved the stone. Which was exactly what the youngest had done, the very first time, at the bottom of the squeeze, small and late, and Stone-First had noticed and put it aside and said nothing, because a stone moved late is still a stone moved, and a small one who has moved one late has learned the thing that matters.

Theirs. That was the second thing. It was not the people's stone, moved for them, the way the youngest had moved their own humming stone. It was a stone they had chosen and carried and set down themselves. The Told had waited a whole life for somebody else to move a stone, and had not known what it would look like, and this was what it looked like: late, and clumsy, and done kneeling, and theirs.

::::: scene 3

::: pov stone-first

The up-person stood, and took one step back, toward the crack. Away from the stone. Leaving the place.

And then said a thing.

It went up at the end. It was said to Stone-First, not to the dark — the light was low and the face was turned and there was nobody else on the silt to say it to — and Stone-First heard it and did not know what it was, and this telling does not say, because the one who was there does not know, and a telling that says more than the one who was there knew is not a telling.

The up-person waited. With the whole body still. The way the small one had waited on the line, the way you wait for a hand to be filled.

Stone-First had been asked. Whatever the words were. Stone-First had stood on the line for longer than the small ones' whole tellings so as to be there on the time when this happened, and it was happening, and there is only one thing the Told do when a stone has been moved for them, and it is not to speak, because a stone is not answered with a word.

Stone-First bent, and put a hand on a rock at the edge of the silt, a good one, and moved it.

The ground spoke. The up-person heard it. Stone-First saw them hear it: the whole body went still in a new way, the way a small one goes still when a sound they did not know turns out to mean something. They stood like that for as long as it takes to say a name.

Then they went back through the crack, slowly, with the light held low, not scraping more than they had to, and up.

Stone-First stood on the line with two stones on it, one on each side of the place where the feet had stopped, and did not go after them, and did not go down, and stayed, for a while, in the light that never changes, so that the telling would have an end.

::::: scene 4

::: pov teddy

Stone-First told it standing up, in the warm, to everyone, and left nothing out, because there was nothing in it that was a reason and nothing that was not the thing itself.

The loud that came on instead of settling. The one light, low. The small up-person out of the crack, and the looking. The stone, carried to the front, and knelt with, and set down on the line, late. The step back. The sound that went up at the end, and what it was, which Stone-First did not know and said so, because saying so is also manners. The rock at the edge of the silt. The going.

The warm was quieter than Carried-It-Sleeping had ever heard it.

"They asked," said Went-Round, from the far side of the amber, and for once did not go round it.

"They began," said Stone-First. "A stone is not asking. It is the thing before. They have done the thing before. That is what a stone is for."

"They said a thing to you. It went up at the end. That is asking. You have said yourself that is what their asking is."

"I do not know what they said."

"Nobody knows what anybody says," said Went-Round, "until the telling goes on. That is what a telling is."

Several people made the small sound. Stone-First heard it, and did not mind it, and did not answer, and Carried-It-Sleeping saw that Stone-First was not going to, and that this was not a thing the people were going to settle tonight, and that it did not matter, because both of them were true. They had begun. They had asked. A stone, and a sound, and a stone back. It was the beginning of a telling, and the whole of the people had heard it now, and it was theirs.

Carried-It-Sleeping lay in the warm and thought about the two stones on the line. The humming stone, carried sleeping, set down at the edge of the up-people's place so that their own ground would tell them someone was here. And now a plain stone of the runners' ground, carried by the smallest of them through the crack, set down late, at the edge of the people's place, so that the people's ground would tell them the same. The same thing, done twice, from both sides. Carried-It-Sleeping had done the first one and had not known, doing it, that it was the first half of anything.

And then the telling began to go round the warm, the way tellings do, from the ones who had heard it to the ones who had been asleep, and Carried-It-Sleeping heard, lying down, what the people were saying of Stone-First now. Not *Stone-First*. Not only. What they said, over and over, so that it was already worn smooth by the time it reached the far end of the warm, was: *the one who moves a stone first, and went up and stood on the line, and waited to be asked, and was.*

A name grows. It is not replaced. A truer thing said later is added on, and the name gets longer, and a long name is a grown one.

Stone-First-Who-Waited.

Carried-It-Sleeping let it go by a second time, and a third, to be certain, and it was, and the small ones were already saying it, so that they would be able to say it when they were old.

::::: scene 5

::: pov stone-first

Stone-First went back up to the line, afterwards, because that is where the margin is and keeping it means being there.

The two stones were where they had been left. Stone-First did not move either of them. A stone that has been moved for you is not yours to move again; it is the beginning of a telling, and you do not tidy a beginning away.

The count did not go up. Stone-First noticed that, standing there, and understood it. The count had been of askings said to the dark, and the last thing said had not been said to the dark. It had been said to Stone-First. There was nothing to count any more, because the thing that was being counted had stopped being nothing. Stone-First had never wanted the number. Stone-First let it go, the way you let a kept thing go once it has done what it was kept for.

What the up-people would do next, Stone-First did not know. What the people would do next, Stone-First did not know either. That was not this telling's. A telling has an end, and this one had reached it, on the line, with a stone on each side of the place where the feet had stopped, and neither side of the line any different from what it had been except that both sides now knew the other was there, and had said so, each in the only way it could.

It was not a telling yet. Stone-First knew that. A stone is not a telling. It is only the beginning of one.

But every telling begins with one, and this one had two.
