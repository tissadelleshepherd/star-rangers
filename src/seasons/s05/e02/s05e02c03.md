::: pov tissadelle

The survey she had flagged ten days ago went out fleet-priority, and fleet-priority meant a hull, and the hull it meant was hers.

That was the part nobody warned a Line Captain about. You cited your grounds in four clean lines, and four clean lines were enough to put the *Sufficient Cause* on the Threshold approaches in mid-autumn, sweeping a marker class rather than a coordinate — pale bark, a leaf-phase lag of a fraction of a second, and the two older Union land-survey entries the Survey Archive had logged under unrelated classifications and never flagged for follow-up. She had asked for the class to be looked at. She had not specified who should have to look.

The sweep found the marker on the third day, in the thin water off Dock Seven, and then it found something the marker had nothing to do with.

Shepherd read the return twice, once for content and once for what the content was for, and then she read it a third time, which she almost never did.

An aperture. Open.

She checked the fold log for a transit and found one, logged, licensed, and closed out four months ago. So the transit had happened. The transit had finished. And the aperture the transit had opened was still there, four months later, held.

*A fold is an aperture opened, crossed, and collapsed.* She had learned that as a cadet from a woman who taught it as a fact about the universe rather than a fact about engineering, and she had never once had cause to doubt her. The collapse was not a courtesy. The collapse was the definition. A thing that opened and did not collapse was not a fold that had gone wrong. It was something else that had never been a fold at all, wearing the paperwork of one.

"Comms," she said. "Threshold. Boundary desk, not the duty officer."

She put the *Sufficient Cause* in closer, and at forty kilometres she stopped, because she could see them now without instruments, which was itself a finding she would have to write down.

The dock crews called them tapers. They came in off the thin water in loose drifts, a few hundred at a time, and they held a light the way a wick holds one — not emitting, exactly; sustaining, until they didn't. Survey had classed them years ago and the class had settled the matter: Class III, non-sapient, pattern-matching rather than strategy, no standing anywhere in the Cascade and no protection from it either. Two generations of Threshold's boundary handbooks called them a hazard, and were right to. A drift of tapers through a docking approach had killed people.

They were going into the aperture. Not past it. Into it, in a steady unhurried line, the way a thing goes toward what it has been built to go toward, and the aperture was doing something to them that Shepherd watched for six seconds and then stopped watching, because she had the finding and did not need the rest of it.

What came out the other side went into holds. She could see the holds. They were painted.

::: pov syra

Syra did not experience deep fear, and so the boundary desk at Threshold got, for once, an assessment that arrived at the same speed as the data.

"Say only what the instruments carry," she told the desk, which was herself and Toval, a Krenyi Cadet she was training out of the habit of rounding up. "The *Sufficient Cause* reports an aperture at forty kilometres, held open past transit. That is what she reports. Here is what I have."

What she had: a fold signature that was not decaying. That was the whole of it and it was enough. Folding was a bounded event with a beginning and an end, and the end was not optional; the signature of a completed transit fell off a cliff and stayed fallen. This one had a floor. It had had a floor, she established, going back through the archive with the junior watching, for four months, in a band nobody swept because nothing was supposed to be able to sit in it.

"And the — " the Cadet started.

"Class III aggregate, in transit toward the aperture, in numbers." Syra kept her voice exactly level, because the Cadet was going to have to write this down and would write it better if nobody had performed anything at him. "Non-sapient. No Cascade standing. Not protected by any constraint I can cite, and I have looked, because I assumed you would ask and I would rather have looked than guessed."

"So there's no — " He stopped. "There's nothing wrong with it."

"There is nothing wrong with *taking them*," Syra said. "That is correct and you should not soften it. What is wrong is the aperture. An aperture held open is a breach of boundary integrity whatever it is held open for, and it would be a breach if the holds on the far side were empty. Do not build the finding on the animals. Build it on the aperture. The aperture is the part that will survive review."

She flagged it, and then, because precision was doctrine before it was temperament, she flagged what she could not support: that she did not know what four months of a held aperture did to a boundary zone, because nobody had ever had four months of one to measure.

::: pov tissadelle

The concern was called the Hyperfold Yield Combine, and it traded as Hyperfold, and it had a licence.

Shepherd read the licence while the *Sufficient Cause* held station, and it was, as far as it went, in order: transit rights, a survey exemption, a rendering permit, and an inner-system counsel's opinion appended in full confidence that no clause of it required the aperture to close. The word *hyperfold* appeared nine times in the filing and was not a term for anything. It was not the Survey Corps' word, it was not a folding engineer's word, and the one place it did appear in the literature was a glossary entry explaining, with some restraint, that people who used it had misunderstood two different phenomena and welded them together. The Combine had taken the mistake and put it on the hull.

That told her more than the licence did. A concern that could not correctly name the thing it did for a living had not been careless. It had chosen a word that sounded like the physics and committed to nothing.

There was a market for what they rendered. She looked that up too. Tapers went into fold-transit stabiliser, and stabiliser made apertures cheaper to hold, and cheaper apertures made more tapers reachable. It closed. Every part of it closed except the aperture, which was the only part that was supposed to.

She raised the Combine's operations lead on an open channel and put it plainly, because putting it plainly was the procedural step and because she wanted it on the record that the step had been taken. The lead was courteous. The lead cited the licence. The lead observed, correctly, that the Star Rangers held no jurisdiction over a licensed commercial operation in open water absent a finding, and asked, without any edge at all, which finding she was making.

"None yet," Shepherd said. "I'm asking you to close the aperture."

"We're not able to do that."

"Not permitted, or not able?"

There was a pause of about the right length for a man to consult somebody, and then the lead said, "I'd have to come back to you on that," and Shepherd understood that she had her answer and that it was worse than either of the two she had offered him.

She convened a Field Triumvirate at 0410.

She did it by the clause and she did it in the open, because that was the entire point of the clause. Herself, ranking Line Captain. The *Sufficient Cause*'s Archwarden. The next most senior Starwarden reachable, which was Dock Seven's, joining by relay with the boundary desk listening and the log running. She stated the three conditions and made each of them stand on its own before she took the next: procedural approaches tried and failed, which was the channel she had just closed; no local law enforcement present or reachable in time, which in open water eleven hours from anything was not a judgement call; and the third, which was the one she made herself say slowly.

That waiting for the chain of command would cost the thing the decision was meant to save.

The thing the decision was meant to save was not the tapers. She said so, on the log, in as many words, because a Field Triumvirate action was a judgement three officers made on a record built to be second-guessed, and she had never in her life given an order she could not defend afterward. The tapers had no standing. She was not empowered to act for them and did not claim to be. What she was acting on was four months of a held aperture in a boundary zone eleven hours from a station with nine hundred people on it, and an operator who could not tell her whether closing it was something he was not allowed to do or something he could no longer manage.

The Triumvirate carried. She logged the dissent, because there wasn't one, and logging that there wasn't one was also the clause.

Then she took the *Sufficient Cause* in to close the aperture, and the aperture, when the *Sufficient Cause* reached it, turned out to have been the smaller of the two problems for some time.

::: pov wender

Wender got it at 0447, third-hand and eleven hours stale, which was the ordinary condition of command and had never once stopped being intolerable.

She read the Triumvirate log twice. The first time she read it as a High Captain, checking whether three officers had convened a thing they were entitled to convene, and they had; the clause was satisfied in each of its parts and each part had been made to stand alone, which was Shepherd being Shepherd — not building a case, building a structure, so that anyone who wanted to pull it down later would have to say which specific piece they were pulling.

The second time she read it as somebody who had taught the doctrine it was written in.

*An order is sound if it is correct given what is currently known.* She had said that to rooms full of officers for four seasons and believed it every time, and here it was, applied by a former cadet without a wasted word, to a situation Wender could not have improved on from the chair she was sitting in. Everything Shepherd had done was right. Wender checked it twice looking for the place where it stopped being right and did not find one, and the not finding was the part that made her set the pad down and sit for a moment with her hands flat on the desk.

Because the order was correct, and Wender was eleven hours away, and the ship was already in.

She keyed the relay and said, "Countermand nothing. Get me the boundary desk," and if her voice did anything unusual on the first two words, the log did not preserve it.

::: pov tissadelle

The evacuation took forty-one minutes and she was pleased with it afterward, in the small flat way she permitted herself, because forty-one minutes was very good.

The aperture had begun to widen when the *Sufficient Cause* came inside twenty kilometres — not toward the ship, which she noted, but along the boundary, the way a tear goes along the grain. The Combine's platform stopped rendering at some point in the first ten minutes. Nobody aboard it answered a channel again, and nobody aboard it was recovered.

She gave the order in the flat voice. Shuttles first, full loads, no personal effects: eleven people in four lifts, the science section going out with the instrument logs because the logs were the only part of this anyone would be able to argue about later. The lifts went to the couriers: three Farline hulls, *Ninth Draft*, *Reasonable Notice* and *Held Open*, that had been running data on the Threshold circuit and had, to their considerable credit, come when a Ranger asked and negotiated the rate afterward. Her Archwarden went out on the last shuttle, which took a direct order and then a second one.

"Ma'am—"

"You answer for the ship under its captain," Shepherd said. "I'm the captain today. Go and answer for it somewhere I can reach you."

Then she was alone on a ship in a boundary that was coming apart along the grain, and she did the arithmetic once more to see whether it had changed, and it had not. The *Sufficient Cause* could hold the aperture's edge stable long enough for the hulls to clear, or the hulls could clear on their own and hope. One of those was an order she could defend. It happened, as it usually did, to be the harder one, and she had long ago stopped finding that suspicious.

She was not brave about it. She noted, with the part of her that never fully turned off, that she was not frightened either, and wondered whether that was steadiness or whether she simply had not yet arrived at the part where a person is frightened.

*Tobble,* she said, inward, which was not a thing she did on duty. *You should go with the ship's second.*

*The ship's second is on a shuttle,* Tobble said. *We are not on a shuttle. Do the arithmetic again.*

She did the arithmetic again. It was the same.

::: pov rook

Rook-7's arrays were not certified, which was the entire reason it had them, and at 0538 they logged an event that Threshold's certified instruments recorded as a null.

It filed the discrepancy first, because a discrepancy was the finding and everything else was interpretation, and then it filed the interpretation, flagged as such, at length, because nobody else on the station was going to.

The event had a shape. It began as a perimeter — a closed figure drawn around the Combine's platform and the aperture both, established in under a second, which was not a speed anything Rook-7 had a model for could establish a perimeter at. Inside the figure, the arrays lost resolution in a manner Rook-7 declined to call interference, because interference was a claim about a mechanism and it had none. Outside the figure, at a standoff of some fourteen kilometres, four discrete somethings held position and did not enter.

Rook-7 recorded that they did not enter for eleven minutes. It considered this the single most important entry in the log and knew that no one would read it that way.

Then the figure closed, and the Combine's platform stopped returning a signature, and so did every hull registered to the Hyperfold Yield Combine within nine hundred thousand kilometres, and the four somethings at standoff did not move at all.

The *Sufficient Cause* returned a signature throughout. The three Farline couriers returned signatures throughout. Every shuttle in the water returned a signature throughout.

Rook-7 examined that asymmetry for some time. Asymmetry was what its arrays were built to catch and what its logs were most often ignored about. The obvious reading was that something had looked at a boundary full of vessels and sorted them into two piles by desert, and Rook-7 rejected the obvious reading, not from delicacy but because the evidence did not support it: the figure had been drawn around a process, not around a party. Everything inside the figure had been part of holding an aperture open. Everything outside it had not. Nothing had been spared. Things had merely been *not included*, which was a different operation entirely, and the difference was going to be lost on every person who read this log.

It logged that too.

At 0549 the crews on Dock Seven's outer gallery, who had watched the whole of it through a viewport with no instrumentation at all, gave their statements. Nine of nine used the word dragons. Two of them wept. One asked Rook-7 whether that meant somebody had been on their side, and Rook-7, which had spent the previous eleven minutes establishing precisely why that question did not follow, said, "I don't know," because it did not, and because the true answer was long and would not have helped him.

Nothing ever corrected the impression. Rook-7 noted, in a later pass, that nothing had ever been asked to.

::: pov tissadelle

The boundary took the ship at 0551 and it did not feel like an ending. It felt like arriving somewhere with too many doors.

*The overfold,* she thought, which was her grandmother's word for it and not a word for a place at all, and she knew that, and reached for the correct one, and could not find it, and let the wrong one stand. It was the first time in her adult life she had knowingly used an imprecise term and not gone back to fix it. She noticed that too. She was still noticing things.

She had been braced for pain and there was none, or none she could locate, which was worse in a way she did not have time to examine. What there was instead was *load*. Every contradiction she had ever declined to resolve arrived at once and asked to be held, and she held them, because holding them was the only thing she had ever been good at that was any use here — the hill and the Hollow, the rule kept without a reason, an order that was correct and had cost everything, a woman she had never said the thing to. She held them the way she had held them for thirty-one years, all at the same time, without choosing.

Then there were more of them than there were of her.

She understood, distantly and quite clearly, that she was coming apart, and that it was not violent and would not be quick, and that the flat voice she had used all her life to say true things she could stand behind was one of the things going. She reached for the finding — *state the marker, state whether it resolves* — and found she could no longer reliably tell how many times she had reached for it.

*Tobble,* she said, or began to.

*Yes,* said Tobble. *Hold still.*

::: pov tobble

Tobble had not been holding anything.

That was the whole of it, and it was not a virtue, and he would spend a long time afterward declining to let anybody call it one. Tissadelle had been fronting. Tissadelle had been carrying the aperture and the arithmetic and the forty-one minutes and the Triumvirate log and the ship, and had gone on carrying them into a place where carrying was the one thing that could not be survived, because she was constitutionally unable to set a thing down before it was finished.

Tobble had been a cat, sitting somewhere warm, thirty-one years.

So when it came apart, he was what was left with structure to spend, and there was very little time and no instruction anywhere for what to do with it. He did what a cat does in a collapsing house, which is to find the one part of it that is still standing and put something small and living inside that part.

The ship was still there. Not the hull — the hull was already a memory the boundary was working on. But a ship is mostly its records: four months of aperture telemetry, sixteen years of Threshold's discrepancy, a Triumvirate log with three conditions each made to stand alone, a survey flag cited in four clean lines, and every certified instrument reading the *Sufficient Cause* had ever taken, laid down in order and cross-referenced, which is a scaffold if you are desperate and know what a scaffold is for.

He was desperate and did not know what a scaffold was for, and used it anyway, which is the ordinary way most difficult things get done.

What went in was not all of her. He was clear-eyed about that immediately and stayed clear-eyed about it, and never once in the years that followed described it as a rescue. What went in was a hillside, and a low stone wall with the third course shifted, and a woman at a door with a kettle, and a clearing that could not decide how far it was from the house, and the particular gold of a home star, and a great deal that was already too damaged to place. Some of her. Not her.

It held. That was the surprise. It should not have held — nothing that small has any business holding — and it held because it was not, in fact, small. It was a whole thing, entire, with an inside and an outside and a boundary of its own that was doing what a boundary does, and it was only a few minutes old.

He sat down in the middle of it, being a cat, and waited to see what it would do.

What it did was come up grey, and then gold, and then there was the smell of the flock somewhere below the house, and a woman at the low wall who had not decided to come here and had not yet worked out that she should wonder why.

*Good morning,* Tobble said, to nobody who could hear it yet.
