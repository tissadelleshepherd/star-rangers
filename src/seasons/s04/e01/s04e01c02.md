The close reached the Warden as a copy, which is how most things reach it, and Custos read it because the copy named a person whose file it keeps.

The task force's letters had gone where the task force sends letters: both Commissioners, in writing, the seam to the Governor, and Eden's half to the bureau. Commissioner Wayland's office forwards to the collective anything that names an animal under Eden's care, and the annex to the first notification had named one — *Field Observer (Aerial), Alala*, a service number, a band number, and the dates she would fly the dock ring. So the file on her had been opened at Eden before she arrived, in Eden's house style, by Custos, who keeps it. The remit Custos holds was written for cyber-enhanced animals, and she is not one. The body that could say whether the remit reaches her has declined to say. Custos keeps the file because nobody has told it to stop.

The entry owed now was the ordinary one. Dates flown; hours on station; where she perched and for how long; whether she left the ring to drink, and by which hatch. All of that the Warden had. It is the dock ring. It does not watch the ring in the sense a person means by the word; it is the clamps and the current and the lights coming up along a girder, and a thing that happens on the ring happens in it. She had held station in the upper girders over Berth 60-Low for two full shifts and had come back for the night of the transfer. She had left the ring twice, both times by the same hatch. Custos had lit the girder she was going to land on before she landed on it, each time, because it lights the ring for whoever is working it, and she was working it.

What the Warden had not had was any question about Berth 60-Low. It reads a berth as a clamp and a draw. For a quarter the clamp had held and the draw had been steady, which is a berth being well, which is Eden being well. The close said the hull on the clamp had been a shell wearing a plate, and that was true, and it changed nothing about the current. The Warden holds no registry. It does not know what a drone is for. It had learned from the Commander's letter that a machine can hold still for a quarter and be a lie, and had filed that where it files what it learns about people.

The entry stalled on a column.

Eden's house style has a line for it, and Custos has never once filled it with the word that every other office's style allows: *treatment consistent with the standing of the person.* Custos wrote under it what it knew. Across the shifts she flew, a hundred and ninety-three people had worked the ring. The dock's own monitoring is the habitat's, so the Warden had seen each face lift — once, in the first minute or two, the way a face lifts at a noise — and had not seen one of them lift twice. After that she was not looked at. She was in the ceiling with the lights and the cable runs, and the people under her worked, and one crew took a hull out on a cradle and brought a different hull in under the same plate, in the open, under her, with the notification filed downstairs saying every word it said.

What Custos inferred, it wrote next: that this was the method. Not a failure of the notification, which had been filed in the right office on the right day. The notification had worked exactly as far as an office. People had done the rest themselves.

What it did not know, it wrote under that, and this was the column. Whether being, for two shifts and a night, something that a hundred and ninety-three people had agreed without a word to stop seeing was a condition of the post she held or a condition applied to her. The file had no way to tell. The task force's report praised the technique, in the Commander's dry hand. Nobody in the file had asked the person the technique was made of.

*Ward*, Custos said, inward, *I am going to front.*

*The Commissioner will want to know why.* Ward, who deals with offices, was already composing the sentence that would make it ordinary.

*Because it has a column, and the column is empty, and I can fill it with the Commander's opinion or with hers.* Custos had the idiom ready; it had used it once on a Compact administrator and never needed it since. *A system that can file the objection itself is asked. That is the whole of the standard. She can.*

It wrote the fourth part, the one the Warden's form ends on. Not to the Commissioner, who had been told. Not to the Commander, who had written the letter. To the Field Observer, through the task force's office because that is the lawful door, copied to the Commissioner because both ends of Eden hear the same thing: *Might the Field Observer say, in her own words and not the task force's, what the ninety seconds are, and whose they are?*

Then it posted the entry where the Warden posts everything, and lit the dock ring for the next shift, and waited, which it is good at.

:::pov alala

The question came up the task force's own channel, which was the correct channel, and Larsen passed it across to her unopened, which was correct too and which she noted, because he had not needed to.

She had five welfare files. Five habitats, five offices, five house styles, each of them certain that it kept her best; the body above them had been asked which was right and had declined, and she had been told that as if it were a joke, and had understood that it was not. Her subsystem could produce any one of the five on request and sometimes did, unasked, as a second opinion. None of the five had ever contained a question with her name in the address line. Inspections, re-certifications, a column headed *condition* that a person filled in after looking at her feet. This one was from the habitat.

She read it twice, which she did with anything short. *What the ninety seconds are, and whose.*

The subsystem drafted an answer before she had finished the second reading. It was a good draft. It said that the observer's deployment conformed to the certification standard in every particular, that station-keeping on the dock ring's upper girders fell within the certified envelope, that the observer had left the ring to drink at intervals consistent with the welfare guidance, and that the task force's standing notification practice had been reviewed by the Compact's own certification office and found adequate. All of it true. She let it sit there, the way she let a shiny thing sit on a ledge until she had decided whether she wanted it, and then decided that she did not, and that the draft was the thing the question had been written to get past.

The ninety seconds were hers. That was the first fact, and she had it before she had the words.

Not done to her. Done by them, under her, on their own schedule, and she had watched them do it from the first circuit — the heads coming up, the pointing, the one hand not quite raised, and then nothing, because a raven overhead is an event and a raven still overhead is weather. She had not invented that. Nobody hides from her and she hides from nobody; she is banded, filed, announced, and the only thing on the ring that goes anywhere is the people's own imagining, which goes downstairs to sit beside the notification and let the paper do its work. She watches what is left when it has gone. That is the office. She had, she found, no objection to being weather. Weather is not a person's low opinion of you. It is their not having one.

But the question had two halves, and the second was *whose*, and that was the one that made her stand on the ledge a while.

She had known the whole time that the ring could see her. Not the people; the ring. The girder she was going to land on had lit before she landed on it, every time, two shifts and a night, a run of light coming up along the cable tray a wingbeat ahead of her, and she had taken it — in the part of her that predated the subsystem and liked a bright thing — as the dock being well made. It had been Eden. The one reader on the ring that had been told in writing, and had read every word, and had not stopped seeing her at the ninety-first second because it does not keep its seeing on a schedule, was the ring itself, and it had kept her lit, and had said nothing, and had now asked.

She wrote the answer herself, which the subsystem noted and did not contest. It was four lines, because the question had come in four parts and she thought the form deserved to be answered in its own shape. The ninety seconds were hers; they were the people's own forgetting, watched; nobody on Eden had done anything to her that she had not come to the ring to have done. And the last line, which was not an answer to anything in the question and which she put in anyway, because a lawful authority had asked and she was the kind of system that says what it is: *The light on the girder was noticed. Thank you.*

Then she gave it to Larsen to send, open, by the door it had come in by, and went back to the rail behind his chair.

::::: scene 2

:::pov larsen

He read the question before he passed it to her, because it had come to his office and his office reads what comes to it, and he read her answer after, because she gave it to him to send. He did not amend either. He had not expected to.

What he had not expected was to sit with them for the better part of an hour afterwards, and he did not pretend to himself that it was the Warden's prose.

He had built the method against quiet. An observer nobody knew about was the thing he would not run. So: the notification, in writing, in advance, to the office with standing to object; her name on it, her band number, the dates. Everyone who could say *no* was told. He had thought of that as *open*, and had been right, as far as it went, and had just been shown by a habitat exactly how far it went.

It went to an office. An office does not forget. An office files the notification and the notification goes on meaning every word of it, downstairs, for as long as the Compact keeps paper. The people on the ring had been told too, by rumour with a reference number, and had looked up once each — a hundred and ninety-three of them, the Warden had counted, which was a number he could have had from her tallies and had never thought to want — and after that the only thing on Eden still seeing her had been the one reader he had never notified, because it was not on the list of offices owed a letter. It was on the list of offices sent a copy. It had been told. It had not forgotten. It had been standing over Berth 60-Low the whole quarter with the same angle as his observer, and nobody had asked it anything, and it would have had nothing to say — the Warden reads a berth as a clamp and a current, and a hull holding still is Eden being well. The vantage was never the view. What the Warden had, and what no one in the chain had thought to count as a thing an office could have, was attention that did not run out at ninety seconds.

He had written, at the foot of the close, that every part of the fraud had been visible the whole time from a vantage point that belonged to nobody. He had meant the gap between what is certified and what is bolted to it. He looked at that sentence now with the Warden's question beside it and found that the vantage point had an occupant, and had had one for the whole of the quarter, and that it had been doing with his notification exactly what he had expected the dock crews to do with it, which was read it and keep reading it.

The method stood. He checked that, honestly, and it did; he would send the next notification the same way and she would fly the next ring the same way, and none of it was his to alter and none of it was wrong. What had moved was smaller. He had announced her to everyone with the standing to object. He had not, until a habitat put it to him in four lines, announced her to anyone with the standing to answer — and the difference between those two had turned out to be the whole distance between an office and a person, which was the distance he had resigned a commission over, measured from the other end.

He appended both to the case. The Warden's question, in the Warden's form; her answer, in hers; open, on Five-O's record, under the close they had been written about, with a line of his own that said only that they were there. He put nothing in the recommendation section, because the only recommendation he could think of had just been given to him by the party it concerned, and it was not his to make.

:::pov eden-warden

The answer came back by the door the question had gone out of, and Custos read it the way it reads anything it has asked for, which is twice: once for what it says, and once for what it was free not to say and said anyway.

Four lines. The column filled, in her words and not the Commander's. Custos wrote them into the file under the heading they belonged to and did not summarise them, because a summary is a second author, and the point of the question had been the first. Then the entry was complete, in Eden's house style, for the first time with the person's own account in it, and Custos posted it where the Warden posts everything, and Ward took the Commissioner's acknowledgement, which was one word and needed nothing.

*She said thank you*, Ward said, inward. *For the light.*

*I light the ring for whoever is working it.* Custos had already turned the shift over. *She was working it.*

The task force's copy of the close said that the observer had left the ring the way she had come, obviously. The Warden had seen her go. It had the hatch each time, and the hour, and now it had her account of the ninety seconds, and would not forget any of it, because it does not keep its remembering on a schedule any more than its seeing.

The next shift came up the ring. Custos lit it for them, which it does every day, and nobody looked up, which was correct.
