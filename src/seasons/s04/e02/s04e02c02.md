:::pov zara-wayland

The crossing came down to Wayland twice on the same afternoon, in two envelopes that disagreed about nothing.

The first was Larsen's own: the file as closed, copied to Eden's Commissioner because the sixth crossing had ended on Eden's dock ring, and the Commander copied a Commissioner anything that touched her habitat whether or not it needed her. The second was the quarter's disposition sheet, forwarded from Calloway's office in the office's flat register, every line resolved to one of the four words, with the one line in it that was none of them. She had seen the sheet's shape four times a year for as long as she had held the post and had never before seen it carry a blank.

She had been read in on the day of the opening. The notary had been her office's, and the notary's statement was in both envelopes: seal intact, seal's log intact, nine crates of ceramic feedstock consigned to Deck 9, manifest exact, nothing else. The notary had written *nothing else* and then initialled it, which Wayland noted, because a notary who initials a negative is a notary who expects to be asked about it later, and has decided in advance what the answer will be.

Then the two offices.

Her post ran upward on two lines, and her one rule for holding it was that neither end ever heard a version of events the other did not get. It was not an expensive rule, usually, but it was not free either. Calloway's office wanted a disposition and the Mayor's office wanted to know what a thing meant for the people on the deck it had happened on, and those are different questions, and the ordinary cost of the rule was two drafts and an hour of making one paragraph answer both without flattering either. She sat down to pay it.

She found there was nothing to pay. The file held a measurement and a second measurement; eight days in which two metrology offices had agreed with each other about everything except one night, and had carried their reference masses across to prove it; a cradle opened under witness and found to hold its manifest and nothing; and a line on the task force's own record, under the Commander's own name, that said *unresolved reading, no disposition* and declined to say more. There was nothing in it for Eden to charge. There was nothing to correct, because nobody had established which figure was wrong, and the Commander had been careful to say so. There was nothing to carry, because the task force had taken the carrying onto its own record precisely so that a habitat would not have to. The cradle had been delivered. The dock ring was as it had been.

She wrote one paragraph. She read it once as Calloway would, for what it disposed of, and once as Petrakis would, for what it left on Deck 9, and could find no sentence that served one reader and not the other. She sent it to both offices in the same minute, unchanged — which she did not usually manage, and which she noted, because she noted things that cost nothing in a post where most things cost something.

The Mayor's office answered in nine minutes, which it did not usually manage either, and asked her to come up.

:::pov imogen-petrakis

Petrakis read the paragraph twice, once as a Mayor and once as a resident, and the two readings did not agree, which was the usual condition of the job and the reason she had wanted it.

The Mayor's reading was that Eden had been handed nothing and owed nothing: a crossing had ended on her habitat's dock, the Compact's own task force had looked at it with everything it had, and had written down that it could not say. Clean. Wayland's paragraphs were always clean; it was the thing Petrakis liked least about them and relied on most.

The resident's reading was that a sealed cradle had arrived on Eden heavier than it had left Halcyon, by an amount two certified instruments could not carry, and nobody could say why, and the sentence that said so had gone up to the Governor with no word beside it, while the people on Deck 9 were not going to be told a thing.

She had been elected on a platform that amounted to *someone who reads the forms before they get filed*. She had read this one. What she had not expected was that it would hand her, in the Compact's own flat prose, the best sentence she had ever had for the one argument she kept losing.

She had asked the Compact three times to fund an independent review of Eden's monitoring capacity — of what the habitat could see for itself, beside the Passage, separate from whatever the Corps and the Defence Command ran for the fold route and did not share. Three times the budget committee had declined, courteously, on the ground that the Passage was monitored by the people chartered to monitor it. She had never had a fact to put against that. She had the *Patience First*, which was more than a century old, and a compliance framework of twelve requirements and counting, and a conviction, which is not a fact. Now she had a cradle. *A sealed consignment arrived on Eden heavier than it left, and no instrument the habitat holds can say why.* Every word of it true. It would move the committee. She knew it would, in the way she knew a quorum by looking at a room.

Wayland came up, and Petrakis did not offer her a chair, because Wayland never took one, and asked the question the paragraph had been built not to answer.

"Is it one of ours."

"Nobody is entitled to say so." Wayland had the file under her arm and did not open it. "The Commander declined to, in writing, and gave the reason: the crossing ran dock to dock, the instruments are the Compact's, nothing in it touched the Passage. He had the standing to write that sentence and chose not to. We have less standing than he has."

"I'm not asking what anyone is entitled to. I'm asking what you'd say to a fabricator on Deck 9 who wants to know why his feedstock was opened in front of two bureaus and a notary before he was allowed to have it."

"The same paragraph I sent you. I sent it to the Chief Commissioner as well." Wayland's voice did not change. "I don't have a third one, Mayor. I looked."

That was, Petrakis thought, the whole of it. She had the sentence. She also had a private rule, never stated in session, that a Mayor who asks every question she is entitled to ask stops being able to trust the answers she gets to the ones that matter, and she had found that it held for facts as it held for questions. A fact she was entitled to use was not a fact she was obliged to spend. Put the cradle in front of the committee and the review would be funded on a number nobody could explain, and the first thing an independent reviewer would ask, on the first morning, was what the number had to do with the monitoring they had been paid to review. The answer was nothing. The cradle had not come from beside Eden. It had come from Halcyon, in bond, through the Compact's own hall, on the Compact's own instruments, and the Compact had looked at it and said so. Attach that to the Passage and she would be doing with a Mayor's sentence what the task force had refused to do with a Commander's.

"The fabricator," she said. "Has he been told what the notary saw."

"He has the notary's statement. He also has a complaint in with the bureau, about five weeks of presses standing idle. That one is the Superintendent's."

Petrakis let the sentence go. She did not file it anywhere; a thing she decided not to say in session she did not keep for a later one. What stayed with her, after Wayland had gone back down, was smaller and worse. She had wanted an independent review because she did not trust the Compact to see what was beside Eden. It had just looked, as hard as it could, at the one object nobody had asked it to look at, and written down that it could not say — which was a finding, and an honest one, and exactly the finding she had been afraid a review would come back with.

::::: scene 2

:::pov rasa-oyelaran

The complaint reached Oyelaran by the ordinary route, which on Eden meant the Inspector's tray, three days late and at volume.

A fabricator on Deck 9 — a two-press shop that made ceramic parts for the ring's own maintenance crews, the kind of business a habitat does not notice until it stops — had waited five weeks and a day for nine crates of feedstock, had then had the crates opened in front of Halcyon's bureau, Eden's bureau and a notary before he was allowed to sign for them, and now wanted to know whom to bill for five weeks of two presses standing cold. Albercombe had read it as a contract matter and said so, in the tone he used for anything that was going to be exactly as boring as it read, and then, because he was the most reliable instrument the bureau possessed under the complaining, had added that the file attached to it was not boring at all and that he would rather the Superintendent decided how much of it the bureau wanted to be holding.

She read the file. She read the Commander's close first, because she had learned to read a Five-O close before the thing it closed, and found it was the shape she already knew from the Commissioner's paragraph: a cradle, two readings, three kilograms and four hundred grams, eight days, nothing. Then the chain of custody, which was the part that was hers. Certified at Halcyon in the quarter's eighth week. Certified at Eden the next day. Held in Eden's bonded hall from the day Halcyon's audit named the crossing — a loading supervisor charged on his own deck, and this cradle not one of his, which was the only reason anybody had looked at it twice — until the day it was opened under witness. Eight of those days the task force's. The rest the two metrology offices', carrying reference masses across to each other's docks and agreeing about them. Every day of it lawful. Nobody at fault anywhere in five weeks and a day, which is a rarer sentence on a habitat than people suppose.

Compensation needs a party. The file had measurements and no party.

She had her own view of Eden's fold-adjacent reports, which was more sceptical than the old committee's and a great deal faster: a report that sits unread for months is not a wrong report, only one nobody with authority wanted to be the one to act on. This was not that. It had been read by everyone. It had been read by two bureaus, two metrology offices, a Governor's task force, the Chief Commissioner and, she assumed, the Governor, and every one of them had done the honest thing, and the honest thing had taken five weeks, and the five weeks were the delay. She could not find a day of it to resent.

Albercombe had asked the fabricator one question, on his own account, because he could not help it. The feedstock had pressed clean, the man said, every crate of it, and had weighed what nine crates of that grade weighed on the shop's own scale. Albercombe had put that in the margin and underlined it twice.

Oyelaran did not put it in the file. A shop scale on Deck 9 reads weight, and weight on a spinning habitat is the one quantity the Compact declines to certify cargo against, for a reason the Commander's close had already stated in plainer words than she would have used. Two sound instruments had failed to agree about this cradle once. She was not going to be the officer who added a third, uncertified, from a workshop, and called it anything.

She wrote the disposition herself, which she did not usually do for a contract matter. *Closed. No party at fault. Delay arises from lawful holding in bond pending audit and verification; no compensation lies against the carrier, the habitat, the Compact or the task force.* It was the only line Eden's own record would ever carry on HC-2290. It took the one word the Commander had not been able to use, and it was true, and it was about two presses.

She put the fabricator's complaint in the tray for things that were finished, and the Commander's close in the other tray, the one she kept for things she intended to think about, where it joined a minute she had been meaning to think about since the spring.

:::pov zara-wayland

The bureau's closure came up through her office the way everything from the bureau did, and she appended it, identically, to the paragraph already on both desks.

Calloway's office acknowledged it without comment, which was how Calloway's office acknowledged a thing that needed nothing. The Mayor's office acknowledged it with one word, *noted*, which from Petrakis after a conversation in which she had plainly wanted something was close to a kindness, and Wayland took it as one and did not say so.

She looked at the two lines of her own file before she closed it. Both ends of the post now held the same two paragraphs in the same words, and she had not once had to choose a word for one reader over the other, because there had been nothing in either paragraph that either office could have wanted more of. The rule had cost nothing. It had never cost nothing before, and she understood, without needing to write it anywhere, that this was not because the rule had got easier.

Eden's own record said *closed*, and was about two presses on Deck 9, and was true. The rest stood on a record that was not hers, under a name that was not hers, and both her offices held it in the same words. She had never, in the post, had so little to say to either of them, and been so certain of every word.
