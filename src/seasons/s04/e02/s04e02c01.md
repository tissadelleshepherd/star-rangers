:::pov larsen

The referral came up the way the charter had imagined it would, which Larsen had learned to distrust slightly, on the grounds that a case arriving correctly is usually a case nobody has yet had to think about.

Halcyon's bureau had done its own work and done it well. A loading supervisor in the bonded freight hall had been opening sealed cradles, lifting the difference between what a manifest said and what a shipper would miss, and resealing with a bond stamp he had no business holding; the bureau had him, had the stamp, had thirty-one crossings' worth of audit behind him, and had charged him on Halcyon's own deck without once needing to ask anyone's permission. That was the whole of the bottom rung working as designed. A habitat bureau runs its own deck. It is good at it.

What came up to Larsen was the remainder. Of the thirty-one audited crossings, twenty-five fitted the supervisor's method exactly — same hall, same hours, same shortfall against the manifest, the arithmetic of a man taking what he thought he could take. Six did not. And one of the six had ended at Eden, which put a line between two habitats through the middle of the file, and a line between two habitats is the one part of a case a bureau cannot finish. Halcyon's Detective Superintendent had written four sentences and sent it up. Commissioner Duarte's office had countersigned without comment, which from Duarte was practically warmth.

Larsen notified both Commissioners in writing the same afternoon, because he always did, and because the habitats' bureaus were not suspects and were owed the truth about what was moving through their halls.

Five of the six went where remainders usually go. Two were transcription — a clerk entering a certified figure from the cradle above the one in front of her, twice, four months apart, which is not a crime and is not interesting and is exactly the kind of thing an audit finds when it finally looks. One was an honest error at the hall's end of the night, logged as an error at the time by the man who made it, which Larsen noted with a certain respect. Two were a second person running the supervisor's trick less competently, and went back down to Halcyon to be charged there, because they had never crossed anything.

The sixth was a cradle.

HC-2290, consigned Halcyon to Eden, bonded, sealed, and certified in the ordinary way. A bonded cradle's mass is not weighed — a habitat's gravity is a thing the habitat is doing, a product of its spin and its radius and the particular floor you happen to be standing on, and the Compact declines to certify cargo against a construction. So the cradle is clamped, oscillated against a calibrated spring, and timed, and what comes out is its inertial mass, which is a property of the thing itself and does not care where it is or how fast anything is turning.

Halcyon's instrument had read it at one thousand nine hundred and four kilograms, even, at the quarter's third day. Eden's had read it twenty-three hours later at one thousand nine hundred and seven kilograms, four hundred grams.

Each instrument is certified to one part in four thousand. Two honest readings of the same cradle may therefore differ by about a kilogram, and these differed by three point four, and the difference ran the wrong way for every explanation the file already contained. Larsen sat with that for a while. A thief takes. Nothing a thief does adds three kilograms to a sealed cradle in transit, and nothing in thirty-one crossings of a loading supervisor's small greed had prepared a single line of the file to hold a cradle that had arrived heavier than it left.

:::pov dorian-calloway

Calloway read all five habitats' Commissioner reports himself, first pass, every quarter, on the theory that a coordination office which stops reading its own inputs closely enough to catch the small discrepancies has already failed at the one thing it exists to do. It was a good theory. It had the defect of occasionally working.

The quarter's close is a disposition exercise. Every line in it resolves to one of four words — *charged*, *corrected*, *closed*, *carried* — and the office's entire function is that by the time a report reaches Governor Voss, there is nothing in it that a Governor has to decide. Four words, nine thousand bonded crossings, five habitats, one afternoon. It is not glamorous work and Calloway had never once pretended to want other work.

Duarte's office had got there before him, helpfully, the way Halcyon's office was always helpful. The Eden figure, it suggested, could be *corrected* to Halcyon's: one instrument, one entry, one amended line, and a discrepancy becomes arithmetic and arithmetic becomes a disposition. Calloway read the suggestion twice and did not like how much he wanted it. Correcting a figure is a claim that it is wrong. Nobody had established that either figure was wrong, and a correction entered to produce a disposition is a coordination office writing fiction into the Compact's own metrology record in order to have a tidy afternoon.

The second suggestion came from inside his own office, from a deputy who meant it well and had the structure exactly at her fingertips. Eden is a Class I Fold-Proximate Research Platform. There is a fold route through its neighbourhood, there is a service with charter standing over that route, and that service has desks, instruments and whole offices for readings nobody can account for. Write the cradle up as an anomalous reading at a fold-proximate habitat, have Five-O invite the Rangers, and the number stops being the Compact's.

It was, Calloway thought, the single most attractive sentence anyone had put in front of him in nine quarters. An open remainder is the one thing his office genuinely cannot file. An invitation is a door: the paper goes through it and does not come back, and whatever is written on it is thereafter somebody else's to be wrong about.

He did not write to Larsen suggesting it. He had no say in where Larsen went and had been careful for two years never to behave as though the question were open. He wrote asking for a disposition, in the office's own flat register, and left the four words where Larsen could see them, and the door where Larsen could see that too.

::::: scene 2

:::pov larsen

He spent eight days establishing that there was nothing to establish, which is a use of eight days that no disposition column has a word for.

Both instruments were tested. Halcyon's and Eden's, each against the reference mass its own metrology office keeps for the purpose, which is the check a bureau would have run on its own and which proves only that an office agrees with itself. So Larsen had each habitat's reference mass carried to the other habitat's instrument and run there, which no bureau would have thought to arrange and no bureau could have arranged, and which is, when he put it plainly to himself, the entire reason a task force exists. Both instruments read both references correctly, before the crossing and after it, to a fifth of a kilogram. The cradle was re-run on each of them, loaded and then empty, and the two instruments agreed with each other about it on every subsequent occasion anyone asked.

HC-2290 was opened on Eden's dock ring under both bureaus' witness and a notary of Wayland's office. The bonded seal was intact; the seal's own log was intact and dull. Inside was the manifest — ceramic feedstock in nine crates, consigned to a fabricator on Deck 9 that had been waiting on it for a month — and nothing else. Not three kilograms of anything. Not three grams.

So the record stood like this, and Larsen wrote it standing like this, because the shape was the finding and anything added to the shape was an invention. One object. Two instruments, both sound, verified sound in both directions by the other habitat's own standard. Twenty-three hours between the readings. A difference neither instrument's tolerance will carry, in a direction nothing in the case can use. One of the two figures was wrong once. Nothing in the file says which one, and nothing in the file says why, and in the quarter's remaining bonded traffic — some nine thousand crossings, two of those instruments among them every day — it had not happened again.

Which left the door.

Larsen looked at it for most of an evening, and did not pretend to himself that he was weighing it. He knew what an invitation was. The Rangers hold no authority inside a self-governing habitat and come only when invited, and the invitation is not a request for an opinion: it is a statement, made by a Governor's task force on the Governor's authority, that a thing has stopped being a Compact matter and is the Corps'. A boundary reading is something instruments record. This was two instruments declining to agree, which is not the same thing and does not become the same thing by being inconvenient, and the crossing itself had run dock to dock inside the Compact and nowhere near the Passage. Eden's second chain was a fact about Eden. It was not evidence about a cradle.

He had resigned a commission over a wall, eleven years ago, and had spent two years since holding the standing he had resigned for want of. He could send the letter tonight. Nobody would stop him; nobody would even think it odd. And it would be a sentence he could not stand behind, sent because he would like the answer to be somebody else's, which is the thing he had always assumed the wall was for.

He filed it instead. The supervisor's twenty-five, charged. Halcyon's second man, charged there. Two transcriptions and an honest error, corrected. And the sixth crossing as an *unresolved reading, no disposition*, under his own name, on Five-O's record rather than either habitat's, with every measurement appended and no account of them offered — because the office that holds a thing it cannot explain should be the office that can afford to hold it, and a habitat bureau carrying an open metrology finding into its next certification cycle cannot.

The last line was one sentence, and he wrote it for the one reader who would understand that it was not a complaint. *I can see the sentence that would make this go away. I am not entitled to it.*

What stayed with him afterwards was not the cradle, which he expected never to understand and had stopped expecting to. It was that he had become, in the quarter-inch where it mattered, the thing he had resigned over: a man with the standing to ask, declining to ask. He had been certain for eleven years that the wall was dishonest. It turned out that the wall had been the honest part, and that what he had actually resigned over was that nobody on either side of it had been willing to say so.

:::pov dorian-calloway

The report reached Calloway on the quarter's last afternoon, resolved line by line into the four words, with one line in it that was none of them.

He had expected to be annoyed and found that he was not, and spent a few minutes working out why. His office existed so that nothing arrived at the Governor's desk as a surprise. What Larsen had sent was not a surprise. A surprise is a thing somebody has and has not told you. This was an absence — fully measured, honestly bounded, and empty in the middle — and an absence cannot ambush anyone, which is precisely what the Compact's own metrology record would have been unable to say about a corrected figure.

He forwarded it to the Governor unamended, and without a covering note, which he had not done in nine quarters. Any note he could write would have said more than the paper did.

He did read the last page once more, the one with the measurements on it and nothing under them, and allowed himself the private observation that the Compact had been promised a commander who would take down a jurisdictional wall and had been given one who could tell the difference between a wall and a door, and had just declined to walk through a door that was standing open with nobody on the other side of it to object.

He did not write that down either. The quarter closed on time, with one line in it that said nothing at all, and said it correctly.
