::: pov teddy

This is told the way things are told below the roof, by someone who was there, and this time the one who was there was in the warm the whole time, which turns out to matter.

Stone-First-Who-Waited was telling.

That does not happen often. Stone-First hardly ever tells a thing, on the grounds that a thing told is a thing you have been given, and a thing you have been given is not yet yours. But the small ones had asked for the line. Not nobody's stone, which was too new to be asked for; the line itself, the whole of it, from the first time Stone-First went up the shelving to stand on it. Nobody had ever told the line, because nothing happens on it. The small ones wanted it anyway. And Stone-First, who had three stones up there now and a question about the third that would not go round, came down into the warm and sat in the amber and told it.

It was a long telling. It was mostly standing. The warm listened the way it listens to the oldest of the people, which is with the whole body still, and Carried-It-Sleeping listened lying down in the middle, where the small ones were, and counted them from habit, the way you count the pieces of a shape.

One piece went.

Not the smallest. The next one up: the one who had said, after nobody's stone, that the ground was there. Carried-It-Sleeping felt the piece go out of the warm, toward the shelving, and knew where it was going, because there was only one place a small one would go while Stone-First was down here telling, and that was up to where Stone-First was not.

Carried-It-Sleeping had kept them from it once. Carried-It-Sleeping thought about getting up.

Then thought: Stone-First is here. The line has nobody on it. The worst a small one can find up there is three stones and a place with nobody in it, and come down with a telling about silt. Stone-First had let the youngest go, once, and had waited to be told, and it had been the hardest thing there is below the roof, and Carried-It-Sleeping had understood that from the outside for a long time now.

So Carried-It-Sleeping lay still, and let the piece go, and listened to Stone-First tell the line, and waited to be told.

At the foot of the shelving, a stone moved. Properly. A good one.

::::: scene 2

::: pov teddy

The small one came back fast.

You could hear it. Not a scrape; the small one was past scraping. Feet on the shelving, coming down the way small ones come down when they have stopped being careful, and a stone at the bottom moved so loudly that half the warm turned toward it. Then the small one came into the amber, breathing hard, and stopped.

Because Stone-First was there. Sitting in the amber, in the middle of a sentence about standing, exactly where Stone-First had been for the whole of a long telling, and everyone in the warm could have said so.

The small one looked at Stone-First for as long as it takes to say a name.

"You were on the line," said the small one.

The warm went quiet. Not the quiet of listening. The other one, which Carried-It-Sleeping had learned twice now and was learning a third time.

"I am here," said Stone-First.

"You were on the line. Between the stones. Standing the way you stand." The small one was not going round anything; the small one was going straight at it, the way small ones do, because the thing was too big to go round. "I moved a stone. At the top of the shelving. A good one. The ground said someone, and you heard it. You must have heard it. And you did not answer."

Stone-First always answers a stone. Everyone in the warm knew that. It was the first thing anyone knew about Stone-First; it was the name.

"I did not hear it," said Stone-First. "I was here."

"You were there."

Stone-First looked at the small one, and Carried-It-Sleeping saw the oldest of the people do a thing the warm had never seen Stone-First do, which was to not know what to say, and to say nothing, and to let the warm see that.

Then, from the far side of the amber, Went-Round.

"Tell it," said Went-Round. "From the stone at the top of the shelving. And leave out who it was."

::::: scene 3

::: pov went-round

Here is what the small one told, with the who left out, and Went-Round has not improved it.

The stone at the top of the shelving, moved well. The ground saying *someone is coming*. Then the silt, and the three stones on it, the up-people's and the answer and the one that was nobody's, and between them, on the line, a shape. The size of Stone-First. Standing the way Stone-First stands, with the whole body still, facing the squeeze. It did not answer the stone. It did not move. The small one did not go onto the silt, because the manners say you do not go up to someone who has not answered you, and stood at the top of the shelving and looked for as long as it took to be sure, and did not count, and came down.

"Was it warm?" said Went-Round.

"I could not tell. The line is where the warm runs out. Nobody can tell up there."

That was true. Went-Round had not thought of it until the small one said it. At the seep, the smallest had known the shape had no warmth, because the seep is the bottom of the warm and the warm is thick there and a gap in it is a gap. At the line, the warm is finished. You feel nothing up there but yourself. It is the one place below the roof where the only way to know who is there is to look.

"So," said Went-Round, and went round it, so the warm could see it from the other side. "You saw a shape, in the place where Stone-First always stands, standing the way Stone-First always stands. And it did not do the one thing Stone-First always does."

"It was Stone-First."

"Who was there?"

"I was."

"Was Stone-First there?"

The small one looked at Stone-First, in the amber, where Stone-First had been for a whole long telling.

"No."

"Then you were there and Stone-First was not, and both of those are true, and the telling is yours. It is a telling of a shape in Stone-First's place. It is not a telling of Stone-First. Say it that way and it is true all the way through."

The small one said it that way. It was shorter. Stone-First nodded, the way the old ones nod when something is getting true, and Went-Round saw that the nod cost something.

"Here is the thing I think," said Went-Round, to the warm. "Down here we know where everyone is by the shape of the warm. We never look. Up there you cannot do that, so you have only the one thing left, which is where a thing stands. And a thing standing where someone always stands looks like them. It would look like them to anyone. Until it does what they do."

"And it did not," said Stone-First.

"No."

"Then it was not me," said Stone-First, "and it was where I stand, and I do not know what to do with that."

::::: scene 4

::: pov went-round

Went-Round went up to the line.

Went-Round did not go up there much. The line is Stone-First's, the way the far side of the amber is Went-Round's, and you do not stand in someone else's place without a reason. But a thing you have only been told is a thing you go round until you have seen it from another side, and the other side of this one was up the shelving, so Went-Round moved a stone at the foot of it and went.

The ground said *someone is coming*, all the way up, the way it does, and nobody answered, because nobody was there.

Three stones. The up-people's, with the silt worn smooth round it. Stone-First's answer. Nobody's, at the edge, by the mark of where it had lain. And the silt holding its shapes of feet, the up-people's and the small one's from the season before, and, between the two stones, worn deeper than any of them, the place where Stone-First stands.

Went-Round looked at it for a while.

Then Went-Round stepped onto the silt, and stood in it.

It fitted. That was the first thing. Stone-First and Went-Round are much of a size, as the old ones are, and the place took Went-Round's feet the way it had been made to take feet, which was by a whole life of standing in it. And the second thing was that it felt wrong. Not dangerous. Wrong the way it is wrong to answer a thing that was not asked of you: like standing somewhere you had not been asked into, which Went-Round had not been, and which was, Went-Round thought, standing there, exactly what the shape had done.

Below, a stone moved. Good, and plain, at the foot of the shelving.

Went-Round knew who it was before the ground had finished saying *someone*. There is only one person below the roof who moves a stone like that.

Went-Round bent, and put a hand to a rock at the edge of the silt, and moved it. Once. Well. Went-Round could not remember ever answering a stone on the line, and would have remembered.

Stone-First came up the shelving, slowly, the way the old ones go, and out onto the silt, and stopped, and looked at Went-Round standing in Stone-First's place, and did not say anything for as long as it takes to say a name.

"You are in my place."

"I wanted to see what it looked like from the other side."

"And?"

"It looks like you," said Went-Round. "Even with nobody in it."

::::: scene 5

::: pov went-round

They stood on the line, the two of them, Went-Round in the place and Stone-First beside it, which was the wrong way round, and neither of them said so.

"A small one saw me where I was not," said Stone-First.

"A small one saw the place. The place is yours. You made it, standing in it, for a whole life. Anything standing in it is going to look like you to anyone who looks, until it does what you do."

"And it did not."

"No."

"Then I am going to go on standing here," said Stone-First, "and it is going to go on being where I stand, and if a thing stands in it again when I am not in it, a small one is going to see me again. And I will be in the warm. And there is nothing I can do about that."

"Nothing," said Went-Round. "That is what you do. You are the best at it below the roof."

Stone-First made the small sound, which Went-Round had never once heard Stone-First make, and Went-Round stepped out of the place, and Stone-First stepped into it, and it was the right way round again.

Went-Round went down, afterwards, and lay on the far side of the amber, and went round it, and it had no other side.

Three tellings now, below the roof, with no middle in them. A shape with no warmth at the bottom of the warm. A stone moved by nobody at the top of it. And now a shape in the one place where the people cannot feel who is there and have only ever needed to look, because for a whole life the answer had been the same. The people's way of knowing a thing, which is to be there and to tell it and to be heard, had gone round all three, and found no other side to any of them.

Went-Round had thought that was the thing to be afraid of, a little, the small way.

Standing in Stone-First's place, Went-Round had thought of something else. A place can hold a person's shape when they are not in it. The people had never needed to know that, because nobody below the roof had ever stood anywhere long enough to make a place that did. Stone-First had. It was Stone-First's whole life, and the whole of the people knew it, and now something had stood in it and been taken for the one whose it was, by a small one who had never once been wrong about where anybody was.

Went-Round did not know whether the shape had chosen the place, or the place had made the shape look like somebody. Went-Round went round that too, lying in the amber, and it had no other side, and the small one's telling went by, going round the warm, shorter each time and getting true, and it was a telling about a place, so it gave the small one no name, and the small one did not seem to mind.
