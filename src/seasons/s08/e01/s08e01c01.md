:::pov brother-fintan

The third hive went wrong at dusk, and Fintan heard it before he reached the stand — which is to say, he heard it the way a keeper hears, in the register just below attention, where a sound is not yet a fact but has stopped being nothing.

A hive in order hums in one voice. Not one note — a hive is thousands of wings and no two of them agree — but one *voice*, the way a congregation is one voice, the way a station's instrument deck, to a man who has stood thirty years of watches over one, is one voice: a body of small independent honesties summing to a single testimony. The third hive's testimony had a seam in it tonight. A half-tone, low in the chord, of the kind you could stand beside for a minute and talk yourself out of.

He set down the smoker and did not use it. He stood.

This was the discipline, and it had cost him more to learn than any qualification the Safety Corps ever printed for him: to attend before acting, and — harder — to attend without requiring the thing attended to explain itself on his schedule. The Corps had trained the opposite reflex into three decades of him. An anomaly opened a procedure; a procedure closed an anomaly; the interval between was a fault to be minimised. It was honest work and he did not repent of it. But it had made him a man who reached for the smoker, and the hill had spent ten patient years unmaking that man, one dusk at a time.

The bees did not explain themselves. The seam in the chord persisted — not worsening, not resolving, steady as a decided thing — and Fintan stood in the cooling air with his hands at his sides and let it be the whole of what he was doing.

He thought, as he always thought at such moments, of the clock.

He had never served at Threshold. He knew its forty seconds the way the whole Corps knew them, as a filing: an instrument discrepancy, eleven years old, faithfully wrong in plain view of every competent person on the station, while each of them assumed the watching was someone else's post. He had been retired nine years before he understood that the story was not about the clock. It was about the standing. Thousands of them, and the discrepancy patient in front of them all, and the asking waited eleven years — not for a cleverer instrument, but for somebody whose office it was to treat a small steady wrongness as worth his whole presence.

On the hill, that office was his. It had the size of the third hive and the salary of nothing, and he had come to believe it was the largest work he had ever held.

The light went. The seam held. He marked the hive for the morning — no smoke, no opening, a cloth weight on the stand rail, the keeper's equivalent of an annex nobody else would read — and climbed to the lodge to keep the other watch, the one with the older name.

:::pov hive-bee

The cold had come and the thousands had drawn in, and she was where she was: one warmth among the warmths, one wing in the great slow beating that kept the middle alive.

The comb carried the sound of everyone. It always did. Comb is where the sound lives — wood on the outside, sisters on the inside, and the long waxen middle that holds every wing's small saying and gives it back as one saying. She did not listen to it. Listening was not a thing done. She was *in* it, the way she was in the warmth, and the saying came through her feet and her folded wings and the touching of the sisters on every side.

There was a wrong place in the saying.

Not near. Somewhere down-comb, where the warmth thinned — a place where the one saying had a gap in it that beat along with everything else, steady, keeping time, wrong. It was not hers. Nothing was hers alone; that was the whole of how the thousands worked. It was everyone's, a small cold seam passed wing to wing through the dark along with the warmth and the sound and the smell of the stores, carried the way everything was carried: together, without being solved.

Through the wood, from the outside, a heaviness came. The big slow warmth that came at dusk — tread, tread, gone still. It stayed a long time. It did not open the world or send in the smoke. It stood, the way the thousands stood, being there.

Then the heaviness went, and a small weight settled on the wood above, and the night went on. The wrong place kept its time. The wings kept theirs. She beat when the beating reached her, and passed it on.

::::: scene 2

:::pov brother-fintan

The night office at Cnoc na mBeach is said alone, which the Communion permits and Fintan has stopped apologising for. A watch is not less a watch for having one watchman. Most of them, in the record and in the Scriptures alike, have exactly one — that is generally the difficulty being recorded.

He lit no lamp but the one. The psalter on the table fell open where ten years of the same hour had taught it to fall: *my soul waiteth for the Lord more than they that watch for the morning.* More than they that watch for the morning. He had kept morning watches on three stations and a boundary cutter, and he knew precisely how much watching for the morning a body could hold, and the psalm's arithmetic still exceeded him. That was why he read it. A text you have finished is a text that has finished with you.

He prayed for the watchers. This was the night office's particular intention, settled years ago, the evening he understood what his retirement was *for*: for everyone posted, this hour, to a small steady wrongness. For the ones standing instrument watches over readings that would not resolve. For the ones at boundaries — the record's word, and a truer one than the record knew — keeping presence beside discrepancies that had not yet consented to become explanations. For the ones who had stopped seeing the forty seconds on their own deck, as he had stopped, for years, on his; that something — he asked it plainly — something would trouble their attention awake before the eleventh year.

And for himself, the standing question, the one from the garden: *could you not watch one hour?* Not solve. Not produce. Watch. The disciples had slept, and the text is gentle with them and terrible with him, because they did not know what the hour held, and he — a man with the whole record on his shelf — has no such mercy available.

The hive's half-tone was still in his ear. He did not pray it away. He prayed it *in* — set it on the table beside the psalter, the night's one instrument reading, and offered the attention rather than the anxiety, which he had learned late are different sacrifices, and only one of them is asked for.

Before the Amen, he kept the forty seconds.

Deliberately forty; counted, the first years, on his Corps-trained pulse, and now simply known, the way the psalter knows its page. Forty seconds of full silence for the discrepancy itself — the one at Threshold, the one in the third hive, the one in him that he had watched for a decade now and would not name even here, patient as an unread instrument, faithful as a wrong clock. Forty seconds of doing, on purpose, the thing the thousands did not do: attending, in person, to the small wrong thing, and asking nothing of it yet.

The silence ended. He said the Amen into it.

Outside, below the lodge, the hives held their voices under the cold — two in order, and one keeping, steady in the dark, its own small vigil of being wrong; and the Keeper of Cnoc na mBeach banked the lamp and went to sleep like a man expected at a post in the morning.

Because he was. That was the whole of it, and it was enough: he was.
